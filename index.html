Venture Global lines up up to 11.5 GW of gas turbines for behind-the-meter data centre power

Energy

Venture Global signed frame agreements with GE Vernova and Baker Hughes for up to 16 more 720 MW combined-cycle power islands, targeting up to 10 GW of compute capacity by end-2034. Roughly $17.2 billion is contemplated if all orders proceed, with the first purchase order expected by end-2026.

Why it matters: Gas-fired on-site generation is becoming a core route to AI power, with carbon-capture economics still unproven.
Source → (DataM Intelligence (Oct 19; deal Oct 15))

AEP, Amazon and X-energy explore 960 MW SMR plant at Indiana coal site

Energy

The project would use 12 X-energy Xe-100 reactors of about 80 MW each at the Breed Generating Station in Sullivan County, Indiana. Amazon would buy the output under a long-term contract if built; no timeline was disclosed. AEP expects Indiana peak load to rise from 2,800 MW to over 7,000 MW by 2030.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are backing new-build nuclear on retired coal sites to secure baseload power.
Source → (Utility Dive (Oct 19))

Anthropic signs first Canadian data centre lease at EverWind's Nova Scotia campus

EnergyCanada

The lease covers a 95 MW first phase at Point Tupper, with a further 325 MW expansion planned for 2028. EverWind's investment exceeds C$10 billion, including C$2 billion for onshore wind, and it says it will cover grid upgrade costs. Nova Scotia's NDP leader criticized the lack of public consultation.

Why it matters: It anchors one of Canada's largest AI campuses and ties data centre growth to wind power.
Source → (Data Centre Magazine (Oct 16-17))

Nvidia unveils DSX POD, a prefabricated AI data centre using dry coolers

Tech

The containerized module packs seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks and Nvidia says it cuts deployment at a prepared site from about six months to as little as 14 days. 17 partners including Dell, HPE, Schneider Electric and Vertiv plan availability in early 2027. A related report says it uses dry coolers instead of evaporative cooling.

Why it matters: Factory-built pods could compress build timelines and reduce water use where power and sites are ready.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 21))

TechEnergy

Vantage secured more than 1 GW of manufacturing capacity for Heron's Heron Link, a 5 MW modular converter that Heron says exceeds 98.5% efficiency from medium voltage to 800 VDC. The claims come from the company and have not been independently tested.

Why it matters: It signals a shift to 800 VDC power architecture for hyperscale AI campuses.

NERC stakeholders approve reliability standards for AI data centres and crypto miners

Policy

The standards cover loads of 50 MW or more connected at 100 kV or higher and still need a NERC Board vote expected in Q4 plus FERC approval. They follow a May Level 3 alert over data centre load losses; NERC forecasts summer peak demand up 224 GW over 10 years.

Why it matters: Ride-through and reporting rules will shape interconnection for every large campus.

Amazon drops Ashburn campus plan under a deal with Loudoun County

PolicyConstruction

Amazon withdrew its application for about 800,000 sq ft of data centres on the former GW campus. The site will be rezoned for mixed use, Amazon will sell about 25 acres to the county for $65 million and give $5 million to a housing trust fund. Loudoun is considering a one-year pause on approvals.

Why it matters: Opposition is now winning concessions even in the world's densest data centre market.

Policy

Why it matters: Memphis, home of xAI's Colossus, becomes a test case for local limits on data centre growth.

Can evaporative cooling in data centres still make sense?

Tech

A 1 MW IT load can use 20,000 to 50,000 gallons of water a day with evaporative cooling. Operators choose open-loop for upfront cost, estimated 60-70% below closed-loop, while critics say closed-loop wins on lifecycle cost. One expert expects a hybrid future alongside liquid cooling.

Why it matters: Water use is pushing cooling design choices as community scrutiny grows.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 23))
