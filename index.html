X-energy and AEP to study 960 MW SMR plant in Indiana to power Amazon

Energy

A joint development agreement covers feasibility work on 12 Xe-100 small modular reactors, each about 80 MW, at the retired Breed Generating Station in Sullivan County, Indiana. Amazon is funding the work in milestones and would sign a long-term power contract if the plant is built. It would be Amazon's second nuclear commitment after a 690 MW Constellation deal on September 30; no cost or construction date is published.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are now underwriting new-build SMRs on retired coal sites, not just buying existing nuclear output.

Microsoft to fund 1,100 MW of Indiana generation under 20-year NiSource deal

Energy

Microsoft will finance roughly 1,100 MW of new generation and transmission and buy the output over 20 years through NiSource for its $1 billion La Porte campus, with service starting in 2028. NiSource estimates about $1.75 billion in savings for existing NIPSCO customers, and a separate 600 MW / 2,400 MWh battery agreement depends on contracts closing by January 31, 2027.

Why it matters: The 'bring your own power' model, with ratepayer savings as the selling point, is becoming the template for large-load deals.

Google expands Voltus flexible-capacity deal to more than 1 GW in PJM

Energy

Google will buy 1,070 MW of additional flexible capacity from Voltus in PJM, bringing the total above 1 GW on a contract running through 2040. Voltus aggregates batteries, smart thermostats and industrial processes into a virtual power plant. PJM's demand response revenue rose 240% from 2024 to 2025, to $523.5 million.

Why it matters: A gigawatt of demand flexibility is a new way to add data centre load without building as much new supply.

Nvidia unveils DSX POD in Berlin as Greenpeace protesters interrupt keynote

Tech

The prefabricated modular reference design lets partners such as Dell, HPE, Schneider Electric and Vertiv build containerized Vera Rubin data centres, cutting deployment from about six months to as little as 14 days. Nvidia says it uses dry coolers rather than evaporative cooling to address water concerns, and units can be placed at brownfield sites such as old warehouses. Greenpeace protesters interrupted the keynote.

Why it matters: Factory-built AI capacity could compress timelines, while the protest shows growing public pushback.

Can evaporative cooling still make sense in data centres?

Tech

Evaporative cooling can use roughly 20,000 to 50,000 gallons of water per day per 1 MW of IT load, but open-loop systems can cost 60% to 70% less upfront than closed-loop ones. Experts disagree on whether open-loop designs will disappear as chips run hotter or give way to hybrids; climate matters heavily.

Why it matters: Water and energy trade-offs in cooling design increasingly drive siting and permitting.
