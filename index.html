AI data centres are fueling a nuclear comeback, with about $20B of new nuclear spending in 2026

Energy

Industrial Info Resource expects nuclear new-build spending of about $20 billion in 2026 and nearly $50 billion by 2029, with $68.3 billion in new plant spending from late 2026 through 2029. Deals include a $5 billion DOE loan to Constellation for uprates adding over 1 GW, Amazon's 1 GW SMR project in Indiana, and the Three Mile Island and Duane Arnold restarts.

Why it matters: Nuclear is moving from pledge to financed build-out, though fusion is not expected before the mid-2030s.

Google and Constellation sign 20-year nuclear deal adding 890 MW to the PJM grid

Energy

Signed Oct. 12, the agreement adds 890 MW through upgrades at 11 existing nuclear units in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, requiring over US$4.3 billion from Constellation. A separate 15-year deal covers 2,700 MW from Constellation's existing PJM fleet, with first new capacity expected by 2028.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are paying to expand existing nuclear output rather than only buying what already exists. Older than 24 hours.

Crusoe debuts Spark Liquid, a 3.5 MW liquid-cooled modular data centre unit

TechConstruction

The sixth-generation modular platform supports up to 3.5 MW per unit and is designed for Nvidia GB300 NVL72 and Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with closed-loop direct liquid cooling. Crusoe claims five times the density of earlier generations, with production in Brighton, Colorado starting in 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built, liquid-cooled capacity is a route around construction delays for dense AI racks.

4

Can optical AI cut inference power use? Lumai tests Iris Nova servers in a UK lab

Tech

Oxford spin-out Lumai uses light for matrix multiplication in the prefill stage of inference, leaving decode to GPUs, in standard air-cooled racks. The ARIA-backed trial aims to produce system-level performance data; the article gives no quantified power savings.

Why it matters: Early-stage and UK-based, but it points to one possible path to easing inference power demand globally.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 6))

Trump's onsite power push meets AI data centre reality

PolicyEnergy

On Oct. 21 President Trump posted that large new data centres should generate their own power, return excess to the grid and consider paying residents a dividend. Some turbine models are reportedly sold out through 2031; Stargate Abilene needs 1.2 GW and Applied Digital has arranged 400 MW of stranded wind for North Dakota.

Why it matters: A stated federal expectation of self-supply collides with equipment backlogs; it is not formal policy.

AI's expansion hits the limits of local power as build times stretch past two years

EnergyConstruction

GlobalData says projects that broke ground between Q3 2025 and Q3 2026 waited just over two years after announcement, versus about 18 months before. It forecasts data centre electricity use rising from 590 TWh in 2025 to 1,520 TWh in 2030, though IDC's Luis Fernandes sees no hard evidence yet that constraints are limiting AI.

Why it matters: Grid access, not capital, is increasingly the schedule driver.

CanadaPolicy

Liquid cooling is changing what data centres need to measure

Tech

Operators need instrumentation for coolant temperature, pressure, flow, level, leaks and quality (pH, conductivity). A common practice is to keep coolant supply at least 2°C above the dew point to avoid condensation, and AI clusters can swing from idle to full load in minutes.

Why it matters: Monitoring is becoming a reliability requirement as liquid cooling becomes standard.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 4))

Liquid cooling retrofits: hard, but sometimes worth it

TechConstruction

Most existing sites were not designed for liquid cooling; AI racks over 3,000 pounds can strain raised floors and downtime can run to months. Retrofits make most sense for selective high-density racks, single-tenant sites or water-constrained regions.

Why it matters: Most of today's installed capacity faces this upgrade decision.

PolicyTech
