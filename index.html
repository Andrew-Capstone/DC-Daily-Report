Tucker County, West Virginia residents push back on a proposed 10,000-acre gas-powered data centre

PolicyEnergy

The proposed natural gas-powered campus would, if completed, be the largest data centre in the world, according to the report. Local tourism operators say it threatens their livelihoods, and residents say they had no opportunity to weigh in. Governor Patrick Morrisey supports the project. A recorded hot mic has the developer, Fundamental Data, saying it helped write the state bill that enabled it; Morrisey denies that. Public filings show air permits were approved August 15.

Why it matters: It shows opposition moving to rural, gas-powered megaprojects, and to questions about how state law was written. The governor's office says "nothing's been approved," which conflicts with the permit record.

2

Government of Canada adds 19 signatories to its Responsible Data Centre Development Principles

CanadaPolicy

Ottawa launched the national framework on September 3, 2026 with five expectations for data centre projects, aimed at helping municipalities assess proposals. The framework commits projects to protecting electricity ratepayers, limiting water use and delivering lasting local benefits. Nineteen more organisations have now signed, bringing the total to 42. Reported signatories include AWS, Anthropic, Bell, Cohere, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and TELUS.

Why it matters: It is a national framework that municipalities can use to assess proposals, and it lands as public support for the buildout is reported to be slipping.
Source → (Government of Canada, Sept 2026)

4

Google and Constellation agree a 20-year deal for 890 MW of new nuclear capacity in PJM

Energy

The capacity comes from uprates at 11 existing Constellation nuclear units across six sites in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, rather than new reactors. Constellation says it will invest more than $4.3B, and the companies also reported a separate 15-year supply agreement for 2,700 MW in PJM. Reporting describes the deal as a direct response to PJM's bring-your-own-power proposal.

Why it matters: It is a large, grid-connected nuclear offtake for AI load. The first uprate timing differs across sources: Constellation says 2028, while ESG Today reports 2032.
Source → (American Nuclear Society, Oct 6, 2026)

Blue Energy and GE Vernova Hitachi advance a 2.5 GW gas-plus-nuclear plant in Texas for a Crusoe campus

EnergyTech

The planned plant at Victoria, Texas would supply about 1 GW from gas turbines to a Crusoe data centre in 2030, followed by up to 1.5 GW from as many as five BWRX-300 small modular reactors starting in 2032. A final investment decision is expected in 2027.

Why it matters: It is a concrete template for pairing fast gas power with SMRs for AI load, though the nuclear capacity is years away. The publication date could not be confirmed, because the page returned an error when opened.

Policy

Lincoln Institute commentary says that on November 3 data centre development became a ballot question rather than a zoning detail. A KPTV report says voters curbed development in 46 of 47 local data centre ballot measures nationwide, citing Ballotpedia. Why it matters: Opposition is now showing up in elections and zoning, not only in hearings. Ballot counts differ across sources (an earlier search cited 34 measures), so verify against Ballotpedia before citing the number.

The $40B Aligned Data Centers acquisition by AIP, MGX and BlackRock's GIP closes

Finance

The consortium bought 100% of the equity from funds managed by Macquarie Asset Management, valuing Aligned at about $40B enterprise value. It also committed $5B in growth capital. Aligned has 51 campuses and more than 6.4 GW of operational and planned capacity, and the existing CEO and Dallas headquarters stay in place.

Why it matters: It is the largest data centre M&A deal on record, and it closed in July after being announced in October 2025. It is outside the 24-hour window.

North American data centre debt hits a record $115B year to date while M&A lags

Finance

Mergermarket data counts $115B of debt issued across 66 North American data centre transactions year to date, including new construction as well as M&A, the highest on record. Its analysts say transformative M&A is largely on hold as owners keep assets longer amid high valuations.

Why it matters: Financing, not ownership change, is driving the buildout, which means lenders now carry more of the sector's risk. The page is undated in search, so check its publication date before citing.

13

Virginia's Data Center Accountability Framework sets five pillars and ends state subsidies for data centres

PolicyEnergy

Governor Abigail Spanberger announced the framework on September 18, 2026 through Executive Order 22. Its five pillars are community transparency, environmental protection, energy affordability, clean energy and grid reliability, and workforce development. It would end state subsidies for data centres in site development programs and remove large data centres from fast-track permitting. The order also bans data centre NDAs and creates an AI Task Force. It stops short of a moratorium.

Why it matters: It ties data centre incentives to community and ratepayer terms. Several parts need legislation in the 2027 session.
Source → (WJLA, Sept 2026)
