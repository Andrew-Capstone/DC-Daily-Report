Google signs 15-year take-or-pay deal for 396 MW of Fervo geothermal power in Utah, with an option toward 1 GW

EnergyTech

Data Center Frontier reports that Google will buy 396 MW from eight generating units at Fervo Energy's Cape Station in Beaver County, Utah, under a 15-year take-or-pay contract. First power is expected in mid-2028 and full contracted capacity by the end of 2028; Google can raise purchases to nearly 1 GW by June 2030, with the additional power deliverable directly to a proposed campus. Cape Station's first 33 MW unit entered commercial operation on Sept 30, and the campus itself still depends on approvals and a Google decision.

Why it matters: Next-generation geothermal moves from pilot to a near-gigawatt, around-the-clock supply contract for a data centre.

TELUS withdraws from two planned Vancouver AI data centres; Westbank to continue with a new partner

CanadaConstruction

DataCentre.ca, citing Daily Hive and both companies, reports that TELUS has decided not to proceed with co-developing a 10-storey facility beside BC Place and the conversion of Westbank's M3 office building in Mount Pleasant. Westbank says it will continue both with a partner it has not named. TELUS had secured an initial 85 MW from BC Hydro for a three-site network expected to exceed 150 MW by 2032, and is still expanding its Kamloops site to 20 MW by the end of 2028. No reason was given.

Why it matters: A flagship urban Canadian AI project, built around heat reuse for district energy, loses its telecom anchor six months after it was announced.

SpaceX says its proposed 6 GW Arizona campus will use dry cooling and no industrial groundwater

TechEnergyPolicy

SpaceX says the roughly $50 billion campus in Cochise County would have nine computing buildings and up to 6 GW at full build-out, cooled by air-based dry coolers instead of evaporative towers. It says it is working with local electric cooperatives and that members' bills could fall by up to 20%, a company claim that has not been independently verified. A petition with about 2,400 signatures asks the county to reject the project, and KVOA reports the Planning and Zoning Commission hearing is set for Dec 9.

Why it matters: Water-free cooling and a bill discount are becoming the opening offer for a mega-campus in an arid county, and the county has not yet approved it.
Source → (Interesting Engineering (Nov 12))

Federal agency: Meta's 1 GW Alberta data centre is too far along to be designated for impact assessment

CanadaPolicy

The Impact Assessment Agency of Canada decided on Nov 10 that construction at Meta's Sturgeon County campus has substantially begun, so the environment minister cannot designate it under the Impact Assessment Act. The request, received Sept 4, also sought a joint assessment with the proposed Greenlight Electricity Centre, a gas-fired plant the agency had already found needed no further assessment. Meta broke ground on July 8 on the 1,750-acre site and expects to invest more than $13 billion.

Why it matters: Canada's largest data centre project clears a federal review risk, and the decision shows that designation requests must arrive before construction starts.
Source → (DataCentre.ca (Nov 11))

SpaceX reported to be building its own 'Kessel' version of Nvidia's Vera Rubin rack

Tech

Baxtel, relaying a Nov 11 SemiAnalysis report, says SpaceX is developing a custom version of Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack for orbital and terrestrial data centres. It keeps the 72-GPU layout but replaces the internal cable cartridges with an 84-layer passive circuit board midplane, with Nvidia supplying chip packages and SpaceX building the rest. Neither company has commented, and the details come from a single research firm.

Why it matters: A large buyer designing its own rack around Nvidia's chips would take on validation and supply-chain risk that Nvidia normally carries.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 12))

Bell sets common power and liquid-cooling standards across seven planned AI Fabric data centres

CanadaTech

DataCentre.ca reports that Bell plans to apply common engineering standards for power and cooling across its AI Fabric network, with liquid cooling in the design and combined power-and-cooling tests before each site enters service. Bell is hiring Calgary-based directors for engineering and procurement and for commissioning; specific power sources were not named.

Why it matters: Bell is moving from announcements to the engineering and commissioning staff needed to deliver gigawatt-scale Canadian capacity.

Mavria raises $130M at a $1B valuation for 5 MW factory-built data centre units

TechFinance

Bisnow reports, citing The Wall Street Journal, that Texas startup Mavria, founded by three former US Department of Energy officials, raised $130 million from investors including Paradigm. Its Terrain unit packs power distribution, liquid cooling, controls and racks into a 5 MW module delivered by truck, to be clustered or placed where smaller pockets of grid power exist. The 35-person company is not yet producing at scale; first units are being built in Houston, with testing planned for 2027.

Why it matters: Capital keeps flowing to factory-built capacity that can use small grid connections rather than wait for large ones.

10

Analysis: Southwest Power Pool's uncapped curtailment terms could push data centre flexibility into private utility deals

EnergyPolicy

A Latitude Media analysis examines the Southwest Power Pool's CHILLS service, approved by federal regulators in June, which gives large loads non-firm transmission for up to seven years while they wait for firm service. The grid operator's filings set no annual cap on curtailment hours and no minimum notice. The author argues this open-ended risk will lead hyperscalers to keep negotiating bounded deals directly with utilities, as Google did for about 1 GW of demand response with five utilities.

Why it matters: How flexibility is contracted decides whether the wider grid, or only one utility and one customer, benefits from it.
Source → (Latitude Media (Nov 12))

ICTC report: Alberta holds 92% of Canada's planned data centre capacity, a pipeline above 12,000 MW

Canada

A report from the Information and Communications Technology Council estimates Alberta has 78% of planned Canadian data centre developments and 92% of planned capacity by megawatts. It says a single 400 MW AI data centre could generate more than $800 million in labour income over 10 years and that about 260,000 Albertans already work in relevant construction and trades occupations. It recommends a workforce observatory to track demand and skills gaps.

Why it matters: It puts numbers on how concentrated Canada's build-out is in one province as Alberta prepares rules to screen speculative projects.

Loudoun County board to take up a pause of up to 12 months on data centre and substation applications on Tuesday

Policy

Loudoun County, Virginia supervisors are scheduled to consider a resolution on Nov 17 that would pause board action on legislative applications for data centres and electrical substations for up to 12 months, the longest period Virginia law allows. The county says the time would be used to finish revised data centre standards, a review expected to conclude in summer 2027. The item was deferred from Oct 20, and the pause would not cover projects needing only staff approval.

Why it matters: A pause in the world's largest data centre market would be a strong signal to other jurisdictions, even with its narrow scope.
Source → (Loudoun County (Oct 20 notice; vote Nov 17))

Final count: voters approved 44 local measures restricting data centres, 21 of them in Ohio

Policy

A Fox report citing Rosebud Consulting research says voters approved 44 ballot measures restricting data centres in the November midterms, nearly half of them in Ohio, with others in Kansas, Illinois, Wisconsin, Maine, New Jersey, Florida, California and Nevada. Harwood, North Dakota was the only community to back a pro-data-centre measure. An October Ohio Northern University poll found 55% of Ohio adults oppose data centre construction in their communities.

Why it matters: The tally has risen from the 40 of 41 first reported, confirming a near-unanimous result at the local ballot box.

Rochester, Minnesota council to vote Monday on a data centre moratorium of up to one year

Policy

City staff have proposed an interim ordinance pausing new data centres and related facilities such as generators and substations, saying the development code does not clearly regulate them. Minnesota law limits such an ordinance to one year, and a public hearing is set for Monday at 6 p.m. Rochester Area Economic Development Inc. has written to the council opposing the pause, arguing the review can proceed without it.

Why it matters: Even mid-sized cities with no project before them are pausing first and writing rules second.

Pew: six in 10 US adults are uncomfortable with a new data centre nearby; two-thirds of planned sites are rural

PolicyEnergy

Pew Research Center reports that the US had more than 3,000 operating data centres and at least 1,500 in development as of February, with two-thirds of planned facilities in rural areas. The share of adults calling data centres mostly bad for the environment rose from 39% in January to 54% in August, and for home energy costs from 38% to 50%. Pew cites data centre electricity use of 183 terawatt-hours in 2024, projected to reach 426 by 2030.

Why it matters: Opposition is rising in both parties and in urban, suburban and rural areas alike, which narrows the list of easy sites.
Source → (Pew Research Center (Nov 11))
