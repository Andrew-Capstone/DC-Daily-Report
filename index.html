Anthropic signs first Canadian data centre lease at EverWind's C$10B Nova Scotia campus

CanadaEnergy

Anthropic signed a lease of at least 20 years for the first phase of EverWind's Point Tupper campus, an 18,600 square metre building due in late 2027. EverWind plans over C$10 billion of investment, with a further 325 MW targeted for 2028 and up to 420 MW in total, plus battery storage and curtailment at grid peaks.

Why it matters: It anchors one of Canada's largest AI campuses and ties data centre growth to Atlantic Canada's wind resources.
Source → (Data Centre Magazine (Oct 16-19))
2

Nvidia unveils DSX POD, a prefabricated Vera Rubin AI data centre module

Tech

The containerized module integrates seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with networking, cabling and cooling, and Nvidia says deployment at a prepared site drops from about six months to as little as two weeks. Seventeen partners including Dell, HPE, Schneider Electric and Vertiv plan availability in early 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built pods could compress build timelines, provided power and sites are ready.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 21))
3

xMEMS begins mass production of solid-state micro-cooling chips

Tech

xMEMS is in mass production of its XMC-2400 and XMC-4800 silicon chips, which move air without blades or bearings to target hotspots in SSDs, memory, retimers and optics that cold plates miss. The company positions them as a complement to rack-level cooling, strongest first in enterprise SSDs and edge AI.

Why it matters: Hotspot cooling for components beyond the GPU is a growing thermal bottleneck.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 28))
4

Google expands Voltus virtual power plant deal to more than 1 GW in PJM

EnergyTech

Google will buy 1,070 MW of additional flexible capacity from Voltus in PJM, on top of 100 MW committed in June. Voltus pools batteries, thermostats and industrial load, under a contract running through 2040.

Why it matters: Data centre developers are paying to create grid flexibility instead of waiting for new generation.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 23))
5

AEP and X-energy study 960 MW SMR plant to power Amazon in Indiana

Energy

X-energy and AEP will study an advanced nuclear plant at a retired coal station in Sullivan County, Indiana, with Amazon funding development work. It is at study stage only and no timeline has been disclosed.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are backing new-build nuclear on retired coal sites to secure baseload power.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 20))

Microsoft to fund 1,100 MW of Indiana generation under 20-year NiSource deal

Energy

Microsoft will pay for all generation and transmission serving its La Porte, Indiana campus under a 20-year agreement with NiSource, with service starting in 2028.

Why it matters: It is a template for large users funding their own grid build-out while protecting ratepayers.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 19))
7

Venture Global lines up gas turbines for behind-the-meter data centre power

Energy

Venture Global signed frame agreements with GE Vernova and Baker Hughes for up to 16 more 720 MW combined-cycle power islands, targeting up to 10 GW of compute by end-2034. Roughly $17.2 billion is contemplated if all orders proceed.

Why it matters: Gas-fired on-site generation is becoming a core route to AI power.
Source → (DataM Intelligence (Oct 19))
8

Hydro-Québec defends proposal to roughly double electricity rates for data centres

CanadaPolicyEnergy

Hydro-Québec proposes a new rate for data centres over 5 MW averaging about 13 cents per kWh, roughly double the current large-power price. Hearings before the Régie de l'énergie began in early October, with Google among the parties questioning the utility's consultants, and continue in December.

Why it matters: Quebec's cheap hydro has drawn data centres; a rate reset could redirect Canadian investment.
Source → (CTV News (early Oct))
9

Alberta's large-load allowance is fully subscribed as data centre requests top 19 GW

CanadaEnergyPolicy

Alberta's Data Centre Regulation, in force since June, favours projects that bring their own generation, and the interim 1,200 MW allowance for new large loads is now fully taken. AESO's list showed roughly 19,565 MW of requested data centre load as of July 30, and a proposed interruptible 'bridging' pathway was still in draft.

Why it matters: Alberta is Canada's most active test of how a grid can absorb AI-scale demand.
Source → (Government of Alberta (current page; request figures as of Jul 30))
10

Canada plans C$1.5-1.85B, 60 MW secure data centre near Ottawa for CSE

CanadaConstruction

Defence Construction Canada posted an advance procurement notice on October 7 for a 60 MW facility for the Communications Security Establishment. Procurement is expected in winter 2027 and the government has not yet approved proceeding.

Why it matters: A sovereign-compute build is a rare public-sector data centre project in Canada.
Source → (BNN Bloomberg (Oct 15))
11

Oregon pauses air and water permits for new data centres until July 2027

Policy

Gov. Tina Kotek directed the state environmental agency on October 9 to pause permits for new or expanded data centres through July 1, 2027, a de facto moratorium. Amazon said it was disappointed, while Food & Water Watch called it a major victory.

Why it matters: The largest state-level restriction yet, in a market home to major hyperscale campuses.
Source → (OPB (Oct 13))

Hyperscale AI spending tops $700 billion, but gas turbines are sold out through 2029

EnergyPolicy

Citing Industrial Info Resources, announced spending by Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Google should exceed $700 billion this year. The US has 53 GW of operating data centre load, and larger gas turbines are sold out through 2029 with $228 billion of gas plant projects in play.

Why it matters: Equipment shortages and opposition, with $130 billion of projects rejected in Q1, are now the binding constraints.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 27))
13

Microsoft exits Nscale's UK data centre deal over power delays

Finance

Microsoft ended its agreement for Nscale's planned Loughton, England site after grid officials reportedly said power would not arrive until the 2030s. Nscale, which says it remains committed to the site, is preparing a New York listing and also works with Microsoft and Anthropic in the US.

Why it matters: A global story with North American relevance: grid delays are a risk for neocloud developers that also build in the US.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 19))

Finance

Why it matters: Weaker public-market appetite could tighten financing for the North American builders that rely on it.
