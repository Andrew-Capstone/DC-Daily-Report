Alberta readies legislation to block speculative data centre proposals and projects too close to homes

CanadaPolicyEnergy

Technology minister Nate Glubish told The Logic that Alberta is preparing rules to discourage data centre projects that lack substance, such as those without customers or financing, and those sited too close to homes or on farmland. Options include province-wide setbacks for data centres and their power plants; he cited the Synapse project in Olds, where regulators rejected a power plant planned 30 metres from homes. The bill could be tabled as early as next week, and its text is not yet public.

Why it matters: Alberta's grid operator is tracking about 28 GW of proposed data centre demand, up from about 20 GW at the end of 2025, so a filter on speculative projects reshapes the queue.

Fermi signs early agreements with an unnamed hyperscaler for 600 MW at Project Matador; TensorWave lease terminated

EnergyFinance

With its Q3 results on Nov 12, Fermi said an undisclosed investment-grade hyperscaler signed a $200 million prepayment agreement and a $15 million early construction agreement to advance an initial 600 MW at its Texas campus beginning in 2027. A long-term lease and a $90 million steel purchase agreement are still being negotiated. The August lease with TensorWave and the related AMD credit support were terminated; Fermi remains pre-revenue and reported a $141.6 million quarterly net loss.

Why it matters: A customer paying before a lease exists shows how scarce powered sites are, but Fermi still has no signed long-term tenant.
Source → (Fermi 8-K exhibit, SEC (Nov 12))

Nova Scotia Power agreement makes EverWind responsible for added system costs from its Point Tupper data centre

CanadaEnergyPolicy

CBC reports that a service contract and letter agreement signed in September make EverWind responsible for any incremental costs to other customers while its $8 billion Point Tupper campus is on an interim industrial tariff. Service is interruptible, with demand cuts possible on 10 minutes' notice, and an early estimate puts system upgrades at $74 million or more. Nova Scotia Power says a new tariff is needed, and the Nova Scotia Energy Board must approve how costs are tracked and paid; no timeline is set.

Why it matters: An early Canadian test of the principle that data centres, not ratepayers, pay for the grid they need.
Source → (CBC News via Yahoo Canada (Nov 13))

Drogue raises $20M to power floating data centres with ocean thermal energy

TechEnergyFinance

TechCrunch reports that Washington, D.C. startup Drogue raised $20 million from investors including XYZ Venture Capital and Toyota Ventures. It plans to generate power from the temperature gap between warm surface water and cold water about 1,000 metres down, using a sealed ammonia loop, with seawater also cooling the servers. A multi-megawatt prototype is to go to sea on a ship in early 2028; the company has approval in principle from the American Bureau of Shipping. No exact capacity was given.

Why it matters: Ocean thermal power is unproven at scale, but it is another sign of capital chasing ways around land, grid and water limits.
Source → (TechCrunch (Nov 13))

Alaska approves 50-year state land lease for a gas-powered data centre campus on the North Slope

EnergyPolicyConstruction

Alaska's Department of Natural Resources approved a 50-year lease of about 715 acres for the STAK Energy Campus near the Dalton Highway, which would pair air-cooled data centres with a multi-gigawatt power plant burning locally produced gas. Initial site development is estimated at $500 million and construction at six years. The department received more than 1,200 comments, most opposed; the decision has a 20-day reconsideration period, and federal wetlands and state air permits are still required.

Why it matters: Stranded Arctic gas and cold air are being pitched as a data centre resource, off any public grid.
Source → (Alaska Beacon via Anchorage Daily News (Nov 11–12))
6

Oregon regulators approve tariff making new 20 MW-plus data centres in Pacific Power territory pay their full cost

PolicyEnergy

The Oregon Public Utility Commission approved a Pacific Power tariff on Nov 13 that creates a separate customer class for facilities able to use 20 MW or more for computing. Those customers must sign long-term service agreements and pay for all the infrastructure and power services they require. The tariff implements the state's 2025 POWER Act and takes effect Jan 1, 2027.

Why it matters: Large-load tariffs are becoming the standard regulatory answer to cost-shifting concerns, state by state.
Source → (KTVZ (Nov 13))

GridAI agrees to buy Wyoming gas producer Pronghorn Resources, targeting 300 MW of AI power in 2027

EnergyFinance

Why it matters: A small company buying its own gas supply shows fuel ownership becoming part of the data centre power pitch.
