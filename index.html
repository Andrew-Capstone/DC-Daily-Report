Microsoft named as the company evaluating a phased 1.4 GW data centre campus on Monarch land in Rockford, Illinois

ConstructionEnergy

The City of Rockford said on Nov 13 that Microsoft is doing due diligence on land controlled by Monarch along Edson and Friday roads, for a multibuilding campus planned in phases up to 1,400 MW. Microsoft has not submitted a proposal or site plans, and power is unresolved: ComEd cancelled its transmission security agreement with Monarch in July and the site is now in a later study process. A state agreement signed in June requires at least $250 million of investment and 100 jobs, and city staff say the local moratorium does not cover the land because of an earlier annexation agreement.

Why it matters: A hyperscaler is evaluating one of the largest Midwest campuses, but ComEd still has to confirm how and when the site can be powered.

Blackstone's data centre REIT agrees to pay about $5B for 75% of 13 AWS powered shells in Indiana and Mississippi

Finance

Bloomberg reports that Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust, which listed in May, will take a 75% stake in two joint ventures owning 13 newly built powered shells on AWS campuses in northern Indiana and Mississippi, with AWS keeping 25%. AWS will lease back the entire portfolio for an initial 12 years, with extensions up to 32 years, and the joint venture has a debt commitment for half the purchase price. It is the vehicle's first investment and is expected to close later this year.

Why it matters: Selling stakes in finished, leased-back campuses gives hyperscalers a way to recycle capital into new construction.
Source → (Yahoo Finance / Bloomberg (Nov 16))

Latitude Media data: utilities have signed contracts for only 22% of the 537 GW of data centre load in their pipelines

Energy

Data released by Latitude Media on Nov 13 and reported by Tech Times covers 53 US service territories: about 120 GW is under contract against 537 GW reported, which is still about 5.5 times the 22 GW operating today. Georgia Power reports 80.1 GW but has signed 12.8 GW, and Dominion has about 12 GW under service agreements out of a 54.9 GW pipeline. Utilities are soliciting about 37 GW of new generation, and Amazon holds an option to cut 1,200 MW from its NIPSCO contract by 2029.

Why it matters: Utility resource plans and rate cases depend on which pipeline numbers are firm, and most of them are not.
Source → (Tech Times / Latitude Media (Nov 16))

Prologis appeals Mississauga's data centre moratorium to the Ontario Land Tribunal

CanadaPolicy

Global News reports that Prologis has challenged the one-year ban Mississauga council passed unanimously in September, one of the first in Canada. The company applied in 2025 for a 220,000-square-foot, two-storey data centre near Tenth Line and Argentia Road. It argues the council acted on ratepayer pressure rather than planning grounds and says it has lost two potential clients to the delay, while the city is preparing its response. Ontario's economic development minister says the province has received about 100 data centre submissions.

Why it matters: The tribunal's ruling could decide whether other Ontario municipalities can use moratoriums to stop data centres.
Source → (Global News (Nov 16))
5

Delta brings an 800 VDC, 2.5 MW in-row liquid CDU and 800 VDC-ready cooling to SC26

TechEnergy

Delta's release says it will show an 800 VDC in-row liquid-to-liquid coolant distribution unit rated at 2.5 MW at SC26 in Chicago from Nov 17 to 19, along with 800 VDC and ±400 VDC power architectures. It also lists a 4U in-rack CDU of up to 200 kW, a 2 MW open in-row CDU for Project Deschutes, a liquid-to-air CDU of up to 60 kW per rack for Google's OCP Project Brazos, and a 40 mm cold plate that handles 3,000 W of compute. These are product announcements, not deployments.

Why it matters: Cooling and power hardware is being designed together for 800 V direct-current racks, which cut conversion losses at megawatt rack scale.
Source → (HPCwire (Nov 13))
6

nVent takes pre-orders for a 2.3 MW CDU and shows an 800 V DC power rack for existing AC halls

Tech

nVent said on Nov 16 that production of its CX121 modular coolant distribution unit, rated at up to 2.3 MW, begins in late 2026 and that it is taking pre-orders at SC26. It is also showing a MAVERICK rack that converts existing AC power to 800 V DC without redesigning the facility's power system, and a liquid-to-air CDU that needs no facility water. nVent says it has deployed more than 4 GW of liquid cooling and is adding its rear-door heat exchanger to AMD's Helios rack platform.

Why it matters: Converting AC to 800 V DC at the rack would let existing data halls take next-generation AI racks without a full electrical rebuild.
Source → (StockTitan / nVent release (Nov 16))

AWS breaks ground on $7B Project Rise campus in Indiana, next to a NIPSCO plant, with new gas generation planned

ConstructionEnergy

AWS has started construction on a 304-acre site in Wheatfield, Jasper County, bought from NIPSCO about half a mile from its R.M. Schahfer station. NIPSCO is separately building a natural gas plant and a peaker plant to serve the campus. AWS will pay $98 million in taxes upfront and about $700 million over 30 years, and will put $25 million into public water infrastructure. Cooling is mostly outside air, with water used about 2% of the year.

Why it matters: It is part of about $33 billion AWS plans in Indiana, where NIPSCO's signed data centre load already exceeds its system peak.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 16))

Why it matters: Former bitcoin miners are now building AI capacity for chipmakers under long leases.

Dell launches what it calls the first direct-liquid-cooled storage for rack-scale AI

Tech

Dell announced at SC26 on Nov 16 a 2-OU storage platform with full direct liquid cooling and up to 32 NVMe drives, along with a 1U platform with 67% more drive density per rack unit. It also launched services to design AI data centres around power, cooling and space requirements, available in the fourth quarter. The release gives no kilowatt or cooling-capacity figures.

Why it matters: Liquid cooling is moving beyond GPUs to storage, which keeps more of the rack out of the air-cooled heat load.
Source → (HPCwire (Nov 16))

12

Oregon's Maxine Dexter introduces a bill to ban new data centres on federal lands

Policy

H.R. 11002, introduced Nov 11, would bar the development, sale or transfer of Forest Service and Interior Department land, including BLM land, for data centres, and land used that way after transfer would revert to public ownership. It has 13 Democratic cosponsors and the backing of nine conservation groups. Dexter's office counts at least 17 campuses proposed on public land in six western states, while the Energy and Defense departments have been offering federal sites.

Why it matters: It directly opposes the administration's use of federal land for AI sites, though it is unlikely to advance in this Congress.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 16))

Calvert County, Maryland holds a hearing tomorrow on banning data centres in every zoning district

Policy

The planning commission takes testimony on Nov 18 on Text Amendment 26-31, which would remove data centres from the I-1 and I-2 industrial zones and prohibit them anywhere in the county. County commissioners hold a separate hearing and the final vote. A six-month moratorium runs to mid-February, and Amazon has already dropped plans for three data centres near the Calvert Cliffs nuclear plant.

Why it matters: A full countywide ban next to a nuclear plant would close off one of the region's few sites with large firm power nearby.

Why immersion cooling is still a niche: about $1M per MW against $650K for direct-to-chip

Tech

A Data Center Knowledge analysis cites estimates of about $1 million per MW of IT load for immersion against $650,000 for direct-to-chip, though 10-year costs for a 64-rack AI cluster favour immersion at $28 million, against $31 million for direct-to-chip and $42 million for advanced air. It argues the bigger barrier is operational, since immersion requires new layouts, maintenance and training, while direct-to-chip keeps the conventional rack model.

Why it matters: Direct-to-chip looks set to remain the default for AI halls, and that affects which cooling suppliers win orders.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 17))

Pitt County, North Carolina approves a two-year data centre moratorium

Policy

WITN reports that commissioners voted unanimously after a public hearing to pause construction, development and permitting of data centres while the county studies their impact. Residents cited noise, water use and power demand. Neighbouring Beaufort County approved a moratorium in October, and other eastern North Carolina counties are considering similar measures.

Why it matters: Moratoriums are spreading across eastern North Carolina, a region developers had been looking at for land and transmission.
