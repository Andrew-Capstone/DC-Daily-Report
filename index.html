Google expands Voltus virtual power plant deal to more than 1 GW in PJM

EnergyTech

Google will buy 1,070 MW of additional flexible capacity from Voltus in the PJM region, on top of 100 MW committed in June. Voltus pools batteries, thermostats and industrial load into a virtual power plant, with Google funding it under a contract running through 2040.

Why it matters: Data centre developers are paying to create grid flexibility instead of waiting for new generation.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 23))
2

AEP and X-energy study 960 MW SMR plant to power Amazon in Indiana

Energy

X-energy and AEP will study an advanced nuclear plant at a retired coal station in Sullivan County, Indiana, with Amazon funding development work. It is a study stage only and no timeline has been disclosed.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are backing new-build nuclear on retired coal sites to secure baseload power.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 20))
3

Anthropic signs first Canadian data centre lease at EverWind's Nova Scotia campus

EnergyCanada

EverWind announced more than C$10 billion (about US$7.1 billion) in private investment for a Point Tupper campus, and Anthropic signed a long-term lease for the first phase. Timelines are developer projections.

Why it matters: It anchors one of Canada's largest AI campuses and ties data centre growth to Atlantic Canada's power resources.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 19))
4

Nvidia unveils DSX POD, a prefabricated Vera Rubin AI data centre module

Tech

The containerized module packs seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with networking and liquid cooling pre-integrated, and Nvidia says deployment at a prepared site falls from about six months to as little as 14 days. Partners including Dell, HPE, Schneider Electric and Vertiv plan availability in early 2027; Greenpeace protested at the unveiling.

Why it matters: Factory-built pods could compress build timelines, provided power and sites are ready.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 21))

Microsoft to fund 1,100 MW of Indiana generation under 20-year NiSource deal

Energy

Microsoft will pay for all generation and transmission serving its La Porte, Indiana campus under a 20-year agreement with NiSource, with service starting in 2028.

Why it matters: It is a template for large users funding their own grid build-out while protecting ratepayers.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 19))

Venture Global lines up up to 11.5 GW of gas turbines for behind-the-meter data centre power

Energy

Venture Global signed frame agreements with GE Vernova and Baker Hughes for up to 16 more 720 MW combined-cycle power islands, targeting up to 10 GW of compute capacity by end-2034. Roughly $17.2 billion is contemplated if all orders proceed.

Why it matters: Gas-fired on-site generation is becoming a core route to AI power.
Source → (DataM Intelligence (Oct 19; deal Oct 15))

Entergy adds 440 MW solar PPA for Google's West Memphis expansion

Energy

Entergy Arkansas plans about $600 million in new generation and transmission for Google's West Memphis campus, including a 440 MW solar contract and two batteries, with Google covering the cost. The plan is subject to state approval.

Why it matters: Shows utilities folding clean generation into data centre contracts under regulatory scrutiny.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 20))

Microsoft introduces Maia 300 custom AI chip

Tech

Maia 300 is slated for production in 2027 and is projected to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems. Maia is not yet offered to Azure customers.

Why it matters: Custom silicon aimed at power efficiency is a response to grid-limited AI growth.

9

Hyperscale AI spending tops $700 billion, but gas turbines are sold out through 2029

EnergyPolicy

Citing Industrial Info Resources, Data Center Knowledge says announced spending by Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Google should exceed $700 billion this year, up from $64 billion in 2018. GE Vernova, Mitsubishi Power and Siemens Energy are sold out of larger gas turbines through 2029, and the US has 53 GW of operating data centre load with 42 GW approved or under construction.

Why it matters: Equipment shortages and opposition, with $130 billion of projects rejected in Q1, are now the binding constraints on AI buildout.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 27))

Canada plans C$1.5-1.85B, 60 MW secure data centre in Ottawa

CanadaConstruction

Defence Construction Canada issued an advance procurement notice for a 60 MW facility, with procurement expected in winter 2027 and contingent on federal approval. The notice mainly lets bidders obtain security clearances, and the project could still be cancelled.

Why it matters: A sovereign-compute build is a rare public-sector data centre project in Canada.
Source → (W.Media (Oct 16))

Finance

Nscale is reportedly negotiating roughly $45 billion of AI compute capacity for Anthropic from a second building at its Monarch campus, which would nearly double the lab's commitments there. Terms are not final.

Why it matters: A deal of this size shows the scale of AI compute commitments now driving campus financing.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 20))
12

Microsoft exits Nscale's UK data centre deal over power delays

Finance

Microsoft terminated its contract for Nscale's planned 50 MW Loughton, England campus after UK Power Networks said it could not supply enough electricity until the early to mid-2030s. Nscale's IPO filing cites Microsoft agreements worth up to $43.8 billion and Anthropic agreements up to $44.6 billion.

Why it matters: Global story with North American relevance: grid delays are a risk for neocloud developers that also build in the US.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 19))

PolicyEnergy

In a Truth Social post, Trump said new large data centres are "mandated" to generate their own electricity and should pay communities a dividend. No details on enforcement, amounts or an executive order were given.

Why it matters: It reinforces self-supplied power and community payments as the political price of new campuses.

Amazon drops 800,000 sq ft Ashburn campus in deal with Loudoun County

PolicyConstruction

Amazon withdrew its plan for four data centres on the 122-acre former George Washington University campus it bought for $427 million. It will donate 25 acres and pay $5 million to the county's Housing Fund, and the site will be rezoned to prohibit data centres.

Why it matters: Opposition is winning concessions even in the densest data centre market.

Cleveland extends data centre moratorium through January 29, 2027

Policy

City Council voted on October 19 to extend its freeze on new data centres until January 29, 2027, or until permanent rules are enacted. Separately, Memphis rejected an 18-month moratorium and chose a 135-day pause on new applications.

Why it matters: Local moratoriums keep spreading even in cities with few large data centres.
