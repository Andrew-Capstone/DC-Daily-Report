1

Nvidia's Rubin platform becomes first AI system with 100% liquid cooling, no fans

TechEnergy

Nvidia's Rubin generation of AI infrastructure runs coolant up to 45°C (113°F) and cools every chip and networking component in a closed loop with zero fans, after moving power modules into an independent "power rack." The design cuts water consumption from roughly 2.6 million gallons per megawatt per year to near zero and removes evaporative cooling towers entirely.

Why it matters: It's the clearest technical answer yet to the water-scarcity fights erupting around data centres across North America, and a template every rack-scale competitor will now have to match.

2

Amazon discloses ownership of GW Ranch, a 7.65-gigawatt Texas gas-power complex

EnergyConstruction

Amazon has taken ownership of an 8,000-acre site near Pecos, Texas, where permits show a 35-turbine, 7.65-gigawatt behind-the-meter gas plant paired with 1.8GW of battery storage and 750MW of solar to power a new data centre campus. Regulatory filings show the gas plant alone would be permitted to emit 33 million tons of CO2 a year — more than any other power plant in the country — with first power targeted for early 2027.

Why it matters: It's the starkest example yet of hyperscalers building dedicated, off-grid gas generation at a scale that rivals entire regional power fleets, rather than waiting on utility interconnection queues.

3

Samsung Heavy Industries and Mousterian sign engineering contract for first US floating data centre

Tech

Samsung Heavy Industries and Texas-based Mousterian Corporation have moved their floating data centre partnership into the engineering phase, targeting factory-built, moored units that each deliver 50 megawatts of critical IT capacity for deployment in Texas and other U.S. markets. The design uses non-evaporative, water-based cooling that requires no potable water and produces no process-water discharge.

Why it matters: Off-site fabrication of floating campuses could sharply cut deployment times versus land-based builds and sidesteps the water and land-use fights now stalling projects across the country.

4

BloombergNEF: data centre gas plants could boost U.S. power emissions by 20%

EnergyPolicy

BloombergNEF's tracker of 99 proposed gas plants built specifically to power data centres — totaling 126 gigawatts of capacity, more than a third of it in Texas — finds they would emit roughly 318 million metric tons of CO2 annually at standard run rates, equal to a 20% jump in total U.S. power-sector emissions. Two projects alone, Amazon's Pecos County site and Microsoft and Chevron's West Texas plant, could together emit 45 million metric tons a year.

Why it matters: It quantifies, for the first time at this scale, how far the AI buildout's reliance on bespoke gas generation is pulling hyperscalers away from their own net-zero pledges.

5

Brookfield and NextEra unveil $100B AI campus at DOE's former Paducah uranium site

EnergyConstruction

Brookfield and NextEra Energy plan to transform part of the Department of Energy's former Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant in Kentucky — a Cold War-era uranium enrichment site — into an AI campus exceeding 1.2 gigawatts of compute, paired with up to 2GW of new natural gas generation and 2.6GW of battery storage. The roughly $100 billion project, developed with local utilities Big Rivers Electric and Jackson Purchase Energy, targets initial operations in 2028 and about 8,000 construction jobs.

Why it matters: Repurposing decommissioned federal nuclear infrastructure for AI compute, with dedicated generation built alongside it, is becoming a blueprint other DOE legacy sites are likely to follow.

Anthropic strikes $9.1 billion data centre lease with Bitcoin miner Riot Platforms

FinanceEnergy

Anthropic signed a 20-year lease for 191 megawatts of IT capacity at Riot Platforms' Rockdale, Texas campus, worth $9.1 billion and potentially $16.1 billion including extension options. Capacity comes online in stages, reaching 96MW by December 2027 and the full 191MW by June 2028.

Why it matters: It's the latest sign that AI labs are locking up power-ready capacity from crypto miners years in advance, converting Bitcoin-mining infrastructure into some of the industry's largest AI compute leases.

Energy

NRG Energy says it is "aligned on principal commercial terms" with an unnamed global hyperscaler for a $3.2 billion, 1.2-gigawatt combined-cycle gas plant in Texas targeted for late 2029, expected to generate $500 million in annual EBITDA. 8

Canada: Hundreds protest Meta's $13B, one-gigawatt data centre in Sturgeon County, Alberta

CanadaPolicy

Hundreds of residents gathered to oppose Meta's proposed one-gigawatt AI data centre near Morinville, Alberta, ahead of an Aug. 20 hearing, arguing the roughly $13-billion project's power and water needs were approved with too little public consultation. Premier Danielle Smith's government says the grid operator will use surplus power for the site until a dedicated gas plant comes online around 2030.

Why it matters: It's the largest single organized protest yet against Alberta's AI buildout, adding to a summer of mounting Canadian resistance to data centres sited near residential communities.

9

Canada: Oakville becomes first Ontario municipality to enact a data centre moratorium

CanadaPolicy

Oakville's town council voted unanimously at a special meeting to enact a one-year pause on new data centres, becoming the first Ontario municipality to do so, and directed staff to study whether local rules need updating for noise, vibration and emissions. Mayor Rob Burton said the move was precautionary rather than a reaction to any specific application; nearby Hamilton had earlier tried and failed to pass a similar pause.

Why it matters: It's the first concrete municipal moratorium to land in Canada's largest province just as Ontario's own province-wide data centre framework enters public consultation, setting up a direct test of how the two levels of government will reconcile.

Macquarie and GIC launch Theseus Infrastructure to build data centres for Anthropic

Anthropic, Macquarie Asset Management and Singapore's GIC formed Theseus Infrastructure, a new platform to develop, own and lease data centre facilities to Anthropic under long-term agreements, with Macquarie and GIC funding the majority of equity for each project and Anthropic anchoring as tenant. The venture will initially focus on identifying and developing new U.S. sites.

Why it matters: It's another major AI lab shifting capital-intensive data centre ownership off its own balance sheet and onto infrastructure investors, a financing pattern now spreading beyond OpenAI and hyperscalers to Anthropic.

12

Microsoft and Chevron's 2.67GW gas plant for West Texas AI campus advances

EnergyConstruction

Microsoft and Chevron's 20-year power agreement for "Project Kilby," a 2.67-gigawatt gas plant near Pecos, Texas dedicated entirely to a Microsoft-operated data centre, remains one of the largest co-located gas-and-data-centre developments in the U.S., with power expected to begin flowing in late 2028. Two large GE Vernova turbines will provide most of the generation, off-grid and not connected to the wider Texas transmission system.

Why it matters: Alongside Amazon's neighboring GW Ranch complex, it cements West Texas as ground zero for a new category of hyperscaler-owned, off-grid gas generation purpose-built for AI.

14

Governor Hochul tours New York touting nation's first statewide data centre moratorium

Policy

Governor Kathy Hochul has been holding regional roundtables across Long Island, the Finger Lakes and Western New York to promote Executive Order 62, her July 14 one-year moratorium on discretionary environmental permits for data centres of 50 megawatts or more — the first statewide such pause in the country. The order also directs Empire State Development to issue guidance letting local communities negotiate benefits like infrastructure or child-care investment from developers.

Why it matters: As the first state to freeze hyperscale permitting outright, New York's approach is becoming the reference point other statehouses cite as they weigh their own moratorium bills.

15

House bill would impose federal electricity excise tax on data centres

Policy

Rep. Andrea Salinas (D-Ore.) introduced the Data Center Community Reinvestment Act of 2026, which would levy a 1-cent-per-kilowatt-hour federal excise tax on electricity used by data centres over 1 megawatt, projected to raise about $1.76 billion annually for housing, conservation, Superfund and energy programs. The bill has been referred to Ways and Means along with Energy and Commerce and Science, Space and Technology.

Why it matters: It's the first serious federal-level proposal to directly tax data centre electricity use, escalating a fight that has so far played out almost entirely at the state and municipal level.

16

California's largest proposed data centre sues water agency after cooling-supply denial

Policy

The developer of the roughly 950,000-square-foot Imperial Valley Data Center — which would need an extra 287 million gallons of water a year — is suing the Imperial Irrigation District in Southern California after it denied the project's request to use Colorado River-sourced water and wastewater for cooling. The case, alongside county-level litigation, could determine whether the project proceeds at all.

Why it matters: It's a bellwether for how drought-stressed Western water authorities will treat AI data centres' cooling demands going forward, with implications well beyond California's border.

17

Data Center Watch: $130 billion in U.S. projects blocked or delayed in Q1 2026 alone

Policy

Data Center Watch's Q1 2026 report found at least 75 U.S. data centre projects worth roughly $130 billion were blocked or delayed in the first three months of the year — nearly matching all of 2025 combined — as organized opposition groups more than doubled to 833 across 49 states and over 300 state bills were introduced.

Why it matters: The scale of blocked capital shows local and state opposition has moved from scattered incidents to a structural risk factor that developers and financiers now have to price in nationwide.
