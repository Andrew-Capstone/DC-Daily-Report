1

Elon Musk aims to double Colossus 2's Nvidia chip count by year-end

Tech

Musk gave his most detailed timetable yet for expanding xAI's Memphis-area Colossus 2 cluster, which is already being built at record speed and adding a third building toward a planned 2-gigawatt, 555,000-GPU footprint. Bloomberg reports the plan would more than double the Nvidia chip count currently installed at the site within about three months, as xAI races to keep pace with rivals training ever-larger frontier models.

Why it matters: A single campus doubling its GPU count in months illustrates how compressed AI infrastructure timelines have become, and how much pressure that puts on chip supply, power delivery and cooling all at once.

2

DOE picks 31 grid-upgrade projects for $5.25 billion, aiming to unlock 23 gigawatts

Energy

The Department of Energy's SPARK initiative (Speed to Power through Accelerated Reconductoring and other Key Advanced Transmission Technology Upgrades) selected 31 projects across 26 states, combining $1.9 billion in federal funding with $3.35 billion in recipient cost-share for $5.25 billion total. Recipients are expected to reconductor or rebuild more than 1,500 miles of transmission lines and deploy grid-enhancing technologies across nearly 21,000 miles, with formal agreements expected between October 2026 and January 2027.

Why it matters: Upgrading existing wires rather than building new transmission is the fastest lever regulators have to free up grid capacity for data centres, and 23 gigawatts is a meaningful dent in the multi-year interconnection backlog.
U.S. Department of Energy → (Sept 24)
3

Woodway Energy to build a 22-mile gas pipeline dedicated to a hyperscale data centre

EnergyConstruction

Woodway Energy Infrastructure, backed by Macquarie Capital Principal Finance, signed definitive agreements to develop, build, own and operate a 22-mile, 30-inch natural gas pipeline delivering dedicated transportation capacity to a behind-the-meter power generation project for an undisclosed hyperscale data centre. The pipeline is expected to enter commercial service in the first half of 2028, with Woodway retaining long-term ownership and 24/7 control-room monitoring.

Why it matters: Purpose-built, behind-the-meter gas pipelines let data centres skip years-long grid interconnection queues entirely, a workaround increasingly favoured by developers who can't wait for utility-scale transmission upgrades.
PR Newswire → (Sept 22)
4

AMD crosses $1 trillion market cap on AI data-centre chip demand

TechFinance

AMD's market capitalization surpassed $1 trillion for the first time after a five-day rally that pushed shares up about 25%, making it the fourth U.S. chipmaker — after Nvidia, Broadcom and SK hynix — to reach the milestone. The move followed a 187% year-to-date rally, with AMD's data-centre business generating $6.7 billion in its most recent quarter, up 107% year-over-year, as CEO Lisa Su has guided toward doubling data-centre sales again in 2027.

Why it matters: AMD joining the trillion-dollar club signals AI chip demand is broadening beyond Nvidia, giving hyperscalers a credible second source for the silicon their data centres depend on.
CNBC → (Sept 21)
5

Nova Scotia's premier now open to data centres as EverWind advances plans for the province's first

CanadaEnergy

EverWind Fuels confirmed to CBC News that its long-term plans for its Point Tupper property near Port Hawkesbury, N.S. include a data centre, alongside its existing green hydrogen and wind projects. Premier Tim Houston, who previously said data centres wouldn't be entertained in Nova Scotia "until far into the future," now says the landscape has changed and he is open to allowing them, having circulated proposed guidelines; local officials say EverWind informed the municipality of its intentions only weeks ago.

Why it matters: A premier reversing course within months shows how quickly provincial governments are being pulled into competing for AI infrastructure investment, even in regions with no existing data centre industry.
CBC News → (Sept 26)
6

New Brunswick opens public comment on data centre policy while a 390-megawatt Saint John project awaits approval

CanadaPolicy

New Brunswick opened a public comment period, running September 1 to October 2, on a proposed policy framework to guide future data centre development in the province. The consultation comes as Alberta-based Beacon AI and Texas-based VoltaGrid seek approval for a west Saint John facility that would draw 390 megawatts and require a new dedicated natural gas plant — with the province currently lacking the kind of interim load cap Alberta has already put in place.

Why it matters: New Brunswick is trying to write its rulebook before, rather than after, its first major project is approved — a sequencing several other jurisdictions have been criticized for getting backwards.
CBC News → (Sept 2026, comment period through Oct 2)
7

Quebec's data centres are overwhelmingly U.S.-owned as Hydro-Québec's rate hike stays stuck in limbo

CanadaEnergy

Canada's National Observer reports that the majority of Quebec's roughly 72 mapped data centres, which have signed contracts with Hydro-Québec totalling nearly 1,488 megawatts, are owned by American interests. Separately, Hydro-Québec's request to nearly double the electricity rate paid by large data centres — filed with the provincial energy board eight months ago — still has no ruling, even as the utility projects data-centre power consumption could grow sevenfold by 2035.

Why it matters: A regulator sitting on a rate case for eight months while foreign-owned facilities keep drawing subsidized power sharpens the ratepayer-fairness debate already driving moratoriums elsewhere in Canada.
Canada's National Observer → (Sept 23)
8

Goldman Sachs: hyperscaler AI spending to hit $1.2 trillion in 2027, above Wall Street estimates

Finance

Goldman Sachs strategists led by Ryan Hammond now expect Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Oracle and Meta to raise combined AI infrastructure spending 54% to $1.2 trillion in 2027, above the Street's $1.1 trillion consensus, following roughly $800 billion in 2026. Goldman estimates hyperscalers need about $300 billion in annual AI revenue just to break even, and projects capex growth cooling to 12% by 2028 as financing needs and infrastructure constraints bite.

Why it matters: A above-consensus capex call from Goldman reinforces that the data centre buildout isn't slowing yet, but its own break-even math is a warning sign for how much AI revenue still has to materialize to justify the spending.
Bloomberg → (Sept 25)
9

Data centre IPO wave hits tougher market as investor scrutiny grows

Finance

A Reuters analysis finds data centre operators queuing up for IPOs — including Switch (confidentially filed, seeking up to a $50 billion valuation), DayOne (planning a public filing in mid-October targeting roughly $20 billion), Nscale and SB Energy — are facing sharper investor questions about thin operating histories, single-tenant concentration and financing risk. The piece frames this as the same credibility pressure that recently pushed SB Energy to delay its own listing.

Why it matters: A wave of nearly a dozen data centre IPO hopefuls hitting the market at once, right as investors turn skeptical, will test whether public markets are willing to keep bankrolling the AI infrastructure boom at private-market valuations.
Reuters via BNN Bloomberg → (Sept 25)
10

Nscale formally files S-1 for NYSE IPO, discloses $103 billion order book

Finance

Nscale filed a Form S-1 with the SEC on September 18 for a proposed NYSE listing under ticker NSCL, led by Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley. The filing discloses revenue of $140.6 million for the first half of 2026 — up 1,252% year-over-year — against a net loss that widened to $1.02 billion, anchored by a contracted order book of roughly $103 billion including its $45 billion Anthropic compute deal.

Why it matters: Nscale going public just days after signing a $44.6 billion deal with Anthropic will let investors put a real market price on the AI infrastructure sector's highest-risk, highest-growth business model: thinly capitalized GPU-cloud startups underwriting gigawatt-scale campuses.
Yahoo Finance → (Sept 22, filed Sept 18)
11

Chicago Mayor Johnson introduces a 12-month data centre construction moratorium

Policy

Mayor Brandon Johnson and several alderpeople introduced an ordinance to City Council that would pause construction of any new data centre for 12 months and block "material expansion" of existing ones, giving the administration time to develop rules on pollution, water use and electricity costs. Johnson said Chicago is "open for business" but "not for sale"; the Data Center Coalition warned the move would push investment and jobs to neighbouring states.

Why it matters: A major U.S. city the size of Chicago pursuing a blanket construction freeze — rather than a suburb or small municipality — marks a significant escalation in the moratorium trend sweeping local governments.
City of Chicago → (Sept 23)
12

Two more Michigan townships pause data centre development

Policy

Brighton Township, in Livingston County, and Port Huron Township both adopted 12-month moratoriums on new data centre applications, giving their planning commissions time to draft ordinances covering noise, lighting and power-demand concerns before any new projects are considered. Local officials cited guidance from the Michigan Townships Association, which recommended members review their zoning based on other jurisdictions' experiences.

Why it matters: Township-level moratoriums spreading across Michigan without any single high-profile trigger project shows local pre-emptive zoning caution, not just reaction to a specific proposal, is becoming standard practice.
WHMI News → (Sept 2026)
13

New surveys: roughly six in ten Americans oppose data centres in their own communities

Policy

KUNC reports that a Pew Research survey conducted this summer found 60% of Americans would be "not too" or "not at all" comfortable with a new data centre in their area, while an Annenberg Public Policy Center survey found 61% opposition and a Gallup poll put local opposition as high as 71%. Respondents cited energy costs, water use and strain on local quality of life as their top concerns.

Why it matters: Consistent opposition numbers across three independent pollsters, all in the 60-70% range, gives local officials pursuing moratoriums hard polling evidence that their constituents actually want the pause.
KUNC → (Sept 25)

$68 billion in U.S. data centre projects were blocked or delayed in a single quarter

Policy

Bloomberg reports that Data Center Watch identified 45 U.S. data centre projects worth an estimated $68 billion that were blocked or delayed by local pushback between April and June 2026 alone. The research group now tracks 843 organized opposition groups and says data centre development is facing scrutiny in 49 states.

Why it matters: Tens of billions of dollars in projects stalling in a single quarter shows community opposition has become a material business risk for developers, not just a political talking point.
Bloomberg → (Sept 21)
15

Georgia releases full impact report on OpenAI's $20 billion Project Camellia, the state's largest-ever electricity draw

ConstructionPolicy

Georgia regulators released the full Developments of Regional Impact report on OpenAI's four-building, 4.4-million-square-foot Project Camellia campus in Effingham County, which is contracted for 3.2 gigawatts of power from Georgia Power delivered in phases through 2032 — more than both of Georgia's new Plant Vogtle nuclear reactors combined. OpenAI has pledged to pay all infrastructure costs so residential electricity rates won't rise, use a closed-loop water system, and fund $80 million in local community benefits.

Why it matters: A single campus becoming a state's largest electricity consumer — bigger than two nuclear reactors combined — illustrates just how far individual AI data centre projects now outscale the power infrastructure built for entire regions.
