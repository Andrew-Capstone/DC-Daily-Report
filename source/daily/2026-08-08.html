1

Lumilens emerges from stealth with $900M+ to replace data-centre copper wiring with light

TechFinance

Optical-interconnect startup Lumilens raised more than $700 million in a new round, taking total funding past $900 million at a $5.51 billion valuation, to build near-package and co-packaged optics that replace short-reach copper connections between AI chips. Founder Ankur Singla says the constraint on AI has shifted from how many GPUs you can buy to how many you can connect, and the company is already shipping its first product into a hyperscaler's live data centres under a multibillion-dollar deal.

Why it matters: As GPU clusters scale toward tens of thousands of chips acting as one system, copper interconnects are becoming the binding bottleneck, and this is one of the largest bets yet that photonics, not more silicon, is the next unlock.

2

Tesla and SpaceX to invest $16.8 billion in "Terafab" chip factory in Texas

TechFinance

Tesla and SpaceX confirmed Terafab, a jointly developed, vertically integrated chip plant in Grimes County, Texas, with an initial $16.8 billion investment that could rise to $119 billion across future phases; Intel is helping refactor the fab's silicon process technology. The 100-million-square-foot facility will make, package and test advanced logic and memory chips to power Tesla's robots and SpaceX's space-based data centres.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest domestic chip-fab commitments outside the traditional foundry players, and ties silicon supply directly to the AI-compute ambitions of two of the industry's most aggressive infrastructure builders.

3

DOE taps Brookfield and NextEra for $100 billion AI data centre at former Kentucky uranium site

EnergyFinance

The Department of Energy selected Brookfield Asset Management and NextEra Energy, alongside three regional utilities, to redevelop its shuttered Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant into a 1.8-gigawatt AI and high-performance-computing campus backed by more than $100 billion in private investment and 4.6 gigawatts of dedicated gas and battery generation. The project is expected to bring roughly 8,000 construction jobs, with completion targeted for 2031.

Why it matters: It's the second former DOE uranium-enrichment site converted to an AI power-and-compute campus this year, showing the federal government is actively repurposing Cold War nuclear infrastructure to solve the industry's power bottleneck.

4

Energy Vault signs deal to deploy 1.25GW of integrated power infrastructure for hyperscaler AI data centre

Energy

Energy Vault will supply battery energy storage systems, grid-forming power conversion systems and AI infrastructure control software for an initial 1.25-gigawatt deployment, working alongside a power-generation EPC deploying Caterpillar gensets for an unnamed hyperscaler AI data centre. The company expects the agreement to contribute roughly $500-600 million in revenue across the second half of 2026 and 2027.

Why it matters: It's another sign that behind-the-meter, integrated power packages — generation, storage and controls bundled together — are becoming the default way hyperscalers get gigawatt-scale sites online without waiting on the grid.

5

Chip startup OLIX raises $312 million for photonic AI inference chip that ditches HBM

TechFinance

OLIX raised $312 million in Series B funding at a $3.3 billion valuation, backed by the UK government's Sovereign AI venture fund along with Arm and Hudson River Trading, for its Optical Tensor Processing Unit — a photonic chip architecture built around the idea that moving data with light rather than copper avoids the energy and bandwidth limits imposed by high-bandwidth memory. It's described as Europe's largest-ever semiconductor funding round.

Why it matters: It's a direct technical bet against the HBM-centric approach every major AI chip currently relies on, and a state-backed vote of confidence that photonic inference chips could reset the economics of running large models.

6

Ocean-powered AI data centre startup Panthalassa raising $225M near $2 billion valuation

TechEnergyFinance

Portland, Oregon-based Panthalassa, backed by Peter Thiel, is reportedly raising about $225 million that would roughly double its valuation to nearly $2 billion, months after a Series B round. The startup builds wave-powered floating data centres and is targeting a pilot deployment of its third-generation Ocean-3 platform in the northern Pacific this year, ahead of commercial operations in 2027.

Why it matters: It's one of the more radical bets on solving AI's power and siting constraints simultaneously — moving compute into international waters to tap wave energy directly rather than competing for scarce onshore grid capacity.

7

Mississauga councillors vote to pause new data centre approvals for up to a year

CanadaPolicy

Mississauga city councillors approved a motion for a bylaw that, if passed in September, would prohibit approval of new data centre development for up to one year. The city says the pause is meant to give it time to study the centres' impacts, work out how they'd be regulated, and consult with residents and industry.

Why it matters: Mississauga is one of the Greater Toronto Area's largest cities, and its move follows Hamilton's council narrowly rejecting a similar pause, showing Ontario's data-centre moratorium debate is far from settled.

Ontario Green Party calls for province-wide moratorium on new AI data centres

CanadaPolicy

Green Party of Ontario leader Mike Schreiner called on the province to freeze approval of all new AI data centres until more is known about their impact on the electricity grid, the environment, public health and jobs, making the announcement at Queen's Park. The call follows a string of municipal-level pause votes across the province.

Why it matters: It elevates Ontario's data-centre fight from city councils to provincial politics, adding pressure on the governing party as Alberta simultaneously courts tens of billions in data-centre investment.

9

Indigenous-led KatloTech pitches hydro-powered data centre campus near Hay River, NWT

CanadaEnergyTech

KatloTech Communications, an Indigenous ICT company, is pitching the IceFox Data Center on K'atl'odeeche First Nation land near Hay River, Northwest Territories, alongside a 329-kilometre open-access fibre route to High Level, Alberta. The modular campus would run on 10MW of renewable hydroelectric power and use a snow-cell cooling system that stores winter snow and meltwater to keep loops chilled between 0-4°C year-round, targeting commercial launch in 2028.

Why it matters: It's a rare Indigenous-led, renewably powered data-centre proposal in Canada's North, and its passive snow-cooling design offers a low-carbon alternative to the mechanical chillers most facilities depend on.

10

Morgan Stanley-led banks move to offload $15 billion of debt tied to Google-backed Anthropic data centre

FinanceTech

Banks led by Morgan Stanley are working to sell $15 billion in debt tied to a 2,000-acre data centre campus under construction in Hubbard, Texas, that's leased to Anthropic and paired with a 1.6-gigawatt gas plant; Google holds roughly a 20% equity stake in the project. The debt is expected to be refinanced through multiple bond sales as lenders grow more cautious about holding large AI-infrastructure loans on their own balance sheets.

Why it matters: It's a concrete look at how Wall Street is structuring and distributing risk on the AI buildout's biggest bets, and a reminder that even well-backed projects need deep bond-market appetite to get built.

SMR firm Nuclea signs MoU to evaluate small modular reactors at US data centre sites

Energy

Nuclea Energy signed a non-binding MoU with New Mining Co. to evaluate co-locating behind-the-meter modular reactors at New Mining's data centre facilities, structured in two phases: engineering scoping for the roughly 30 megawatts of load New Mining currently operates, followed by evaluation of scaling toward 100 megawatts. The agreement creates no binding power-purchase commitments.

Why it matters: It's another small but telling data point in the rush of SMR developers signing early-stage agreements with data centre and compute operators well ahead of any reactor actually being licensed or built.

13

Trump signs order imposing 15% tariff and price floors on polysilicon used in chips and solar panels

Policy

President Trump signed a proclamation under Section 232 setting minimum import prices for polysilicon and related products — including $21/kg for polysilicon and $100/kg for ingots and wafers — plus a 15% tariff on goods made with it, effective December 4. The order also directs Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to create incentives for domestic polysilicon production.

Why it matters: Polysilicon feeds both semiconductor wafers and solar panels, so the tariff touches two supply chains data centres depend on directly — the chips inside them and the solar capacity increasingly paired with their power deals.

Why it matters: It's a rare case of a hyperscaler walking away from a project after a direct electoral rebuke, showing local ballot-box opposition can kill a proposed campus even at a site with an obvious co-located power source.

Americans are rallying against data centres — but surprisingly few are actually being blocked

Policy

A recent Gallup poll found 71% of Americans oppose data centres in their communities, and politicians are increasingly running against them, yet only about half of the AI computing capacity scheduled to activate through 2028 is actually expected to come online. CNN reports labor shortages — the industry needs roughly 500,000 more electricians, 300,000 welders and 550,000 plumbers — are as big a constraint as local opposition.

Why it matters: It reframes the moratorium wave: construction is being throttled as much by a skilled-trades shortage as by community pushback, meaning even a political retreat from opposition wouldn't necessarily speed up delivery.

Westlake, Texas data centre advances despite pushback from neighboring Buddhist monastery

Policy

The Westlake Planning and Zoning Commission voted to advance plans for a more than 1-million-square-foot, 87-acre data centre campus after nearly four hours of public testimony, sending it to the town council.
