1

Trump's on-site power push meets AI data centre reality

EnergyPolicy

In an Oct 21 post, President Trump said large new data centres should generate their own electricity and share surplus with the local grid. Data Center Knowledge notes some gas turbine models are reportedly sold out through 2031, and experts expect most sites to use a hybrid of on-site generation, storage and grid supply.

Why it matters: A federal lean toward self-powered campuses could force projects already secured on grid or PPA power to rethink their plans.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 3))
2

OpenAI pledges to cut Georgia campus power use by up to 1 GW when the grid needs relief

EnergyPolicy

OpenAI says its planned Effingham County, Georgia campus will curtail up to 1 gigawatt of demand, about 700,000 homes' worth, during grid stress, and plans a "Grid Protectors Program" to extend similar commitments nationwide. Terms will be negotiated with utilities; the item cites Axios as its original source.

Why it matters: Demand flexibility is becoming a bargaining chip for interconnection as FERC and the Senate look at limiting large-load use at peak times.
Source → (KuCoin News flash, citing Axios (Nov 2))
3

Project Jupiter tops out first building, switching to Bloom fuel cells for on-site power

EnergyConstruction

Clayco and Kiewit topped out the first 855,000 sq ft building of the Doña Ana County, New Mexico campus leased by Oracle for OpenAI. Bloom fuel cells replace gas turbines and diesel generators in the design, cutting nitrogen oxide emissions by about 92%, according to the article.

Why it matters: A major campus is moving to fuel-cell microgrid power instead of waiting on grid or pipeline.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 27))
4

AI's expansion hits the limits of local power, with timelines stretching past two years

EnergyPolicy

GlobalData says projects that broke ground between Q3 2025 and Q3 2026 waited just over two years after announcement, versus about 18 months before. It forecasts data centre electricity use rising from 590 TWh in 2025 to 1,520 TWh in 2030, with new grid infrastructure taking five to 15 years.

Why it matters: Grid access, labour shortages and local opposition are now the main drivers of delay, more than chips or capital.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 3))
5

FirstEnergy data centre pipeline reaches 26 GW, with West Virginia at 6 GW

Energy

FirstEnergy's pipeline grew 4.8% in Q3 to 26 GW, and its West Virginia portion rose from 4.3 GW to 6 GW. Contracted large-load demand is 7.9 GW, with about 4 GW more under negotiation.

Why it matters: Utilities are now planning generation specifically for data centre load.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 30))

Anthropic signs first Canadian data centre lease at EverWind's Nova Scotia campus

CanadaEnergy

EverWind announced more than C$10 billion of private investment at its Point Tupper site in Richmond County, Nova Scotia. Anthropic confirmed a long-term lease for the first phase.

Why it matters: It anchors one of Canada's largest AI campuses and ties growth to Atlantic Canada's wind resources.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 19))
7

Hydro-Québec defends proposal to roughly double electricity rates for data centres

CanadaPolicyEnergy

Hydro-Québec proposes a new rate for data centres over 5 MW averaging about 13 cents per kWh, roughly double the current large-power price. Régie de l'énergie hearings began in early October and continue in December.

Why it matters: Quebec's cheap hydro has drawn data centres; a rate reset could redirect Canadian investment.
Source → (CTV News (early Oct))
8

Aligned launches Delta Cube 3.0 air cooling with Munters, citing 10% more capacity and efficiency

Tech

The third-generation unit is described as delivering 10% more cooling capacity and 10% better energy efficiency in the same footprint, using a closed loop that needs no water for operational cooling. Munters builds the units in Selma, Texas and Daleville, Virginia.

Why it matters: Air cooling is being pushed to ultra-high densities and made water-free, an alternative to liquid-only designs.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 2; launch Oct 28))

Tech

Oracle will offer Google's eighth-generation TPUs on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, with capacity launching in 2027 and rack-scale direct liquid cooling.

Why it matters: A multi-gigawatt commitment to non-Nvidia silicon could reshape how AI campuses are designed and powered.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 28))
10

Nvidia unveils DSX Pod, a prebuilt AI data centre it says deploys in as little as 14 days

TechConstruction

The containerized pod packs seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with networking, cabling and cooling preinstalled, cutting deployment at a prepared site from about six months to as little as 14 days, per Nvidia. Availability is slated for early 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built capacity could compress build timelines that are now gated by construction.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 21))
11

Liquid cooling is changing what data centres need to measure

Tech

A Vaisala industry perspective says direct-to-chip cooling adds liquid-side monitoring of temperature, pressure, flow, leaks and coolant chemistry. AI workloads can swing from idle to full load within minutes, so cooling controls must respond in real time.

Why it matters: As rack densities climb, instrumentation and controls become part of the cooling design, not an afterthought.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 2))
12

Liquid cooling retrofits are hard, but sometimes worth it

Tech

Fully loaded AI racks can exceed 3,000 pounds, straining raised floors, and older plants may lack pump and heat-exchanger capacity. Retrofits make most sense for partial deployments, single-tenant sites, water-constrained regions, or where cheaper than new build.

Why it matters: Most existing capacity is air-cooled, so retrofit economics decide how much legacy space can host AI.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 2))

Cloud infrastructure spending hits $168B in Q3, up 55%, as AI capacity expands

Finance

Synergy Research puts Q3 worldwide cloud infrastructure spending at $168 billion, up 55% year over year, with Amazon at 26% share, Microsoft 19% and Google 16%. CBRE counts 7,481 MW under construction across eight North American markets in H1, with more than 80% preleased.

Why it matters: The market has doubled in eight quarters, and power limits are now the constraint on delivering capacity.
