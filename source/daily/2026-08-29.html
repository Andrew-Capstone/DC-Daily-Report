1

Nvidia pauses cloud revenue-sharing deals over antitrust concerns

TechFinance

Nvidia has paused some of the cloud revenue-sharing arrangements it announced in July 2026, in which it acted as a financial backstop for companies building large-scale GPU clusters in exchange for a cut of the resulting revenue. A quarterly filing disclosed $36 billion in total commitments tied to the program before employees flagged internal concern that the structure — and how much influence it gives Nvidia over customers' businesses — could draw antitrust scrutiny.

Why it matters: It's a rare instance of Nvidia pulling back one of its own AI-financing mechanisms rather than expanding it, and a signal that the deal structures underpinning the AI infrastructure boom are starting to draw real regulatory risk.

Amazon acquires 8,000-acre Texas site for what could be the largest US gas plant

EnergyConstruction

Amazon has acquired an 8,000-acre site in Pecos, Texas for a behind-the-meter data centre that would be powered by a dedicated natural gas plant of up to 7.65 gigawatts across 35 turbines, according to a report — a scale that would make it the largest gas-fired power plant in the United States. The behind-the-meter design lets Amazon bypass grid interconnection queues that have stalled thousands of Texas data centre projects.

Why it matters: It's one of the clearest signs yet that hyperscalers are choosing to build dedicated gas generation rather than wait years for grid connections, reinforcing gas's role as the near-term bridge fuel for AI power demand.

3

NuScale's 6GW TVA small modular reactor program awaits make-or-break PPA

Energy

NuScale's financing partner ENTRA1 Energy is working toward a power purchase agreement by year-end 2026 for its landmark 6-gigawatt small modular reactor deployment program with the Tennessee Valley Authority — potentially the largest SMR buildout in the world. A signed PPA would let construction begin and give the industry its clearest proof yet that SMRs can be commercially financed after a string of earlier project failures.

Why it matters: A PPA at this scale would be a pivotal validation moment for small modular reactors as a credible power source for AI data centres, not just a demonstration technology.

4

Lam Research breaks ground on $620M AI chip lab in Oregon

TechConstruction

Semiconductor equipment maker Lam Research broke ground August 26 on a $620 million, 120,000-square-foot R&D lab in Tualatin, Oregon, part of a $1.5 billion investment there over the next decade and a broader $3 billion global lab expansion aimed at advanced deposition and etch technology for AI-era chips. The facility, expected to open in 2028, will boost the site's cleanroom lab space by more than 50% and create about 400 jobs.

Why it matters: It's a reminder that the AI buildout isn't only about GPUs and data centre shells — the equipment that manufactures next-generation chips is getting its own multi-billion-dollar capacity expansion.

CoreWeave signs multibillion-dollar deal to power Hudson River Trading with Nvidia Vera Rubin chips

TechFinance

CoreWeave signed a multiyear, multibillion-dollar agreement with quantitative trading firm Hudson River Trading to build its next-generation AI research platform on CoreWeave's cloud, using Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 and HGX B200 GPU systems over Spectrum-X Ethernet networking. HRT becomes one of the first customers with wide-scale access to Nvidia's newest Rubin-generation silicon.

Why it matters: It shows CoreWeave winning a sophisticated, latency-sensitive financial-sector client away from the hyperscalers, and demonstrates real commercial demand for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform as it ramps toward volume production.

6

Canada: Ottawa moves to draft a policy giving council power to pause hyperscale data centres

CanadaPolicy

Ottawa city council directed staff to draw up a data centre policy after a motion asking to be alerted to any new "hyperscale" (50-megawatt-plus) data centre application, so councillors have the option to pass an interim control bylaw and temporarily block it while the framework is pending. City officials say the measure is meant to buy time for policy, not to function as an outright ban.

Why it matters: Ottawa joins a growing list of Canadian municipalities — after Oakville, Mississauga and Toronto — moving to give themselves emergency brakes on data centre approvals ahead of any provincial or federal framework.

Canada: Montreal-based 5C secures $605 million Brookfield-led financing for North American AI campuses

CanadaFinance

Montreal-headquartered AI data centre developer 5C secured $605 million in debt financing led by Brookfield Asset Management, on top of $835 million it raised in 2025, to fund a campus acquisition in Memphis and build out sites in Phoenix and Ohio. The company says it has over 1.5 gigawatts of roadmap capacity across its North American portfolio.

Why it matters: It's a sign Canadian-headquartered developers are raising real scale capital to build AI infrastructure in the US, even as several Canadian provinces move to restrict data centres at home.

8

Samsung Heavy Industries advances floating data centre design for Texas

TechConstruction

Samsung Heavy Industries and Dallas-based Mousterian Corporation signed an engineering contract to move their moored floating data centre concept into detailed design, targeting 50-megawatt units for deployment in Texas and other US markets. The design uses non-evaporative, water-based cooling that Mousterian says consumes no potable water and discharges nothing into surrounding waterways.

Why it matters: Floating, factory-built data centres could let developers sidestep the land, grid-interconnection and community-opposition bottlenecks slowing land-based projects, offering a genuinely new construction model rather than an incremental one.

9

Data centre gas plants set to boost US power-sector emissions by 20%

EnergyPolicy

Bloomberg reports that the wave of bespoke natural-gas plants being built to power AI data centres — nearly 100 proposed plants tracked by BloombergNEF — could add about 318 million metric tons of carbon dioxide annually if run at typical rates, pushing US power-sector emissions up roughly 20%. Gas-fired capacity in development has jumped 50% to 378 gigawatts nationally, with Texas leading the buildout.

Why it matters: It quantifies, for the first time at this scale, how much the AI power boom could set back US power-sector decarbonization even as hyperscalers tout clean-energy commitments elsewhere.

10

Community backlash now stalling or blocking $156 billion in US data centre projects

Policy

Bloomberg reports that local opposition has helped stall or block at least 48 US data centre projects worth roughly $156 billion, with McKinsey's Maria Goodpaster saying developers and investors are now "pivoting" away from some sites and abandoning others outright. Investors are increasingly prioritizing projects with clearer entitlements and stronger community buy-in over speed to market.

Why it matters: It's evidence the political backlash to data centres has moved from a reputational headache to a measurable drag on how fast — and where — new AI capacity actually gets built.
Bloomberg → (Aug 26)
11

Big Tech ramps up lobbying against California data centre regulation bills

Policy

Tech companies and utilities are spending heavily to fight seven data centre regulation bills moving through California's legislature, with Pacific Gas & Electric logging $2.86 million in second-quarter lobbying — its second-highest quarterly total since 1999 — and Amazon spending over $500,000 across 33 bills. The proposed measures would shift infrastructure costs to data centre operators, mandate resource-use disclosures and require environmental reviews.

Why it matters: California is a bellwether for state-level data centre regulation, and the scale of industry spending shows how high the stakes are seen to be for who ultimately pays for AI's power and water demands.
KPBS → (Aug 28)
12

House bill would impose federal electricity excise tax on large data centres

Policy

Rep. Andrea Salinas (D-Ore.) introduced the Data Center Community Reinvestment Act of 2026 on August 13, which would levy a 1-cent-per-kilowatt-hour federal excise tax on electricity used by data centres drawing more than 1 megawatt, projected to raise about $1.76 billion a year. Revenue would fund affordable housing, conservation, environmental cleanup, clean energy and infrastructure repair.

Why it matters: It's one of the first federal-level proposals to directly tax data centre electricity use, and could set a template — or a flashpoint — for how Washington eventually weighs in on a debate that has so far played out almost entirely at the state and local level.
Rep. Andrea Salinas → (Aug 13)
13

Amazon's $10B "Project Eagle" campus near Houston hits regulatory headwinds

ConstructionPolicy

Amazon's proposed $10 billion, 10-building "Project Eagle" data centre campus in Wharton County, southwest of Houston, is facing delays as Texas Governor Greg Abbott's statewide audit of data centre grid hookups slows power-connection approvals and the company still needs additional county permits before construction can proceed. A nondisclosure agreement between Wharton County and a consulting firm working with Amazon has also limited public information about the project's status.

Why it matters: Even one of the best-capitalized developers in the industry isn't immune to Texas's compliance-driven slowdown, underscoring how much the state's audit process is now shaping the pace of data centre construction statewide.
