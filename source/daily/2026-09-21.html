1

Anthropic and OpenAI pivot toward smaller, 20-30MW data centres to outrun gigawatt-campus delays

TechEnergy

Both AI labs are exploring 20-30 megawatt deployments at existing powered sites in the UK, Nordic countries and potentially the US, betting that inference workloads — unlike concentrated training runs — can be split across smaller, geographically dispersed clusters that skip years-long grid interconnection queues. JLL projects inference will grow from 9% of AI workloads in 2025 to 37% by 2030, overtaking training by 2027, even as both companies keep their gigawatt-scale commitments (Anthropic's 460MW Nscale deal and 5GW Amazon partnership) intact.

Why it matters: A shift toward a portfolio of smaller, faster-to-power sites is a genuinely new siting strategy for frontier labs, and a tacit admission that gigawatt megacampuses can no longer keep pace with near-term inference demand.

2

New Era Energy secures 20-year, up to 207MW natural-gas PPA with Vistra for its Texas data centre

Energy

New Era's subsidiary TCDC PowerCo signed a 20-year power purchase agreement with Vistra's Luminant unit for a minimum of 200MW and up to 207MW, sourced from Vistra's adjacent 1,180MW gas-fired Odessa, Texas plant, to power Phase 1 of the 493-acre Texas Critical Data Center in the Permian Basin. Power is expected online in Q3 2027; Vistra will also receive a 5% non-voting interest in the powered portion and rights of first refusal on future expansion at the site, which is designed to scale toward 1.4 gigawatts.

Why it matters: Pairing a long-term PPA with an equity stake for the power supplier is becoming a template for de-risking behind-the-meter gas deals, converting speculative "permitted land" into bankable, powered capacity.

3

Alberta's Danielle Smith sets $200 billion AI data centre investment target at sold-out Calgary conference

CanadaEnergy

Premier Danielle Smith used the Data Centre West conference in Calgary — which drew more than 500 delegates and sold out — to outline Alberta's ambition to attract as much as USD $200 billion in AI data centre investment, building on Meta's existing C$13 billion commitment to build Canada's largest data centre in the province. Technology Minister Nate Glubish framed the province's approach as "the right project, in the right location, done the right way," with officials floating a hybrid grid-plus-on-site-generation power model; the event proved popular enough that it will expand to two days in 2027.

Why it matters: A $200 billion provincial target — roughly 15 times Meta's anchor investment — signals Alberta is positioning itself as Canada's primary AI infrastructure hub, leaning into cheap power and a lighter regulatory touch as Ontario municipalities move the opposite direction.

4

Nvidia-backed Nscale files for $35 billion US IPO days after a "going concern" warning

FinanceTech

AI cloud and data centre builder Nscale filed S-1 paperwork on September 18 seeking a $35 billion valuation, just days after Nvidia extended $3.1 billion via unsecured convertible loan notes on September 15. The filing discloses a $1 billion net loss on just $140.6 million of revenue in the first half of 2026, cost of revenue that exceeded revenue, a previously disclosed "substantial doubt" about its ability to continue as a going concern, and $56.4 billion in remaining performance obligations over the next seven years.

Why it matters: A money-losing infrastructure builder chasing a $35 billion valuation, propped up by its own chip supplier's financing, is a stress test for how much public markets will tolerate in the AI data centre financing boom.

45 US data centre projects worth $68 billion were blocked or delayed by local opposition in Q2 alone

Policy

Research group Data Center Watch found that between April and June 2026, 45 data centre projects worth a combined $68 billion were blocked or delayed by local pushback, following $130 billion in disrupted projects in the first quarter. The group now tracks 843 opposition groups across 49 states (all but Hawaii), roughly 30 state legislatures acting on siting, power or water rules, and one Tennessee project petition that alone drew more than 500,000 Change.org signatures — plus the first opposition activity the group has documented outside North America, in Europe, Australia and South Africa.

Why it matters: Disrupted investment is now running in the tens of billions of dollars per quarter, hard evidence that community and regulatory pushback has become a real constraint on the pace of the AI buildout, not just a news-cycle irritant.

6

Blackstone to acquire liquid-cooling components maker Flow Control Holdings

FinanceTech

Blackstone agreed to acquire Cincinnati-based Flow Control Holdings, a maker of engineered components for data centre liquid cooling — including coolant distribution units and in-row manifolds — from Audax Private Equity, which will retain a minority stake. The deal, expected to close in the fourth quarter, follows Blackstone securing a first-lien loan to fund the acquisition, with FCH having completed ten of its own bolt-on acquisitions under Audax's ownership.

Why it matters: A private equity giant betting on a components supplier rather than a data centre operator underscores how liquid cooling has become its own investable layer of the AI infrastructure stack as rack densities climb.
Investing.com → (Sept 10)
7

CoreWeave prices upsized $3.7 billion convertible note offering to fund AI infrastructure growth

Finance

CoreWeave priced $3.7 billion of 2.875% convertible senior notes due 2033, upsized from an initially planned $3 billion, with an additional $500 million available to initial purchasers and settlement due September 22. The notes convert at roughly $97.85 per share — a 22.5% premium to CoreWeave's September 17 close — with about $498.8 million earmarked for capped-call transactions to limit shareholder dilution and the remainder for general corporate purposes.

Why it matters: An upsized, oversubscribed-looking convertible offering shows debt markets remain wide open for AI infrastructure players even as questions mount elsewhere about the sector's financing quality.

8

Roughly 200 protesters rally at Missouri Capitol demanding a statewide data centre construction moratorium

Policy

Demonstrators filled the Missouri Capitol rotunda on September 17 during the legislature's annual veto session, then marched to the Governor's Mansion to deliver petitions demanding a construction freeze, elimination of data centre tax incentives, and community transparency requirements; one arrest was made during the march. The rally came after months of pushback against an Amazon data centre in Montgomery County that broke ground in June, and a scheduled House hearing on data centres was canceled the same day.

Why it matters: A veto-session rally at the state capitol, rather than just local council meetings, shows Missouri's data centre opposition escalating to the statewide political stage ahead of the 2027 legislative session.
