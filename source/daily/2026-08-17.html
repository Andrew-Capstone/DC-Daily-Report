1

Nvidia partners with Wall Street giants on $500B AI compute financing platforms

FinanceTech

Nvidia announced partnerships with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to build independent financing platforms aimed at mobilizing more than $500 billion in third-party capital for AI data centre buildouts and Nvidia hardware purchases. The arrangement is designed to let hyperscalers, frontier AI labs and enterprises acquire compute without loading the cost onto their own balance sheets, treating GPU infrastructure like an investable asset class similar to real estate or toll roads. CEO Jensen Huang called Nvidia compute "broadly adopted, flexible... fungible and transferable" — the qualities Wall Street needs to lend against it; the deals remain subject to final agreements.

Why it matters: It's the clearest sign yet that Wall Street sees AI data centre compute as a durable, bankable asset class of its own — a shift that could unlock a new wave of off-balance-sheet financing for the entire industry.

2

Texas's data centre grid freeze could delay 49.8GW of projects and cost up to $15B, BNEF warns

EnergyPolicy

Governor Greg Abbott's August 3 order pausing new grid connections until the Public Utility Commission and ERCOT audit the state's 474-gigawatt interconnection queue — about 90% of it data centres — is stretching into its second week without clear audit criteria, even as officials and industry groups publicly tout it. BloombergNEF estimates the freeze could delay 49.8 gigawatts of projects, nearly a fifth of the entire US development pipeline, and cost developers up to $15 billion if it drags on three months; ERCOT has already postponed its Batch Zero large-load classifications and is seeking a good-cause exemption at the PUCT's August 20 meeting.

Why it matters: Texas is the epicentre of US data centre growth, so an open-ended, criteria-light freeze there ripples through GPU supply chains, financing timelines and the national AI buildout far beyond state lines.

3

Most electricity requested for US AI data centres will never materialize, Wood Mackenzie finds

EnergyPolicy

Wood Mackenzie projects that US grid operators and utilities will likely commit to only about 28% of the 1,066 gigawatts of electricity requested for proposed AI data centre projects, meaning more than two-thirds of the demand behind today's grid-planning fights is "phantom" — inflated by speculative and duplicate interconnection requests. The finding complicates the debate over who should pay for new transmission and generation, since utilities and regulators have been planning around gross request totals the analysis says wildly overstate real future load.

Why it matters: It undercuts a core justification for rushed grid buildouts and rate hikes tied to data centres, and argues planners need far better filters for which projects are real before locking in costly new infrastructure.

4

PJM sends FERC a plan forcing data centres to bring their own power or face curtailment

EnergyPolicy

PJM Interconnection, the largest US grid operator, filed an Interim Resource Adequacy Service plan with FERC requiring new large loads like data centres to secure their own power supply — a "bring your own new capacity" approach — or risk having their power cut during grid emergencies starting in June 2027. The proposal follows a record $16.4 billion capacity auction in June and forecasts of 30-34 gigawatts of new large-load demand by the early 2030s, rising to as much as 70 gigawatts by 2038, and leans on individual states to build their own large-load tariffs.

Why it matters: PJM serves 13 states and roughly 65 million people, so how FERC rules on this plan will set the template for how much of the US forces AI data centres to internalize their own power costs and risk.

5

Vantage Data Centers weighs $100B IPO, which would be the largest in data centre history

Finance

Vantage Data Centers, backed by Silver Lake and DigitalBridge, is exploring an IPO that could value the company at roughly $100 billion and raise about $10 billion, which would make it the largest data centre listing on record; a sale is also under consideration, though talks remain early-stage and informal. The move follows KKR and GIP quietly lining up banks for a potential 2027 IPO of CyrusOne that could raise roughly $5 billion, as private-equity owners across the sector look to cash out of the AI infrastructure boom.

Why it matters: A $100 billion data centre IPO would be a landmark test of how public markets price AI infrastructure assets, and could open the floodgates for a wave of similar listings from other PE-backed operators.

6

Alpha Compute signs deal for natural gas-powered Pennsylvania data centre campus, scaling to 1GW

Energy

Alpha Compute signed a binding term sheet to acquire mineral, surface and pore-space assets in northern Pennsylvania and build a behind-the-meter data centre campus running on Marcellus Shale gas, starting at 200 megawatts with room to expand to 1 gigawatt. The company is targeting an all-in power cost of about 5.85 cents per kilowatt-hour for the initial phase, aiming to bypass grid interconnection queues entirely by generating its own power on site.

Why it matters: It's another entrant in the fast-growing "bring your own power" model using cheap regional gas, a strategy developers increasingly favour to sidestep the exact kind of grid bottlenecks now freezing projects in Texas.

7

Energy Vault signs 1.25GW battery storage deal for Texas hyperscale data centre

EnergyTech

Energy Vault signed its largest deal to date, agreeing to design, procure and commission 1.25 gigawatts of integrated battery energy storage, grid-forming power conversion systems and AI infrastructure control software for an unnamed developer's hyperscale data centre in Texas. The fully off-grid deployment is meant to let the data centre connect and begin operating without waiting on traditional utility interconnection schedules, with initial deployments starting within four to twelve months and an expected $500-600 million in revenue for Energy Vault through 2027.

Why it matters: It shows battery storage moving from a backup role to a primary power strategy for gigawatt-scale AI campuses trying to outrun grid queues altogether.

8

Quantinuum and Oracle bring quantum computing inside a US AI data centre

Tech

Quantinuum and Oracle announced a multi-year partnership to deploy Quantinuum's Helios quantum computer inside a US-based Oracle Cloud Infrastructure data centre, enabling hybrid quantum-AI workloads as a native OCI service — Quantinuum's first on-premises deployment inside a major public cloud provider's AI data centre. The companies are targeting applications in drug discovery, materials science, financial modelling and large-scale optimization alongside conventional AI workloads.

Why it matters: It's an early, concrete step toward quantum and classical AI compute sharing the same data centre floor, rather than living in separate research facilities.

9

Canada: Oakville becomes first Ontario municipality to enact a data centre moratorium

CanadaPolicy

Oakville town council voted unanimously to enact a one-year moratorium on new data centres, becoming the first Ontario municipality to do so, with staff directed to study noise, vibration, emissions and infrastructure impacts before the pause lifts. Mayor Rob Burton framed the move as being "alert" rather than acting from fear; Mississauga, which approved its own pause in principle in July, is set to finalize its moratorium bylaw at a September 16 council vote, while Toronto and Waterloo Region are exploring similar frameworks.

Why it matters: Oakville's move turns Ontario's data centre fight from talk into binding local law, adding pressure on the province's new Data Centre Playbook to answer community concerns before more municipalities follow suit.

10

Canada: Alberta launches town hall series to sell its AI data centre push to residents

CanadaPolicy

Alberta's government launched a public information website and a five-stop town hall series — starting in Ponoka on August 19, then Sturgeon County on August 20, a virtual session on August 27, and Grande Prairie on September 11 — to explain AI data centre development to residents amid mounting protests, including one that drew hundreds to Morinville over Meta's $13 billion, 1-gigawatt Sturgeon County campus. Technology Minister Nate Glubish said the province wants to give Albertans "real answers," promising residents won't subsidize projects and that grid reliability takes priority, as Alberta courts nearly $200 billion in potential private data centre investment.

Why it matters: It's a real-time test of whether a government can win back public trust on data centres through outreach and safeguards after the fact, rather than consultation before deals like Meta's were signed.

11

Canada: DeepInfra opens Toronto cluster, its first data centre outside the US, with 1,000+ Blackwell GPUs

CanadaTech

AI inference cloud provider DeepInfra deployed its first international data centre in an eStruxture facility in Toronto, a 1.7-megawatt cluster hosting more than 1,000 Nvidia Blackwell B300 GPUs, following the company's $107 million Series B round. The Toronto site is DeepInfra's ninth data centre overall and is meant to cut latency for Canadian and other international customers running large language model and multimodal AI workloads.

Why it matters: It gives Canadian AI developers a domestic option for the latest Blackwell-generation GPU inference capacity, at a moment when data residency and sovereignty are becoming bigger considerations for Canadian AI buyers.

AMD's data centre revenue surges 107% to $6.7B as Anthropic and Microsoft scale up new MI450 GPUs

TechFinance

AMD reported second-quarter revenue of $11.5 billion, with data centre revenue more than doubling to $6.7 billion — now 58% of total company revenue — driven by EPYC processors and the ramp of Instinct GPUs. AMD and Anthropic struck a deal to deploy up to 2 gigawatts of Instinct MI450 GPUs in AMD's Helios racks, while Microsoft expanded its Azure deployment of Helios and 6th-generation EPYC chips, and AMD said data centre sales should accelerate further in the second half of the year.

Why it matters: It's more evidence that AMD is converting years of GPU roadmap promises into real hyperscaler and AI-lab commitments, giving the AI buildout a credible second major chip supplier alongside Nvidia.

13

Fort Worth City Council advances first step toward a 90-day data centre moratorium

PolicyConstruction

Fort Worth City Council voted unanimously to begin the process toward a 90-day moratorium on new data centre construction, alongside 10-1 votes requiring developers to secure utility approval before applying and creating an advisory committee to study impacts. The pause, if finalized after two required public hearings and a three-fourths supermajority vote, would take effect February 16, 2027 and run through May 17, giving staff time to study noise, water use and land-use compatibility.

Why it matters: It's a large Texas city moving toward a formal moratorium even as the state itself has frozen new grid connections, showing local and state-level pushback compounding rather than one substituting for the other.

Policy

The Spotsylvania County Board of Supervisors voted 4-2 to formally back state Sen. 17

Data centre backlash grows, but few projects are actually being stopped, Goldman Sachs analysis shows

PolicyConstruction

About a dozen US states have proposed data centre moratoriums this year, with New York and Texas enacting temporary bans, yet Goldman Sachs finds that only about half of the AI computing capacity scheduled to activate through 2028 will likely come online on time — down from a historical on-time rate of about 72% — mainly due to shortages of materials, power and skilled labour rather than cancellations. Meeting current buildout targets would require roughly 500,000 more electricians, 300,000 more welders and 550,000 more plumbers in the US, even as data centre construction spending jumped 46% year-over-year to $68.3 billion in June.

Why it matters: The bottleneck increasingly isn't political opposition killing projects outright — it's a physical shortage of power, materials and workers slowing an industry still growing at a near-50% annual clip.
