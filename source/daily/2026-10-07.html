1

Michigan regulators approve DTE–Google power contracts for 1 GW Van Buren Township campus

EnergyPolicyConstruction

The Michigan Public Service Commission approved the 20-year DTE Energy arrangements on October 1 for Google's roughly 1 GW campus on 282 acres. First power is due December 2027 and full contracted load in December 2028, with a minimum billing demand of 80% of contracted capacity. The package includes funding for up to 1,600 MW of renewables and up to 480 MW of battery storage.

Why it matters: Take-or-pay terms plus funded new generation are becoming the regulatory template for gigawatt-scale loads.

Alberta's gas supply could fuel Canada's AI build-out, with Meta's Greenlight plant the lead project

CanadaEnergy

An October 3 analysis puts firm Canadian AI data centre projects at about 6 GW. Meta's Alberta Greenlight Electricity Centre is a 932 MW combined-cycle gas plant (expandable to 1,864 MW) costing about C$4.6 billion, with final investment decision reached in July 2026 and service targeted for the second half of 2030. Bitdeer's Fox Creek site pairs about 101 MW of on-site gas with 100 MW of compute, targeted for Q2 2027.

Why it matters: Gas-backed, bring-your-own-power campuses are setting the pace for Canadian AI capacity.

3

AI power swings force chip-to-grid redesign of data centres

TechEnergy

Rack power has climbed from 5-10 kW historically to about 150 kW for Blackwell-class and about 240 kW for Vera Rubin, with 1 MW racks projected alongside 800-volt DC distribution. AI loads can swing hundreds of megawatts in milliseconds, which could trigger wide-area grid disturbances. Proposed fixes include rack-level storage, supercapacitors and batteries, ride-through controls and smaller on-site generation blocks, such as Oracle's possible 30 MW units.

Why it matters: Load volatility, not just load size, is now a grid-reliability problem that chip makers, operators and utilities must solve together.

McKinsey: data centre power demand to grow 24% a year through 2030, with supply short of compute demand

Energy

McKinsey's Global Energy Perspective 2026 says its most likely scenarios all have power supply below IT compute demand through 2030. Constraints include interconnection and permitting delays, transformer lead times and gas pipeline capacity. More than 60% of operators now plan to combine on-site generation with grid access.

Why it matters: Early access to scarce power, not capital, is increasingly what separates winners in the build-out.

The UPS is uninterruptible by design, but 800 V DC and solid-state transformers threaten its role

Tech

UPS failures account for 39% of power-related data centre outages, and lithium-ion batteries are a documented fire risk. Hyperscalers are shifting to rack-level battery backup units, while 800 V DC architectures and battery energy storage systems challenge centralised UPS designs. Solid-state transformers are expected to reduce UPS demand in large AI facilities from 2029.

Why it matters: The backup-power architecture of AI facilities is being redesigned to protect the grid as much as the IT load.

Policy
