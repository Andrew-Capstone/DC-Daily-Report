1

TELUS exit from Vancouver AI data centres tied to lack of local support; sites were planned for 63,000 Nvidia chips and up to 163 MW

CanadaPolicy

The Globe and Mail, as republished by HeadTopics, reports that TELUS gave no official reason for ending its role in the two Westbank projects, but a source said a lack of local support led it to reconsider. Hundreds protested over the summer, one site still needs rezoning, and a pending city council motion could require independent environmental assessments and possible fees. The projects were planned for about 63,000 Nvidia chips and up to 163 MW, the memorandum with the federal government was never finalized, and TELUS says it will keep expanding in Kamloops and is evaluating other locations.

Why it matters: Community opposition, not power or capital, is the reported reason a flagship project under Ottawa's sovereign compute push lost its anchor.
Source → (The Globe and Mail via HeadTopics (Nov 15))
2

ERCOT chief to ask Texas lawmakers for power to curtail data centres before a grid emergency is declared

EnergyPolicy

Bloomberg reports that ERCOT chief executive Pablo Vegas told the BloombergNEF Summit in Houston he will ask the legislature to let the grid operator require data centres to cut power use at level one of its three-stage emergency scale or earlier, instead of level two as now. Texas's 2025 Senate Bill 6 already lets ERCOT order large loads to reduce use on 24 hours' notice, a power it has not yet used. Vegas said hyperscalers are willing to integrate with the grid and that data centres reduced demand voluntarily last winter.

Why it matters: Earlier curtailment would make flexibility a standing condition of connecting large loads in the biggest US data centre growth market.
Source → (Insurance Journal / Bloomberg (Nov 12))
3

Lenovo shows a fanless, fully water-cooled AMD EPYC server tray at SC26 that runs on 45°C inlet water

Tech

Lenovo's product guide for the ThinkSystem SC755 V5 Neptune, updated Nov 13, describes a tray with four AMD EPYC 9006 'Venice' processors of up to 256 cores and 600 W each, cooled entirely by direct water with no system fans. Inlet water can be as warm as 45°C, and Lenovo claims up to 40% lower data centre energy use than an equivalent air-cooled system, a vendor figure that depends on reduced chiller use. Each tray can run as two dual-socket or four single-socket nodes, and three 13U enclosures of eight trays fit in a rack.

Why it matters: Warm-water, fan-free designs let operators drop mechanical chillers, which is where most cooling energy and water goes.
Source → (Lenovo Press (updated Nov 13))
4

ISO New England files large-load plan with FERC: three service tiers and a bring-your-own-capacity rule for firm service

EnergyPolicy

ISO New England, NEPOOL and the region's transmission owners jointly filed a proposal covering large loads such as data centres, with a study process starting June 1, 2027. Firm service would require new capacity and network upgrades under a bring-your-own-new-capacity requirement, while interim and permanent non-firm options would need fewer upgrades. Non-firm and interim customers would have to cut consumption or run eligible generation when local energy prices reach $250 per MWh, which now happens in about 1% of hours. It is a proposal and FERC has not accepted it.

Why it matters: New England joins PJM, SPP and Texas in asking data centres to bring supply or accept interruption in exchange for a faster connection.
Source → (ISO Newswire (Nov 13))
5

StratGrid begins consultation on up to 240 MW of gas generation for a data centre site east of Calgary

CanadaEnergy

Alberta Data Centre Watch's Nov 13 weekly reports that consultation has begun on the Wheatland proposal, with up to 240 MW of net gas generation on 360 acres. An Alberta Utilities Commission application is targeted for the first quarter of 2027 and operation for the fourth quarter of 2028, with approvals outstanding. The tracker notes that earlier capacity figures of about 200 MW and 250 MW have not been reconciled, and that the Travers campus now describes a 10 MW first phase against an earlier concept of up to 2 GW.

Why it matters: Alberta's rules favour projects that bring their own generation, and smaller self-powered proposals are now moving through consultation.
Source → (Alberta Data Centre Watch (Nov 13))

Nvidia reports Tuesday, its first results since Huang's resignation: consensus revenue near $109B and EPS of about $2.47

TechFinance

StoneX's preview puts consensus at about $108.8B of revenue, close to 90% growth year over year, against company guidance of $108B plus or minus 2%. Data centre revenue was $89B in the prior quarter, and guidance excludes data centre compute sales to China. Investors are watching fourth-quarter guidance, the Blackwell Ultra ramp and when Vera Rubin contributes meaningfully, with interim chief executive Colette Kress leading the call and the stock about 30% below last month's record.

Why it matters: Nvidia's guidance is the clearest read on whether hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending is still accelerating.
Source → (StoneX (Nov 13))
7

Howden: US data centre power demand is rising 19% a year against grid growth of about 2%

Energy

A report by insurance broker Howden, covered by TechRadar, says data centres took 5% of US grid capacity in 2025, up from 0.1% in 2000, and that demand now exceeds available capacity by about ten times. It estimates up to 58% of data centre demand would need to come from alternative sources to keep expanding without adding grid load. Howden expects new supply chains to ease equipment constraints by late 2027 or early 2028, and says power costs in some states could rise by as much as 50% by 2030.

Why it matters: An insurer's view supports what developers are already doing: building on-site generation because the grid cannot keep pace.
Source → (TechRadar via Yahoo Finance (Nov 12))
8

Entergy seeks expropriation of 14 tracts for a 58-mile, 500 kV line serving Meta's Louisiana campus; landowners sue over fast-track approvals

EnergyPolicy

The Gulf States Newsroom reports that Entergy Louisiana sued in Lincoln Parish on Oct 14 for rights across 14 properties for a nearly 58-mile transmission line from Mount Olive to Sarepta, intended to serve Meta's Richland Parish data centre. Entergy says the line also supports grid reliability and was certified by state regulators. On Nov 9 the Louisiana Landowners Coalition and two other groups sued the Public Service Commission over its fast-track 'Lightning Directive', which Entergy plans to use in December to seek approval of seven gas plants for the campus.

Why it matters: Transmission land rights and expedited approvals are becoming the legal front for the largest data centre power build in the US South.
Source → (WRKF / Gulf States Newsroom (Nov 12))
9

Marvell brings optical memory links and 1.6T cables to SC26 as interconnect becomes an AI bottleneck

Tech

Marvell says it will demonstrate a Photonic Fabric memory module at SC26 in Chicago, using optical interconnect so memory and compute can scale across multiple racks. Its booth also covers CXL memory expansion, Teralynx switch silicon and 1.6T and 800G active electrical cables. The company joins a Nov 18 panel on photonics for AI and HPC clusters focused on bandwidth and power limits.

Why it matters: Moving memory and scale-up links to optics is one route to denser AI clusters without a matching rise in power.
Source → (HPCwire (Nov 13))
10

Bethlehem, Pennsylvania planners approve a 120 MW data centre with basement gas generators and an $86M power upgrade

ConstructionEnergy

WFMZ reports that the city planning commission approved the land development plan for a 65,590-square-foot, two-storey facility on 6.75 acres in Lehigh Valley Industrial Park I, proposed by Boyle Construction and TensoriIQ. Three natural gas generators below the building would supply about 20% of its power, and the company would spend about $86 million on power upgrades and pay for a new gas line. Cooling is closed-loop, though the generators would use about 45 million gallons of water, and the project is exempt from the city's newly amended data centre rules.

Why it matters: A 120 MW load on under seven acres shows how compact, partly self-powered urban sites are being fitted into existing industrial parks.
Source → (WFMZ (Nov 12))
11

Prologis sues Trenton, Ohio over voter-approved ban, citing $42M already spent on a permitted data centre

PolicyConstruction

About 80% of Trenton voters approved a charter amendment on Nov 3 prohibiting data centres above 25 MW. Prologis filed a federal suit about a week later, arguing the ban cannot be applied to its nearly one-million-square-foot project, for which the city issued zoning certificates and building permits months earlier and on which it has spent more than $42 million. Attorneys for local taxpayers have asked the city to revoke the zoning certificate.

Why it matters: This is an early test of whether the midterm ballot bans can reach projects that were already permitted and under construction.
Source → (Ideastream / WVXU (Nov 13))

Josh Hawley becomes the first Republican senator to back a federal data centre moratorium

Policy

Sinclair's national desk reports that the Missouri senator supports pausing data centre development until Congress passes legislation ensuring utility rates do not rise. The report says more Republicans now support rules covering conditions for building data centres and ratepayer protections, while noting a federal moratorium faces significant hurdles to becoming law. It is a stated position and no vote is scheduled.

Why it matters: Support for a federal pause is no longer confined to one party, even if passage remains unlikely.
Source → (Baltimore Sun / Sinclair (Nov 13))
13

Texas Democrats weigh extending the data centre permit pause and ending tax breaks after taking statewide offices and the House

Policy

KUT reports that governor-elect Gina Hinojosa campaigned on a moratorium on new data centre development, an end to industry tax breaks and more local say. Governor Abbott paused new permit approvals by executive order in September, and an analyst quoted says extending that pause is largely within the governor's authority, while tax and local-control changes need the legislature, where Republicans still hold the Senate. Projects already under way are expected to continue.

Why it matters: The most active US data centre market may shift from incentives to conditions when the new administration takes office.
Source → (Texas Public Radio / KUT (Nov 12))
14

Pennsylvania Democrats, now in control of the state Senate, line up moratorium and tax-exemption repeal bills for 2027

Policy

Inside Climate News reports that bills likely to return include a three-year statewide moratorium, authority for municipalities to impose 180-day pauses and repeal of the data centre sales tax exemption, which the state estimates could cost about $2 billion by mid-2031. Twenty-seven projects are in some stage of state permitting. The environment secretary said on Nov 10 that no developer has yet signed on to the governor's standards requiring projects to supply all of their own electricity, and advocates caution that the Senate majority is slim.

Why it matters: Pennsylvania has one of the largest utility pipelines in PJM, so legislative limits there would affect regional capacity planning.
Source → (Inside Climate News (Nov 13))

Four more data centre ballot measures set for December, including up to $55B in bonds for a Serverfarm project in Arkansas

PolicyFinance

Ballotpedia counts 47 data centre measures decided in 2026, 46 of which limited development. On Dec 1, Clarksville, Arkansas votes on whether to uphold two ordinances authorizing up to $50 billion and $5 billion in industrial development bonds with payment-in-lieu-of-taxes agreements for a Serverfarm project; the votes concern financing, not whether the facility is built. Hollis, Maine votes Dec 8 on a 180-day moratorium, and Mandan, North Dakota votes Dec 15 on prohibiting data centres of 25 MW or more.

Why it matters: Voters are now being asked to rule on incentive financing as well as zoning.
Source → (Ballotpedia News (Nov 15))
16

Judge strikes renewable energy and community-benefit conditions on a $3.1B, 120 MW St. Louis data centre

PolicyEnergy

A circuit judge ruled that the city's Board of Adjustment exceeded its authority when it required developer TerraWatt to use 2.4 MW of renewable energy and to obtain Board of Aldermen approval of a community benefits agreement for the project in a former Midtown warehouse. TerraWatt had called the conditions practically impossible on the 11-acre site. A separate suit by the Missouri Coalition for the Environment and others, challenging the use permit itself, continues.

Why it matters: The ruling limits how far local appeal boards can attach energy conditions to data centre permits.
Source → (St. Louis Public Radio (Nov 12))
17

Westlake, Texas approves PowerHouse's 88-acre campus after a settlement setting noise limits and about $1.9M in payments

ConstructionPolicy

The town council approved a development agreement for four buildings totalling about 1.1 million square feet, a week after a settlement with the neighbouring city of Keller and a homeowners association ended a lawsuit. PowerHouse must pay $1.5 million into a noise mitigation fund, plus fire protection and legal costs, and meet limits of 49 decibels overnight and 56 by day at residential property lines, with three years of sound monitoring. Backup generators must meet Tier 4 emissions standards.

Why it matters: Negotiated noise limits and mitigation funds are becoming the price of approval beside residential neighbourhoods.
Source → (NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth (Nov 13))
18

Virginia governor's 2027 agenda would bar data centre NDAs with local boards

Policy

VPM reports that Governor Abigail Spanberger's proposals would prohibit nondisclosure agreements tied to data centre facilities for boards of supervisors, planning commissions and boards of zoning appeals, and set limits on data centre planning and resource use. No bill language has been released, and it is unclear whether the ban would cover economic development authorities, one of which signed an NDA with Google in Chesterfield County years before plans became public.

Why it matters: Disclosure rules in the world's largest data centre market would change how early-stage site selection is conducted.
Source → (VPM News (Nov 12))
19

St. Cloud, Minnesota holds a hearing tonight on rules confining hyperscale data centres to industrial land

Policy

The proposed ordinance defines hyperscale as at least 2,000 servers in buildings over 10,000 square feet, or at least 50 MW, and would limit such sites to the industrial district at least a quarter mile from homes, with a conditional use permit and impact studies. The council may vote or postpone after Monday's hearing; no developer has proposed a large project there. MPR also notes that Minneapolis's temporary moratorium expires Nov 21 and its council reconvenes Nov 20 to address a possible veto.

Why it matters: Mid-sized cities are writing siting rules before any proposal arrives, instead of reaching for a moratorium afterwards.
Source → (MPR News (Nov 15))
20

Marietta, Georgia extends its data centre moratorium through 2027, rejecting a plan to lift it

Policy

The city council voted unanimously to extend the pause from Jan 1 to Dec 31, 2027, setting aside an earlier proposal to end it and confine new data centres to heavy industrial areas. Council members said they need more time for information and public feedback. The mayor has previously said the city lacks the electrical capacity for large-scale facilities.

Why it matters: Even where staff recommend zoning rules, councils in metro Atlanta are choosing to extend pauses.
Source → (WSB Radio (Nov 13))
