Wednesday, September 30, 2026 · North America focus
Morning brief: Grid and power dominated the day. FERC accepted but suspended until Feb. 28 PJM's plan to add capacity for data centres, Fervo and Google signed a 396-megawatt enhanced geothermal agreement in Utah, and Google described converting data-centre backup power into grid support while moving toward 800-volt DC. In Canada, Ottawa welcomed Bell's plan for up to 900 megawatts of sovereign AI capacity in Saskatchewan, with capital investment of up to 52.5 billion dollars, and 19 more companies signed the national responsible data-centre principles, for 42 in total. Deal flow centred on power and cooling: Flex is paying 4.4 billion dollars for EPC Power, SLB 4.1 billion for Kelvion, AMD 8.2 billion in stock for World Labs, and Samsung committed 1 billion dollars to KKR- and Nvidia-backed Helix. Policy friction continued, with a California ruling requiring a full environmental review of a 330-megawatt project, an executive order limiting foreign grid equipment, and new vetting rules aimed at speculative projects.
1

Fervo and Google sign a 396 MW enhanced-geothermal PPA in Utah, the largest of its kind

EnergyTech

Fervo Energy and Google signed a 396 MW power purchase agreement for the Cape Station GeoCluster in Utah, expected online in 2028, described as the largest enhanced geothermal PPA to date. Google holds an option to expand offtake by about 600 MW, for nearly 1 GW by June 2030, and the power is designed to anchor a potential Utah data centre.

Why it matters: Firm, carbon-free geothermal is emerging as a scalable alternative to gas and nuclear for round-the-clock AI load.

2

Bell's up-to-$52.5 billion Saskatchewan AI expansion could add 900 MW of sovereign capacity

CanadaConstructionFinance

Ottawa welcomed Bell Canada's plan to expand Bell AI Fabric in Saskatchewan by up to 900 MW, a path toward a 1.2 GW Canadian AI hub, with total capital investment of up to $52.5 billion and 4,500 jobs. The government says community and Indigenous engagement will precede specific project announcements, and it is exploring six gigawatts of new capacity nationwide.

Why it matters: It is the largest private investment in Saskatchewan's history and a flagship for Canada's sovereign-compute push.
Canada.ca → (Sept 14)
3

FERC accepts PJM's data-centre capacity plan but suspends it until Feb. 28

EnergyPolicy

The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission accepted the filing from PJM Interconnection, the biggest U.S. grid operator, to procure new capacity for a region struggling to meet data-centre demand, but suspended it until Feb. 28. The plan is meant to accelerate the addition of new power supply.

Why it matters: A five-month pause delays relief in the country's most data-centre-dense grid, prolonging capacity-price and interconnection uncertainty.
Bloomberg → (Sept 30)
4

Google turns AI data centres from backup power consumers into grid partners

TechEnergy

Google is converting backup power systems into grid-supportive resources, adding battery storage and liquid cooling, and moving from 54-volt DC at the rack toward 800-volt DC distribution as rack density approaches 1 MW. It now processes over 3 quadrillion AI tokens a month, up sevenfold between May 2025 and May 2026.

Why it matters: Flexible, grid-interactive campuses could speed interconnection and ease the political case against data-centre load.
Data Center Knowledge → (Sept 25)
5

Canada's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles gain 19 more signatories, reaching 42

CanadaPolicy

Launched Sept. 3 with 23 signatories including AWS, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Meta, the five-point framework covers local benefits, ratepayer protection, water and environmental limits, transparency and strategic value to Canada. On Sept. 22 Ottawa added 19 signatories, among them AMD, Intel, IBM and NVIDIA.

Why it matters: A voluntary national standard sets the terms for hyperscale entry into Canada alongside provincial and Indigenous processes.
Canada.ca → (Sept 3 launch; Sept 22 update)

Flex pays $4.4 billion for EPC Power to capture 800V DC and grid-forming technology

TechFinance

Flex is acquiring EPC Power, which specializes in 800V DC technology and grid-forming systems that deliver higher power with lower electrical losses. Coverage notes power conversion is moving from a supporting component to a strategic control point.

Why it matters: Power architecture is becoming as contested as chips as AI racks head toward megawatt densities.

AMD to acquire Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2 billion in stock

TechFinance

AMD will buy the world-model lab World Labs, bringing Fei-Fei Li on as executive vice president and chief scientist. The deal is aimed at physical AI and robotics and at understanding future chip requirements, positioning AMD against Nvidia's early lead.

Why it matters: It signals that chipmakers are buying model expertise to shape the next generation of AI accelerator demand.

SLB buys cooling specialist Kelvion for $4.1 billion

TechFinance

SLB is acquiring thermal-management company Kelvion to move beyond modular construction into cooling and heat rejection. The combined business targets over $2 billion of 2026 revenue, growing to $4.5 to $5 billion by 2028.

Why it matters: Oilfield-services and industrial giants are pushing into data-centre thermal systems as heat becomes the binding constraint.

Samsung commits $1 billion to KKR- and Nvidia-backed AI infrastructure firm Helix

FinanceConstruction

Samsung Electronics will invest $500 million and five affiliates the rest in Helix Digital Infrastructure, formed by KKR in June 2026 and led by former AWS CEO Adam Selipsky. Backers include Nvidia, the Kuwait Investment Authority and Vistra; Helix pairs hyperscale sites with power generation, transmission and fibre, and had over $10 billion committed beforehand.

Why it matters: Integrated power-plus-compute platforms are attracting supply-chain and sovereign capital.
CNBC → (Sept 29)
10

Meta breaks ground on $10 billion, 1 GW Lebanon, Indiana campus

Construction

Turner Construction is one of the contractors on the four-million-square-foot campus, which supports over 4,000 construction jobs at peak and about 300 permanent roles. Meta says it will match energy use with clean energy, use closed-loop liquid cooling and pay the full cost of the energy it uses.

Why it matters: Ratepayer-protection and water pledges are now standard terms for a gigawatt-scale project.
Data Centre Magazine → (Sept 2026)

Trump executive order restricts foreign-made grid equipment, adding data-centre procurement risk

PolicyEnergy

An August executive order declares a national emergency over the bulk power system and restricts foreign-produced grid equipment such as transformers and inverters. The Department of Energy has 120 days to publish implementation rules.

Why it matters: Transformer and inverter lead times are already long; sourcing limits could slow energisation of new campuses.
Data Center Knowledge → (Aug 28)

Efforts to curb data-centre speculation gain ground across the U.S.

Policy

More than $170 billion of AI data-centre capacity has been blocked or stalled by local opposition since January 2024, against $581 billion hyperscalers plan to invest this year. Utilities and regulators are adding vetting rules and large-load tariffs to separate real projects from speculative ones.

Why it matters: Tighter queue rules will shape which of the many proposed campuses ever get power.

California judge orders full environmental review of a 330 MW Imperial County data centre

PolicyConstruction

In a tentative ruling, a judge found Imperial County wrongly treated approvals as ministerial and relied on a 2017 zoning review that did not cover the facility's batteries, generators, water and transmission. The ruling says hyperscale sites cannot bypass CEQA through piecemeal approvals.

Why it matters: It sets a precedent that fast-track approvals for large campuses can be overturned.

Space data centres inch toward reality, but launch cost and heat remain hurdles

Tech

Launch costs of $2,500 to $3,000 per kilogram would need to fall to $200 to $500, and thermal management and bandwidth remain unsolved. Experts call for 100 to 500 kW-class proof points, and the U.S. Air Force CIO says the technology is not ready for operational use.

Why it matters: It tempers orbital-compute hype with the engineering gaps still open.
