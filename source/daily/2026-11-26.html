1

Fluidstack launches Blackbird, a modular 400 MW data centre design it says can be built in six months

TechConstruction

Fluidstack's Blackbird is a standard modular design that scales from one compute zone of 10+ MW up to a 400 MW facility. The company targets six months from groundbreaking to completion, compared with the usual 18 to 24 months. The system has two main parts. A self-supporting Compute Module carries power, cooling and cabling overhead, includes hot-aisle containment, and works with standard racks and any chip. An Electrical Skid takes a 35 kV-class supply and delivers 480 V or 415 V. Everything is built at a 1.1 million sq ft factory in Glendale, Arizona, which the company says will create 1,250 permanent jobs.

Why it matters: If the six-month timeline holds, factory-built AI capacity could cut build times by two-thirds. The claim comes only from Fluidstack so far, and no customer site has been named.

2

Ohio approves 2,150 MW of off-grid gas generation, plus 1,040 MW of batteries, for Vantage and EdgeConneX campuses

EnergyPolicy

On Nov 19, the Ohio Power Siting Board approved Advanced Power's Fairfield Energy Center in Millersport, a 1,350 MW gas plant with a 1,040 MW battery storage facility on 122 acres, to power Vantage's Millersport campus. It also approved PowerConneX's 800 MW Ashville Energy Center in Pickaway County, which will serve data centres owned by its parent, EdgeConneX. Neither plant will connect to the public grid. These approvals bring Ohio to 10 behind-the-meter gas plants under way for data centres, most of them near Columbus.

Why it matters: Ohio has become the test case for data centres building their own gas plants to avoid grid queues. Ashville residents voted down the data centre agreement on Nov 3 and have asked the board to reopen the case.
Source → (Farm and Dairy, Nov 24; OPSB cases 26-160-EL-BGN and 26-196-EL-BGN)
3

Nova Scotia's new Liberal leader calls for a pause on the 420 MW Point Tupper data centre that Anthropic has leased

CanadaPolicy

Becky Druhan won the Nova Scotia Liberal leadership. At her first news conference on Nov 25, she called on the PC government to pause EverWind's planned 420 MW, $10B AI data centre at Point Tupper in Cape Breton. Her party is proposing a Data Centre Water and Ratepayer Protection Act covering water use, power rates and infrastructure costs. EverWind says the project is going ahead as planned, and it has signed an agreement with Nova Scotia Power stating that ratepayers won't bear the cost of the data centre's power demand. Premier Tim Houston noted that the Carney government supports the project, and the NDP has called for an emergency debate.

Why it matters: This is Canada's most prominent hyperscale AI lease, and it is now a provincial political issue, in the same week as Trump's threat to tax US firms that build data centres abroad.
Source → (Global News, Nov 25; also National Observer)
4

US Army asks developers to propose power plants, including advanced nuclear, with AI data centres at nine bases

EnergyPolicy

A Nov 19 request for proposals offers 25-year leases at nine bases, including Fort Bragg, Fort Hood, Blue Grass Army Depot and JBLM. Eligible power sources include gas turbines, reciprocating engines, fuel cells, hydrogen, advanced nuclear reactors, batteries, and solar paired with dispatchable generation. A power plant alone qualifies, but a data centre must come with its own power. The Army prefers classified-workload facilities, then hyperscale AI facilities with a PUE of 1.15 or lower. Each project must be able to power its host base within an hour of a grid outage and keep it running for at least 14 days. Proposals are due Jan 18, 2027.

Why it matters: Some of these sites are under exclusive federal jurisdiction, so developers could build there without facing the local moratoriums spreading across the country.
Source → (National Law Review, Nov 25; RFP DACA27-1-27-0015900)
5

Applied Digital signs an 18-year, $5.4B lease for 150 MW with an investment-grade hyperscaler

FinanceConstruction

Applied Digital announced an 18-year lease for 150 MW of critical IT capacity. Base-term revenue is about $5.4B, rising to about $12.7B if every renewal is exercised over a full 33-year term. Deliveries are expected to start in the first half of 2028. The company did not name the campus. It expects to finish project financing for Delta Forge 1 and Polaris Forge 3 in Q1 2027, and for Delta Forge 2 in Q2 2027.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are still locking in capacity for 2028 under long, investment-grade leases, which supports financing for former crypto miners turned AI developers.
Source → (Investing.com, Nov 23; also DCD)
6

Google books a 2029 Blue Origin launch to test rack-scale TPU computing in orbit

Tech

Under Project Suncatcher, Google has booked a New Glenn launch in Q1 2029. It will place satellites carrying Tensor Processing Units in sun-synchronous orbit to test whether space-based systems can run AI workloads at about the scale of one data centre rack. Analysts say heat rejection is the main obstacle, and that hardware would need to last at least five years, matching normal depreciation cycles, for the economics to work. The deal was announced Nov 19.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are moving from talking about orbital compute to booking launches, but practical capacity in orbit is still years away.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge, Nov 25)
7

CCPA: Canada's data centre build-out is bypassing local opposition

CanadaPolicy

In a Nov 26 analysis, CCPA senior researcher Simon Enoch argues that provinces are steering data centre approvals around municipalities. He points to three examples. Alberta's proposed law would make the AUC the main regulator for sites over 10 MW and let it override local rules. In Saskatchewan, after most of the RM of Sherwood council resigned, the province appointed four non-resident members, who approved Bell's campus as it grew from 300 MW to 1.2 GW. In Ontario, developers can challenge municipal moratoriums at the Ontario Land Tribunal.

Why it matters: Canada's pitch is speed and certainty, but this argument suggests local legitimacy could become the next point of friction.
Source → (Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives, Nov 26)
8

Arizona AG asks Cochise County to delay its Dec 9 vote on SpaceX's $50B, 6 GW campus

EnergyPolicy

Attorney General Kris Mayes asked the Planning and Zoning Commission to hold off on SpaceX's Special Use Permit until state and federal reviews are complete. Her letter notes that the campus needs 1.5 GW at start-up, which is 62% more than the Arizona Electric Power Cooperative's entire fleet, and 6 GW at full build-out. SpaceX has proposed $3.6B in grid investment but has not said how it will cover the remaining 4.5 GW. She also raised concerns about about 6,150 Tesla Megapacks on a site outside any fire district, and about effects on Fort Huachuca. She holds a town hall on Dec 3.

Why it matters: Rural cooperatives are being asked to supply gigawatt-scale loads, and state officials are now stepping into county-level approvals.
Source → (Arizona's Family, Nov 19)
9

Rolling Meadows approves Digital Realty's 156 MW campus on a vacant Chicago-area office site, 4-2

ConstructionFinance

On Nov 24, Rolling Meadows city council approved zoning relief and a tax incentive for a $466.9M redevelopment of the 40-acre Atrium Corporate Center. Digital Realty bought the site in September for $81.5M. The 485,000 sq ft office building will be demolished. The campus will have 156 MW of critical power, fed by a 240 MW ComEd substation. Residents raised concerns about noise and disputed how far the nearest home is from the site. Work may not start until 2029.

Why it matters: Converting empty suburban offices into data centres gives builders sites close to the city that already have power and zoning. Brennan Investment Group sold the land for three times what it paid.
Source → (The Inference, Nov 25; also DCD)
10

Pennsylvania Sen. Lisa Baker plans a bill banning data centre NDAs for public agencies and requiring two public meetings

Policy

State Sen. Lisa Baker (R) said she will introduce a bill in the coming weeks. It would bar public agencies from signing nondisclosure agreements about proposed data centres and require at least two public meetings before a municipality approves one. Applicants would have to show they have applied for electric service and paid the related fees before those meetings, and reporting rules would be tightened so data centre costs don't shift onto other ratepayers. Baker wants brownfields to come before farmland.

Why it matters: Pennsylvania is one of the fastest-growing PJM markets. Banning NDAs would take away the secrecy many developers rely on early in a project.
Source → (Times Leader, Nov 25)
11

Warrick County, Indiana becomes the first county in its area to adopt data centre rules: a floating overlay with 500-foot setbacks

Policy

The Warrick County Commission unanimously approved a Floating Data Center Overlay District on Nov 23. It sets a 500-foot minimum setback, addresses noise, and lets the county adjust requirements for each project. Commission President Sarah Seaton called it a "baby step" and said the minimums could be strengthened. Some residents want limits on on-site power generation, which they say adds noise.

Why it matters: Southern Indiana counties are writing rules to head off lawsuits as AWS spends heavily elsewhere in the state.
Source → (WNIN, Nov 24)
12

Pennington County, South Dakota postpones a decision on a 20 MW modular data centre until January and asks for a noise study

Policy

The Planning Commission postponed its decision on Nodiac Development's conditional use permit for a 20 MW modular data centre near Old Folsom Road to its Jan 25, 2027 meeting. Staff asked for platting work and a detailed acoustical study. The project would use closed-loop cooling that needs about 28,000 to 30,000 gallons of coolant at setup and no new well. The developer would pay for any required electrical upgrades.

Why it matters: Even small modular projects now face noise and water review, using county rules that took effect in October 2025.
Source → (KOTA, Nov 25)
