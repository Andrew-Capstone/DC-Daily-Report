Morning brief: Regulation and capital, not new chips, drove the week. New York's Governor Hochul signed the nation's first statewide moratorium on hyperscale data centres (50MW+) on July 14, and the shockwaves are still spreading: Michigan rolled out its own ratepayer-protection pledge, Brookhaven, NY imposed an 18-month local pause that has stalled a $1 billion Long Island project, and Canadian cities are now asking the same question, with Hamilton voting down a similar bylaw 10-6 and Vancouver deferring a Telus AI data centre rezoning until after its October election. On the money side, a BlackRock-led consortium closed the largest data-centre acquisition ever — $40 billion for Aligned Data Centers — while also leading a $12 billion-plus bond sale to fund Meta's El Paso campus; Intel raised its 2026 capex above $20 billion on AI chip demand. On power, New Mexico regulators rejected, for a second time, the gas pipeline Oracle needs for its 2.5GW Project Jupiter site, and a bipartisan U.S. House committee advanced a five-bill package, including the Ratepayer Protection Act, to shield electricity customers from data-centre-driven grid costs. A Data Center Watch report found opposition groups have more than doubled to 833 across 49 states, with $130 billion in projects blocked or delayed in Q1 alone.
1

New York becomes first US state to impose a moratorium on new hyperscale data centres

Policy

Governor Kathy Hochul signed an executive order on July 14 pausing state environmental permits for up to a year for new "hyperscaler" data centres using 50 megawatts or more of power, while the state builds a regulatory framework covering ratepayer protection, the grid, and community impact. Existing approved projects are unaffected, and Hochul is separately pursuing legislation to repeal sales-tax exemptions for large data centres.

Why it matters: As the first state-level construction pause in the country, New York's move hands every other state and province — several of which are already debating similar bylaws — a concrete precedent to point to.

2

BlackRock-led consortium closes record $40 billion acquisition of Aligned Data Centers

Finance

AIP, MGX and BlackRock's Global Infrastructure Partners closed their acquisition of Aligned Data Centers on July 21, buying 100% of the equity from funds managed by Macquarie Asset Management. The consortium is also committing an additional $5 billion to fund Aligned's expansion; the operator runs 51 campuses with more than 6.4GW of operational and planned capacity across the Americas.

Why it matters: At $40 billion, it's the largest data-centre acquisition on record, more than double the previous benchmark, and confirms sovereign wealth and infrastructure funds are now the dominant source of capital for the industry's growth.

3

New Mexico regulators reject gas pipeline for Oracle's 2.5GW Project Jupiter data centre — again

Energy

New Mexico's Commissioner of Public Lands denied, for the second time, Energy Transfer's request to build the 17-mile "Green Chile" natural gas lateral across state land needed to power Oracle's 2.5GW Project Jupiter campus in Doña Ana County, citing water use, CO2 emissions and insufficient state revenue. Oracle says the project remains on schedule, but SemiAnalysis now expects power on site to slip to 2029, from an original 2027 target.

Why it matters: A state repeatedly blocking the fuel supply for a flagship Stargate-linked campus shows local land and environmental authorities can now meaningfully delay even the best-funded AI infrastructure projects.

4

House committee advances Ratepayer Protection Act and four-bill grid package, 52-0

EnergyPolicy

The House Energy and Commerce Committee voted 52-0 on July 22 to advance the Ratepayer Protection Act (H.R. 9340), which would push states toward a federal standard requiring data centres and other large loads to pay the full cost of new generation and transmission built to serve them. The committee also passed four companion bills on grid forecasting, transmission technology and high-capacity conductors as part of the broader POWER Act permitting package.

Why it matters: Unanimous, bipartisan committee support for making data centres pay their own grid costs shows Washington converging fast on a policy that, until recently, was mostly a state-by-state fight.

5

Intel raises 2026 capex above $20 billion as AI and data-centre chip demand outstrips supply

FinanceTech

Intel beat Q2 2026 estimates with $16.1 billion in revenue and record data-centre server growth, and raised its planned 2026 capital spending from roughly $18 billion to more than $20 billion, mostly for 14A and 18A manufacturing tooling. Executives said supply, not demand, is now the ceiling on the company's data-centre chip business, with 2027 capex expected to run "significantly above" 2026.

Why it matters: Intel joining the ranks of chipmakers raising already-huge capex confirms AI data-centre demand for processors is still outrunning the industry's ability to build capacity.

6

Data centre opposition groups double to 833 across 49 states as $130 billion in projects stall

Policy

A Data Center Watch report published July 24 found at least 75 US data-centre projects worth roughly $130 billion were blocked or delayed in the first quarter of 2026 alone — matching all of 2025's disruption in three months — while active opposition groups more than doubled, from 396 at the end of 2025 to 833 by March, spanning 49 states. Researchers say over 300 state-level data-centre bills were filed in the first six weeks of 2026, with 14 states floating statewide moratoriums.

Why it matters: The numbers turn a string of anecdotal local fights into a documented, nationwide structural shift that developers and financiers now have to underwrite as a real project risk.

7

BlackRock leads $12 billion-plus bond sale to fund Meta's El Paso data centre

Finance

BlackRock's infrastructure and private-credit arms, which own 80% of the roughly 1-gigawatt El Paso, Texas project with Meta holding the remaining 20% and leasing it back, are marketing at least $12 billion in bonds through JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley this week. The structure mirrors the one used for Meta's Hyperion campus in Louisiana, where a similar joint venture sold $27 billion in bonds last year; the El Paso site is expected online in 2028.

Why it matters: BlackRock alone moved roughly $57 billion across data-centre deals this week between this bond sale and the Aligned acquisition, underlining how central a single asset manager has become to funding the AI buildout.

8

Hamilton, Ontario council votes down proposed data centre moratorium, 10-6

CanadaPolicy

Hamilton city council rejected a proposed interim control bylaw that would have paused new data-centre development in the city for a year, voting 10-6 against after a closed-door session in which the city's legal team advised a McMaster University-linked data-centre project would likely have been exempt anyway. The vote followed weeks of public hearings and would have made Hamilton the first Canadian municipality to impose such a pause.

Why it matters: Hamilton was widely seen as a bellwether for Canadian municipal policy on data centres, so the bylaw's defeat is a signal to other cities watching that local moratoriums face real political headwinds even amid vocal opposition.

9

Vancouver council defers decision on Telus-Westbank AI data centre until after October election

CanadaPolicy

Vancouver city council voted unanimously on July 21 to reverse its earlier referral of the Telus-Westbank "M3" AI data centre rezoning in Mount Pleasant to a public hearing, citing overwhelming public interest and concern that a formal hearing would gag council members ahead of the October 17 civic election. The proposal, one of three Telus AI data centres planned for the region, returns to city staff for further review rather than being approved or rejected.

Why it matters: Punting a high-profile AI data centre decision past an election shows how politically charged the siting fights have become in Canada's largest western city, mirroring the backlash already reshaping US state and local politics.

New York's data centre pause fuels Canadian debate as federal NDP pushes for a national moratorium

CanadaPolicy

Following New York's statewide moratorium, Canadian coverage notes the country currently has just five operating hyperscale data centres but many more planned or under construction, and federal NDP politicians are now pushing Ottawa to consider a similar nationwide pause. The debate has already played out locally in Hamilton, where council rejected its own proposed bylaw days after the New York order was signed.

Why it matters: A US state's regulatory move directly shaping federal Canadian political debate shows how tightly linked North American data-centre policy has become, even across the border.

11

Brookhaven, NY's 18-month data centre moratorium puts $1 billion Long Island project in limbo

PolicyConstruction

Brookhaven's town board voted unanimously on July 16 to halt all new data-centre applications for 18 months while planners draft zoning rules covering siting, environmental mitigation and utility interaction, following Governor Hochul's statewide executive order days earlier. The local pause, running until January 2028, has stalled a roughly $1 billion data-centre project already proposed for the town.

Why it matters: A state moratorium triggering an even stricter local one within days shows how quickly regulatory momentum can cascade from the state level down to individual towns once the first domino falls.

12

Oracle takes Wisconsin regulators to court over $7 billion data-centre collateral rule

FinancePolicy

Oracle filed for judicial review in Ozaukee County Circuit Court against Wisconsin's Public Service Commission, arguing its ruling requiring more than $7 billion in collateral for Oracle's 1GW We Energies-served campus was unsupported and irrational; the PSC declined to reconsider the requirement in mid-July. The dispute comes as S&P's recent downgrade of Oracle to BBB- puts the company two notches below the credit threshold the utility's tariff requires.

Why it matters: A court fight over collateral rules is an early legal test of how far state utility regulators can go in pricing hyperscaler credit risk, with implications for every state weighing similar rules.

14

Pennsylvania budget requires large data centres to disclose energy and water use or face daily fines

Policy

Pennsylvania's newly passed $50.8 billion state budget requires data centres with peak demand of 10 megawatts or more to annually report electricity and water use, projected future demand, and any on-site power generation to the Department of Environmental Protection starting July 2027; non-compliance carries fines of $10,000 a day. Lawmakers left the state's roughly $2 billion data-centre sales-tax exemption untouched despite a flurry of stricter proposals during budget negotiations.

Why it matters: Mandatory public reporting of resource use — even without new taxes — gives communities and regulators hard data to negotiate with going forward, a template other states are likely to copy.

Why it matters: Michigan becomes the latest and largest Midwest data-centre market to formally adopt the "pay your own way" framework spreading across state governments this summer.
