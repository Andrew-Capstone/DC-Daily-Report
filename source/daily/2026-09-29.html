1

Google's first Project Suncatcher satellite, carrying four TPUs, is set to launch into orbit Oct. 1

Tech

Google and Planet Labs will fly a refrigerator-sized prototype satellite called MVP on a SpaceX Falcon 9 Transporter-18 rideshare. It carries four TPUs and about one kilowatt of solar power, and will test radiation resilience, cooling in vacuum and failure points ahead of a proposed constellation of orbiting compute.

Why it matters: It is the first real-world test of whether AI chips can survive and run in space, an idea Google, SpaceX and startups are betting could relieve pressure on terrestrial power grids.

2

South Korea picks a 6.3-gigawatt Texas gas plant as first project under its $350 billion U.S. investment pact

Energy

Reuters reports Seoul selected a gas-fired plant in Encinal, Texas as the first project under its U.S. investment package, at a planned cost above $20 billion. The project still needs U.S. investment-committee approval and further negotiation, and the ownership structure envisions shared returns over 20 years.

Why it matters: Foreign capital is now being routed into gigawatt-scale gas generation explicitly tied to AI load forecasts, while a 20-year plant life sits against fast-changing AI demand.
Reuters → (Sept 21; details via Dave Friedman's weekly report)
3

Idaho's first data centre on federal land: 3,750-acre Rhea Data campus with gas plant proposed near Boise

ConstructionEnergy

Arevia Power subsidiary Rhea Data proposes a roughly 4-million-square-foot campus on about 3,750 acres of Bureau of Land Management land in southern Ada County, with a natural gas plant and links to a proposed 400 MW solar project. The BLM is in pre-planning, expects to publish a federal notice of intent before year-end, and a full NEPA review would follow; Boise's mayor has called the push shortsighted.

Why it matters: Federal-land siting with on-site gas generation is a template other Western developers may copy, and it is already drawing local political fire.
East Idaho News → (Sept 2026)
4

Floating data centres move toward infrastructure scale, with Texas and offshore projects in the pipeline

Tech

Data Center Frontier surveys Samsung Heavy Industries and Mousterian's 50 MW floating units (aimed at Texas and other U.S. markets), Seatrium's 30 MW modular barge design with approval in principle, and Atomarine's 75 to 100 MW compute barges targeting a gas-powered pilot in 2028. Environmental and permitting questions remain unresolved.

Why it matters: Barge-based compute could sidestep land-use fights and grid queues, but regulators have not yet said how they will treat it.
Data Center Frontier → (Sept 23)
5

Saskatchewan: new data centres must be Canadian-owned and bring their own power

CanadaPolicy

The province's new framework requires new data centres to be Canadian-owned and to supply their own electricity, with applicants showing experience and job-creation capacity through a centralized intake system. Bell's $1.7 billion AI data centre near Regina is exempt because it was approved earlier and draws SaskPower grid power; the NDP has questioned the transparency of that approval.

Why it matters: Saskatchewan is tying data sovereignty and ratepayer protection together, a sharp contrast with provinces courting foreign hyperscalers.
ConstructConnect → (Sept 1)
6

FERC rejects ComEd's bid to cancel a 1.8 GW, $20 billion Illinois data centre agreement backed by a $1 letter of credit

EnergyPolicy

FERC refused to accept Commonwealth Edison's cancellation of a transmission security agreement with PowerHouse Hillwood for a 1.8 GW campus in Joliet, and declined to assert primary jurisdiction, so the dispute proceeds in federal district court. Commissioner David LaCerte called the $1 letter of credit an "embarrassing legal fiction."

Why it matters: It puts a spotlight on thin financial security for large-load interconnection requests, the same speculation problem regulators are moving to fix.
Utility Dive → (Sept 22)
7

Abbott orders Texas to halt all data centre permits pending ERCOT and water audits

PolicyEnergy

Governor Greg Abbott directed the TCEQ to stop acting on every data centre permit until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish their audits, on top of the earlier interconnection pause. TCEQ must report on compliance by Oct. 19, and the governor signalled legislation to end financial incentives. Separately, the PUCT adopted rules requiring 75 MW-plus projects to post roughly $50 million in security.

Why it matters: Texas, the top destination for new capacity, now has power, water and environmental gates all closed until audits finish, with ERCOT targeting a Dec. 10 completion.
Texas Governor's Office → (Sept 21)
8

Oracle invokes force majeure on the New Mexico Project Jupiter campus over power delays

FinanceConstruction

Oracle told partners it cannot secure power to the New Mexico site, pushing completion back about a year. Blue Owl, with roughly $3 billion of equity in the STACK-built campus for OpenAI, earns lower returns during construction; Oracle and Blue Owl shares fell about 3.5%.

Why it matters: Even investment-grade tenants are exposed to power-delivery risk, and lenders are now scrutinizing other pending projects.
Investing.com (Reuters analysis) → (Sept 24-25)
9

Cipher extends its 300 MW Barber Lake lease, adding about $5.2 billion of contracted revenue

Finance

Per an SEC filing, Cipher amended its Fluidstack lease to add a binding second 10-year term from an unnamed AI lab, lifting total contracted revenue above $9 billion. Delivery is now Q4 2026 to Q1 2027, and Cipher absorbs the first $359.3 million of cost overruns before costs are split 50/50.

Why it matters: Longer lease terms match financing to the life of the building and power, not GPU depreciation, though the second tenant's credit remains undisclosed.
SEC filing → (Sept 24)
10

Applied Digital confirms $3.2 billion Delta Forge 2 AI campus in Brookwood, Alabama

Construction

The roughly 1.26-million-square-foot campus sits on part of a nearly 1,300-acre site in Tuscaloosa County, with over 1,000 construction workers at peak and about 100 permanent jobs. Applied Digital commits $270 million in community benefit payments over 20 years plus about $131.5 million in education taxes.

Why it matters: Large upfront community-benefit packages are becoming the price of approval as opposition grows in traditionally friendly states.

11

Data centres drove a 248% jump in U.S. construction starts in August, UBS says

ConstructionFinance

Data centre starts rose 248% year over year in August, helping lift building starts 31.8%, while warehouse starts fell 21.7% and non-data-centre office starts fell 15%. UBS expects nonresidential construction to accelerate in the second half.

Why it matters: The buildout is now large enough to move national construction statistics on its own.
Seeking Alpha → (Sept 2026)
12

WSJ: the AI build-out is becoming the biggest economic bet in U.S. history

Finance

The Wall Street Journal reports data centre and AI infrastructure investment is projected at $10.3 trillion from 2025 to 2032, about 3.6% of annual GDP and more than canals, railroads, highways and internet infrastructure achieved historically.

Why it matters: The scale frames why power, financing and public acceptance have become national economic questions.
Wall Street Journal (via Mission Critical News) → (Sept 28)
13

Bond buyers get pickier as AI debt floods the investment-grade market

Finance

Reuters reports AI-related investment-grade spreads near 115 basis points versus about 78 for the broader market, with some double-A AI issuers priced near triple-B levels. Hyperscaler issuance is projected to reach $420 billion in 2027, 60% above 2026.

Why it matters: Rising financing costs are the clearest market signal that supply of AI debt is testing investor appetite.
Reuters → (Sept 22; details via Dave Friedman's weekly report)
14

Virginia governor bars state help and fast-track permits for data centres of 25 MW or more

Policy

Governor Abigail Spanberger's executive order excludes 25 MW-plus projects from state site assistance and expedited permitting, bans NDAs on data centre details and incentives, and orders new rules on noise, emissions, water and power within 120 to 240 days. Maryland's governor signed similar guardrails the same week.

Why it matters: The world's largest data centre market is pulling back incentives, with major changes likely in the 2027 legislative session.
Construction Dive → (Sept 18)
15

Prince William County votes unanimously to shrink its data centre overlay district by 62%

Policy

Supervisors cut the Data Center Opportunity Zone Overlay from about 9,700 acres to about 3,641, ending most by-right development. Owners inside the district have a 90-day window to file applications to keep by-right status.

Why it matters: Prince William's turn away from by-right approval in Northern Virginia hands the board control over future projects.
Virginia Business → (Sept 23)

States move to curb data centre speculation with large-load tariffs and connection rules

PolicyEnergy

Utility Dive rounds up Texas's pause on its 474 GW queue, Florida's law making 50 MW-plus data centres pay full connection costs, Pennsylvania's new permitting framework for 25 MW-plus loads, and tariffs with minimum terms and collateral. Over $170 billion of capacity has been blocked, withdrawn or stalled since January 2024.

Why it matters: Tariff design is becoming the main tool for separating credible projects from placeholder requests.

Policy

About 25 congregations and community groups asked Microsoft to fund the community with 1 to 2 percent of annual operating costs, an estimated $30 to 40 million a year, for its planned Granger, Indiana campus. Microsoft has gone quiet, according to Ars Technica.

Why it matters: Community-benefit demands are shifting from one-time pledges to recurring payments.

Osage Nation legislator moves to require congressional approval for hyperscale sites on tribal land

Policy

Legislation (ONCA 26-70) would require tribal entities to seek Congress's approval before permitting hyperscale data centres, amid talks over Google's 827-acre Project Spring in Sand Springs, Oklahoma. The Natural Resources Committee postponed its decision pending budget deliberations.

Why it matters: It adds a sovereignty and consent question to siting on tribal land.
KOSU → (Sept 28; via Data Centers: Daily Notes)

Tulare County, California formally opposes most data centre development and extends its moratorium

Policy

Supervisors voted 3-2 to oppose most data centre development countywide and extended the unincorporated-area moratorium by about ten and a half months. The position excludes enterprise facilities serving a single operator's own operations.

Why it matters: California's Central Valley counties are pre-emptively closing the door as the state enacts its own data centre laws.
