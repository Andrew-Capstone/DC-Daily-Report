1

Texas Governor Abbott freezes new data-centre grid connections pending a statewide audit

PolicyEnergy

Governor Greg Abbott directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas and ERCOT to conduct a "comprehensive verification and audit" of every data-centre project seeking to connect to the state grid, requiring developers to disclose tax incentives, power and water usage, cooling technology, and community-impact mitigation before any project can proceed. ERCOT has more than 1,800 projects and 474 gigawatts of interconnection requests in queue — about five times the grid's peak-demand record, with roughly 90% coming from data centres — and postponed its next transmission planning study to implement the directive.

Why it matters: It's the most aggressive statewide intervention yet in the AI buildout's largest interconnection queue, and signals that developers must now prove financing and permits are real rather than simply holding a place in line.

2

Samsung Heavy Industries advances floating AI data-centre plans into engineering phase

Tech

Samsung Heavy Industries signed an engineering agreement with Texas-based Mousterian Corporation to design moored, purpose-built floating data-centre units, each delivering 50 megawatts of critical IT capacity with liquid cooling and non-evaporative, no-potable-water cooling systems. Initial deployments are planned for Texas and other U.S. markets, with Samsung Heavy targeting commercialization by the second quarter of 2028; shipyard-based fabrication would let units be built in parallel with land-side site prep.

Why it matters: Floating, factory-built data centres offer a way around the land, water and power-siting fights slowing onshore projects — a genuinely new construction model rather than an incremental efficiency gain.

3

Empromptu launches "Grid Guard" AI software to smooth GPU power spikes

TechEnergy

San Francisco-based Empromptu AI launched Grid Guard, software that predicts GPU power-demand swings and staggers workloads in 50-to-200-millisecond bursts to convert sharp spikes into gradual ramps, while sending advance signals to on-site batteries and generators. The company says thousands of GPUs firing simultaneously can swing demand by tens of megawatts in milliseconds, risking generator damage and forcing costly overbuilding; early testing shows an average 80% reduction in power volatility without hurting AI performance.

Why it matters: It's a software fix for one of the buildout's more obscure but costly problems — grid-damaging power swings — that can deploy immediately across existing infrastructure instead of requiring new hardware.

4

Canada: Hamilton's "mysterious" data-centre moratorium reversal is reshaping other Ontario councils' votes

CanadaPolicy

Hamilton council voted 10-6 in mid-July against a one-year moratorium on new data centres — reversing course after councillors emerged from an hours-long closed-door legal briefing, with Councillor Tammy Hwang flipping her vote after initially backing the pause. Slate Asset Management's appeal of its rejected Steelport data-centre proposal, filed two weeks before the vote, and warnings the bylaw could be legally vulnerable were central to the closed session; neighbouring Burlington and Toronto councils are now watching Hamilton's reversal as they weigh their own proposed pauses.

Why it matters: Hamilton would have been the first Canadian municipality to freeze data-centre development; its abrupt, legally-driven reversal is now the cautionary case other Ontario councils are citing as they debate whether a moratorium can survive a legal challenge.

5

Loudoun County, Virginia moves toward a data-centre moratorium, rejects 780MW Spring Valley campus

PolicyConstruction

Loudoun County's Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend denying Active Infrastructure's Spring Valley Technology Park, a proposed 325-acre, 3.25-million-square-foot, 12-building campus with 780 megawatts of capacity near Beaverdam Reservoir. The Board of Supervisors separately voted 6-1 to direct staff to draft options for pausing new data-centre applications, site plans and substation permits, with a decision due at the board's September 15 meeting.

Why it matters: Loudoun is the heart of the world's largest data-centre market — a formal pause there, even a temporary one, would be a far bigger signal to developers than the moratoriums already adopted in smaller jurisdictions.

6

Data-centre construction starts hit $22.3 billion in June, second-highest month on record

FinanceConstruction

ConstructConnect's latest tracking shows U.S. data-centre construction starts totalled $22.3 billion in June — behind only January's $25.5 billion — with 23 projects breaking ground that month and 116 for the year so far. Year-to-date spending through June reached $81.5 billion, already surpassing all of 2025's $72.5 billion, as activity concentrates in North Carolina, Indiana, Illinois and Michigan alongside Virginia and Texas; median construction costs have climbed to $570 per square foot, up 38% from 2025.

Why it matters: The spending and cost data confirm the buildout is still accelerating in raw dollar terms even as the same states and counties driving those numbers move toward pauses and audits — the two trends are now colliding directly.
