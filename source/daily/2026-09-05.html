Canada: Algoma Steel and Red Jar Energy pitch 400MW AI data centre for Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario

CanadaConstruction

Algoma Steel and private infrastructure firm Red Jar Energy Partners have submitted an application to Ontario's Independent Electricity System Operator for a 400-megawatt AI data centre in the Algoma region, targeting an October 2029 in-service date. The companies say the project is at an early, non-binding stage and would still need to clear Ontario's newly published data centre approval playbook and local review.

Why it matters: It's another gigawatt-scale AI proposal landing in Ontario just as Oakville's moratorium holds and Mississauga's own one-year pause heads to a final council vote on September 16 — testing whether the province's new provincial framework can get ahead of municipal backlash.

2

PJM asks FERC to cut power to new data centres first unless they bring their own generation

EnergyPolicy

PJM Interconnection, the grid operator for 13 mid-Atlantic and Midwest states, filed a tariff change asking FERC to let it curtail large loads of 50MW or more, built after June 1, 2027, ahead of everyone else during shortages if they haven't secured their own power supply. PJM cited an expected 70GW of new large-load demand by 2038 against roughly 15GW of generation retired since 2022, and wants the rule effective October 12, 2026.

Why it matters: It's the largest U.S. grid operator formally telling data centre developers that free-riding on the shared grid is over — accelerating the industry's shift toward on-site gas, nuclear and battery generation that deals like ONE Nuclear's Louisiana project (below) already reflect.

ONE Nuclear signs binding deal for 2.88GW gas plant and battery system to power Louisiana data centre campus

EnergyConstruction

ONE Nuclear Energy executed a binding letter of intent with a Louisiana landowner group for site control of "Project Cayman": a 2.88-gigawatt gas-fired power plant paired with a 700MW/2.88GWh battery storage system, co-located with a data centre campus near the RiverPlex MegaPark between Baton Rouge and New Orleans. The Florida-based developer, which plans to eventually pivot to small modular reactors, has not yet disclosed the data centre's capacity, timeline or tenant.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single behind-the-meter gas-and-battery power projects tied to a data centre announced this year, and its scale near Entergy's existing transmission build-out shows how thoroughly gas has become the default bridge fuel for gigawatt-class AI campuses.

Northrop Grumman wins $7M DARPA contract to triple chip power density with diamond cooling

Tech

Northrop Grumman has been awarded $7 million under Phase II of DARPA's THREADS program to integrate diamond-based heat dissipation directly into next-generation semiconductors. Diamond conducts heat roughly five times faster than copper and outperforms silicon carbide and gallium nitride; having already demonstrated a 3.3x power-density boost in Phase I, the company is now targeting another tripling.

Why it matters: Thermal limits at the transistor level, not just rack-level cooling, are becoming the ceiling on chip power density — a diamond-cooling breakthrough that reaches production could eventually filter from defense radar chips into the high-density AI accelerators straining today's liquid-cooling systems.

Flex to pay $4.4B for EPC Power as AI data centres push toward 800-volt DC architecture

TechFinance

Manufacturing giant Flex agreed to acquire power-conversion specialist EPC Power for $4.4 billion, adding 800V DC and grid-forming inverter technology built for AI data centres and grid applications; EPC is expected to generate about $800 million in revenue this year. The deal is expected to close in Q4 2026, and EPC will join the Cloud and Power Infrastructure unit Flex plans to spin off as a standalone public company in early 2027.

Why it matters: 800V DC distribution is emerging as the industry's answer to the power losses and cabling bulk of next-generation AI racks, and a $4.4 billion bet signals Flex expects that architecture shift to happen fast and at scale.

Meta's $1.2B Kuna, Idaho data centre goes live with closed-loop cooling

TechConstruction

Meta officially switched on its 750,000-square-foot data centre in Kuna, Idaho — its 28th U.S. facility and first in the state — capping a four-year, $1.2 billion build. It's only the second Meta site to use a closed-loop, radiator-style cooling system that needs no extra water for most of the year, backed by more than $100 million Meta invested in a dedicated water and wastewater system for the city.

Why it matters: Water-free cooling at gigawatt-adjacent scale directly answers the drought and water-stress objections driving local opposition elsewhere, and Meta positioning it as a template suggests closed-loop designs are moving from pilot to standard practice.

GOP leaders stall on bringing bipartisan data centre "Ratepayer Protection Act" to House floor

Policy

A bipartisan bill from Reps. Gabe Evans (R-CO) and Kathy Castor (D-FL) that would codify President Trump's "Ratepayer Protection Pledge" — signed by more than 300 utilities, co-ops and AI hyperscalers — cleared the House Energy and Commerce Committee 52-0 in July but still has no floor vote scheduled. Both sponsors face competitive re-election races, and it's unclear the bill can move before the House's shortened September session ends.

Why it matters: It's the closest Congress has come to a federal response to utility bill-shifting from data centres, and continued GOP leadership delay pushes the issue back onto the patchwork of state legislatures and municipal moratoriums already straining to keep up.

Nscale commits up to $6B in compute and takes equity stake in humanoid-robotics firm Figure

FinanceTech

AI cloud provider Nscale signed a multi-year deal committing at least $3.5 billion — and up to $6 billion — in compute to Figure, the humanoid-robotics startup, while also taking a strategic equity stake and becoming Figure's preferred compute provider. The infrastructure, running on Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform at a Barstow, Texas site, could eventually give Figure access to 100,000 GPUs beginning in the second half of 2027.

Why it matters: It's a striking example of AI infrastructure financing turning circular and vertically integrated — a compute provider betting billions on, and taking equity in, one of its own biggest future customers before that customer has proven out its business.

HPE and Oracle deepen networking deal to gigawatt-scale AI infrastructure

TechFinance

HPE expanded its networking collaboration with Oracle on its Q3 earnings call, committing to a multi-year, global deployment of HPE Juniper routers and switches — including Broadcom Tomahawk 6-based QFX switches and Express 5-silicon PTX routers — across Oracle's AI data centres. HPE CEO Antonio Neri called it an expansion of Juniper's networking business "now... at gigawatt scale," with cumulative "Networks for AI" orders already topping HPE's full-year target.

Why it matters: As GPU shortages ease slightly, networking and interconnect are emerging as the next bottleneck for AI clusters — a gigawatt-scale, multi-year commitment from a hyperscaler-adjacent buyer like Oracle is a strong signal of where infrastructure spend is shifting next.

GoPro to merge with optics maker Starman for $285M, pivoting into AI data centre supply chain

TechFinance

GoPro signed a definitive merger agreement with Starman Optical, under which GoPro shareholders receive $285 million in cash and the company's $92 million of debt is paid off, leaving a debt-free combined entity. Starman brings U.S.-made optical transceivers aimed at the AI infrastructure market, while GoPro contributes more than 2,500 patents in imaging and optics; the combined company will also target defense, robotics and aerospace customers.

Why it matters: It's another sign that struggling consumer-hardware makers see AI data centre optics and interconnect — not their original markets — as their clearest path back to relevance, adding a new domestic supplier to a transceiver market largely dependent on Asian manufacturing.

Why it matters: Another former heavy-industry site — this time a shuttered aluminum smelter — being repurposed for AI/HPC capacity shows how existing grid interconnection and industrial land, not just raw acreage, is now the scarce resource developers are chasing.

Microsoft breaks out Azure revenue for investors for the first time

Finance

Microsoft has begun disclosing Azure's revenue as a standalone figure in its financial reporting rather than folding it into the broader "Intelligent Cloud" segment, a reporting change the company attributes to investor demand for clearer visibility into AI-driven cloud growth. Why it matters: With hyperscaler capex approaching $700 billion in 2026, investors have been pushing for granular proof that AI infrastructure spending is converting into revenue — Microsoft's new transparency raises the bar for Meta and other cloud players that still bundle their numbers.

TechEnergy

Why it matters: With Texas emerging as the epicenter of gigawatt-scale AI buildout in one of the country's more water-stressed regions, treatment systems that unlock non-potable water sources are becoming as critical to project approval as power itself.

Finance
