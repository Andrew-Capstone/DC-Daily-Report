1

Memphis City Council to vote Tuesday on freezing new data centre permits, aimed at xAI's Colossus

TechPolicy

Memphis City Council will vote August 18 on an ordinance, sponsored by four council members, that would pause acceptance and approval of new zoning and building permits for data centres citywide while the city writes rules for gigawatt-scale facilities. The freeze would not affect existing operations like xAI's Colossus, which already runs over 500 megawatts on gas turbines, paid $25 million in county property taxes in its first year, and plans to scale toward roughly 2 gigawatts, but has drawn noise complaints and a Clean Air Act lawsuit over unpermitted turbines.

Why it matters: It's a live test of whether a major US city can write ground rules for gigawatt-scale AI campuses before, rather than after, the next wave of projects arrives.

2

Fermi America signs up to $6.5B lease with TensorWave for 650MW at Project Matador, Texas

EnergyFinanceTech

Fermi America signed a binding lease with AI cloud provider TensorWave for an initial 222 megawatts at its Project Matador site near Amarillo, Texas, with expansion rights up to 650 megawatts, expected to generate roughly $6.5 billion in revenue over a 15-year term. The facility will host tens of thousands of next-generation AMD Instinct GPUs, with first power targeted for later this year at a site Fermi plans to eventually scale to 17 gigawatts via natural gas, nuclear, solar and battery storage.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest AMD-anchored AI infrastructure leases signed to date, and a test of whether massive "bring your own power" energy campuses can outrun Texas's grid-interconnection bottlenecks.

3

Six major US grid operators face Monday deadline to rewrite rules for data centre power

EnergyPolicy

Under a show-cause order FERC issued June 18, PJM, MISO, Southwest Power Pool, CAISO, ISO New England and NYISO must, by Monday, August 17, either justify that their existing tariffs already accommodate gigawatt-scale data centres and other large loads or file proposed reforms. The order targets transmission application queues, cost allocation, co-location rules and flexible-service arrangements across markets that serve roughly 200 million Americans and nearly two-thirds of the country's commission-jurisdictional electricity demand.

Why it matters: The filings due this week will set the ground rules for how quickly — and at whose cost — new AI data centres can connect to the grid across most of the country.

4

Nebius Q2 AI cloud revenue jumps 454% to $575M as Cerebras more than doubles core revenue

FinanceTech

Nebius reported second-quarter revenue of $582 million, up 454% year-over-year, with its core AI cloud business rising sixfold to $575 million and adjusted EBITDA swinging to a $236 million profit from a year-ago loss, even as the company posted a $190 million net loss from continuing operations; it also landed four contracts averaging over $1 billion each. Cerebras' core revenue more than doubled to $210 million with its inference cloud business nearly quadrupling, though it too posted a large net loss.

Why it matters: The neocloud earnings season is showing the same pattern across the sector — explosive contracted revenue growth funded by equally steep losses and prepayments, a bet that scale now outweighs profitability later.

5

Canada: SaskPower disputes claim that coal-plant extension was meant to power Bell's Regina AI data centre

CanadaEnergyPolicy

SaskPower has rejected an NDP allegation, based on leaked internal documents, that Saskatchewan's decision to keep coal units running past their planned retirement was made to free up supply for Bell's $1.7 billion, 300-megawatt AI data centre under construction near Regina. NDP leader Carla Beck cited documents suggesting the coal extension "allowed SaskPower to have supply available to accommodate the Bell data centre load," in a refurbishment plan the opposition says could cost up to $26 billion.

Why it matters: It puts a concrete dollar figure and a specific power source behind a question dogging data centre buildouts everywhere — whether AI's electricity needs are quietly locking in higher-emission generation that would otherwise have been retired.

6

Canada: Saskatchewan opposition presses for transparency after power denial kills Moose Jaw AI data centre

CanadaPolicy

NDP leader Carla Beck and MLAs held a Moose Jaw open house to field residents' questions after the province denied Aztec AI Health's request for 150 megawatts of power, effectively stalling its proposed $485 million data centre at the former Valley View Centre. The NDP argues the government's "not right now" framing leaves the door open to a revived or similar proposal without a firm commitment to residents, who have collected thousands of petition signatures against the project since June.

Why it matters: It shows a provincial power denial alone hasn't settled a data centre fight once a community mobilizes — residents and opposition politicians are now pushing for a permanent policy answer, not just a stalled project.

7

Annenberg survey: 61% of Americans now oppose new data centres in their area, up from 49%

Policy

A nationally representative Annenberg Public Policy Center survey of 1,320 US adults, conducted June 16–July 19, found 61% somewhat or strongly oppose new data centre construction in their area, up 12 points from 49% in a survey ending in March, while support fell to just 14%. Opposition is bipartisan — 69% of Democrats, 54% of Republicans and 53% of independents — and highest among adults under 30, at 70%.

Why it matters: It's the clearest national polling yet that data centre backlash has moved from scattered local disputes to a majority position across the political spectrum, in just four months.

8

Virginia Gov. Spanberger rejects state senator's call for statewide data centre moratorium

Policy

Republican state Sen. Glen Sturtevant asked Governor Abigail Spanberger to pause new data centre approvals until the General Assembly receives a December report on affordability impacts, but Spanberger's office rejected the idea, pointing instead to measures requiring data centres to pay their own energy and transmission costs. The request followed Loudoun County's rejection of a 3.25-million-square-foot campus and growing bipartisan unease in a state that hosts roughly 35% of the world's hyperscale data centres.

Why it matters: Virginia is the bellwether for US data centre policy, and its governor choosing cost-recovery rules over an outright pause signals where other high-growth states may land as similar moratorium pushes spread.

9

Marshall, Michigan investigates threats against city council over data centre moratorium fight

Policy

Marshall police determined threats made against city council members, including a death threat directed at Councilor James Hackworth, were credible enough to investigate, though no criminal charges followed. The threats trace back to the council's unanimous August 3 vote for a one-year moratorium on new data centre developments, pausing review of a 270-acre Alterra Development project at Brooks Industrial Park amid resident concerns over power demand, low-frequency noise and wetland impacts.

Why it matters: It's a stark sign of how heated local data centre fights have become, with police now urging "civil dialogue" in a dispute that started as a routine zoning moratorium.

10

California data centre developer sues over Colorado River water denial for state's largest project

Policy

Imperial Valley Computer Manufacturing is suing the Imperial Irrigation District after it denied the company's request for 880 acre-feet of Colorado River water annually to cool a proposed 950,000-square-foot data centre — what would be California's largest. The company is also separately suing Imperial County over its data centre moratorium, while the City of Imperial and the Sierra Club have challenged the project's environmental review in a third case, with rulings on the moratorium and environmental-review questions expected within 90 days of late-July hearings.

Why it matters: It's a concentrated example of the legal fights now common wherever hyperscale data centres collide with drought-stressed water supplies in the arid West.
