Tuesday, October 20, 2026 · North America focus
Morning brief: Power and policy lead today. Microsoft signed a 20-year, 1,100 megawatt power deal with NiSource for its northwest Indiana campus, Google agreed to 890 megawatts of nuclear uprates from Constellation, and FERC rejected the core of PJM's plan for curtailing data centres, leaving the largest US grid without a clear rulebook. On technology, the OCP Global Summit centred on 800 volt DC power, multi-megawatt cooling units and solid-state transformers, while Nvidia opened its Vera Rubin rack designs. In Canada, EverWind's C$10 billion Nova Scotia campus leased to Anthropic and Calgary's Enerflex won more than a gigawatt of on-site gas generation, while Hydro-Québec defended a plan to double data centre rates and New Brunswick reported 10 applications for up to 900 megawatts. Fresh news was moderate, so several stories are one to three weeks old and are dated. DatacenterDynamics and Data Centre Magazine could not be fetched directly.
1

Microsoft signs 20-year, 1,100 MW power deal with NiSource for northwest Indiana campus

EnergyFinance

NiSource's generation subsidiary will add roughly 1,100 MW for Microsoft's planned northwest Indiana data centre campus, with service starting in 2028. Microsoft covers generation and transmission costs, and NiSource raised its projected NIPSCO customer savings to about $1.75 billion from $1.4 billion.

Why it matters: A hyperscaler paying for dedicated new generation is becoming the template for protecting ratepayers; financial details are due on NiSource's Oct. 28 earnings call.

Google signs 20-year, 890 MW nuclear uprate deal with Constellation across 11 reactors

Energy

Constellation will add 890 MW through uprates at 11 existing units in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, with the first coming online in 2028 and $4.3 billion of investment. A separate 15-year deal covers another 2,700 MW across PJM, with load-shaping and demand-response features.

Why it matters: New nuclear megawatts from existing plants are the fastest firm power available; PJM is about 6.8 GW short of new capacity.

3

FERC rejects core of PJM's data centre plan, including bring-your-own-capacity and curtailment-first rules

PolicyEnergy

FERC said PJM overstepped by intruding on state retail policy and would unfairly discriminate against large loads. It rejected a requirement to curtail data centres lacking new capacity, a cut-them-first provision, and a bar on auction participation, while approving a load-tracking database and ramp-up schedules.

Why it matters: The largest US grid is left without a clear rulebook, pushing curtailment decisions to states such as Pennsylvania and Ohio.
Bisnow → (Oct 14)
4

OCP Global Summit 2026: 800 Vdc sidecars, multi-megawatt CDUs and solid-state transformers dominate

Tech

The sold-out San Jose summit drew about 15,000 attendees. Showings included CDUs of 2.5 to 3.2 MW, in-row 800 Vdc power racks, a draft solid-state transformer spec for units up to 10 MW, and an OCP grid resilience spec to restore near-full load within two seconds of a disturbance.

Why it matters: Power delivery and liquid cooling are being standardized for 250 kW racks today and a path to 1 MW racks.

Nvidia opens Vera Rubin rack designs via MGX 1.2 and unveils USFP optics with Cisco

Tech

MGX 1.2 supports racks up to 250 kW with a liquid-cooled 5,000 A busbar. The new USFP optical form factor offers 400 Gb/s per lane, about four times the density of 200 Gb/s optics with roughly 1.3 times better power efficiency, and Meta is adopting Spectrum-6 switches for its Vera Rubin factories.

Why it matters: Opening the rack lets more suppliers build to one design, though the core GPU and networking stack stays proprietary.

EverWind announces C$10B Point Tupper, Nova Scotia data centre with 20-year AI lease

CanadaEnergyFinance

EverWind Fuels says Anthropic signed a 20-year lease for a campus drawing 95 MW in phase one (online late 2027), with a 325 MW expansion in mid-2028. The plan includes $2B for onshore wind, with 650 MW of approved wind farms 51% owned by a Membertou-led First Nations consortium.

Why it matters: Billed as the largest private investment in Nova Scotia history, with EverWind pledging to cover grid upgrade costs.

Calgary's Enerflex wins over 1 GW of behind-the-meter gas generation for an unnamed North American data centre

EnergyCanada

Deliveries run through 2028, lifting Enerflex's behind-the-meter gas backlog above 1.75 GW. The company authorized about $100 million for Engineered Systems facilities, and its shares rose 13% on the day. Customer, site and dollar value were not disclosed.

Why it matters: On-site gas is the main way developers are skipping interconnection queues, and Canadian suppliers are central to it.

We Energies strikes deal to power Oracle's Wisconsin campus with Point Beach nuclear output

Energy

Oracle could subscribe to 10% to 20% of the Point Beach plant's output for Project Lighthouse in Port Washington, co-developed with OpenAI. The Public Service Commission must approve; We Energies says it could save other customers roughly $300 million in fuel costs over five years.

Why it matters: A utility-brokered nuclear subscription is a model other states may copy, but it still awaits regulatory approval.

Hydro-Québec defends plan to double electricity rates for large data centres

CanadaPolicyEnergy

The utility wants an average of 13 cents per kWh, around twice what large-power customers pay now, and the Régie de l'énergie is reviewing it at a public hearing. Consultant Dunsky expects Quebec data-centre demand to more than double between 2024 and 2030.

Why it matters: Cheap hydro power has made Quebec a draw; a rate shock would change its competitiveness against other provinces.

CanadaEnergyConstruction

Proposals include a 400 MW, $2 billion hub in Saint John from Beacon Data Centers and VoltaGrid, and a $2.7 billion, 375 MW plan from Faster Than Light in Fredericton. None has final approval, and an Angus Reid poll found 64% of Canadians want provinces to limit large data centres.

Why it matters: The Maritimes are emerging as a new data centre region, with public opinion pushing back.

Ottawa's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles sign 23 companies, with no enforcement

CanadaPolicy

Signatories including Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Bell and Telus commit to local benefits, no higher power prices for residents, minimal water use and better disclosure. There is no compliance mechanism or penalty; provinces and municipalities enforce through approvals.

Why it matters: It sets Canada's expectations for new builds, but the real leverage sits with provincial regulators.

PolicyEnergy

Gov. Spanberger's Sept. 18 framework bars new NDAs and proposes ending by-right approval above 25 MW. A new GS-5 rate class brings 14-year minimum commitments from Jan. 1, 2027, and Loudoun County votes Oct. 20 on deferring data centre applications by up to 12 months.

Why it matters: The world's largest data centre market is shifting risk onto developers.

Microsoft exits Nscale's planned UK data centre over power delays

FinanceEnergy

UK Power Networks told Nscale it could not supply enough electricity until the early-to-mid 2030s, so Microsoft terminated the contract for the 50 MW (scalable to 90 MW) Loughton site. Nscale's SEC IPO filing shows up to $43.8 billion in Microsoft agreements and up to $44.6 billion with Anthropic.
