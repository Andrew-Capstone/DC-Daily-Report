1

AI data centres are fueling a nuclear comeback, with about $20B of new nuclear spending in 2026

Energy

Industrial Info Resource estimates new nuclear project spending of about $20 billion in 2026, rising to nearly $50 billion by 2029, across more than 100 North American projects including 61 SMRs and 11 new conventional reactors. DOE offered Constellation a loan of up to $5 billion for upgrades at eight plants, and hyperscalers back Amazon's 1 GW SMR in Indiana and the Three Mile Island and Duane Arnold restarts.

Why it matters: Nuclear is moving from pledge to financed build-out, though commercial fusion is not expected before the mid-2030s.

2

Crusoe debuts Spark Liquid, a 3.5 MW liquid-cooled modular data centre unit

TechConstruction

The sixth-generation modular platform is designed for Nvidia GB300 NVL72 and Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with closed-loop direct liquid cooling. Crusoe claims five times the compute density of earlier Spark generations. Production starts in 2027 at its 352,000 sq ft Brighton, Colorado factory.

Why it matters: Factory-built, liquid-cooled capacity is a route around construction delays for dense AI racks.

4

Trump's onsite power push meets AI data centre reality

PolicyEnergy

On Oct. 21 President Trump posted that large new data centres should generate their own electricity and supply any surplus to communities or the grid, and suggested developers pay host communities a dividend. Some turbine models are reportedly sold out through 2031, and NEMA's Patrick Hughes says a hybrid of onsite generation, storage and grid connection is most likely.

Why it matters: A stated federal expectation of self-supply collides with equipment backlogs; it is not formal policy.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 3))

Can optical AI cut inference power use? Lumai tests Iris Nova servers in a UK lab

Tech

Oxford spin-out Lumai uses optics for matrix multiplication in the prefill phase of inference, leaving decode to GPUs, in standard air-cooled racks. The trial is backed by UK agency ARIA. The article gives no quantified power savings.

Why it matters: Early-stage and UK-based, but it points to one possible path to easing inference power demand globally.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 6))

Canada is urged to embrace AI data centres as municipal moratoriums spread

CanadaPolicy

A Nov. 3 commentary argues Canada should welcome AI data centres, citing JP Morgan projections of $900 billion of AI capital spending in 2026 and $5.5 trillion by 2030. Separate reports describe a proposed moratorium in Hamilton and a Toronto council review of data centre rules. This is opinion, not a policy change.

Why it matters: It captures the tension between national AI ambitions and local resistance.

8

Canada's voluntary data centre principles draw OpenAI, Anthropic and Microsoft as opinion sours

CanadaPolicy

Announced Sept. 3 by AI Minister Evan Solomon, the framework has 23 signatories and five expectations: local benefits, electricity costs, water use, transparency and strategic value. It is voluntary with no penalties, and Angus Reid polling shows over two-thirds of Canadians oppose data centres near their homes.

Why it matters: Older than 24 hours, but it remains Canada's main federal policy position as provinces and cities set their own rules.
Source → (BetaKit (Sept 3; background))
