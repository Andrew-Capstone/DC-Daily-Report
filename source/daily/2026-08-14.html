1

Nvidia recruits Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR for $500B AI financing push

FinanceTech

Nvidia signed preliminary agreements with six of the world's largest capital providers to mobilize more than $500 billion in third-party financing so hyperscalers, frontier AI labs and enterprises can build data centres and buy Nvidia hardware. The structure uses debt backed by compute itself as collateral, with special-purpose vehicles issuing bonds and private offerings; Nvidia may kick in up to 25% financing support per deal, and CEO Jensen Huang called AI compute an emerging "investable asset class."

Why it matters: It's the clearest sign yet that AI infrastructure financing is shifting from corporate balance sheets to structured, asset-backed capital markets at a scale that could reshape how every future data centre gets built.

2

Canada: Ontario launches Data Centre Playbook, proposes higher electricity rates for new facilities

CanadaPolicyEnergy

Ontario opened a 30-day public comment period through the Environmental Registry of Ontario for its new Data Centre Playbook, which requires data centres to pay the full cost of electricity with no subsidies, sets a higher rate for facilities above 1 megawatt, and prioritizes projects using closed-loop, low-water cooling. Premier Doug Ford said the province "can build the economy of the future... or get left behind," while Energy Minister Stephen Lecce confirmed the new higher data-centre rate.

Why it matters: It's Canada's most detailed provincial framework yet for balancing AI investment against ratepayer protection, and could become the template other provinces follow.

3

SpaceX to power $16.8B "Terafab" chip campus entirely off the Texas grid

EnergyTechConstruction

A signed tax-abatement agreement confirms SpaceX and Tesla's semiconductor and AI-computing campus in Grimes County, Texas will run on on-site natural gas plants and large battery arrays rather than grid power. The $16.8 billion initial investment is the first phase of a four-phase project that could reach $119 billion, aiming to produce enough chips to support 1 terawatt of computing capacity annually. (Agreement reported August 11, 2026.)

Why it matters: It's one of the largest "private utility" bets yet, with a chipmaker choosing to build and own its own power rather than wait in an interconnection queue.

Samsung Heavy Industries and Mousterian advance factory-built floating data centres for Texas waters

TechEnergy

Samsung Heavy Industries and Mousterian Corporation signed an engineering contract to jointly design and build moored, factory-built floating data centres delivering 50MW of critical IT capacity each, with a pilot site planned next to an existing gas turbine power plant in Houston. Because the units are built in a shipyard, fabrication runs in parallel with sitework, and the water-based cooling design is said to avoid drawing on potable water or discharging into waterways. (Signed August 3, 2026.)

Why it matters: It's a genuine new construction and cooling model that could cut deployment time and water use for AI capacity in power- and water-constrained Gulf Coast markets.

Canada: Montreal's 5C secures $605M Brookfield-led financing for North American AI campuses

CanadaFinance

Montreal-based AI data centre developer 5C closed $605 million in debt financing led by Brookfield Asset Management, bringing its total capital raised since 2025 to more than $1.4 billion. The new funds will support acquisition and construction of 5C's Memphis campus and expansion of sites in Ohio and Phoenix, ranking among the largest AI debt rounds ever raised by a Canadian company.

Why it matters: It shows a Canadian-headquartered developer competing directly for the same institutional capital pools as US hyperscalers to build out American AI capacity.

Louisville Metro Council passes data centre moratorium 24-1 after warehouse-conversion proposal

Policy

Louisville Metro Council voted 24-1 to pause new data centre construction and conversions for six months or until comprehensive zoning rules are adopted, after a developer proposed converting a 350,000-square-foot warehouse in southwest Louisville into a data centre. The city's planning commission is separately drafting rules that would ban hyperscale data centres over 500,000 square feet from Jefferson County.

Why it matters: It resolves a vote that was pending as of yesterday's report and makes Louisville one of the largest US metros to formally halt data centre approvals.

7

Lambda prices $926M term loan, first investment-grade-rated debt deal by a private neocloud

FinanceTech

GPU cloud provider Lambda priced a $926 million senior secured term loan B facility rated Baa2 by Moody's, pricing at SOFR plus 3.00% and maturing in December 2030. The proceeds will fund GPU server acquisition tied to an investment-grade customer, and Morgan Stanley led the heavily oversubscribed deal, which tightened 75 basis points from initial talks.

Why it matters: It's institutional validation of GPU-backed debt as an investable asset class, setting a lower-cost financing benchmark that rivals CoreWeave and Crusoe will now be compared against.

Energy Vault signs 1.25GW off-grid power deal for Texas hyperscaler using Caterpillar gensets

Energy

Energy Vault will supply battery energy storage, grid-forming power conversion systems and AI infrastructure control software for 1.25GW of integrated, off-grid power at an undisclosed hyperscaler's Texas AI data centre, paired with Caterpillar generator sets from an EPC partner. Why it matters: It's Energy Vault's largest contract ever and another sign hyperscalers are bypassing slow utility interconnection queues by building fully self-contained power systems.

Equinix agrees to cover 100% of grid-upgrade costs for new Georgia data centre

EnergyPolicy

Equinix and Central Georgia Electric Membership Corporation signed a 20-year take-or-pay contract under which Equinix pays 100% of the utility's costs to serve its Hampton, Georgia facility, including a new high-voltage substation and two transmission lines, so local ratepayers bear none of the infrastructure cost. 12

Copper, lithium and cobalt shortages emerge as AI data centres' next supply-chain bottleneck

Tech

A single hyperscale facility requires roughly 50,000 tons of copper, but only 3% of global copper consumption is refined in the US, new mines take an average of 23 years to develop, and incoming tariffs will phase in a 15% transitional rate starting in 2027. Canada, Australia and Chile are highlighted as key supply partners, alongside recovery technologies like MIT spinout SiTration's copper-from-mining-waste process. (Published August 13, 2026.)

Why it matters: It's a structural constraint that could throttle AI data centre buildouts as hard as power or water, and one North America is poorly positioned to solve domestically.

TechFinance

Energy

Why it matters: It's a stark quantification of the gap between Texas's data centre ambitions and its ability to actually power them, reinforcing why so many operators are now building their own generation.

Policy

Farmville's town commissioners voted unanimously to freeze new data centres for 12 months so staff can develop ordinance language and land-use standards, after residents raised concerns about noise, water use and electricity rates.
