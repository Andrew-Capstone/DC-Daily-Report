Google buys Crusoe's Wyoming site for a $17B Cheyenne campus powered by fuel cells, gas and carbon capture

EnergyConstruction

Google acquired Crusoe's southeast Wyoming data centre site after Crusoe completed site selection, government approvals and power planning. Crusoe and Tallgrass Energy designed a hybrid power strategy combining fuel cells, grid power and natural gas turbines, with long-term carbon capture through Tallgrass's regional CO2 sequestration hub; DCD reports the campus near Cheyenne at $17 billion. Price and megawatts were not disclosed.

Why it matters: Wyoming is becoming a behind-the-meter power hub for Google, alongside last week's 702 MW AEP–Bloom fuel-cell plan.

CoreWeave backlog hits $132.5B and 2026 guidance rises; 1.85 GW of active power still targeted by year-end

FinanceEnergy

CoreWeave posted Q3 revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168% year over year, and a backlog of $132.5 billion, up from $104 billion in June. It raised 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0–13.3 billion, targets $37–39 billion of 2026 capex, and still expects 1.85 GW of active power by end-2026. It carried about $47 billion of debt at quarter-end, with some bond yields above 16%.

Why it matters: The bellwether neocloud says demand still outruns capacity, a counterweight to this week's AI-credit jitters.
Source → (CNBC (Nov 10))

University of Waterloo hybrid cooling cuts simulated chip hotspots by up to 17°C in 15 seconds

TechCanada

Waterloo researchers pair immersion cooling in inexpensive deionized water with drops of a denser, lower-boiling electronics coolant that sit on hotspots, wrapped in a silicone-oil shell with ceramic particles to control boiling. In lab tests a single drop cut a simulated hotspot by up to 17°C within 15 seconds, several times faster than conventional immersion. The work is lab-scale and published in the Journal of Colloid and Interface Science.

Why it matters: Canadian research aimed at the transient heat spikes that limit AI chip density; still far from deployment.
Source → (HPCwire (Nov 12))

Bell's Regina AI Fabric campus, 300 MW now and up to 1.2 GW planned, faces First Nation opposition

CanadaConstruction

Bell's AI Fabric data centre just south of Regina has a 300 MW first phase under construction, more than 1,100 workers on site, and its first data hall due in the first half of 2027; Bell announced in September it could quadruple to 1.2 GW. Standing Buffalo Dakota Nation says the campus is on unceded territory and wants technical reports and approval records, which it says remain incomplete. Bell says it completed provincially requested consultations and that George Gordon First Nation is a partner.

Why it matters: Canada's largest telecom-led AI build tests how Indigenous consultation will shape the national data centre buildout.
Source → (Canadian Press via JOC (Nov 11))

DOE closes $5.1B loan to Alliant Energy to add 2.6 GW in Iowa and Wisconsin

EnergyFinance

The US Department of Energy closed a $5.1 billion loan to Alliant Energy to add 2.6 GW of power in Iowa and Wisconsin. Investments may include natural gas plants, storage and upgrades to existing generation. Alliant separately said this month it expects to sign 1 GW of data centre supply contracts within six months.

Why it matters: Federal credit is now directly underwriting the generation needed for Midwest data centre load.

SoftBank books about $3.7B in losses from changed equipment procurement for US AI data centres, including Ohio

Finance

SoftBank disclosed roughly $3.7 billion in losses tied to changes in equipment procurement for its US AI data centre developments, including Ohio: about $2.85 billion from nonrefundable advance payments and about $880 million from contract modifications and settlements. It had committed about $9.5 billion in advance payments, and potential discounts on future equipment purchases could reach $2.1 billion.

Why it matters: A rare large write-down in the Stargate orbit shows how fast AI build plans and equipment specs are shifting.

8

Florida regulators reject Duke Energy's data centre plan over cost-shifting law

PolicyEnergy

The Florida Public Service Commission rejected Duke Energy's data centre proposal, finding it did not show compliance with a state law requiring large-load customers to bear their full cost of service rather than shifting it to other ratepayers.

Why it matters: State regulators are enforcing large-load tariff rules, not just writing them, raising the bar for utility data centre deals.
Source → (Yahoo News (Nov 5–8))

Danfoss unveils higher-flow liquid-cooling couplings and a 233 kW CoolTrain 2 ahead of SC26

Tech

Danfoss introduced a 2.5-inch Hansen FD83 quick-disconnect coupling it says roughly doubles flow with lower pressure drop, DataCryo 7G crimp fittings, the plug-and-play CoolTrain 2 with up to 233 kW of liquid cooling capacity, and smart rack valves controlling up to 333 litres per minute with DCIM telemetry. The products will be shown at SC26 in Chicago, Nov. 16–19.

Why it matters: Bigger, smarter fluid hardware is what lets direct-to-chip cooling keep pace with rising rack densities.
Source → (ACHR News (Nov 11))

Torys: US midterm backlash could steer data centre investment to Canada's clearer provincial frameworks

CanadaPolicy

Law firm Torys says US voters passed 40 of 41 local ballot measures limiting data centres and that the climate could push investment north. It points to Alberta's bring-your-own-power regulation, Ontario's Data Centre Playbook (final version expected this year), Saskatchewan's sovereignty-focused framework, BC Hydro's competitive process and Ottawa's principles with 23 signatories, while noting municipal moratoria in Mississauga and Oakville.

Why it matters: A useful map of where Canada's rules stand as US developers look for friendlier jurisdictions.
Source → (Torys LLP (Nov 9))

More cities move to ban data centres after midterms: Boise adopts mega-data-centre moratorium, Edmond extends pause

Policy

Boise approved a moratorium on mega data centres with immediate effect after a Nov. 10 hearing, though it has no pending applications. Edmond, Oklahoma voted 5-0 on Nov. 9 to extend its pause to June 30, 2027, while Memphis and Marshalltown, Iowa continued drafting rules. Ballotpedia counted 46 of 47 decided 2026 local measures favouring tighter controls.

Why it matters: Post-election local restrictions are spreading beyond traditional hubs into fast-growing secondary markets.

Oakland extends data centre moratorium to October 2027 while it drafts rules

Policy

Oakland's council unanimously extended its moratorium through Oct. 5, 2027 and passed an emergency ordinance barring permits, zoning changes and land-use agreements for data centres. The city will study grid, water and air-quality effects; a Behring Companies downtown proposal is pending. San Francisco and Richmond have passed similar pauses.

Why it matters: The Bay Area is closing to new data centres just as AI inference demand pushes for sites near users.

Romulus, Michigan adopts a 180-day moratorium on all data centres

Policy

Romulus's council unanimously approved an immediate 180-day pause, first aimed at facilities of 1 GW or more and then widened to all sizes, directing planners to study how to measure electrical demand and environmental impact. The city has no data centres and no proposals; some residents and councillors want a full ban.

Why it matters: Pre-emptive pauses in Detroit's suburbs add to Michigan's uncertainty after the Saline Township Oracle fight.
