1

Microsoft plans to triple data-centre capacity to 38 gigawatts by 2032

TechFinance

Bloomberg reports Microsoft's internal roadmap calls for total data-centre power capacity to surpass 38 gigawatts by 2032, up from roughly 12 gigawatts today, with AI-specific silicon growing from about 2 gigawatts to roughly a third of the total. The push follows capacity bottlenecks that forced Azure to turn away paying customers across 2025 and into 2026 — Temu reportedly signed with Oracle instead after Microsoft couldn't supply capacity — and comes alongside guidance for $50 billion of capital spending in fiscal Q1 2027 alone.

Why it matters: It's one of the most concrete long-range capacity targets any hyperscaler has disclosed, and a direct admission that lost business — not just AI ambition — is now driving the scale of the buildout.

2

Pentagon in talks to lend AI cloud startup Fluidstack $5 billion

FinancePolicy

The Department of War's Office of Strategic Capital is negotiating a $5 billion loan to Fluidstack, a New York-based AI infrastructure startup that operates GPU clusters and data centres, to strengthen U.S. manufacturing capacity and supply chains for data-centre components. The loan would be six times the size of Fluidstack's recent $830 million Series A round and by far the largest the OSC has issued to date.

Why it matters: It signals the Pentagon now treats AI data-centre supply chains as strategic infrastructure worth direct federal financing, not just a commercial buildout for hyperscalers to fund themselves.

3

CoreWeave launches Physical AI Field Engineering to embed specialists with customers

Tech

CoreWeave introduced Physical AI Field Engineering, pairing its own automotive, aerospace and robotics engineers with customer teams to build production AI models from data companies already own — test-bench results, simulation output, production sensors and live telemetry. The offering builds on the team and methods CoreWeave acquired with Monolith AI and has already been applied across more than 100 engineering projects.

Why it matters: It's a sign neoclouds are moving beyond raw GPU rental into higher-margin, stickier services that embed them directly inside industrial customers' engineering workflows.

4

Flex to acquire power-conversion maker EPC Power for $4.4 billion

TechFinance

Flex is acquiring EPC Power, a California-based developer of 800V power-conversion architectures — digital rectifiers and solid-state transformers — for AI data centres and grid applications, in a deal expected to close in Q4 2026. EPC Power, which has deployed more than 15 gigawatts across 62 countries, is projected to generate about $800 million in 2026 revenue; Flex plans to spin its Cloud and Power Infrastructure segment into an independent public company in early 2027.

Why it matters: Higher-voltage 800V power architecture is becoming the standard for next-generation AI racks, and a $4.4 billion bet shows how much value is shifting toward the electrical hardware inside data centres, not just the chips.

5

NRG's 1.2GW Texas gas plant advances through ERCOT's Batch Zero review

Energy

NRG's proposed 1.2-gigawatt, $3.2 billion "bring your own power" combined-cycle gas plant — backed by a 15-year, investment-grade hyperscaler contract — has been conditionally included as a studied load in ERCOT's Batch Zero large-load interconnection process. The project, targeted for late-2029 commercial operation and expandable to 2.4 gigawatts, is moving forward even as Texas Governor Greg Abbott's order for a statewide data-centre verification review has delayed Batch Zero's broader timeline.

Why it matters: It's an early test of whether the "bring your own power" model can keep gigawatt-scale gas projects moving through Texas's grid queue even as the state tightens oversight of data-centre power demand.

6

AI cloud firm Nscale seeks $3.5 billion in pre-IPO funding with Nvidia anchoring

FinanceTech

London-based Nscale, which owns and operates its own GPU-powered data centres, is seeking about $2 billion from Nvidia plus $1.5 billion in convertible notes led by Third Point ahead of a planned U.S. listing, on top of a potential $3 billion IPO raise. The company's contracted revenue backlog has more than doubled to $103 billion in four weeks, anchored by a six-year, $45 billion compute deal with Anthropic signed August 26.

Why it matters: It shows Nvidia extending its reach beyond chip sales into equity stakes in the neocloud operators that buy those chips, tightening the financial links across the AI supply chain.

7

Ontario's public consultation on its Data Centre Playbook closes today

CanadaPolicy

Ontario's 30-day comment period on its draft Data Centre Playbook — released August 13 — ends today, September 12. The framework would require large data centres to pay the full cost of the electricity they use, establish a separate electricity rate class for projects over one megawatt, and deliver community and economic benefits in exchange for provincial support; some Hamilton and Burlington councillors have criticized the consultation window as too short and too transparent-light.

Why it matters: Ontario is the largest Canadian jurisdiction to attempt a formal cost-recovery and benefits framework for data centres rather than a blanket moratorium, and today's deadline sets up the province's final rules ahead of its AI Industrial Strategy this fall.

8

HIVE subsidiary BUZZ HPC signs Canada's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles

CanadaPolicy

BUZZ HPC, a subsidiary of HIVE Digital Technologies, signed onto Ottawa's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles on September 8, joining Google, Microsoft, AWS, Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI. The five-point framework, published September 3 by AI Minister Evan Solomon, commits signatories to generate lasting local benefits, avoid shifting electricity costs to Canadian households, minimize water use, and be transparent about local power, water, noise and emissions impacts.

Why it matters: A homegrown Canadian miner-turned-AI-infrastructure company signing the same voluntary pledge as the American hyperscalers suggests the framework is becoming a de facto industry norm even before any province makes it mandatory.

9

Meta promotes $13 billion Alberta data centre buildout at industrial conference

CanadaConstruction

Meta used the Alberta Industrial Heartland Association's annual conference to publicize progress on its $13 billion Sturgeon County campus, with community engagement manager Matt Sexton citing 3,000 construction jobs, 300 permanent positions and more than 25 million work hours, plus $60 million in local infrastructure commitments. The one-gigawatt facility will eventually draw power from the Greenlight Electricity Centre, a natural-gas plant coming online in 2030.

Why it matters: It's Meta's most detailed public accounting yet of the jobs and local spending behind its largest data centre outside the US, at a moment when Canadian communities are increasingly skeptical of exactly these claims.

Blue Owl plans data-centre REIT IPO seeded with $6.5 billion in assets

Finance

Blue Owl Capital is preparing to take a data-centre real estate investment trust public, seeding it with roughly $6.5 billion of its own data-centre assets before raising additional capital through an IPO and follow-on share sales to keep acquiring facilities.

11

Maryland's data-centre backlash is reshaping Governor Wes Moore's re-election race

Policy

With Maryland's general election roughly two months away, environmental groups are pressing Annapolis for a statewide data-centre policy after opposition ousted several county officials in last month's primaries, including the council chair in Frederick County and three commissioners in Calvert County. Moore faces Republican challenger Dan Cox, who supports a statewide ban on hyperscale data centres, making the issue a defining contrast in the race.

Why it matters: It's one of the clearest examples yet of data-centre opposition directly threatening a sitting governor's re-election, not just local council seats.

San Francisco and Oakland both move to pause new data centres this week

Policy

San Francisco Supervisor Shamann Walton is introducing a 45-day moratorium on new data-centre construction — extendable up to 22 months — targeting Bayview-Hunters Point projects including NOVVA's expansion, with a Board of Supervisors vote set for September 15. Across the bay, Oakland Councilmember Carroll Fife introduced a parallel 45-day moratorium measure the same day, headed to committee September 22.

Why it matters: Two major Bay Area cities moving on moratoriums in the same week shows the backlash has spread well beyond the exurban and rural sites that dominated earlier coverage.

13

Memphis pauses road extension near xAI's Colossus site after Boxtown pushback

Policy

Memphis has halted a nearly decade-old plan to extend Paul R. Lowry Road past xAI's 785,000-square-foot Colossus 1 facility after residents of the historic Boxtown neighborhood complained of constant digging, dust and heavy truck traffic. Mayor Paul Young acknowledged the complaints as legitimate and said the city would reassess whether the project, first envisioned in 2017, still makes sense given how the industrial park has changed.

Why it matters: It's a concrete example of a data-centre operator's construction and traffic footprint — not just its power or water draw — becoming enough of a nuisance to reverse a decade-old municipal infrastructure plan.

14

Virginia's first-in-the-nation data centre electricity tax begins its first collection this month

PolicyEnergy

Virginia's new $0.011-per-kilowatt-hour electricity consumption tax on data centres, effective since July 1, sees its first collection period in September, covering usage from July 1 through September 1. The tax is capped at $600 million annually across all taxpayers, with any excess refunded pro rata, and is set to sunset in mid-2028.

Why it matters: Virginia hosts the largest concentration of data centres in North America, so how this novel per-kilowatt-hour tax performs in its first billing cycle will be closely watched by other states weighing similar consumption levies.

15

North Carolina regulators face September 20 deadline on Duke Energy data-centre rate case

EnergyPolicy

The North Carolina Utilities Commission has until September 20 to rule on Duke Energy Carolinas' rate case, a proceeding that has become centered on how data centres should pay for the grid upgrades their growth requires. The settlement under review would spin off a separate "large load tariff" proceeding specifically to determine data-centre cost allocation.

Why it matters: The ruling will be an early test case for how a major US utility apportions grid-upgrade costs between data centres and ordinary residential ratepayers, a template other states are watching closely.
