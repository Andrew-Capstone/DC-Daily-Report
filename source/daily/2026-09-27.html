Anthropic capped an extraordinary compute-buying week, committing up to $44.6 billion to Nscale for 460 megawatts at a new West Virginia campus and roughly $35 billion to Nvidia-backed Lambda for 350 megawatts in Texas — $80 billion in two deals in eight days — even as analysts warn the industry's bottleneck has shifted from signing contracts to actually financing, permitting and energizing them. In Canada, Bell and Ottawa announced a $52.5 billion, 1.2-gigawatt data centre expansion in Saskatchewan — the largest capital investment in the province's history — while Mississauga became the latest Ontario municipality to freeze new data centre construction for a year, and 19 more companies signed onto Canada's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles. Regulators moved aggressively elsewhere too: California Governor Newsom signed seven bills described as the nation's most comprehensive data centre laws, Texas Governor Abbott ordered a halt to all new data centre environmental permits pending a grid and water audit, and Wall Street's growing skepticism pushed SoftBank's SB Energy to delay a $50 billion IPO for its planned 10-gigawatt Ohio campus.
1

Google's AI data centres shift from grid backup to active grid partner

TechEnergy

Data Center Knowledge details how Google is turning battery systems installed for uptime protection into bidirectional grid assets, using software that continuously weighs compute workload, thermal load and grid conditions to decide whether discharging batteries or curtailing non-critical jobs better relieves stress on the local feeder. The approach, outlined at Data Center World Power, pairs full-stack facility design — low-voltage DC power, liquid cooling — with a partnership between Google, grid operator PJM Interconnection and Alphabet's Tapestry moonshot to speed up new power interconnections.

Why it matters: Because the batteries already exist for backup, enabling bidirectional flow is mostly a software and interconnection upgrade rather than new capital — a comparatively cheap way to turn hyperscale AI campuses from a grid liability into a stabilizing resource.

2

Anthropic commits $80 billion in a single week — to Nscale in West Virginia and Lambda in Texas — as AI infrastructure financing gets harder

FinanceTech

Anthropic signed a GPU services agreement worth up to $44.6 billion with Nscale for roughly 460 megawatts of Nvidia Vera Rubin-based capacity at a new Monarch Compute Campus in Mason County, West Virginia — built around an on-site microgrid — days before committing about $35 billion to Nvidia-backed Lambda for 350 megawatts at a Hut 8-developed Texas campus. Data Center Frontier's analysis notes the industry's real constraint has moved from signing multibillion-dollar contracts to actually financing, permitting, cooling and energizing them on schedule, citing Nscale's own $140.6 million in first-half revenue against a $1.02 billion net loss.

Why it matters: Anthropic is now underwriting entire greenfield campuses built by thinly capitalized infrastructure startups rather than leasing from established operators, a pattern that concentrates execution risk exactly where the capital is thinnest.

3

Arizona's SRP approves 1.675-gigawatt Marigold Energy Center to meet data centre-driven demand

Energy

Salt River Project's board approved the Marigold Energy Center in Stanfield, Arizona, combining 600 megawatts of solar, 400 megawatts of eight-hour battery storage and 675 megawatts of natural gas generation — 1.675 gigawatts in total — with its own substation and new transmission line. SRP says the mixed-resource plant is part of a plan to roughly double its power capacity by 2035 to keep pace with growth across Maricopa and Pinal counties.

Why it matters: Pairing gas with storage and solar in one project shows utilities increasingly building for both speed (gas can come online fast) and long-term decarbonization commitments simultaneously, rather than choosing between them.
In Business Phoenix → (Sept 14)
4

Fervo Energy and Google sign the world's largest enhanced geothermal power deal

EnergyTech

Fervo Energy and Google signed a 396-megawatt power purchase agreement for carbon-free, round-the-clock electricity from Fervo's Cape Station enhanced-geothermal project, intended as a building block for a potential Google data centre in Utah when the GeoCluster comes online in 2028. The deal includes an option for Google to expand its offtake by roughly 600 megawatts, to nearly a full gigawatt, by June 2030.

Why it matters: Enhanced geothermal offers firm, always-on power without the multi-year permitting timelines of new nuclear or the emissions of gas, positioning it as a genuine third pillar — alongside nuclear and gas — for hyperscalers racing to secure baseload capacity.
GlobeNewswire → (Sept 1)
5

Exascale and Compal to build a native 800-volt DC validation platform for next-generation AI racks

Tech

Exascale Labs and Compal Electronics signed a non-binding letter of intent to jointly develop a U.S.-based, native 800-volt direct-current platform using solid-state transformer technology, combining Compal's GPU server and liquid-cooling hardware with Exascale's modular data centre and power architecture to validate power, compute, cooling and controls as one integrated system. Compal's eventual purchase of Exascale infrastructure would follow successful acceptance testing and a definitive agreement.

Why it matters: As GPU rack densities climb toward 300 kilowatts, 800V DC architectures that cut power-conversion losses are shaping up as the successor standard to today's 415V AC systems — whoever validates it first sets the template other vendors build to.
GlobeNewswire → (Sept 14)
6

AirJoule Technologies acquires cooling and power infrastructure firm BitSink

TechFinance

AirJoule Technologies acquired 100% of BitSink, a U.S. manufacturer of liquid cooling, precision air handling and electrical switchgear for AI and HPC data centres with more than 220 megawatts of equipment already deployed across North America, for $18 million cash plus $9 million in stock, with the deal potentially worth up to $67 million. AirJoule plans to integrate BitSink's closed-loop cooling systems with its own water-from-air generation technology, which uses the low-grade waste heat those systems reject.

Why it matters: The deal bundles three of the tightest AI data-centre constraints — cooling, power infrastructure and water — under one roof, a bet that integrated hardware platforms will beat point-solution vendors as rack densities rise.
GlobeNewswire → (Sept 14)
7

Google, Nvidia and Anthropic back Emerald AI to squeeze more data centres onto the existing grid

TechEnergy

TechCrunch reports that Google, Nvidia and Anthropic are backing Emerald AI, a startup whose software lets data centres flex their power draw in real time so utilities can interconnect more AI capacity without waiting years for new transmission and generation. The tool has already been piloted with real hyperscale facilities to prove flexible load can be curtailed automatically during grid stress without disrupting AI training or inference.

Why it matters: Three of the industry's most influential compute buyers and suppliers backing the same grid-flexibility startup signals that demand-flexibility software, not just new power plants, is becoming a mainstream tool for unlocking interconnection queues.
TechCrunch → (Sept 17)
8

Huawei unveils grid-interactive AI data centre solution built around "tokens per watt"

TechEnergy

At Huawei Connect 2026 in Shanghai, Huawei launched a grid-interactive AIDC solution combining grid-friendly UPS units, intelligent lithium batteries and grid-forming energy storage that lets AI data centres both draw from and support the power grid while smoothing out AI workload fluctuations. The company said the design shifts efficiency measurement away from power usage effectiveness (PUE) toward "tokens per watt," an end-to-end metric it says it has already improved 80% through full-stack optimization.

Why it matters: A rival power-and-cooling architecture from China's largest infrastructure vendor, competing directly with Nvidia's DSX Ready and Vertiv/LG/Tesla partnerships, underscores that grid-friendly AI-factory design is now a global competitive front, not just a North American one.

9

Bell and Ottawa quadruple planned Saskatchewan AI data centre to $52.5 billion, 1.2 gigawatts

CanadaFinance

Prime Minister Mark Carney and Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe announced Bell Canada will expand its planned AI data centre in the province to a total capital investment of up to $52.5 billion — the largest single capital investment in Saskatchewan's history — boosting capacity to 1.2 gigawatts and adding up to 900 megawatts of new capacity and roughly 4,500 jobs.

Why it matters: A quadrupled, federally-backed AI campus of this scale signals Ottawa is willing to underwrite gigawatt-class sovereign AI infrastructure well outside its traditional Ontario and Quebec tech corridors.

10

Mississauga council votes unanimously for a one-year data centre construction moratorium

CanadaPolicy

Mississauga city council voted unanimously to freeze new data centre projects with energy demands over 10 megawatts for one year, giving the city time to study guardrails around energy, water and noise impacts; the pause appears to put a 40-megawatt Prologis project in the city's northwest in limbo. Mississauga follows neighbouring Oakville, which passed Ontario's first such moratorium the previous month.

Why it matters: A second major Greater Toronto Area municipality pausing construction within weeks suggests local moratoriums are becoming the default first response for Ontario councils rather than a one-off outlier.
CBC News → (Sept 16)
11

19 more companies sign Canada's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles, bringing total to 42

CanadaPolicy

The federal government said 19 additional companies have signed Canada's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles, launched September 3, bringing the total to 42 signatories across the country's data centre, cloud, AI and technology sectors. The voluntary principles commit signatories to creating lasting local benefits, protecting electricity ratepayers, minimizing water use, being transparent about local impacts and delivering strategic value to Canada.

Why it matters: Rapid industry sign-on to a voluntary federal framework gives Ottawa a soft-law tool to shape data centre siting nationally while provinces and municipalities pursue their own, sometimes conflicting, hard rules and moratoriums.

Data Centre West 2026: Alberta's data centre pitch shifts from "why us" to "how fast can you deliver"

CanadaConstruction

Coverage of Calgary's Data Centre West conference finds hyperscalers, colocation operators, utilities and developers spending far less time making the case for Alberta as an AI data centre destination and far more on power procurement, grid access, supply-chain constraints and labour availability — the operational blockers now standing between announced projects and delivered megawatts. The shift follows Meta's C$13 billion, gigawatt-scale Sturgeon County project and a wave of other Alberta announcements earlier this year.

Why it matters: Alberta's own industry has stopped debating whether the province will become a major data centre market and started grappling with whether it can actually execute at the pace hyperscalers expect.

13

Large-load tariffs to weed out speculative data centre projects spread to 25 states

PolicyEnergy

Utility Dive reports that 25 states have now approved at least one large-load tariff and seven more have proposals pending, up from just 41 proposed and approved tariffs a year ago to 104 today; the tariffs require minimum contract lengths, collateral, upfront payments for impact studies and exit fees designed to filter out data centre projects that never materialize. Some states are pairing the requirements with incentives for developers who bring their own generation or agree to flexible, curtailable usage.

Why it matters: Utilities are converging on a standardized playbook to separate serious gigawatt-scale commitments from speculative land-banking, which should make interconnection queues faster and more credible even as it raises the cost of entry for smaller developers.
Utility Dive → (Sept 25)
14

California Governor Newsom signs seven bills billed as the nation's most comprehensive data centre laws

Policy

Governor Gavin Newsom signed seven bills regulating data centres on electricity costs, energy reporting and local oversight, including SB 886, which requires the California Public Utilities Commission to adopt new tariffs by January 2028 assigning transmission upgrade costs to data centres and imposing a 10-year early termination fee. The package marks a sharp reversal from last year, when Newsom vetoed a water-disclosure bill over concerns about stifling AI growth.

Why it matters: The largest U.S. state codifying ratepayer-protection and land-use rules for data centres in one sweep gives other states a ready-made template and raises the compliance bar for every developer operating there.
Office of Governor Newsom → (Sept 21)
15

Texas Governor Abbott orders a halt on all new data centre environmental permits pending a grid and water audit

PolicyEnergy

Abbott directed the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality to stop issuing permits for new data centre, AI and cryptocurrency-mining projects until ERCOT completes an audit of risks to the power grid, broadening his earlier moratorium on approvals. TCEQ must report back by October 19, while ERCOT's full audit is expected in December; Abbott said projects must ultimately cover their own grid-infrastructure costs and report water and electricity usage.

Why it matters: Texas has the largest data centre interconnection queue in the country at more than 474 gigawatts — over five times peak grid demand — so a statewide construction freeze there ripples through the entire U.S. AI buildout timeline.
The Texas Tribune → (Sept 21)
16

U.S. House passes Ratepayer Protection Act 417-3 to keep data centres from shifting grid costs onto households

Policy

The House passed the bipartisan Ratepayer Protection Act, directing states to consider standards requiring loads larger than 100 megawatts — squarely aimed at data centres — to cover the costs of the new grid infrastructure they require rather than spreading those costs across existing ratepayers. The bill passed the Energy and Commerce Committee 52-0 in July before clearing the full House with only three dissenting votes, and now goes to the Senate.

Why it matters: Near-unanimous bipartisan support for shielding household electricity bills from AI-driven grid costs shows data centre backlash has become one of the few issues uniting both parties in an otherwise gridlocked Congress.
Utility Dive → (Sept 16)
17

SoftBank's SB Energy delays $50 billion IPO as Wall Street grows wary of the data centre boom

FinancePolicy

SB Energy, which is developing what would become the world's largest data centre campus at 10 gigawatts in Ohio, pushed its IPO from this month to mid- or late October after bankers could not find enough buyers at its targeted $50 billion-plus valuation. At least three AI-infrastructure-linked companies have now delayed or shelved public listings within weeks of each other, as investors increasingly demand operating track records rather than multi-decade revenue projections.

Why it matters: Public-market resistance to unproven, capital-intensive data centre developers — following the same community and political backlash reshaping state policy — suggests the AI infrastructure financing boom is starting to hit a credibility wall, not just a permitting one.

18

Colorado's largest-ever data centre could be approved next to homes without a public hearing

PolicyConstruction

Vantage Data Centers' proposed "Trident" campus in Aurora would draw about 500 megawatts — roughly 11 times the power used by Denver International Airport — across 862,000 square feet next to the Windler, Aurora Highlands and Green Valley Ranch neighbourhoods, an area with worse air pollution and higher asthma hospitalization rates than most of the state. City staff have confirmed the project can be approved administratively with no public hearing or council vote required, though state officials have suggested Vantage "strongly consider" holding one voluntarily.

Why it matters: A project of this scale clearing local approval with zero mandatory public input is exactly the kind of process gap driving the moratorium wave sweeping other states and provinces.

19

Blue Owl-backed CoreWeave data centre raises $1.1 billion in junk bonds at a 9.25% yield

Finance

A data centre developer sponsored by affiliates of Blue Owl Capital sold $1.1 billion in five-year notes, priced at 98.5 cents on the dollar to yield 9.25% — about 2.7 percentage points above comparable investment-grade debt — to finance its "Digital Drive" campus, which will provide 76 megawatts of IT capacity entirely leased to CoreWeave under a 15-year, $2.94 billion contract. Goldman Sachs led the offering.

Why it matters: High-yield financing at nearly double the going institutional rate shows AI data centre debt is still pricing in real credit risk around single-tenant concentration, even for campuses backed by a marquee AI cloud tenant.

U.S. spending on data centres and computing hardware overtakes residential investment for the first time

Finance

Inflation-adjusted U.S. spending on data centres and other information-processing equipment hit $752 billion in the second quarter of 2026, edging past the $748 billion spent on residential investment — up 51% from its recent baseline even as housing investment sits 18% below its early-2021 peak amid mortgage rates near 7%. A San Francisco Fed economist called it "a pivotal shift in the US economy: investment is shifting away from residential investment and towards computers."

Why it matters: AI infrastructure has become large enough to outweigh an entire traditional pillar of the U.S. economy, a milestone that raises the stakes of every financing wobble, permitting freeze and community backlash covered elsewhere in this report.
