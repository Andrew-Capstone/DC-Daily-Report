1

Cloud infrastructure spending hits $168B in Q3, up 55%, as AI capacity expands

Finance

Synergy Research puts Q3 worldwide cloud infrastructure spending at $168 billion, up 55% year over year, with Amazon at 26% share, Microsoft 19% and Google 16%. CBRE counts 7,481 MW under construction across eight North American markets in H1, with more than 80% preleased.

Why it matters: The market has doubled in eight quarters, and power limits are now the constraint on delivering capacity.

AI's expansion hits the limits of local power, with timelines stretching past two years

EnergyPolicy

GlobalData says projects that broke ground between Q3 2025 and Q3 2026 waited just over two years after announcement, versus about 18 months before. It forecasts data centre electricity use rising from 590 TWh in 2025 to 1,520 TWh in 2030, with new grid infrastructure taking five to 15 years.

Why it matters: Grid access, labour shortages and local opposition are now the main drivers of delay, more than chips or capital.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 3))

Aligned launches Delta Cube 3.0 air cooling with Munters, citing 10% more capacity and efficiency

Tech

The third-generation unit is described as delivering 10% more cooling capacity and 10% better energy efficiency in the same footprint, using a closed loop that needs no water for operational cooling. Munters builds the units in Selma, Texas and Daleville, Virginia.

Why it matters: Air cooling is being pushed to ultra-high densities and made water-free, an alternative to liquid-only designs.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 2; launch Oct 28))

Prometheus Hyperscale exits planned 1.25 GW gas-powered Casper, Wyoming campus

EnergyConstruction

Prometheus said it will not build the campus planned for 940 acres near Casper, which was to use on-site natural gas generation and closed-loop liquid cooling. No permit application had been filed; the landowner says the project will continue under an unnamed new developer.

Why it matters: It shows how speculative gigawatt-scale, self-powered announcements can unravel before reaching permitting.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 2))
5

Envision says its Inner Mongolia AI campus runs the world's first 800VDC power system

TechEnergy

Solid-state transformers convert 10 kV grid AC to an 800V DC bus, paired with batteries and AI-driven control. The campus is planned at 3 GW, and the claim is Envision's own; the system's size and customer are undisclosed. This is a China story, included for its relevance to 800V designs now emerging in North America.

Why it matters: 800VDC cuts copper and cabling for dense AI racks; North American suppliers are also moving to 800V architectures.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 2))
6

Liquid cooling retrofits are hard, but sometimes worth it

Tech

Fully loaded AI racks can exceed 3,000 pounds, straining raised floors, and older plants may lack pump and heat-exchanger capacity. Retrofits make most sense for partial deployments, single-tenant sites, water-constrained regions, or where cheaper than new build.

Why it matters: Most existing capacity is air-cooled, so retrofit economics decide how much legacy space can host AI.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 2))
