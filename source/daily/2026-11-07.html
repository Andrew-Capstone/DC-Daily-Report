Thursday, November 7, 2026 · North America focus
Morning brief: Power is the story today. Google will invest 17 billion dollars in its first Wyoming data centre, backed by a 702 megawatt fuel cell plant, and NTT signed a 900 megawatt fuel cell deal with Bloom Energy. President Trump's call for data centres to supply their own power meets turbine backlogs stretching to 2031, while Caterpillar reports a record 78 billion dollar order backlog. Politics stays hot: Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa pledged to pause new approvals, and voters backed local limits in 40 of 41 ballot measures. In Canada, Anthropic's Nova Scotia lease and Bell's plan to grow its Saskatchewan campus to 1.2 gigawatts anchor the coverage. 1

Google to invest $17B in first Wyoming data centre, powered by a 702 MW fuel cell plant

EnergyConstructionFinance

Google's Project Tembo near Cheyenne spans 716 acres. AEP will build and own a 702 MW Bloom Energy solid-oxide fuel cell plant under a 20-year PPA, with Tallgrass storing captured CO2. Google targets full build-out in 2031 with air cooling and says it will pay for 100% of its power.

Why it matters: A hyperscaler is pairing on-site fuel cells with carbon capture rather than waiting on the grid.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 5-6))
2

NTT signs 900 MW fuel cell deal with Bloom Energy as primary power for its data centres

EnergyTech

Bloom calls it its largest international project, with shipments starting in 2027. NTT has not said how the capacity splits across sites or how much is firmly purchased. Bloom shares closed about 16% lower on Nov. 5, though the decline reportedly began before the announcement.

Why it matters: Fuel cells are becoming a mainstream speed-to-power option where grid capacity is scarce.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 6))
3

Trump's onsite power push meets AI data centre reality

PolicyEnergy

In an Oct. 21 post, President Trump said large new data centres should generate their own electricity and return any excess to the grid. Some turbine models are reportedly sold out through 2031, and experts say supplying 100% of needs around the clock is hard, pointing toward hybrid models such as Abilene's gas-plus-renewables plan.

Why it matters: A possible federal expectation of self-supplied power collides with equipment backlogs.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 3))

Anthropic signs first Canadian lease at EverWind's C$10B Nova Scotia campus

CanadaEnergy

EverWind announced more than C$10 billion in private investment at Point Tupper, and Anthropic signed a long-term lease for the first phase. EverWind Fuels has also been exploring adding data centres to the industrial site.

Why it matters: This is Canada's flagship AI campus and a test of provincial power and consultation rules.

CanadaEnergyConstruction

Crusoe debuts Spark Liquid, a 3.5 MW factory-built liquid-cooled modular unit

TechConstruction

The sixth-generation modular platform supports Nvidia GB300 and Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with closed-loop direct liquid cooling, and Crusoe claims five times the compute density of earlier generations. Production joins its Brighton, Colorado factory in 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built capacity helps with construction delays, though interconnection remains the gating item.

7

Can optical AI cut data centre inference power use? Lumai deploys Iris Nova servers

Tech

Oxford spin-out Lumai is deploying Iris Nova optical servers in a UK lab. They handle the matrix-heavy prefill phase of inference while GPUs handle token generation, and run in standard air-cooled racks. The article cites no measured power or performance figures.

Why it matters: Early-stage, but a possible path to cutting inference power; treat claims as unproven.
