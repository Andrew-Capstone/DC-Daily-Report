1

Nvidia unveils DSX Pod: a prebuilt AI data centre it says deploys in as little as 14 days

TechConstruction

The containerized pod packs seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with networking, cabling and cooling preinstalled, cutting deployment at a prepared site from about six months to as little as 14 days, per Nvidia. It is being developed with 17 partners including Dell, HPE, Schneider Electric and Vertiv, with availability slated for early 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built capacity could compress build timelines that are now gated by construction.

2

FirstEnergy data centre pipeline reaches 26 GW, with West Virginia at 6 GW

Energy

FirstEnergy's pipeline grew 4.8% in Q3 to 26 GW, and contracted large-load demand is 7.9 GW, nearly double the start of the year, with about 4 GW more under negotiation. Its utilities plan the roughly $2.7 billion Maidsville gas-and-solar project, which faces organized opposition.

Why it matters: Utilities are now building generation specifically for data centre load, and local pushback follows.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 30))
3

Project Jupiter tops out first building, switching to Bloom fuel cells for on-site power

EnergyConstruction

Clayco and Kiewit topped out the first 855,000 sq ft building of the Doña Ana County, New Mexico campus leased by Oracle for OpenAI. On-site Bloom Energy fuel cells replace gas turbines and diesel generators in the design, cutting nitrogen oxide emissions by about 92%, according to the article.

Why it matters: A major campus is moving to fuel-cell microgrid power instead of waiting on grid or pipeline.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 27))
4

Sabey pilots renewable diesel (HVO) for backup generators at Austin-area campus

EnergyTech

Sabey is testing hydrotreated vegetable oil as the primary fuel for backup generators at its Round Rock, Texas campus. A 2022 Argonne study found waste-based HVO cuts lifecycle greenhouse gas emissions 79% to 86% versus petroleum diesel. Results will decide whether it expands across the portfolio.

Why it matters: Backup generation is a growing emissions and permitting issue for operators.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 30))
5

Hydro-Québec defends proposal to roughly double electricity rates for data centres

CanadaPolicyEnergy

Hydro-Québec proposes a new rate for data centres over 5 MW averaging about 13 cents per kWh, roughly double the current large-power price. Régie de l'énergie hearings began in early October and continue in December.

Why it matters: Quebec's cheap hydro has drawn data centres; a rate reset could redirect Canadian investment.
Source → (CTV News (early Oct))

Anthropic signs first Canadian data centre lease at EverWind's Nova Scotia campus

CanadaEnergy

Anthropic signed a lease of at least 20 years for the first phase of EverWind's Point Tupper campus, an 18,600 square metre building due in late 2027. EverWind plans over C$10 billion of investment and up to 420 MW in total, plus battery storage.

Why it matters: It anchors one of Canada's largest AI campuses and ties growth to Atlantic Canada's wind resources.
Source → (Data Centre Magazine (Oct 16-19))

Google named as user behind 1,200 MW Goochland, Virginia campus

Goochland County confirmed that Google is the customer for a roughly 1,000-acre, 1,200 MW campus in West Creek Business Park. The project holds four engineering letters of authorization with Dominion Energy, placing it in the utility's queue.

Why it matters: A gigawatt-class Google campus will test how much new load Virginia's grid can take.

TechFinance

9

ZutaCore unveils two-phase flow boiling design for AI cold plates

Tech

Announced at the OCP Global Summit, the design uses 0.6 to 0.7 litres per minute per kW versus 1.5 to 2.0 for single-phase cooling, according to ZutaCore, with cold plates as thin as 11.6 mm. Deployments for Nvidia RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition are underway this quarter, per the company.

Why it matters: Higher-density cooling with less coolant flow targets a key limit on rack power.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 26))

Oracle Cloud to offer Google TPUs and a wider range of AI chips

Tech

At Oracle AI World, Oracle said it will be the first hyperscaler to offer Google TPUs outside Google Cloud, arriving in 2027. Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 systems are generally available, with Cerebras, Positron and SambaNova inference chips also planned.

Why it matters: A multi-chip cloud could ease Nvidia dependence and shift data centre design.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 28))

EnergyPolicy

PG&E's pipeline rose to 12,870 MW, mostly early-stage, with 240 MW under construction and 174 MW energized since 2024. The utility is deferring load beyond an initial 1.8 GW and cut $2 billion from its 2027 capital plan.

Why it matters: Even a large California utility is rationing how fast it commits to data centre load.

12

NERC stakeholders approve reliability standards for AI data centres and crypto miners

PolicyEnergy

The standards cover loads of 50 MW or more connected at 100 kV or higher, and still need a NERC Board vote, expected in Q4, and FERC approval. They follow a May alert about data centres that unexpectedly dropped load or shifted demand rapidly.

Why it matters: First binding grid-reliability rules for large computational loads would shape ride-through and design requirements.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 15))

Umatilla, Oregon declares emergency over governor's data centre permit pause

Policy

Governor Tina Kotek's order pauses state environmental permits for new and expanded data centres. Umatilla estimates more than $5 billion of private investment and 1,000 construction jobs are at risk and authorized possible legal action; an OPB-commissioned poll found nearly two-thirds of likely voters back a temporary ban.

Why it matters: A statewide pause is a notable escalation in government limits on data centre growth.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 25))
