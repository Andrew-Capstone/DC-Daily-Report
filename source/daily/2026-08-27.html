1

Georgia regulators approve 3.2GW power deal for OpenAI's Project Camellia

EnergyPolicy

The Georgia Public Service Commission approved Georgia Power's agreement to supply up to 3.2 gigawatts to OpenAI's planned campus in Effingham County's Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub, with power delivered in phases from 2028 through 2032. OpenAI, which could spend more than $30 billion on the project, must fund all infrastructure built specifically to serve it and has committed to making up to 1,000 megawatts of its demand curtailable during grid stress.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single power-supply approvals ever granted to an AI data centre, and a template other states are watching for how to make hyperscale load pay its own way rather than shifting costs onto ratepayers.

2

Anthropic signs $45 billion compute deal with Nscale ahead of planned IPO

FinanceTech

Anthropic agreed to a six-year, roughly $45 billion deal to lease about 460 megawatts of capacity at an Nscale data centre campus in West Virginia, powered by Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips and slated to come online by late 2027. The commitment — enough power for roughly 345,000 US homes — is Anthropic's latest move to lock in compute ahead of a planned public offering, following a string of similar mega-deals over the past eight months.

Why it matters: It shows Anthropic racing to match OpenAI's capacity commitments dollar-for-dollar, and further cements Nscale as a favored landlord for frontier-lab compute in the US.

3

PJM fast-tracks $20 billion backstop auction to close 6.8GW data centre-driven shortfall

EnergyPolicy

PJM Interconnection's latest capacity auction left the grid operator 6.8 gigawatts short of its 2028/29 reliability requirement, prompting a Reliability Backstop Procurement auction — moved up to run September 30 through October 21 — that will pay up to $555/MW-day and a total of roughly $20 billion for new power plants serving data centres built through 2027. Pennsylvania officials called the prior year-long stakeholder process, which produced 12 competing proposals and no consensus, "a failure."

Why it matters: It's the clearest sign yet that the largest US grid operator is now designing emergency procurement mechanisms specifically around AI data centre load growth, with the costs ultimately flowing to utility ratepayers across 13 states and DC.

4

Cloverleaf and Nvidia partner on Michigan AI campus using DSX platform and closed-loop cooling

TechConstruction

Infrastructure developer Cloverleaf struck a strategic partnership with Nvidia, which also took a minority stake in the company, to apply Nvidia's DSX platform — unifying site, power, cooling and compute decisions early in design — to Project Ironwood, a proposed multi-building AI campus on 200 acres in Dundee Township, Michigan. The facility's closed-loop cooling system requires only a one-time water fill and is designed to use no more water day-to-day than a small office building.

Why it matters: It's a concrete example of chipmakers moving upstream into infrastructure design itself, betting that co-optimizing power, cooling and compute from day one — not bolting efficiency on later — is what lets AI factories get built and energized faster.

5

Delaware becomes first state to require data centres to "bring their own" clean power

PolicyEnergy

Governor Matt Meyer signed legislation making Delaware the first state requiring hyperscale data centres to generate or procure their own clean energy — wind, solar or nuclear — over a 10-year phase-in, with a portion of backup power also required to come from clean sources rather than diesel. A facility unable to arrange its own power cannot operate in the state, and the law bars data centre infrastructure and capacity costs from being passed on to ordinary ratepayers.

Why it matters: It's the most aggressive "bring-your-own-power" statute passed by any state so far, giving other legislatures grappling with data centre-driven rate increases a fully-formed model to copy.

6

Intel details Diamond Rapids server chip and Crescent Island AI inference GPU at Hot Chips

Tech

At Hot Chips 2026, Intel outlined three data centre-bound architectures: next-generation Xeon "Diamond Rapids," built for enterprise-scale agentic AI with a unified memory fabric and flexible I/O; "Crescent Island," a new datacenter GPU optimized specifically for AI inference; and Wildcat Lake processors. The chips use Foveros Direct 3D and UCIe-S packaging along with a high-bandwidth memory subsystem and expanded matrix-extension instructions.

Why it matters: A dedicated inference-optimized GPU signals Intel is trying to carve out ground Nvidia and AMD haven't fully claimed, at a moment when inference — not training — is becoming the larger share of data centre compute demand.

7

Starcloud raises $250 million more for orbital data centres as Nvidia joins the round

TechFinance

Orbital data centre startup Starcloud added $250 million to its Series A, more than doubling its valuation to $2.3 billion just five months after its last raise, led by Manhattan West with Nvidia contributing $25 million alongside Cisco Investments and existing backers. The company, which put the first Nvidia H100 GPU into orbit on its Starcloud-1 satellite, is working toward an 88,000-satellite constellation targeting 20 gigawatts of orbital compute.

Why it matters: With terrestrial sites facing years-long interconnection queues and mounting community opposition, orbital compute — powered by essentially unlimited solar and radiative cooling — is attracting serious capital as a genuine long-shot alternative rather than a novelty.

8

262-tonne transformer arrives as Three Mile Island's Microsoft-backed restart advances

Energy

A 262-tonne transformer shipped from South Korea arrived in Pennsylvania this week as work continues on reviving Three Mile Island Unit 1 under Constellation Energy's 20-year power purchase agreement with Microsoft, with the plant slated to begin generating power again in 2027. The milestone comes as the restart, and the broader nuclear-for-AI push it symbolizes, face growing local scrutiny over cost and safety.

Why it matters: It's a tangible sign that one of the highest-profile nuclear restarts tied to AI power demand is on a physical, not just contractual, path to completion — even as public patience with the industry's power grab narrows.

9

Canada: Premier Danielle Smith joins virtual town hall on Alberta's AI data centre backlash

CanadaPolicy

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith and Technology Minister Nate Glubish held a virtual town hall today, the third of four planned sessions, after earlier in-person events in Lacombe and Sturgeon County drew jeers, boos and demands for stronger environmental and water safeguards from residents worried about projects like Meta's proposed $13 billion campus north of Edmonton. The government has continued pitching data centres as a source of jobs and royalty revenue even as public frustration mounts.

Why it matters: It's a rare case of a premier personally fronting damage control on data centre opposition, underscoring how far public sentiment has shifted in the province most aggressively courting the industry.

10

Canada: Petition against Sherbrooke, Quebec mega data centre tops 9,000 signatures

CanadaPolicy

A Change.org petition opposing a proposed 96MW mega data centre in Sherbrooke, Quebec passed 9,000 verified signatures as of August 22, with residents accusing the municipal council of pushing the project through with little transparency. Organizers are demanding an exhaustive public environmental study and a public meeting with elected officials before any approval proceeds.

Why it matters: It extends Canada's data centre backlash into Quebec, a province that has marketed itself on cheap hydro power, showing that even the most eager provincial hosts aren't immune to local pushback.

11

Kimmeridge: up to half of planned US data centres at risk of delay or cancellation

FinancePolicy

Investment firm Kimmeridge Energy Management said as many as half of proposed US data centres face delays or cancellation as political backlash collides with the physical realities of construction, with managing partner Ben Dell saying "the Silicon Valley model is running into a real-world infrastructure constraint." A companion Bloomberg report found growing opposition is broadly slowing and reshaping the industry's pipeline, and Kimmeridge expects the delays to trim forecasts for US natural gas demand tied to new AI-driven power plants.

Why it matters: It's one of the starkest warnings yet from a serious energy investor — not an activist — that the AI buildout's announced capacity numbers are running well ahead of what can actually get sited, permitted and built.

12

Rum Group signs up to $13.7 billion GPU lease deal for Georgia campus

Finance

Rum Group (formerly Rumble) signed a six-year capacity agreement worth up to $13.7 billion with an unnamed US cloud customer for GPU services at its Maysville, Georgia campus, currently under development at 120MW and expanding to 180MW, with operations expected in Q1 2027. The deal includes warrants for the customer to buy up to 50.81 million Rum Group shares at $0.01 each, and the company says it will need to raise additional debt or equity to fund the build.

Why it matters: It's another example of a smaller, newer entrant landing a multibillion-dollar anchor contract before it has secured the capital to build the facility — a financing pattern now common across the sector.

13

Canada: New Brunswick residents keep fighting 400MW Lorneville data centre planned on wetland

CanadaPolicy

Alberta-based Beacon Data Centers and Texas-based VoltaGrid are proposing to fill in wetland near Saint John's Lorneville neighbourhood — within a UNESCO Geopark boundary — for a 400MW data centre expected to become one of New Brunswick's largest greenhouse-gas emitters and consume over 10% of the province's electricity demand. Construction is planned to begin this year for 2028 operation, despite sustained community rallies and the provincial government's refusal to impose a moratorium.

Why it matters: It's a case study in how far behind provincial oversight has fallen the pace of proposals, with no cap, moratorium or block-allocation process in place even as a single project threatens to reshape the province's emissions profile.

14

ERCOT says Texas data centre grid audit will wrap by December

PolicyEnergy

ERCOT told Texas regulators it plans to complete Governor Abbott's ordered audit of roughly 250-300 large energy consumers in "Batch Zero" by December, verifying submitted data and collecting community-impact information as directed. The audit, launched August 3, has effectively paused new interconnection reviews for a queue requesting about 474 gigawatts — more than five times ERCOT's record peak demand, with data centres accounting for roughly 90% of it.

Why it matters: A firm December end date gives the roughly 1,800 paused projects a concrete timeline, but it also confirms new Texas grid connections for data centres are frozen for several more months at minimum.

15

OpenAI folds energy planning into its data centre organization

EnergyTech

OpenAI is hiring a Clean Energy and New Technology Lead inside its Industrial Compute group, moving energy strategy from external procurement into the same team that plans data centre builds. Researchers note AI facilities could plausibly offer demand flexibility equal to 25-40% of their nameplate capacity, echoing OpenAI's own Project Camellia commitment to curtail up to 1,000MW of demand on request.

Why it matters: Treating power and compute as one integrated design problem, rather than sequential procurement steps, suggests OpenAI is trying to build a repeatable, faster playbook for energizing sites at the pace its capacity commitments now require.
