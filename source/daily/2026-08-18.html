1

Nvidia backs up to $105B in financing for OpenAI's 8GW Ohio data centre

FinanceTechEnergy

Nvidia confirmed it will back as much as $105 billion in financing — including a $1.5 billion direct investment in SB Energy — to secure land, power and a shell for the "PORTS-Pike Technology Campus" in Ohio, an 8-gigawatt facility that will open with 4.25 gigawatts of Nvidia-exclusive compute and can expand by another 3.75 gigawatts. SB Energy and SoftBank will build and own the site, which OpenAI will lease for 20 years starting in 2028, while SB Energy separately invests at least $4.2 billion in regional grid infrastructure and supports 35,000 construction jobs.

Why it matters: At up to $200 billion in potential chip revenue through 2030, it's one of the largest single infrastructure financing commitments in the AI buildout, and a template for Nvidia underwriting land and power directly rather than just supplying GPUs.

2

NextEra unlocks $3.3B in US-Japan funding for 10GW of new gas generation

Energy

NextEra Energy finalized commercial terms with the US Department of Commerce and the Government of Japan, releasing an initial $3.3 billion toward building up to 10 gigawatts of natural gas-fired generation in Texas and Pennsylvania, anchored by two projects — South Mon and Project Anderson — worth a combined $33 billion. The funding stems from Japan's $550 billion US investment commitment struck under the two countries' trade agreement.

Why it matters: It shows sovereign trade-deal capital, not just private equity, now flowing directly into US data-centre power generation, adding a new and potentially much larger funding channel to the grid buildout race.

3

Blue Energy and GE Vernova advance 2.5GW gas-plus-SMR plant to power a Texas data centre

EnergyTech

Blue Energy and GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear Energy signed an agreement to launch the next phase of a 2.5-gigawatt hybrid plant near Victoria, Texas, pairing two 7HA.02 gas turbines delivering about 1 gigawatt by 2030 with up to five BWRX-300 small modular reactors adding 1.5 gigawatts from 2032. The initial gas phase will directly supply a nearby Crusoe data centre, with a final investment decision targeted for 2027.

Why it matters: Pairing fast-to-build gas with SMRs on the same site is becoming the go-to blueprint for delivering firm power to gigawatt-scale AI campuses without waiting on an overloaded grid queue.

4

Amazon explores grid connection for its 8,000-acre, up to 7.65GW Texas AI campus

Energy

Amazon is "actively exploring" converting its 8,000-acre GW Ranch site near Pecos, Texas — designed around a private, behind-the-meter gas plant of up to 7.65 gigawatts plus 1.8 gigawatts of batteries and 750 megawatts of solar — into a front-of-the-meter facility that could eventually sell surplus power to other customers. First power is targeted for the first quarter of 2027, with 1 gigawatt online in 2028 and more than 5 gigawatts by 2031.

Why it matters: A hyperscaler positioning its own private power plant to eventually feed the public grid blurs the line between data-centre operator and utility, at a moment when Texas is desperate for new generation.

5

Sunrun and Voltus bring residential home batteries into the AI data centre power push

EnergyTech

Sunrun agreed to supply residential solar-plus-battery capacity across the PJM and MISO grid regions to Voltus's "Bring Your Own Capacity" program, which aggregates distributed home batteries and flexible loads so AI hyperscalers can secure grid interconnection without waiting on new power plants. Neither company disclosed the capacity involved or which hyperscaler projects will benefit; participating households will be compensated.

Why it matters: It's a further sign hyperscalers are becoming direct buyers of distributed home energy resources, though analysts caution aggregated batteries solve regional capacity shortfalls rather than local transmission bottlenecks.

6

Molex invests in CAEPlus to advance active liquid cooling for AI chips

Tech

Molex made a strategic investment in cooling startup CAEPlus, gaining exclusive licensing rights for its pluggable I/O applications, to accelerate development of CAEPlus's BoundaryCool platform — an active liquid-cooling architecture designed to pull more heat from CPUs, GPUs and ASICs than conventional passive cold plates while staying compatible with existing data centre designs. Financial terms were not disclosed.

Why it matters: As GPU power densities keep climbing, active cooling technologies that outperform passive cold plates without requiring a full facility redesign could become essential for squeezing more compute into existing data halls.

7

Groq raises $350M at $3.5B valuation as Nvidia backs its pivot to neocloud data centre operator

FinanceTech

Groq raised $350 million led by investment firm Disruptive, with Nvidia's participation planned, at a $3.5 billion valuation — about half its September 2025 peak, before Nvidia hired away founder Jonathan Ross and other staff as part of a licensing deal. Groq now runs Nvidia-powered infrastructure rather than its own chips, and plans to scale from 54 megawatts to more than 200 megawatts of data centre capacity across 13 sites by 2027.

Why it matters: It's a striking reversal for a company once positioned as a chip rival to Nvidia, and shows how even well-funded AI chip startups are being pulled into Nvidia's orbit as capacity operators instead.

8

Microsoft-tied "Project Odyssey" bond draws $8B in orders for QTS Georgia campus

Finance

Investors placed more than $8 billion in orders for a Microsoft-linked data centre bond sale dubbed "Project Odyssey," pushing the deal size up roughly $1 billion to $3.9 billion; the bonds, sold through Blackstone-backed QTS Realty Trust, will finance a data centre project in Georgia. Despite carrying an investment-grade rating, the notes are pricing at low-to-mid 7% yields, similar to single-B junk debt.

Why it matters: Strong demand even at junk-like yields shows investors are still hungry for hyperscaler-backed data centre debt, reinforcing bond markets as a primary funding engine for the AI buildout.

9

Canada: Doug Ford says Ontario "can't let Trump control our data" in push to build more data centres

CanadaPolicy

Ontario Premier Doug Ford told delegates at the Association of Municipalities of Ontario conference that failing to build data centres domestically risks Canadians' data ending up in the United States and "in the hands of" President Trump, arguing the province holds leverage because developers "need our energy, they need the cool environment and most importantly they need the workforce." His comments came days after the province unveiled a Data Centre Playbook requiring operators to pay the full cost of their electricity with no financial incentives.

Why it matters: Ford is reframing data centres as a data-sovereignty and economic-leverage issue just as Oakville's moratorium and Mississauga's pending vote show growing municipal resistance across the province.

10

Canada: Hamilton and Burlington councillors say Ontario's data centre playbook still lacks transparency

CanadaPolicy

Hamilton councillor Nrinder Nann said Ontario's newly released Data Centre Playbook "doesn't get into details in terms of policy and regulation," leaving residents' questions unanswered, while Burlington's Lisa Kearns — whose motion for a one-year data centre moratorium failed last month — continues pushing for firmer local rules. Both councillors want clearer noise, water and environmental standards spelled out before more projects advance in their municipalities.

Why it matters: It shows the province's new framework hasn't settled the fight — local officials in communities without formal moratoriums are still demanding the transparency that triggered bans in Oakville and pending votes in Mississauga.

11

Cherokee Nation bans hyperscale data centres on its tribally owned and trust lands

Policy

Principal Chief Chuck Hoskin Jr. announced a ban on hyperscale data centres (facilities over 10,000 square feet drawing more than 100 megawatts) on Cherokee Nation-owned and trust land, following a Data Center Task Force report that found 64% of 1,593 citizens surveyed opposed such projects over water and energy consumption, air quality, and noise concerns. For other reservation land, the tribe says it won't support hyperscale projects without "robust" developer consultation and community input.

Why it matters: As the largest US tribe with more than 475,000 citizens, Cherokee Nation's ban adds tribal sovereignty to the fast-expanding list of jurisdictions — alongside states, counties and cities — asserting control over where hyperscale AI infrastructure can be built.

12

Texas will audit up to 300 mostly-data-centre projects representing 200GW of demand

PolicyEnergy

ERCOT and the Public Utility Commission of Texas confirmed the audit ordered by Governor Abbott on August 3 will cover roughly 250-300 projects — the vast majority data centres — representing about 200 gigawatts of future demand, more than double ERCOT's current peak load record, out of more than 1,800 total projects in the interconnection queue. Developers must submit information on tax incentives, power and water use, and community-impact plans, with the verification process expected to take several months and further details due at PUCT's August 20 open meeting.

Why it matters: With concrete project counts and gigawatt figures now attached to Abbott's freeze, the scale of what's actually paused in the country's largest data-centre market is coming into sharper focus.
