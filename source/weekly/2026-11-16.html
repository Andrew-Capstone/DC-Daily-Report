Data Centre Weekly Overview

Weekly Overview

November 10 – 16, 2026 · North America focus
Week in review: Firm, owned power was the week's main thread: Google signed a 15-year take-or-pay contract for 396 MW of Fervo geothermal power in Utah with an option toward 1 GW, Anthropic was reported as the buyer of 1.7 GW of gas generators for $3.4B while leasing Riot's entire 910 MW Texas campus, Venture Global outlined about 11.5 GW of off-grid gas for Louisiana, and Oklo locked in HALEU fuel for its 1.2 GW Ohio campus serving Meta. The biggest shock was at Nvidia, where Jensen Huang resigned and CFO Colette Kress became interim chief days before the company's Nov 17 results. In Canada, Alberta is preparing legislation to screen out speculative projects just as a Pembina-linked company filed for a 3.6 GW site beside Meta's campus, while TELUS walked away from two Vancouver AI data centres over a reported lack of local support, Bell's Regina campus drew First Nation opposition, and Nova Scotia Power's agreement put grid costs on EverWind. Technology news clustered around SC26 and cooling, including Lenovo's fanless warm-water server tray and a University of Waterloo hotspot-cooling method, alongside new prefabricated power-and-compute units. Local resistance kept hardening after the US midterms, with the final count at 44 voter-approved measures restricting data centres and ERCOT seeking authority to curtail large loads earlier.
1

Google signs 15-year take-or-pay deal for 396 MW of Fervo geothermal power in Utah, with an option toward 1 GW

EnergyTech

Data Center Frontier reports that Google will buy 396 MW from eight generating units at Fervo Energy's Cape Station in Beaver County, Utah, under a 15-year take-or-pay contract. First power is expected in mid-2028 and full contracted capacity by the end of 2028; Google can raise purchases to nearly 1 GW by June 2030, with the additional power deliverable directly to a proposed campus. Cape Station's first 33 MW unit entered commercial operation on Sept 30, and the campus itself still depends on approvals and a Google decision.

Why it matters: Next-generation geothermal moves from pilot to a near-gigawatt, around-the-clock supply contract for a data centre.
Source → (Data Center Frontier (Nov 11))
2

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns; CFO Colette Kress named interim chief as analysts weigh the AI infrastructure roadmap

TechFinance

Nvidia said on Nov 12 that founder and CEO Jensen Huang had resigned, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO while the board weighs internal and external successors. It followed reports of an affair with an employee who reported to him and an earlier confirmed relationship with a person suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset. Analysts told Data Center Knowledge little changes for buyers near term, but execution 12 to 24 months out is the risk; Nvidia reports earnings on Nov 17.

Why it matters: Nvidia holds about 80% of the data centre AI accelerator market, so its succession is a supply-chain question for every AI build.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 12))
3

Riot leases entire 910 MW Corsicana campus to Anthropic in ~$28B, 15-year deal

EnergyFinanceConstruction

Riot Platforms signed a 15-year triple-net powered land lease for its whole Corsicana, Texas campus, 910 MW of critical IT capacity, with a frontier AI lab its 10-Q identifies as Anthropic. Average rent is about US$1.8B a year, with delivery in three phases from September 2027 to July 2028 and total capex of about US$5.5B. Riot says value could reach US$54B if both five-year extensions are exercised.

Why it matters: A bitcoin miner's power-rich site becomes one of the largest single AI leases yet, showing grid-connected megawatts are the scarcest asset.
Source → (w.media (Nov 9))
4

Anthropic reported as buyer in $3.4B deal for 1.7 GW of gas generators

EnergyFinance

Citing the Financial Times, Fortune reports Anthropic agreed to buy 1.7 GW of heavy-duty natural gas generators from San Antonio-based OnePWR Solutions for $3.4 billion, taking over OnePWR's Caterpillar commitments, with engineering and construction included. The generators are expected within two years; sites were not named. Anthropic's IPO filing lists $900.9 billion of compute and infrastructure commitments and flags gas price, pipeline and permitting risks. Neither company commented.

Why it matters: Described as the largest direct purchase of generation equipment by an AI company: labs are now buying their own power plants.
Source → (Fortune (Nov 10))
5

Alberta readies legislation to block speculative data centre proposals and projects too close to homes

CanadaPolicyEnergy

Technology minister Nate Glubish told The Logic that Alberta is preparing rules to discourage data centre projects that lack substance, such as those without customers or financing, and those sited too close to homes or on farmland. Options include province-wide setbacks for data centres and their power plants; he cited the Synapse project in Olds, where regulators rejected a power plant planned 30 metres from homes. The bill could be tabled as early as the week of Nov 16, and its text is not yet public.

Why it matters: Alberta's grid operator is tracking about 28 GW of proposed data centre demand, up from about 20 GW at the end of 2025, so a filter on speculative projects reshapes the queue.
Source → (The Logic (Nov 14))
6

Pembina-linked company files for Lynx, a data centre of up to 3.6 GW beside Meta's Alberta campus

CanadaEnergyConstruction

The Logic reports a newly incorporated company linked to Pembina Pipeline bought about 134 acres in Sturgeon County in October and filed with Alberta's grid operator for Lynx, a data centre of up to 3.6 GW, plus two gas-fired plants targeting service in June 2031. That is more than three times Meta's 1 GW campus next door. The project is early in the connection process with no customer named, and Pembina declined to comment on the property.

Why it matters: Alberta's Industrial Heartland is turning into a multi-gigawatt gas-powered cluster, with provincial data centre legislation expected soon.
Source → (The Logic (Nov 11))
7

TELUS exit from Vancouver AI data centres tied to lack of local support; sites were planned for 63,000 Nvidia chips and up to 163 MW

CanadaPolicy

The Globe and Mail, as republished by HeadTopics, reports that TELUS gave no official reason for ending its role in the two Westbank projects, but a source said a lack of local support led it to reconsider. Hundreds protested over the summer, one site still needs rezoning, and a pending city council motion could require independent environmental assessments and possible fees. The projects were planned for about 63,000 Nvidia chips and up to 163 MW, the memorandum with the federal government was never finalized, and TELUS says it will keep expanding in Kamloops and is evaluating other locations.

Why it matters: Community opposition, not power or capital, is the reported reason a flagship project under Ottawa's sovereign compute push lost its anchor.
Source → (The Globe and Mail via HeadTopics (Nov 15))
8

Oklo and Centrus finalize HALEU fuel supply for the 1.2 GW Ohio nuclear campus serving Meta

EnergyTech

Centrus and Oklo signed definitive agreements, following a June letter of intent, to supply high-assay low-enriched uranium for up to five Aurora powerhouses at Oklo's planned 1.2 GW Ohio campus. Deliveries from Centrus's Piketon, Ohio plant are scheduled to start in 2029. Meta's prepayment for future Oklo power funds the fuel purchase. No fuel quantity was disclosed.

Why it matters: Fuel is the main bottleneck for advanced reactors; a hyperscaler's prepayment is now financing domestic enrichment.
Source → (World Nuclear News (Nov 13))
9

Venture Global plans up to 16 off-grid gas power islands, ~11.5 GW, for AI data centres in Louisiana

Energy

The LNG exporter has agreements with GE Vernova, GE Grid Solutions and Baker Hughes for up to 16 combined-cycle power islands of roughly 720 MW each at Cameron Parish and Plaquemines Parish sites, running independently of the public grid. It targets the first 250 MW of compute in late 2028, 5 GW by end-2031 and potentially 10 GW by 2034, ordering one plant before year-end.

Why it matters: Behind-the-meter gas at gigawatt scale is becoming a mainstream answer to grid delays; it is a plan matched to future customer commitments, not contracted capacity.
Source → (Venture Global Q3 remarks (Nov 10))
10

Lenovo shows a fanless, fully water-cooled AMD EPYC server tray at SC26 that runs on 45°C inlet water

Tech

Lenovo's product guide for the ThinkSystem SC755 V5 Neptune, updated Nov 13, describes a tray with four AMD EPYC 9006 'Venice' processors of up to 256 cores and 600 W each, cooled entirely by direct water with no system fans. Inlet water can be as warm as 45°C, and Lenovo claims up to 40% lower data centre energy use than an equivalent air-cooled system, a vendor figure that depends on reduced chiller use. Each tray can run as two dual-socket or four single-socket nodes, and three 13U enclosures of eight trays fit in a rack.

Why it matters: Warm-water, fan-free designs let operators drop mechanical chillers, which is where most cooling energy and water goes.
Source → (Lenovo Press (updated Nov 13))
11

University of Waterloo hybrid cooling cuts simulated chip hotspots by up to 17°C in 15 seconds

TechCanada

Waterloo researchers pair immersion cooling in inexpensive deionized water with drops of a denser, lower-boiling electronics coolant that sit on hotspots, wrapped in a silicone-oil shell with ceramic particles to control boiling. In lab tests a single drop cut a simulated hotspot by up to 17°C within 15 seconds, several times faster than conventional immersion. The work is lab-scale and published in the Journal of Colloid and Interface Science.

Why it matters: Canadian research aimed at the transient heat spikes that limit AI chip density; still far from deployment.
Source → (HPCwire (Nov 12))
12

Torus raises $500M and launches Foundry, a prefabricated 1 to 20 MW power-and-compute unit

TechFinanceEnergy

Salt Lake City energy-storage firm Torus raised $500 million led by Magnetar and launched Foundry, a prefabricated unit combining power, storage, cooling, networking and compute for sites of 1 to 20 MW. The company says units install at existing commercial and industrial sites in 12 to 16 weeks. Its GigaOne factory line is expected to reach 500 MW of IT load, expanding to 1 GW; Hydra Host is an early customer.

Why it matters: Small modular units that use spare capacity on existing grid connections sidestep the interconnection queue for inference.
Source → (Utah Business (Nov 12))
13

ERCOT chief to ask Texas lawmakers for power to curtail data centres before a grid emergency is declared

EnergyPolicy

Bloomberg reports that ERCOT chief executive Pablo Vegas told the BloombergNEF Summit in Houston he will ask the legislature to let the grid operator require data centres to cut power use at level one of its three-stage emergency scale or earlier, instead of level two as now. Texas's 2025 Senate Bill 6 already lets ERCOT order large loads to reduce use on 24 hours' notice, a power it has not yet used. Vegas said hyperscalers are willing to integrate with the grid and that data centres reduced demand voluntarily last winter.

Why it matters: Earlier curtailment would make flexibility a standing condition of connecting large loads in the biggest US data centre growth market.
Source → (Insurance Journal / Bloomberg (Nov 12))
14

Bell's Regina AI Fabric campus, 300 MW now and up to 1.2 GW planned, faces First Nation opposition

CanadaConstruction

Bell's AI Fabric data centre just south of Regina has a 300 MW first phase under construction, more than 1,100 workers on site, and its first data hall due in the first half of 2027; Bell announced in September it could quadruple to 1.2 GW. Standing Buffalo Dakota Nation says the campus is on unceded territory and wants technical reports and approval records, which it says remain incomplete. Bell says it completed provincially requested consultations and that George Gordon First Nation is a partner.

Why it matters: Canada's largest telecom-led AI build tests how Indigenous consultation will shape the national data centre buildout.
Source → (Canadian Press via JOC (Nov 11))
15

Nova Scotia Power agreement makes EverWind responsible for added system costs from its Point Tupper data centre

CanadaEnergyPolicy

CBC reports that a service contract and letter agreement signed in September make EverWind responsible for any incremental costs to other customers while its $8 billion Point Tupper campus is on an interim industrial tariff. Service is interruptible, with demand cuts possible on 10 minutes' notice, and an early estimate puts system upgrades at $74 million or more. Nova Scotia Power says a new tariff is needed, and the Nova Scotia Energy Board must approve how costs are tracked and paid; no timeline is set.

Why it matters: An early Canadian test of the principle that data centres, not ratepayers, pay for the grid they need.
Source → (CBC News via Yahoo Canada (Nov 13))
16

Google adds TPU 8tx and 8i Max, and will let select customers run TPUs in their own data centres

Tech

TPU 8tx uses TPU 8t silicon with hardware-level encryption for secure inference and small-scale training, paired with custom AMD CPU hosts. TPU 8i Max packages two TPU 8i chips for double the compute and 384 GB of HBM. Google says select customers will be able to deploy TPU 8tx on premises, and Oracle Cloud will offer Google TPUs.

Why it matters: TPUs leaving Google's own facilities opens a new accelerator option for colocation and enterprise sites.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 9))
17

CoreWeave backlog hits $132.5B and 2026 guidance rises; 1.85 GW of active power still targeted by year-end

FinanceEnergy

CoreWeave posted Q3 revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168% year over year, and a backlog of $132.5 billion, up from $104 billion in June. It raised 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0–13.3 billion, targets $37–39 billion of 2026 capex, and still expects 1.85 GW of active power by end-2026. It carried about $47 billion of debt at quarter-end, with some bond yields above 16%.

Why it matters: The bellwether neocloud says demand still outruns capacity, a counterweight to this week's AI-credit jitters.
Source → (CNBC (Nov 10))
18

SpaceX outlines more than $74B for AI supercomputing campuses at Starbase, Texas and Cochise County, Arizona

ConstructionEnergy

SpaceX announced on Nov 12 up to $24 billion of supercomputing infrastructure on about 444 acres near Starbase, with on-site generation independent of the grid, and a roughly $50 billion campus in Cochise County, Arizona, powered through local electric cooperatives and dry-cooled. Megawatts, chip suppliers and schedules were not given. The Arizona site needs local approval, with a hearing set for Dec 9.

Why it matters: Puts dollar figures on two campuses built around their own power arrangements; capacity and timing remain unstated.
Source → (Converge Digest (Nov 12))
19

Final count: voters approved 44 local measures restricting data centres, 21 of them in Ohio

Policy

A Fox report citing Rosebud Consulting research says voters approved 44 ballot measures restricting data centres in the November midterms, nearly half of them in Ohio, with others in Kansas, Illinois, Wisconsin, Maine, New Jersey, Florida, California and Nevada. Harwood, North Dakota was the only community to back a pro-data-centre measure. An October Ohio Northern University poll found 55% of Ohio adults oppose data centre construction in their communities.

Why it matters: The tally has risen from the 40 of 41 first reported, confirming a near-unanimous result at the local ballot box.
Source → (FOX 2 Detroit via FOX 26 Houston (Nov 14))
20

Drogue raises $20M to power floating data centres with ocean thermal energy

TechEnergyFinance

TechCrunch reports that Washington, D.C. startup Drogue raised $20 million from investors including XYZ Venture Capital and Toyota Ventures. It plans to generate power from the temperature gap between warm surface water and cold water about 1,000 metres down, using a sealed ammonia loop, with seawater also cooling the servers. A multi-megawatt prototype is to go to sea on a ship in early 2028; the company has approval in principle from the American Bureau of Shipping. No exact capacity was given.

Why it matters: Ocean thermal power is unproven at scale, but it is another sign of capital chasing ways around land, grid and water limits.
Source → (TechCrunch (Nov 13))
