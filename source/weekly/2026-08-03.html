Data Centre Weekly Overview

Weekly Overview

July 27 – August 2, 2026 · North America focus
Week in review: Canada had its biggest data-centre week yet: Meta broke ground on its first Canadian facility, a CAD $13 billion, 1-gigawatt campus in Alberta, even as Alberta power prices were projected to nearly triple, Ontario demanded data centres cover 100% of grid-connection costs, Mississauga became the largest Canadian city to pass a moratorium, and Hydro-Québec pushed to double large-load rates. Infrastructure financing hit new extremes — Brookfield and NextEra's ~$100 billion Paducah, Kentucky campus, Nvidia's reported $250 billion guarantee for OpenAI's 10GW Ohio megasite, Morgan Stanley's $15 billion Anthropic Texas package, and Core Scientific's $14 billion AMD deal all landed in a single week. Grid strain became impossible to ignore: FERC warned PJM it faces a "grave legitimacy crisis," PJM moved to force new large loads to bring their own power or face first-in-line curtailment, and Exelon publicly cut its data-centre pipeline nearly in half. On the technology side, AMD's Instinct MI400 launch, Crusoe and Aalo Atomics' nuclear-microreactor pairing, Samsung's floating SMR platform, and a new quantum-classical supercomputing testbed all pointed to the next wave of compute and power solutions advancing in parallel with record hyperscaler capex and mounting local opposition.
1

Canada: Meta breaks ground on its first Canadian data centre, a $13B, 1GW campus in Alberta

CanadaEnergy

Meta broke ground on a 1-gigawatt, AI-optimized data centre in Sturgeon County, Alberta — its first facility in Canada and 33rd globally — representing over CAD $13 billion in direct investment plus roughly CAD $60 million for local roads and water infrastructure. Meta says it will fully fund new generation and grid infrastructure to serve the site, use a closed-loop dry-cooling system with near-zero operational water use, and match its power draw with 100% clean and renewable energy.

Why it matters: It's the clearest signal yet that Alberta has become the epicentre of Canada's data-centre boom, and Meta funding its own generation could become the template other hyperscalers use to get ahead of Canadian grid constraints.

2

Brookfield and NextEra unveil ~$100 billion AI campus at DOE's former Paducah, Kentucky uranium site

EnergyConstructionFinance

Brookfield will develop and operate a campus targeting 1.2+ gigawatts of AI compute (up to 1.8GW of utility-delivered capacity by 2032) at the Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant site, while NextEra builds up to 2GW of dedicated natural-gas generation and 2.6GW of battery storage under a "bring-your-own-power" model. The plan involves Big Rivers Electric, Jackson Purchase Energy Cooperative and Paducah Power System, and still needs Kentucky Public Service Commission approval and a signed anchor AI tenant.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest dedicated-generation data-centre plans yet proposed, and a template for developers building their own power rather than waiting in grid interconnection queues.

3

Nvidia in talks to guarantee $250 billion in financing for OpenAI's 10GW Ohio megasite

TechEnergyFinance

Nvidia is reportedly negotiating to backstop roughly $250 billion of debt and lease financing so OpenAI can occupy a 10-gigawatt data centre that SoftBank's energy arm is developing on a former uranium enrichment site in southern Ohio, with the Wall Street Journal putting the full project's cost — including chips — above $500 billion. The first 800MW phase is targeted for 2028; Nvidia is separately discussing financing up to $350 billion of OpenAI's chip purchases, though terms remain unsettled.

Why it matters: A guarantee this size would be the largest infrastructure financing commitment ever tied to a single AI company, and would mark OpenAI's first real step toward owning its compute instead of renting it from Microsoft, Amazon and Oracle.

4

AMD launches Instinct MI400 series GPUs, staking its claim to frontier and sovereign AI compute

Tech

At its Advancing AI 2026 event, AMD unveiled the Instinct MI400 GPU family, including the MI455X for frontier AI training and large-scale inference and the MI430X, which delivers up to 288 TFLOPS of FP64 performance for scientific computing and sovereign AI workloads. Both chips use HBM4 memory and support AMD's open ROCm software stack, with a staggered rollout running from late 2026 through 2027.

Why it matters: A credible, purpose-built alternative to Nvidia across both commercial AI factories and government-grade sovereign compute gives hyperscalers and nations more leverage in an increasingly chip-constrained buildout.

5

Crusoe and Aalo Atomics to build the first nuclear-powered AI data centre, at Idaho National Laboratory

TechEnergy

Crusoe will deploy one of its modular "Spark" data centres at Idaho National Laboratory in 2027, powered directly by Aalo's 50-megawatt "Pod" reactor — five sodium- and air-cooled Aalo-1 microreactors feeding a single turbine — as a proof-of-concept for pairing small modular reactors directly with AI compute. If successful, the partners plan to deploy Aalo's reactors across Crusoe's data-centre fleet by the end of 2029.

Why it matters: It's the most concrete timeline yet for actually running an AI data centre directly off a small modular reactor rather than a grid-connected PPA, removing two of the biggest siting constraints — water and interconnection queues — facing AI campuses today.

6

Core Scientific doubles AI capacity to 1.1GW in $14 billion, 15-year AMD infrastructure deal

EnergyTechFinance

Core Scientific and AMD signed a 15-year infrastructure agreement covering 530MW of AI data-centre capacity across five campuses in Texas, Oklahoma, Alabama and Georgia, representing more than $14 billion in potential base contracted revenue and doubling Core Scientific's leased AI footprint to roughly 1.1GW. AMD also secured exclusive rights to reserve up to an additional 2GW of future capacity, with deliveries beginning in the first half of 2027.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single infrastructure agreements of the year and marks a major pivot for a company built on bitcoin mining into a core piece of AMD's answer to Nvidia's AI data-centre dominance.

7

Morgan Stanley leads $15 billion debt package for Google-backed Anthropic AI campus in Texas

FinanceEnergyTech

Morgan Stanley is arranging roughly $15 billion in debt — a $14 billion bridge loan plus a revolving credit facility — for Nexus Data Centers to build a Hubbard, Texas campus for Anthropic, which will include its own 1.6-gigawatt natural-gas power plant. Google is backstopping the deal, taking a roughly 20% equity stake and guaranteeing billions of dollars in lease and power payments if Anthropic defaults on the four data-centre leases involved.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest project-finance deals yet for a single AI lab's dedicated infrastructure, and shows how deep-pocketed cloud providers are underwriting rivals' compute buildouts to keep their chips and platforms central to the AI race.

8

Canada: Alberta's electricity industry braces for power prices to nearly triple as data-centre demand surges

CanadaEnergy

Alberta power executives now expect wholesale prices to climb toward $85–$100 per megawatt-hour — up from roughly $32 in the first quarter of 2026 — after the province's grid operator confirmed data centres have requested more than 20,000 megawatts of new load against current peak demand of about 12,000 MW. The Alberta Electric System Operator says it can reliably absorb only about 1,200 MW of new large load without additional generation.

Why it matters: Alberta marketed itself as a low-cost, deregulated destination for AI data centres like Meta's $13 billion Sturgeon County project, so a near-tripling of power prices could reshape the economics for both new hyperscale tenants and existing ratepayers.
Canada's National Observer →
9

FERC chair warns PJM faces a "grave legitimacy crisis," gives it until September to reform

EnergyPolicy

FERC Chairman Laura Swett told PJM Interconnection — the nation's largest grid operator, spanning 13 mid-Atlantic and Midwest states carrying much of the country's data-centre load — that it faces a "grave legitimacy crisis" as some transmission owners openly discuss leaving the RTO. Swett gave PJM until the end of September to adopt governance and stakeholder-process reforms, including board independence and an expanded state role, or FERC will impose them itself.

Why it matters: PJM's territory hosts the country's densest concentration of data centres, so a regulator-forced governance overhaul could reshape how quickly — and under what rules — new AI campuses get connected to the grid across the entire mid-Atlantic.

10

Canada: Ontario says data centres must cover 100% of grid connection costs — but rules aren't written yet

CanadaEnergyPolicy

Ontario Energy Minister Stephen Lecce said data-centre operators will only be approved for grid connections if they agree to pay the full cost of the generation, transmission and nuclear-fleet maintenance their projects require, under authority granted by last year's Bill 40. Regulations to enforce the pledge are still being drafted, and one energy-policy academic warned that without clear approval criteria, costs could still flow through to consumers.

Why it matters: With data centres projected to drive 13% of Ontario's new electricity demand by 2035, how tightly the province defines "full cost" will determine whether Ontarians are shielded from — or quietly absorb — the AI buildout's grid bill.
Canada's National Observer →
11

PJM: new AI data centres must bring their own capacity or face first-in-line curtailment

PolicyEnergy

Grid operator PJM's board approved a rule that, starting June 1, 2027, any new "Large Load" of 50MW or more that hasn't secured its own generation or contracted supply will be first curtailed during capacity shortages. PJM framed the move as protecting existing ratepayers from bearing the cost of capacity built to serve rapidly growing AI and data-centre demand.

Why it matters: It's one of the most consequential grid-policy shifts of the year for data-centre developers in the largest US grid region, likely accelerating the industry's pivot toward dedicated generation, storage and flexible-load contracts over simple grid interconnection.

12

Samsung Heavy and Sargent & Lundy to jointly develop universal floating SMR platform

TechEnergy

Samsung Heavy Industries and Chicago-based nuclear engineering firm Sargent & Lundy signed a memorandum of understanding to jointly develop a technology-agnostic floating small modular reactor platform capable of accommodating multiple reactor designs. The partnership moves Samsung Heavy's floating SMR programme beyond the conceptual design it completed in 2025 and into licensing, plant standardisation, US regulatory approval and commercial deployment planning.

Why it matters: Offshore, reactor-agnostic nuclear platforms sidestep the land-use and grid-interconnection fights slowing onshore projects, giving data centre developers a potential new pathway to firm, carbon-free power at gigawatt scale.

13

Canada: Mississauga approves one-year data-centre moratorium, a possible first for a major Canadian city

CanadaPolicy

Mississauga city council voted to direct staff to prepare an interim control bylaw blocking new data-centre development for at least one year — extendable to two — while the city studies grid, water and community impacts and consults residents and Indigenous groups; the bylaw still needs a final council vote in September. The move contrasts with neighbouring Hamilton, which rejected a similar moratorium.

Why it matters: As the largest Canadian municipality yet to formally pause data-centre development, Mississauga's move could become the reference case other Ontario cities point to ahead of October's municipal elections.

14

Amazon raises 2026 capex guidance to $220 billion as AWS backlog hits $496 billion

Finance

Amazon's Q2 2026 results showed AWS revenue of $42.2 billion, up 37% year-over-year — its fastest growth since 2021 — beating estimates, while CEO Andy Jassy raised full-year capital spending guidance to $220 billion, citing higher memory costs and continued AI demand. Management disclosed an AWS backlog of roughly $496 billion and warned capacity constraints will persist into 2027 and 2028.

Why it matters: Amazon's numbers confirmed the same story Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta told this earnings season: demand and spending are both still accelerating, with power and capacity — not chips — the binding constraint.

15

Canada: Alberta's AI data-centre buildout runs into a years-long global shortage of gas turbines

CanadaEnergy

Developers of Alberta's roughly $100 billion pipeline of AI data centres — including the $15 billion Meta campus and the newly announced $4.6 billion Greenlight gas plant — are facing turbine wait times of up to five years, with GE Vernova alone sitting on a backlog representing 100 gigawatts of potential capacity. Most new Alberta gas-turbine orders won't arrive before 2030, running straight into the province's mandate that new data centres largely self-supply their own power.

Why it matters: Alberta has staked its data-centre strategy on operators building their own gas generation rather than waiting for grid capacity, and a global turbine shortage undercuts that entire model just as tens of billions of dollars in projects break ground.

16

Rigetti, HPE and Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center to build "TangleLab" hybrid quantum-classical testbed

Tech

Rigetti Computing, HPE and the Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center announced a $5 million NSF-funded testbed called TangleLab that integrates a 9-qubit Rigetti Novera quantum processor with HPE ProLiant, DL385 and Cray high-performance computing and storage systems. Construction begins September 1, 2026 inside PSC's new data centre, with full operation targeted for 2027 to support research into hybrid quantum-classical workflows, AI and simulation.

Why it matters: It's a concrete, funded step toward running quantum processors directly alongside classical HPC and AI infrastructure inside a production data centre, rather than in an isolated lab.

17

Exelon slashes its data-centre pipeline from 18 gigawatts to 11 amid mounting opposition

EnergyPolicy

Exelon's Q2 earnings presentation showed its "high probability" data-centre pipeline cut nearly in half, to 11GW from 18GW, and its broader pipeline through 2027 cut to 25GW from 43GW. CFO Jeanne Jones said the revision "weeded out speculative projects," now counting only those in advanced design or backed by FERC-approved transmission security agreements, as regional opposition and ratepayer concerns mount.

Why it matters: Exelon is one of the largest US utilities serving PJM territory, so a major utility publicly conceding that much of its data-centre pipeline was speculative is a signal the sector's most-hyped growth numbers may be overstated.

18

Canada: Hydro-Québec pushes to roughly double electricity rates for large data centres

CanadaEnergy

Hydro-Québec has asked the province's energy regulator to raise rates for data centres consuming more than 5MW to about 13¢/kWh — roughly double what large-power customers currently pay — phased in over five years for facilities already connected. A hearing before the Régie de l'énergie is expected this fall, with a decision possible by early 2027; some data-centre operators have already signalled they will contest the tariff.

Why it matters: Quebec has marketed itself as a low-cost, hydro-powered destination for data centres, so a utility-led move to double rates would reshape the province's competitive pitch just as demand is projected to grow sevenfold by 2035.

19

Montgomery County, Maryland council unanimously passes 18-month data-centre moratorium and zoning ban

Policy

The county council passed Expedited Bill 19-26, an 18-month moratorium barring the Department of Permitting Services from issuing new data-centre permits, alongside a zoning text amendment that defines hyperscale data centres and prohibits them countywide for now. The compromise splits the difference between a 6-month pause and a 2-year moratorium previously proposed by different councilmembers, giving the county time to complete a best-practices review and build a permanent regulatory framework.

Why it matters: Montgomery County sits just outside Washington, D.C. in one of the country's most contested data-centre growth corridors, making its outright — if temporary — ban one of the strongest local pushbacks yet.

20

Antora Energy raises $550 million to scale thermal batteries for AI data centres

TechEnergy

Antora closed an oversubscribed $550 million Series C, co-led by G2 Venture Partners and Eclipse, bringing its total funding to roughly $1 billion. Its thermal batteries store electricity as heat in insulated carbon blocks and discharge it on demand as power or steam, letting data centres and heavy industry buy cheap off-peak power and avoid straining the grid during demand spikes; the funding will support a second US manufacturing hub.

Why it matters: As gas turbines and grid interconnects both become bottlenecks, thermal storage is emerging as one of the few near-term ways operators can smooth AI's spiky power draw without waiting years for new generation.
