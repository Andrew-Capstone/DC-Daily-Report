Data Centre Weekly Overview

Weekly Overview

October 6 – October 12, 2026 · North America focus
Week in review: Firm power and grid strain defined the week. Google signed a 20-year Constellation deal covering 890 MW of nuclear uprates across 11 PJM reactors, Black Hills committed $1.8 billion of new generation for Google's Cheyenne campus, and Michigan approved DTE's contracts for a 1 GW Google site. Gas and nuclear ambitions kept growing, with AWS filing a 36-building plan at the 4.5 GW Homer City campus, Aligned breaking ground on a 2 GW former coal site, and Valar Atomics revising its Utah SMR campus to 9.4 GW. The DOE put $1.9 billion toward 31 grid projects, while PJM asked FERC to make new data centres bring their own power. On technology, Nvidia's Vera Rubin racks pushed 100% liquid cooling and 800 V DC, and Google and industry panelists described AI power swings forcing chip-to-grid redesigns. In Canada, Bell's Saskatchewan campus grew to as much as 1.2 GW and $50 billion, Alberta pitched surplus power to AI developers, and an Angus Reid poll found 64% of Canadians want provinces to limit large data centres. Capital kept flowing, led by Crusoe's $3.9 billion raise and Microsoft's reported 38 GW plan, as local pushback such as Loudoun County's pause grew.
1

Google signs 20-year nuclear PPA with Constellation for 890 MW across 11 PJM reactors

Energy

Google will buy 890 MW of new nuclear output from Constellation under a 20-year agreement covering 11 reactors in the PJM Interconnection. Constellation plans to spend over $4.3 billion on reactor upgrades.

Why it matters: One of the largest hyperscaler nuclear contracts to date, adding firm carbon-free supply in the most constrained grid region.

2

AWS files 36-building plan at 4.5 GW gas-powered Homer City campus in Pennsylvania

ConstructionEnergy

Amazon Data Services filed a preliminary land development plan for 36 buildings on the former Homer City coal plant site, to be powered by a gas plant of roughly 4.4 to 4.5 GW. Construction is slated to begin in December with completion targeted for 2036, though the county planning commission tabled the application after more than 100 residents raised noise, water and boundary concerns.

Why it matters: A hyperscaler is anchoring one of the largest gas-powered campuses in North America, with local opposition already surfacing.
Baxtel → (Oct 7)
3

Valar Atomics files for 9.4 GW SMR-powered data centre campus in Utah

EnergyTech

Valar Atomics is seeking federal approval for Project Beehive, a campus on more than 9,000 acres of Bureau of Land Management land near Price, Utah. It would run on 456 small modular reactors built on site, according to NPR.

Why it matters: An ambitious on-site SMR concept that would dwarf current plans, though it still depends on federal approvals.
Baxtel → (Oct 1)
4

Bell's Saskatchewan AI data centre quadruples to about 1.2 GW and $50B+

CanadaEnergy

Announced Sept 14 at the Canada Investment Summit, the Regina-area project keeps its original 300 MW from the provincial grid and adds up to 900 MW that Bell supplies itself under Saskatchewan's Bring Your Own Power policy. The agreement is a non-binding MOU, and the province cites up to 500 permanent jobs.

Why it matters: Canada's largest announced sovereign AI project, and a test of the bring-your-own-power model.
CTV News → (Sep 14)
5

Black Hills signs definitive agreements to power Google's Cheyenne campus with $1.8B of new generation

EnergyConstruction

The agreements, effective September 30 and running through 2048, have Black Hills provide up to 590 MW of grid-connected service and manage about 2.1 GW of third-party Wyoming resources through a private microgrid. It will spend $1.8 billion from 2027 to 2029 on 564 MW of company-owned generation, with service starting late 2027 and peak load in 2030.

Why it matters: A utility-built, ring-fenced power package is becoming the model for gigawatt campuses.
SEC Form 8-K exhibit (Black Hills) → (Oct 6)
6

Michigan regulators approve DTE–Google power contracts for 1 GW Van Buren Township campus

EnergyPolicyConstruction

The Michigan Public Service Commission approved the 20-year DTE Energy arrangements on October 1 for Google's roughly 1 GW campus on 282 acres. First power is due December 2027 and full contracted load in December 2028, with a minimum billing demand of 80% of contracted capacity. The package includes funding for up to 1,600 MW of renewables and up to 480 MW of battery storage.

Why it matters: Take-or-pay terms plus funded new generation are becoming the regulatory template for gigawatt-scale loads.
Construction Review Online → (Oct 1-3)
7

DOE selects $1.9B for 31 grid projects meant to unlock 23 GW of transfer capacity for data centres

EnergyPolicy

DOE's SPARK initiative would fund 31 projects in 26 states, with $3.35B of recipient cost-share bringing total value to $5.25B. Work covers upgrades on more than 1,500 miles of lines. These are selections, not final awards; agreements are due between October 2026 and January 2027, and TechRadar notes the money is largely repackaged infrastructure-law funding.

Why it matters: Moving more power over existing corridors is the fastest lever for connecting large loads.
DataCenterDynamics → (Sep 24)
8

Google details grid-interactive AI data centres, from backup to grid partner

TechEnergy

Google says it processes more than 3 quadrillion AI tokens per month, a sevenfold rise in compute demand from May 2025 to May 2026. It uses battery storage to buffer fluctuating workloads, has moved to rack-level 54 VDC battery backup and is migrating toward 800 VDC, and works with Meta, Microsoft and Nvidia on a utility interface specification.

Why it matters: Shows how the largest operators plan to turn load volatility into a grid-support tool.
Data Center Knowledge → (Sep 25)
9

AI power spikes force chip-to-grid design changes, panelists warn

TechEnergy

At Data Center World Power in Dallas, panelists said AI loads swing in milliseconds while grid generation takes minutes to ramp. Nvidia has added rack-level storage and controls to smooth swings, and Oracle is exploring smaller on-site generation blocks in Abilene.

Why it matters: Load volatility is becoming a grid-stability issue that chip makers, operators and utilities must solve together.
Data Center Knowledge → (Oct 6)
10

Nvidia's Vera Rubin racks push 100% liquid cooling and 800 V DC power

Tech

DCD reports the NVL144 rack design uses 45°C liquid cooling and an 800-volt DC architecture, which Nvidia says carries over 150% more power through the same copper. Vera Rubin is slated for the second half of 2026, with Kyber racks in 2027. This coverage dates from the GTC announcements earlier this year.

Why it matters: Operators designing new halls now have to plan for liquid cooling and DC distribution rather than treat them as options.
DCD → (earlier in 2026)
11

Flex to acquire EPC Power for $4.4B as AI data centres move to 800V DC

FinanceTechEnergy

EPC Power makes 800V DC and grid-forming power-conversion systems and has a new South Carolina plant. The deal is expected to close in Q4 2026 and EPC expects about $800 million revenue in 2026.

Why it matters: Power conversion is becoming a strategic layer of AI infrastructure.
Data Center Knowledge → (Sep 4)
12

Alberta pitches surplus power as Meta's $13B+ Sturgeon County campus advances

CanadaEnergy

Calgary.Tech reports Meta's project is backed by the 932 MW Greenlight gas plant, due in service in the second half of 2030, and a 250 MW Capital Power supply deal starting in the second half of 2028. Alberta has about 1.2 GW of surplus generation.

Why it matters: Shows how gas-backed supply is shaping where Canada's AI campuses are built.
Calgary.Tech → (Oct 1)
13

Angus Reid poll: 64% of Canadians want provinces to limit large data centres

CanadaPolicy

The survey of 4,417 adults, released Oct 7, found only 18% would encourage more development. Opposition is highest in Manitoba and Atlantic Canada (72% each) and Saskatchewan (70%), driven by water use (51%) and electricity costs (38%).

Why it matters: Public sentiment is turning against rapid build-out just as provinces court AI investment.
Angus Reid Institute → (Oct 7)
14

Aligned breaks ground on 2 GW Project Phoenix campus on a former coal site in Pennsylvania

EnergyConstruction

The three-facility campus at Shippingport is pegged at about $10B of regional investment and is planned to run on its own on-site power, next to a redeveloped Shippingport power station. Aligned estimates about 3,000 construction jobs and 640 permanent roles. Sources differ on the neighbouring plant's capacity.

Why it matters: Coal-to-compute conversions with dedicated generation are becoming a template for gigawatt sites.
DataCenterDynamics → (Sep 10-21)
15

Meta's 1 GW El Paso campus to run on 366 MW of modular gas units

EnergyConstruction

Search coverage reports Meta would cover all generation costs during a five-year bridge period when the site stays off the grid. Inside Climate News reports the local utility only considered gas to serve the campus, and critics say it could be rate-based.

Why it matters: Behind-the-meter gas is becoming the default fast path to power, raising ratepayer and permitting questions.
Inside Climate News → (Sep 24)
16

PJM asks FERC to require new data centres to bring their own power, with an order requested by Oct 12

PolicyEnergy

Under the proposal, new large loads that have not brought their own power would be first to be moved to backup generators or curtailed on the hottest and coldest days. PJM asked FERC to act by October 12, and Ohio regulators argue the definition should be limited to data centres and exclude manufacturers.

Why it matters: A FERC decision this week could reshape power procurement across 13 states.
Virginia Mercury via 12 On Your Side → (Aug 27 (decision pending))

Crusoe raises $3.9B Series F at a $30.9B valuation

Finance

The funds will go toward expanding AI data centres across the US.

Why it matters: Private capital continues to flow to AI infrastructure developers.

18

Microsoft reportedly plans 38 GW of data centre capacity by 2032

FinanceConstruction

Bloomberg-sourced reports say Microsoft targets more than 38 GW, up from about 12 GW, with roughly a third AI-specific. Microsoft has not confirmed the figure, and outlets differ on whether leased neocloud capacity is included.

Why it matters: Signals the scale of hyperscaler demand for power, land and equipment.
DataCenterDynamics → (Sep 14)
19

AMD to buy World Labs for about $8.2B in all-stock deal

FinanceTech

The deal, announced September 28, will bring World Labs CEO Fei-Fei Li to AMD as chief scientist and is expected to close by end of 2026. Analysts see it as a way to co-design hardware around physical-AI models, where Nvidia holds an early lead.

Why it matters: Chipmakers are buying model expertise to shape future compute demand.
Data Center Knowledge → (Sep 28-29)
20

Loudoun County, Virginia, pauses new data centre applications for up to a year

Policy

The Board of Supervisors voted 7-1 in mid-September to pause new applications while it studies resource impacts and drafts rules; a permanent ban was deemed unworkable under state law. Reports differ on the date (Sept 15 or 18), and a follow-up resolution is due Oct 20.

Why it matters: The world's largest data centre market is slowing new approvals.
