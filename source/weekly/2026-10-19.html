Data Centre Weekly Overview

Weekly Overview

October 13 – October 19, 2026 · North America focus
Week in review: Nuclear, gas and custom silicon defined the week. EverWind announced a C$10 billion Point Tupper, Nova Scotia campus with a 20-year frontier-AI lease reported to be to Anthropic, the week's biggest Canadian story, while Defence Construction Canada flagged a 60 MW, $1.5–1.85 billion secure government data centre in Ottawa. Amazon signed a 690 MW, 20-year nuclear deal at Calvert Cliffs, and Google's Constellation agreements grew to include 2,700 MW of supply alongside the 890 MW of reactor uprates. On technology, Microsoft unveiled its Maia 300 chip claiming 40% more tokens per megawatt, Google detailed 800 VDC and grid-interactive designs, Flex agreed to buy EPC Power for $4.4 billion, and aluminum-air backup power and floating solar GPU pods were tested. Gas-fired campuses kept advancing in Texas (a 1 GW Taylor approval, a 1.5 GW islanded Pecos plan, Anthropic-linked Project Longhorn), even as NERC load standards advanced, Oregon paused data centre air and water permits until July 2027, and BlackRock's Larry Fink warned a construction halt could tip the US into recession.
1

EverWind announces C$10B Point Tupper, Nova Scotia data centre with 20-year AI lease

CanadaEnergyFinance

EverWind Fuels says Anthropic signed a 20-year lease for an 18,600 m² campus drawing 95 MW in phase one (online late 2027), with a 325 MW expansion in mid-2028 for 420 MW total. The $10B plan is $8B for the campus and $2B for onshore wind, with 650 MW of approved wind farms 51% owned by a Membertou-led First Nations consortium and about 200 MWh of on-site batteries.

Why it matters: It is billed as the largest private investment in Nova Scotia history, and EverWind says it will cover grid upgrade costs so ratepayers are not exposed; the opposition questions how that is enforced.
BNN Bloomberg / Canadian Press → (Oct 16)
2

Google signs Constellation nuclear deals: 890 MW of reactor uprates plus 2,700 MW supply

Energy

A 20-year agreement adds 890 MW by upgrading 11 reactors at six sites in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, with Constellation spending over $4.3B and the first upgrade online in 2028. A separate 15-year deal supplies another 2,700 MW from Constellation's fleet on the PJM grid.

Why it matters: Uprating existing reactors is one of the fastest routes to new firm clean capacity, and it extends hyperscaler nuclear buying beyond new-build SMRs.
Android Headlines → (Oct 7)
3

Amazon and Constellation sign 20-year, 690 MW nuclear deal at Calvert Cliffs

EnergyPolicy

The agreement covers 690 MW from Maryland's Calvert Cliffs plant, including 190 MW of uprates due 2030 to 2032. Constellation plans about $3 billion of improvements and says the contract supports relicensing for another 20 years.

Why it matters: New capacity is being added through uprates rather than new reactors.
Maryland Matters → (Sep 30)
4

Microsoft unveils Maia 300 AI chip, claims 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU systems

Tech

The third-generation inference-first accelerator is slated for production in 2027, with up to seven times Maia 200's rack-scale throughput in a 96-accelerator rack. Maia 200 is already deployed in US, European and Australian data centres, and Microsoft plans multi-gigawatt Maia deployments in 2027.

Why it matters: Silicon designed around tokens per watt targets the power constraint directly, though Microsoft has not said whether Maia will be offered to Azure customers.
Data Center Knowledge → (Oct 13)
5

Aluminum-air generators tested as emission-free backup power for data centres

TechEnergyCanada

Phinergy, Canadian cleantech AlumaPower and Voya Energy are developing aluminum-based generators that make electricity without combustion, as an alternative to diesel. AlumaPower reports nearly 200 hours of testing with the Net Zero Innovation Hub.

Why it matters: Backup diesel is a permitting target in many jurisdictions; this is early-stage testing rather than a deployed product.
Phinergy (via Rich Miller's newsletter) → (Oct 17)
6

Google details grid-interactive AI data centres: 54 VDC racks, 800 VDC roadmap, 1 MW racks

EnergyTech

At Data Center World Power, Google said rack-level 54 VDC battery backup replaced centralized UPS for about 1.5% efficiency gain, racks may approach 1 MW within one to two years, and it is moving toward 800 VDC. Google can shift noncritical workloads during grid events, and Google, Meta, Microsoft and Nvidia co-defined a utility interface spec.

Why it matters: It shows how hyperscalers plan to turn data centres into flexible grid assets rather than passive loads.
Data Center Knowledge → (Sep 25)
7

Flex to buy EPC Power for $4.4B as AI data centres move to 800V

EnergyFinanceTech

EPC Power makes grid-forming power-conversion systems and 800V DC platforms, with a new South Carolina plant of 27 GW annual capacity, and expects about $800M revenue in 2026. The deal should close in Q4 2026, and Flex plans to spin off its Cloud and Power Infrastructure segment in Q1 2027.

Why it matters: Power-conversion equipment is becoming a strategic asset as rack voltages rise.
Data Center Knowledge → (Sep 4)
8

Lumentum, Astera Labs and Corning demo optical PCIe for AI racks at OCP Summit

Tech

The proof of concept sends PCIe signals as light over multimode fibre using VCSELs, running an error-free PCIe 5 link with multiple 32 GB/s channels in a rack-representative system. Optical can span tens to hundreds of metres, versus a few metres for passive copper.

Why it matters: Longer reach could let operators disaggregate GPUs, switches and memory across racks, but Lumentum says integration, reliability and qualification work remain before deployment.
Data Center Knowledge → (Oct 19)
9

NetworkOcean runs customer AI workloads on solar-powered floating GPU data centre in San Francisco Bay

TechEnergy

A single Nvidia H100 ran customer workloads on a 20 kW solar array in a Bay trial. The startup went from design to in-water deployment in 53 days and plans a 1 MW offshore array next year and up to 1 GW of compute by 2030.

Why it matters: An early, small-scale test of off-grid floating compute; the 1 GW target is a company aspiration.
Baxtel → (Oct 3)
10

Canada plans $1.5–1.85B, 60 MW secure data centre in Ottawa area for CSE

CanadaConstruction

Defence Construction Canada posted an advance procurement notice for a 60 MW facility to give the Communications Security Establishment more secure computing capacity. Procurement is expected from winter 2027, subject to government approval, which has not yet been given.

Why it matters: Sovereign government compute at this scale signals federal appetite for domestic AI infrastructure; Hydro Ottawa says data centres account for 653 MW of 1,075 MW of requests.
CTV News → (Oct 15)
11

Google's $735M Beauharnois, Quebec data centre still unbuilt as Hydro-Québec power costs rise

CanadaEnergy

Five years after its announcement, the site has no construction and no approved power supply. Hydro-Québec proposes about 13 cents per kWh for large data centres, and Google calls the increase "unreasonable and punitive." York University counts 167 projects in Alberta versus seven planned in Quebec.

Why it matters: Power pricing is steering Canadian data centre investment toward Alberta and away from Quebec.
The Labradorian → (Oct 12)
12

Big Watt and PowerHouse win approval for 1 GW gas-powered hyperscale campus in Taylor, Texas

EnergyConstruction

The development agreement covers 664 acres outside Taylor with potential for up to 1 GW of hyperscale capacity, powered by dedicated behind-the-meter natural gas generation targeted for the first half of 2028. Developers will also fund upgrades to the city's reclaimed-water infrastructure.

Why it matters: ERCOT is tracking 438,000 MW of large-load requests, nearly 89% for data centres, and private gas power is becoming the way to skip the interconnection queue.
W.Media → (Oct 13)
13

Prometheus plans 1.5 GW islanded gas-powered data centre campus in Pecos, Texas

EnergyConstruction

The campus would initially run off the ERCOT grid on onsite generation, mainly pipeline natural gas, with plans to expand to 2.5 GW. Prometheus estimates islanded power at $100 to $125 per MWh and says modular construction could deliver 100 to 150 MW in 2027, contingent on a customer commitment.

Why it matters: A test of whether private, behind-the-meter power can bypass interconnection queues at gigawatt scale.
Data Center Knowledge → (Aug 24)
14

Project Longhorn files $366M first building at Anthropic-linked Texas campus with 710 MW gas plant

ConstructionEnergy

Pacifico Energy and BlackChamber filed a $366 million, 498,165 sq ft first building at the 2,842-acre Cedar Creek campus in Bastrop County. Anthropic is reportedly negotiating to lease it, though no lease is final. The campus would pair with an off-grid 710 MW gas plant and faces neighbour legal challenges.

Why it matters: Shows the off-grid gas model now common for AI campuses, and the local pushback it draws.
Baxtel → (Oct 7 filing)
15

Duke Energy and tech giants agree on large-load tariff for North Carolina data centres

PolicyEnergy

The settlement reportedly has a 50 MW trigger and 75% minimum billing and needs N.C. Utilities Commission approval. Environmental groups say it falls short on cost allocation and transmission upgrades.

Why it matters: Large-load tariffs are becoming the template for who pays for the grid build-out behind AI campuses.
BPR → (Oct 7)
16

NERC stakeholders approve reliability standards for AI data centres and crypto miners

PolicyEnergy

The standards (Project 2026-02) cover computational loads of 50 MW or more connected at 100 kV or higher, including interconnection modelling, operational data and load-protection coordination. NERC's Board is expected to vote in Q4 before FERC review; they follow a May Level 3 alert on sudden data centre load drops.

Why it matters: A continent-wide rulebook for large loads will shape interconnection for every new campus; NERC projects 224 GW of summer peak growth over 10 years.
Data Center Knowledge → (Oct 15)
17

Trump order targets foreign grid equipment as data centres expand

PolicyEnergy

An Aug. 26 executive order declares a national emergency over the bulk power system and directs DOE to restrict foreign-produced grid equipment including transformers, generators, inverters, batteries and turbines. DOE rules are due within 120 days; Jefferies expects longer lead times and higher domestic prices.

Why it matters: Equipment already has long lead times, so new restrictions could slow data centre power buildouts.
Data Center Knowledge → (Aug 28)
18

Oregon governor pauses air and water permits for new data centres until July 2027

Policy

Gov. Tina Kotek asked DEQ in an Oct. 9 letter to hold air and water permits for new or expanded data centres through July 1, 2027, saying she lacks authority for a full moratorium without legislation. Amazon, which has more than a dozen hyperscale sites in Morrow and Umatilla counties, said it was disappointed.

Why it matters: A de facto statewide pause in a major cloud region follows New York's hyperscale order and may spread; comments on the advisory committee report close Oct. 24.
OPB → (Oct 13)
19

$15B ServerDomes campus breaks ground near El Paso as Texas permit pause continues

ConstructionPolicyEnergy

Groundbreaking on the 350 MW Hueco Ranch campus in Hudspeth County came Oct. 7, before the county judge learned of it; no state permit filings have surfaced. Up to 90% of power is planned behind the meter, and the developers present to the Commissioners Court Oct. 27. Baxtel notes Gov. Abbott's order halting TCEQ data centre permits.

Why it matters: Texas counties lack zoning authority over private land, so disclosure is their main lever.
Baxtel → (Oct 11)
