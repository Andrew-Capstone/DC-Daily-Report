Data Centre Weekly Overview

Weekly Overview

September 29 – October 5, 2026 · North America focus
Week in review: Power dominated the week. Google's first Project Suncatcher satellite, carrying four TPUs, launched on a Falcon 9 to test AI compute in orbit, and Fervo's Cape Station began selling power as the world's first commercial enhanced geothermal plant. Nuclear deals piled up: Amazon signed a 20-year Calvert Cliffs PPA with Constellation, We Energies offered Oracle a share of Point Beach output, and Valar Atomics proposed a 456-reactor, 9.6 GW campus on Utah public land. In Canada, Meta's Alberta campus gained a 932 MW gas plant and Capital Power supply, Bell's Saskatchewan project quadrupled to 1.2 GW and broke ground, and Enerflex won a 450 MW gas-generator order, while protests in Calgary and a B.C. Greens moratorium proposal showed opposition spreading. On the money side, banks lined up a $60 billion debt package for Anthropic's Broadcom chips and Akamai signed an $11.6 billion Anthropic compute deal, even as Texas froze data centre permits pending audits and hyperscalers such as Amazon moved to buy community goodwill.
1

Google launches Project Suncatcher prototype: four TPUs head to orbit on a Falcon 9

Tech

A SpaceX Falcon 9 carried a refrigerator-sized Google satellite with four TPUs and about one kilowatt of solar power into orbit on the Transporter-18 rideshare mission. The test will gauge how the chips handle launch stress, radiation, thermal extremes, cooling limits and communications.

Why it matters: The first hardware test of orbital AI compute, aimed at escaping terrestrial power and permitting limits.
CNN → (Oct 1)
2

Fervo's Cape Station becomes the world's first commercial enhanced geothermal plant

EnergyTech

The first third of a planned 100 MW plant began selling power to the grid on September 30, 23 months after groundbreaking. Google and Southern California Edison are among the buyers, the site could reach 4 GW, and Fervo aims to build later phases in about 18 months.

Why it matters: Fast, phased, firm clean power matches how data centres ramp, and this is the first proof at commercial scale.
TechCrunch → (Oct 1)
3

Amazon signs 20-year nuclear PPA with Constellation for Maryland's Calvert Cliffs

Energy

The deal covers the 1,790 MW plant and enables more than $3 billion in upgrades, including about 190 MW of new capacity from uprates in 2030-2032, supplying Amazon operations across 13 states in PJM.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers keep locking up existing nuclear baseload while funding added capacity.
EnergyTech → (Oct 1)
4

Startup proposes 456-reactor, nuclear-powered data centre campus on Utah public land

EnergyTechPolicy

Valar Atomics' Project Beehive would span more than 9,000 acres of Bureau of Land Management land near Price, Utah, with about 9.6 GW from 25 MW high-temperature gas reactors. Non-construction work could start late this year with first reactors targeted for 2028, but a federal review of water, fuel transport and emergency planning is still ahead.

Why it matters: The most ambitious nuclear-for-AI proposal yet, and a test of whether regulators can keep pace.
NPR → (Sept 30)
5

We Energies strikes deal to let Oracle subscribe to Point Beach nuclear output for Project Lighthouse

Energy

Oracle could subscribe to 10% to 20% of Point Beach Nuclear Plant's generation to serve the Port Washington, Wisconsin campus it is co-developing with OpenAI. The utility says the arrangement could save other customers about $300 million in fuel costs over five years, but it needs Wisconsin Public Service Commission approval.

Why it matters: A utility-structured nuclear subscription that pitches ratepayer savings is a template regulators elsewhere may copy.
Civic Media → (Oct 2)
6

Supermicro begins shipping Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with 1.8 MW liquid-cooling units

Tech

Supermicro has started shipping NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 racks built on its DLC-2 direct liquid cooling and 1.8 MW in-row coolant distribution units with N+1 redundancy. Each rack holds 72 Rubin GPUs and 36 Vera CPUs with 20.7 TB of HBM4, and a 16-rack scalable unit spans 1,152 GPUs and 331 TB of HBM4.

Why it matters: Fully liquid-cooled rack-scale systems are moving from announcement to delivery, setting the cooling and power design baseline for the next wave of AI halls.
StorageReview → (Sept 25)
7

Meta's $13B+ Alberta data centre pairs with a 932 MW gas plant and a Capital Power supply deal

CanadaEnergyConstruction

Meta's Sturgeon County campus is Alberta's largest tech infrastructure investment, with operations expected in the second half of 2030. The 932 MW Greenlight Electricity Centre supplies power behind the fence, and Capital Power commits 250 MW from the second half of 2028.

Why it matters: Alberta's bring-your-own-generation model is becoming Canada's template for gigawatt AI campuses.
Calgary.Tech → (Oct 1)
8

Bell marks construction milestone at Saskatchewan AI data centre as project grows to 1.2 GW

CanadaConstruction

Structural steel is rising on all four first-phase buildings with more than 500 workers on site, and the first data hall is expected in the first half of 2027. The initial 300 MW comes from SaskPower, while the added 900 MW will come from a Bell-built natural gas plant. An NDP MLA criticized the limited public consultation.

Why it matters: Canada's flagship sovereign-AI campus is moving from announcement to steel, with on-site gas generation as the power answer.
BNN Bloomberg → (Oct 1)
9

Enerflex signs deal to build 450 MW of natural-gas power for a data centre developer

EnergyCanada

Calgary-based Enerflex will design and assemble about 450 MW of behind-the-meter gas generation units, with deliveries from 2027 to 2028.

Why it matters: On-site gas is a growing answer for developers that cannot wait for grid interconnection.
CTV News → (Oct 1)
10

Banks line up $60B debt package to finance Anthropic's Broadcom AI chips

FinanceTech

Banks are raising $60 billion, including a $42 billion senior-secured tranche and an $18 billion junior tranche led by Blackstone, so Anthropic can lease Broadcom chips.

Why it matters: Chip-lease financing at this scale is a new route for funding AI compute.
Dataconomy → (Oct 2)
11

Akamai signs $11.6B, seven-year compute deal with Anthropic

Finance

Anthropic will use Akamai Cloud for CPU workloads, with potential expansion of up to $9B more. Akamai estimates about $5.5B in related capital spending and issued Anthropic a warrant for up to roughly 5% of its stock; service starts in the second half of 2027.

Why it matters: Frontier AI labs are now contracting capacity from distributed cloud providers, not just hyperscalers.
DatacenterDynamics → (Sept 24)
12

Michigan regulators approve DTE contracts for Google's 1 GW Van Buren Township data center

EnergyPolicy

The Michigan Public Service Commission unanimously approved 20-year contracts with an 80% minimum billing demand, and Google must fund 1,600 MW of renewables and 480 MW of battery storage.

Why it matters: A gigawatt-scale precedent for making a hyperscaler fund its own clean power and guarantee its bill.
Planet Detroit → (Oct)
13

Arizona commission approves 954-acre Grande Valley campus powered by on-site Bloom fuel cells

EnergyTechPolicy

Casa Grande's Planning and Zoning Commission voted 6-1 for the project, which plans no grid connection and will use natural-gas solid oxide fuel cells from Bloom Energy, with water capped at 300 acre-feet a year. No City Council vote is required, and residents objected at the hearing.

Why it matters: Off-grid fuel-cell power is moving from pilot to campus scale, while local approval processes draw fire.
KJZZ → (Oct 2)
14

Aligned breaks ground on 2 GW, $10B Project Phoenix campus in Shippingport, Pennsylvania

ConstructionEnergy

The campus on a former coal plant site will use dedicated on-site power rather than the grid and create about 3,000 construction and 640 permanent jobs.

Why it matters: Behind-the-meter power is becoming standard for gigawatt campuses.
DatacenterDynamics → (Sept 10)
15

Abbott orders Texas to halt all data centre permits pending ERCOT and water audits

PolicyEnergy

Governor Greg Abbott directed the TCEQ to stop acting on every data centre permit until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board finish their audits, on top of the earlier interconnection pause. TCEQ must report on compliance by Oct. 19, and the governor signalled legislation to end financial incentives. Separately, the PUCT adopted rules requiring 75 MW-plus projects to post roughly $50 million in security.

Why it matters: Texas, the top destination for new capacity, now has power, water and environmental gates all closed until audits finish, with ERCOT targeting a Dec. 10 completion.
Texas Governor's Office → (Sept 21)
16

Flex pays $4.4 billion for EPC Power to capture 800V DC and grid-forming technology

TechFinance

Flex is acquiring EPC Power, which specializes in 800V DC technology and grid-forming systems that deliver higher power with lower electrical losses. Coverage notes power conversion is moving from a supporting component to a strategic control point.

Why it matters: Power architecture is becoming as contested as chips as AI racks head toward megawatt densities.
Data Center Knowledge → (Sept 4)
17

Amazon pledges $1B for data centre communities and ends NDAs with local governments

Policy

Amazon will invest an additional $1 billion over five years in education, job training, energy affordability and water projects. AWS CEO Matt Garman said the U.S. "can't afford to lose" the AI race; CBS reports grassroots efforts have blocked at least 45 projects worth $68 billion.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are paying for social licence as opposition spreads.
CBS News → (Oct 2)
18

Canada's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles gain 19 more signatories, reaching 42

CanadaPolicy

Launched Sept. 3 with 23 signatories including AWS, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Meta, the five-point framework covers local benefits, ratepayer protection, water and environmental limits, transparency and strategic value to Canada. On Sept. 22 Ottawa added 19 signatories, among them AMD, Intel, IBM and NVIDIA.

Why it matters: A voluntary national standard sets the terms for hyperscale entry into Canada alongside provincial and Indigenous processes.
Canada.ca → (Sept 3 launch; Sept 22 update)
19

Calgarians protest at city hall for stricter regulation of AI data centres

CanadaPolicy

A protest at Calgary City Hall on Saturday, October 3 was part of rallies across North America calling for stricter regulation of AI data centres.

Why it matters: Opposition is reaching the Alberta heartland of Canada's data centre boom.
CTV News Calgary → (Oct 3)
20

B.C. Greens propose moratorium on AI data centres until a public-interest audit is done

CanadaPolicy

Leader Emily Lowan announced a pause on construction and operation of AI data centres until an audit and regulatory framework are in place, citing stranded-asset risk and the impact on B.C.'s hydro system.

Why it matters: Hydro-rich provinces are now debating whether to ration power for AI.
Victoria News → (Oct 2)
