Data Centre Monthly Overview

Monthly Overview — October 2026

October 1–31, 2026 · North America focus · compiled from 31 daily and 4 weekly reports
Month in review: October was defined by the race for firm power. Google and Amazon signed large nuclear deals with Constellation, X-energy and Valar floated SMR campuses, Venture Global lined up up to 11.5 GW of gas turbines, and Fervo opened the first commercial enhanced geothermal plant. Technology shifted toward 800 V DC power, megawatt-class cooling and custom silicon such as Microsoft's Maia 300, with Nvidia's DSX Pod promising 14-day deployments. In Canada, Bell (1.2 GW), Meta (C$13B in Alberta) and Anthropic's EverWind lease (Nova Scotia) set the pace, while Hydro-Québec's planned rate doubling and Alberta's full large-load allowance showed the limits. Capital commitments were enormous, led by Anthropic's roughly $417B of disclosed compute obligations and Meta's $97.5B equity plan, though weak IPOs hinted at strain. Regulators pushed back with Oregon's permit pause, FERC's rejection of PJM's plan and a growing list of local moratoria.

Firm power is the deal currency: nuclear for baseload, gas for speed

October's biggest announcements were about electrons, not buildings. Nuclear deals centred on existing fleets: Google signed a 20-year, 890 MW uprate agreement with Constellation across 11 PJM reactors (about 3.6 GW with a separate 2,700 MW supply deal) and Amazon signed a 20-year Calvert Cliffs agreement (690 MW including 190 MW of uprates, per most reports). Oracle may subscribe to 10–20% of Point Beach output. New-build nuclear stayed at study or proposal stage: X-energy and AEP are studying a 960 MW, 12-reactor plant in Indiana for Amazon, and Valar Atomics proposed a 456-reactor campus of roughly 9.4–9.6 GW in Utah.

Gas moved fastest: Venture Global lined up frame agreements for up to 11.5 GW of turbines, NRG aligned on a 1.2 GW plant, Enerflex won over 1 GW of behind-the-meter orders, and Homer City (about 4.5 GW), Taylor (1 GW) and Pecos (1.5 GW) campuses plan dedicated gas. Yet turbines are reportedly sold out through 2029, and fuel cells (Project Jupiter, Grande Valley) and geothermal (Fervo's Cape Station, 396 MW to Google) filled gaps.

Technology direction: higher density, 800 V DC and silicon diversification

Racks keep climbing in power, and the supporting stack is being redesigned around them. The OCP Global Summit showed 800 Vdc sidecars, 2.5–3.2 MW coolant distribution units and a draft solid-state transformer spec for units up to 10 MW; Google said it plans 800 VDC within about two years; Vantage reserved over 1 GW of solid-state transformer capacity; Flex agreed to buy EPC Power for $4.4B. Nvidia's DSX Pod promises deployment in as little as 14 days, and Supermicro began shipping Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with 1.8 MW cooling units.

Compute supply is diversifying: Microsoft unveiled Maia 300 (claiming 40% more tokens per megawatt), Oracle will offer Google TPUs in 2027, and HPE launched AMD Venice servers. Power swings of hundreds of megawatts in milliseconds drove interest in supercapacitors and rack-level storage.

Canada: gas-backed megaprojects, Atlantic entry and a Quebec rate fight

Canadian news was dominated by scale. Bell's Saskatchewan campus quadrupled to as much as 1.2 GW (over $50B), with 900 MW of added load on Bell-built natural gas. Meta broke ground on a C$13B, 1 GW Sturgeon County campus backed by the 932 MW Greenlight gas plant. Anthropic signed its first Canadian lease at EverWind's C$10B Point Tupper campus (95 MW first phase, 420 MW planned). Together these three exceed C$73B of announced investment.

Friction followed: Hydro-Québec proposed roughly doubling large data centre rates to about 13 cents/kWh, Alberta's 1,200 MW interim allowance is fully subscribed against about 19.6 GW of requests, NB Power holds 10 applications for up to 900 MW, and an Angus Reid poll found 64% of Canadians want provinces to limit large data centres. Ottawa launched voluntary Responsible Data Centre Development Principles (23 signatories, no enforcement) and flagged a C$1.5–1.85B, 60 MW secure facility for the CSE.

Capital: record commitments, with financing strain appearing at the edges

Headline numbers were enormous. Anthropic's S-1 disclosed about $417B of compute commitments, banks lined up $60B to finance its Broadcom chips, and Meta planned a $97.5B equity raise with $4B from Nvidia and AMD. Hyperscale capex was reported above $700B for the year, and ConstructConnect counted $98.5B of data centre construction spending year to date through August. Vertiv's Q3 sales rose 61%.

Warning signs accompanied the boom: Elice fell 32% on its IPO debut, Firmus pulled a $5B IPO, Microsoft exited Nscale's UK site over power delays, Crusoe cancelled a $1.25B turbine order, and BlackRock's Larry Fink warned a construction halt could trigger a recession.

Regulation: ratepayer protection rises while moratoria spread

  • Federal and grid: FERC rejected the core of PJM's bring-your-own-capacity and curtail-first plan; NERC reliability standards for loads of 50 MW or more cleared stakeholders; DOE selected $1.9B for 31 grid projects; the House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417-3 but the Senate stalled it.
  • State and local pauses: Oregon paused air and water permits for new data centres until July 2027 (Umatilla declared an emergency); Memphis chose a 135-day pause; Cleveland extended its freeze to January 29, 2027; Loudoun County paused applications for up to 12 months; Warren County, Ohio enacted six months; the Cheyenne and Arapaho Tribes voted for a three-year moratorium.
  • Cost allocation: large-load tariffs advanced in North Carolina, Texas and elsewhere, and Michigan approved 80% minimum-billing contracts for Google.
  • Politics: data centres drew $86.5M in mostly negative midterm ads.
Why it matters: The policy question has shifted from whether to build to who pays and how fast.
