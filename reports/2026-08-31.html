Nvidia guarantees $105B for OpenAI's Ohio data centre, backs 9.2GW gas plant

EnergyFinanceTech

OpenAI signed a 20-year lease for its "Ports-Pike" data centre campus on former Department of Energy land near Piketon, Ohio, with Nvidia guaranteeing up to $105 billion in conditional lease and power payment obligations to developer SB Energy and investing $1.5 billion directly in the company as its exclusive chip supplier for the site. SB Energy will build a 9.2-gigawatt on-site natural gas power plant to feed the campus, with Japan committing $33 billion to the gas project and initial capacity due online in 2028.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest financing guarantees ever extended by a chipmaker for a single data centre site, and the scale of the accompanying gas plant shows how far hyperscalers and their suppliers will go to lock in dedicated power ahead of grid capacity.

AWS and Nvidia to deploy 2 million additional GPUs by 2028

TechFinance

AWS and Nvidia announced plans to deploy 2 million additional Nvidia GPUs — including Blackwell Ultra, Rubin and Rubin Ultra chips — across AWS infrastructure in 2027–2028, expanding on a prior commitment of more than 1 million GPUs that demand consumed faster than either company expected. The expanded partnership also covers new AI factories, CPUs, networking and robotics work, including a 100,000-GPU buildout for US government infrastructure.

Why it matters: Reordering at this scale before the previous commitment was even fully delivered is a stark signal that AI compute demand keeps outrunning even hyperscalers' most aggressive capacity plans.

TerraPower signs Hyundai as builder for up to 8 Natrium reactors feeding Meta data centres

EnergyTech

Bill Gates-backed TerraPower signed framework agreements with HD Hyundai and Hyundai Engineering & Construction under which HDEC will serve as engineering, procurement and construction contractor for up to eight of TerraPower's 345-megawatt Natrium reactors, with completion, price and performance guarantees meant to unlock conventional commercial financing. The reactors will be built in the US and will supply power to Meta's data centres, while a related term sheet with SK Innovation aims to bring Natrium technology to South Korea.

Why it matters: Repeatable, guaranteed-price construction contracts are exactly what advanced nuclear needs to move from one-off demonstration projects to a fleet that can keep pace with AI-driven power demand.

Quantinuum and Oracle to put a 98-qubit quantum computer inside an OCI data centre

Tech

Quantinuum and Oracle announced a multi-year partnership to deploy Quantinuum's 98-qubit Helios trapped-ion quantum computer inside a US-based Oracle Cloud Infrastructure data centre, offered as an OCI service for hybrid quantum-AI workloads. It marks Quantinuum's first on-premises deployment inside a major public cloud provider's AI data centre footprint, with a developer preview expected in the coming months.

Why it matters: Housing a full quantum computer physically alongside AI compute — rather than accessing it remotely — is a first step toward quantum and AI infrastructure being provisioned as a single stack inside the data centre.
Oracle → (Aug 11)

PJM formally asks FERC to make data centres bring their own power or face curtailment

EnergyPolicy

PJM Interconnection, the largest US grid operator serving 65 million people, formally submitted a framework to FERC requiring new "Large Loads" — data centres chief among them — to either bring their own generation or accept a new Interim Resource Adequacy Service that subjects them to curtailment ahead of emergency load shedding during capacity shortages. PJM has requested an October 12, 2026 effective date, with the rules applying to load added after June 1, 2027.

Why it matters: It's the most concrete move yet by a major US grid operator to formally shift the burden of new power supply onto data centre developers rather than existing ratepayers.
Data Center Knowledge → (Aug 13)

Data centres pin their hopes on small modular reactors as gas turbines back up

Energy

With gas turbine orders backlogged for years and states like Texas pausing new data centre approvals over energy and water strain, developers and utilities are increasingly banking on small modular reactors as a way around the bottleneck — even though no US SMR has yet begun commercial operation. The piece notes that ENTRA1's proposed 6-gigawatt SMR fleet with the Tennessee Valley Authority would be the largest ever attempted if built.

Why it matters: The rhetoric-to-reality gap is still wide — betting a data centre buildout on reactor designs that haven't been commercially proven anywhere in the US carries real timeline and financing risk.
Inside Climate News → (Aug 30)

Google reveals it's behind 506-acre Project Clydesdale in Tulsa County, Oklahoma

ConstructionTech

Google confirmed it is the end user behind Project Clydesdale, a 506-acre, roughly $1 billion data centre campus developed by Beale Infrastructure near the Cherokee Industrial Park in Tulsa County, Oklahoma. Google is funding 1.5 miles of new water-line infrastructure, while Beale is funding a new on-site substation to be powered by Public Service Company of Oklahoma.

Why it matters: It's another example of hyperscalers unmasking previously anonymous "project code name" developments only once construction is well underway, a pattern that has fed local trust concerns in other data centre fights this year.

Amazon raises 2026 AI capex to $220B, citing soaring memory prices

FinanceTech

Amazon lifted its 2026 capital spending target by $20 billion to roughly $220 billion, with CEO Andy Jassy pointing to soaring DRAM and HBM memory prices — driven by manufacturers reallocating capacity to AI accelerators — as the main driver. Amazon said it still won't have enough computing capacity to meet demand through 2027 even at the higher spending level, against a cloud order backlog that has grown 2.5x over the past year to $496 billion.

Why it matters: Memory, not just GPUs, has become a binding constraint on the AI buildout, and Amazon's willingness to keep raising capex despite it shows hyperscalers see no near-term relief.
Fierce Network → (Aug 24)

Canada: Alberta minister jeered at second AI data centre town hall in Redwater

CanadaPolicy

Alberta Technology Minister Nate Glubish faced jeers, heckling and boos for a second consecutive night, this time at a town hall in Redwater, as residents pressed the government over transparency and consultation on the wave of AI data centre projects planned for the province, including Meta's proposed 1-gigawatt Sturgeon County campus. The government has launched an online information hub on data centres alongside its town hall series.

Why it matters: Alberta has positioned itself as Canada's most aggressive courter of hyperscale data centre investment, and the backlash at these town halls suggests that strategy is running well ahead of local buy-in.

Canada: Saskatchewan requires new data centres to be Canadian-owned and bring their own power

CanadaEnergyPolicy

Saskatchewan unveiled a new data centre framework requiring all future projects to be Canadian-owned and to generate their own power rather than draw on SaskPower's grid or displace interprovincial export capacity; a previously approved Bell project will be the last allowed to use utility power. More than 30 new data centre applications are already in the queue to be assessed under the new rules.

Why it matters: It's one of the most restrictive province-level data centre frameworks in Canada yet, combining a Canadian-ownership test with a hard bring-your-own-power requirement in a single policy.
Canada's National Observer → (Aug 27)

Data centre backlash reaches the ballot box: at least 18 officials lost elections

Policy

A Data Center Knowledge investigation found at least 18 incumbent US officeholders lost elections between August 2025 and August 2026 after taking action on, or becoming associated with, a data centre fight — including four Festus, Missouri city council members who lost despite splitting their votes on a $6 billion project. The report frames data centres as an emerging referendum issue in this year's midterms.

Why it matters: When votes on both sides of a data centre fight cost incumbents their seats, it signals the politics have shifted from a local nuisance issue to genuine electoral risk that developers and their political allies can no longer ignore.

Microsoft moves to reassure employees over data centre environmental impact

PolicyTech

Microsoft's data centre chief Noelle Walsh told employees in an internal message that facilities must be built "with discipline, transparency and a relentless focus on reducing impact," after staff raised questions about the energy, water and emissions tied to the company's AI buildout. Walsh acknowledged AI has made prior emissions commitments harder to hit — Microsoft's emissions rose 25% in 2025 — while pointing to hardware efficiency gains and renewable energy plans as offsets.

Why it matters: Public and regulatory pressure on data centres has now visibly reached inside the hyperscalers themselves, with employees pressing their own employer for answers on the same questions communities are raising outside the gate.

Constellation completes Calpine-related divestitures with $860M sale of Texas gas plant

FinanceEnergy

LS Power agreed to acquire Constellation Energy's 606-megawatt Brazos Valley Energy Center, a natural gas-fired plant in ERCOT, for $860 million, marking the final divestiture required by regulators as a condition of Constellation's $16.4 billion acquisition of Calpine. The deal is expected to close by the end of 2026 pending Department of Justice and other approvals.

Why it matters: It clears the last regulatory hurdle on one of the largest power-sector mergers of the year, consolidating a generation fleet increasingly valued for its ability to serve data centre load.
