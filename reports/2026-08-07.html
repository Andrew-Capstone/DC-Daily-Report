1

AMD to acquire Toronto chip startup Taalas, which etches AI models directly into silicon

TechCanada

AMD agreed to acquire Taalas, a Toronto-founded startup that bakes model weights directly into silicon rather than running them on general-purpose accelerators, a process it says can boost inference performance by an order of magnitude or more. Taalas raised $169 million in February on roughly $219 million in total funding; the deal is subject to regulatory approval and expected to close in the fourth quarter.

Why it matters: It's the clearest sign yet that chipmakers see hardwired, model-specific silicon as the next lever for cutting inference costs, echoing Nvidia's $20 billion Groq licensing deal and putting a Canadian-founded startup at the center of that shift.

2

NRG nears $3.2 billion, 1.2-gigawatt gas plant deal with unnamed hyperscaler in Texas

EnergyFinance

NRG Energy said it has aligned on principal commercial terms with a "global cloud and AI hyperscaler" for a 1.2-gigawatt combined-cycle gas plant in Texas, expected online in late 2029 with room to double to 2.4 gigawatts. The $3.2 billion "bring your own power" project is backed by a 15-year, investment-grade contract with a parent-company guarantee, and NRG's CEO said it isn't threatened by the state's new interconnection pause.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest dedicated gas-to-data-centre deals struck since Texas began auditing its interconnection queue, showing utilities can still move fast when customers fund their own generation rather than waiting on the grid.

3

Anthropic confirms it is building an in-house chip design team for Claude

Tech

Anthropic said it is assembling a dedicated team, anchored by recent hire Clive Chan, to design custom AI chips co-developed alongside Claude models, targeting roughly a 50% cut in per-token inference costs. The company gave no timeline for results and didn't say whether it plans to handle manufacturing itself, and continues to rely on AWS, Google, Nvidia and AMD hardware in the meantime.

Why it matters: It puts Anthropic alongside OpenAI, Google, Amazon and Meta in pursuing custom silicon, a sign that every major AI lab now sees chip co-design as essential to controlling inference economics at scale.

4

Canada's Electrovaya launches high-power battery platform for AI data-centre demand spikes

CanadaEnergyTech

Mississauga, Ontario-based Electrovaya launched the ElvaPulse 1500, a containerized 1500VDC battery system delivering up to 7 megawatts of continuous power and 2.88 megawatt-hours of capacity, able to discharge its full rating in under 30 minutes. It's designed to absorb the uneven, bursty power draw AI workloads put on data-centre facilities, with initial deliveries from its Jamestown, New York plant targeted for Q2 2027.

Why it matters: As grid interconnects and gas turbines both stay bottlenecked, high-power-density storage like this gives operators a way to smooth AI's spiky load without waiting years for new generation, and it's a Canadian company supplying it.

5

Rep. Ro Khanna introduces federal "Data Center Bill of Rights"

Policy

California Democrat Ro Khanna introduced a House resolution that would bar data centres within 2,500 feet of homes, schools, childcare centres, hospitals and nursing homes, protect local governments' authority to reject projects without state override, and call for water-use disclosures, community benefit agreements and no added utility costs for households. It's a resolution rather than binding law, but it lays out a federal framework as pressure builds from the other direction to exempt data centres from environmental review.

Why it matters: It's the most prominent federal counterweight yet to the wave of state and local moratoriums, and signals data-centre siting fights are becoming a national political issue rather than a purely local one.

6

"Today's GPUs suck," says former Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger

Tech

Speaking at the Ai4 2026 conference, Gelsinger, now a general partner at Playground Global, argued current GPUs are power-inefficient and computationally limited, and that AI's future depends on solving constraints in memory, networking, energy availability and economics rather than just deploying more accelerators. He singled out reliance on high-bandwidth memory as inefficient, if currently the best available option.

Why it matters: Coming from the former head of the industry's dominant chipmaker, it's a pointed public argument that the GPU-centric scaling model the entire AI buildout depends on is closer to its limits than headline capex numbers suggest.

7

Redaptive and Recurve launch platform to unlock hidden grid capacity for data centres

EnergyTech

The pairing combines Redaptive's on-site efficiency and distributed-generation deployment with Recurve's Flex software, which measures and verifies demand-flexibility capacity using smart-meter data so utilities can plan around it with confidence. Inside facilities it targets cooling optimization, battery integration and real-time energy management; across the grid it finances efficiency retrofits and demand-response assets.

Why it matters: As new gas turbines and grid interconnects both face multi-year waits, squeezing measurable capacity out of existing infrastructure is emerging as one of the few ways to get data centres online faster without new generation.

8

Indianapolis's full council votes Monday on a data-centre moratorium through 2027

Policy

The City-County Council's Metropolitan Economic Development Committee voted 10-3 along party lines to recommend a moratorium on new data centres through December 31, 2027, sponsored by Council President Maggie Lewis and backed by Mayor Joe Hogsett. The full council takes up the measure at its August 10 meeting after months of resident pressure over water use, energy demand and utility-bill impacts.

Why it matters: Indianapolis would become one of the largest US cities to freeze data-centre development outright, a notable escalation beyond the zoning tweaks and siting reviews most municipalities have tried first.

Oregon Democrats propose three-year moratorium on new large data centres

Policy

State Sens. Jeff Golden and Courtney Neron Misslin and state Reps. Lesly Muñoz and Farrah Chaichi will introduce a three-year data-centre moratorium in the 2027 legislative session, citing energy and water demand, farmland loss and lack of industry transparency. It follows a one-year pause the legislature already placed on a popular data-centre tax incentive in March.

Why it matters: Oregon would join 15 other states that have proposed moratoriums in the past year, though only New York's has actually passed, underscoring how much political energy the issue is generating even where bills stall.

Partners Group takes $1 billion majority stake in data-centre power firm AVK

FinanceEnergy

Swiss private equity firm Partners Group became majority shareholder in AVK, a 36-year-old UK power-solutions provider, in its first-ever outside investment, aimed at scaling AVK's microgrid and backup-power offering for data centres across FLAP-D and emerging markets. AVK has delivered more than 20,000 projects and roughly 3.5 gigawatts of installed power, with a 2-gigawatt pipeline including its first large-scale microgrid at a Dublin data centre.

Why it matters: It's another example of private equity racing to back the physical power infrastructure layer of the AI buildout, not just the data centres themselves, as on-site generation becomes standard rather than a backup measure.

16

Yakima County, Washington, moves toward a six-month data-centre moratorium despite no pending projects

Policy

Yakima County commissioners unanimously directed staff to draft a six-month data-centre moratorium after more than three dozen residents raised concerns about water use during the county's fourth consecutive drought year, even though no formal data-centre application has been filed. The pause would give the county time to study utility capacity, water availability and land-use rules before any proposal arrives.

Why it matters: Pre-emptive moratoriums enacted before a single project is even proposed show how far ahead of actual development local opposition has moved in some agricultural regions.
