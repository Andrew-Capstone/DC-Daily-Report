Morning brief: Canada and technology lead today. Nova Scotia's opposition parties are demanding the legislature be recalled over EverWind's C$10 billion Point Tupper campus leased to Anthropic, while New Brunswick reports 10 applications for up to 900 megawatts. On technology, the OCP summit centred on 800 volt DC power and multi-megawatt cooling, and Lumentum, Astera Labs and Corning showed an error-free optical PCIe link over fibre. In power, Google's 890 megawatt nuclear deal with Constellation and its Fervo geothermal deal show the hunt for firm supply, and Airsys proposed a new metric to reclaim stranded power. Local pushback continues, with Montgomery County, Tennessee, and Pettis County, Missouri acting, and Loudoun and Volusia votes on Oct 20 unconfirmed. Elice's weak IPO hints at cooling investor appetite. Fresh news was moderate, so many stories are one to three weeks old and are dated. EverWind's Point Tupper campus starts at 95 MW in late 2027, with a 325 MW expansion in 2028 and 420 MW at full capacity. Tech

Why it matters: Longer reach could let AI racks spread out, easing power and cooling density, but it is early-stage.

Airsys proposes power compute effectiveness (PCE) metric to reclaim stranded data centre power

TechEnergy

PCE is provisioned compute power divided by total provisioned power. In the paper's examples, cutting cooling from 20 MW to 12 MW frees 8 MW for compute, and liquid-cooled sites could reach the high 0.80s. No validated industry average exists yet.

Why it matters: Where power is the constraint, measuring compute share rather than PUE may expose capacity already built.
