Morgan Stanley leads $15 billion debt package for Google-backed Anthropic AI campus in Texas

FinanceEnergyTech

Morgan Stanley is arranging roughly $15 billion in debt — a $14 billion bridge loan plus a revolving credit facility — for Nexus Data Centers to build a Hubbard, Texas campus for Anthropic, which will include its own 1.6-gigawatt natural-gas power plant. Google is backstopping the deal, taking a roughly 20% equity stake and guaranteeing billions of dollars in lease and power payments if Anthropic defaults on the four data-centre leases involved; the campus will run chips co-designed by Google and Broadcom.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest project-finance deals yet for a single AI lab's dedicated infrastructure, and shows how deep-pocketed cloud providers are underwriting rivals' compute buildouts to keep their chips and platforms central to the AI race.

Crusoe and Aalo Atomics to build the first nuclear-powered AI data centre, at Idaho National Laboratory

TechEnergy

Crusoe will deploy one of its modular "Spark" data centres at Idaho National Laboratory in 2027, powered directly by Aalo's 50-megawatt "Pod" reactor — five 10MW Aalo-1 microreactors feeding a single turbine — as a proof-of-concept for pairing small modular reactors directly with AI compute. If successful, the partners plan to deploy Aalo's reactors across Crusoe's data-centre fleet by the end of 2029.

Why it matters: It's the most concrete timeline yet for actually running an AI data centre directly off a small modular reactor rather than a grid-connected PPA, a milestone the nuclear-for-AI trend has promised for years but not yet delivered.

Amazon raises 2026 capex guidance to $220 billion as AWS backlog hits $496 billion

Finance

Amazon's Q2 2026 results showed AWS revenue of $42.2 billion, up 37% year-over-year — its fastest growth since 2021 — beating estimates, while CEO Andy Jassy raised full-year capital spending guidance to $220 billion, citing higher memory costs and continued AI demand. Management disclosed an AWS backlog of roughly $496 billion and warned capacity constraints will persist into 2027 and 2028.

Why it matters: Amazon was the last of the four hyperscalers to report this earnings season, and its numbers confirm the same story Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta told this week: demand and spending are both still accelerating, with power and capacity — not chips — the binding constraint.

Meta discloses $279 billion in future AI data-centre leases not yet on its balance sheet

Finance

In its Q2 filings, Meta said it has committed to almost $700 billion in future AI-related spending overall, including $279 billion in operating and finance leases for data centres that haven't yet begun and aren't yet reflected on its balance sheet. The company's non-cancelable contractual commitments rose by $107 billion in the quarter alone, driven by new multi-year cloud deals and eight infrastructure purchase agreements.

Why it matters: The scale of off-balance-sheet leasing shows how much of the AI buildout is now financed through long-term lease structures rather than direct capex, a pattern bond investors and regulators are watching closely for hidden risk.

Exelon slashes its data-centre pipeline from 18 gigawatts to 11 amid mounting opposition

EnergyPolicy

Exelon's Q2 earnings presentation showed its "high probability" data-centre pipeline cut nearly in half, to 11GW from 18GW, and its broader pipeline through 2027 cut to 25GW from 43GW. CFO Jeanne Jones said the revision "weeded out speculative projects," now counting only those in advanced design or backed by FERC-approved transmission security agreements, as regional opposition and ratepayer concerns mount.

Why it matters: Exelon is one of the largest US utilities serving PJM territory, so a major utility publicly conceding that much of its data-centre pipeline was speculative is a signal the sector's most-hyped growth numbers may be overstated.

Alberta's AI data-centre buildout runs into a years-long global shortage of gas turbines

CanadaEnergy

Developers of Alberta's roughly $100 billion pipeline of AI data centres — including the $15 billion Meta campus and the newly announced $4.6 billion Greenlight gas plant — are facing turbine wait times of up to five years, with GE Vernova alone sitting on a backlog representing 100 gigawatts of potential capacity. Most new Alberta gas-turbine orders won't arrive before 2030, running straight into the province's mandate that new data centres largely self-supply their own power.

Why it matters: Alberta has staked its data-centre strategy on operators building their own gas generation rather than waiting for grid capacity, and a global turbine shortage undercuts that entire model just as tens of billions of dollars in projects break ground.

7

Antora Energy raises $550 million to scale thermal batteries for AI data centres

TechEnergy

Antora closed an oversubscribed $550 million Series C, co-led by G2 Venture Partners and Eclipse, bringing its total funding to roughly $1 billion. Its thermal batteries store electricity as heat in insulated carbon blocks and discharge it on demand as power or steam, letting data centres and heavy industry buy cheap off-peak power and avoid straining the grid during demand spikes; the funding will support a second US manufacturing hub.

Why it matters: As gas turbines and grid interconnects both become bottlenecks, thermal storage is emerging as one of the few near-term ways operators can smooth AI's spiky power draw without waiting years for new generation.

8

Virginia study finds no location in the state's coastal plain can support a major new data-centre groundwater withdrawal

Policy

A 144-page Virginia DEQ report, ordered by the General Assembly, tested whether a hypothetical facility could obtain a permit to withdraw 3 million gallons of groundwater a day anywhere in the eastern coastal plain; none of the regions tested qualified, with allowable withdrawals ranging from under 30,000 to 360,000 gallons a day. The report recommends the legislature tighten permitting authority and require water-reuse studies before large withdrawals are approved.

Why it matters: Virginia hosts the world's densest concentration of data centres, so a state-commissioned finding that groundwater can't support major new withdrawals adds water — alongside power — to the list of hard constraints on how much more the region can build.

TechPolicy

Why it matters: It opens a new government and defense revenue channel for CoreWeave beyond the AI labs and hyperscalers that dominate its backlog, and reflects a broader push to build classified, dedicated AI infrastructure for national-security use.

Clarksville, Arkansas approves up to $55 billion in bonds to back Serverfarm's $6.6 billion data centre

Finance

The Clarksville City Council approved issuing up to $55 billion in industrial revenue bonds to support a $6.6 billion data centre already under construction for Serverfarm, one of six large data-centre projects announced in Arkansas. Why it matters: The bond authorization size dwarfs the project's own cost, illustrating how aggressively smaller US municipalities are structuring tax incentives to compete for hyperscale investment — and the community friction that can follow when deals move faster than public input.

Veolia to operate a 350MW gas-powered microgrid for an Ohio data-centre campus

Energy

Veolia will design, build and operate a 350-megawatt natural-gas microgrid dedicated to powering a data-centre campus in Ohio, giving the site power independent from the local grid. Why it matters: Behind-the-meter gas generation is becoming a default strategy for data centres trying to avoid multi-year grid interconnection queues, and utilities-turned-operators like Veolia are positioning themselves as long-term power partners for the sector.
