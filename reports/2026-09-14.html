IonQ unveils Superion 256, its sixth-generation quantum computing platform

Tech

IonQ launched Superion 256 at its 2026 Investor Day: a 256-qubit trapped-ion quantum computer built around Electronic Qubit Control, which uses on-chip semiconductor electronics rather than laser systems to control the qubits. The company has already fabricated its first fully integrated 256-qubit processors at its SkyWater subsidiary and is accepting orders now, with customer deliveries beginning in 2027.

Why it matters: IonQ says the shift from laser- to semiconductor-based control could cut cost-per-qubit by more than 300x across its roadmap, a manufacturing breakthrough aimed at making fault-tolerant quantum systems producible at the volumes data-centre-scale deployment would require.

AI data-centre boom is reshaping the real estate bond market, Bloomberg finds

Finance

Data-centre asset-backed and mortgage-backed securities issuance has hit $61 billion year-to-date in 2026, up from roughly $4 billion in 2020, with JPMorgan projecting annual issuance could reach $30-40 billion in 2026 and 2027. Bloomberg reports CMBS buyers are increasingly underwriting risks — power availability, grid constraints, cooling and computing density — that have historically had little to do with commercial real estate, since highly specialized buildings could be costly to repurpose if a hyperscale tenant's lease ends and it walks away.

Why it matters: As debt increasingly funds the AI buildout, the resilience of that financing chain now hinges on bond investors correctly judging tenant credit and future compute demand years out — a real vulnerability if a major hyperscaler's needs shift.
Bloomberg → (Sept 14)

Samsung and SK Hynix's memory inventories fall below 10 days as HBM4 devours DRAM capacity

Tech

Finished DRAM inventory at Samsung and SK Hynix — which together control roughly 64% of global DRAM revenue — fell below 10 days of supply on September 7, a level analysts say is unseen in prior shortage cycles. HBM4 production for Nvidia's next AI-accelerator platform consumes roughly three times the wafer capacity of conventional DRAM, and Samsung alone is now running about 150,000 DRAM wafers a month through its HBM lines, with roughly half dedicated to HBM4.

Why it matters: Up to 70% of memory chips made globally in 2026 are destined for AI data centres; the resulting squeeze is already pushing memory prices sharply higher and threatens to become a hard ceiling on new AI-accelerator deployments through 2027.
Tech Times → (Sept 7)
4

Mississauga set to vote on what would be Canada's first data-centre moratorium bylaw

CanadaPolicy

Mississauga city staff are bringing an Interim Control By-law before council for approval on September 16 that would prohibit new large-scale digital-infrastructure development — AI, cloud and hyperscale data centres — for up to a year, following council's 11-0 vote in July directing staff to prepare it. The pause is meant to give the city time to complete a comprehensive review of impacts on municipal infrastructure, the environment, noise, and energy and water consumption before updating its Official Plan and zoning bylaw.

Why it matters: If approved, it would be the first bylaw of its kind in Canada, a bellwether for how other fast-growing Ontario municipalities weighing similar measures decide to handle data-centre pressure on land and grid capacity.
City of Mississauga → (vote scheduled Sept 16)
5

Gallatin, Tennessee advances 12-month data centre moratorium toward a formal vote

Policy

Gallatin City Council voted unanimously to advance a 12-month moratorium on new data-centre construction, following a 5-2 recommendation from the city's Planning Commission, with a formal council vote expected in the days ahead. The city — already home to a 900-plus-acre Meta data-centre campus — says the pause would let staff write regulations distinguishing hyperscale facilities from small edge and telecom infrastructure.

Why it matters: A community that already hosts a hyperscale campus choosing to pause further development shows how even established data-centre host towns are now demanding clearer rules before approving the next project.
Fox17 → (Sept 8)
6

Miamisburg, Ohio council unanimously approves nine-month data centre moratorium

Policy

Miamisburg City Council voted 6-0 to impose a nine-month moratorium on new data-centre development, a response to community concern over a potential large-scale project proposed for the former DP&L Hutchings Power Station site. City Manager Chris Fine said the move is meant to avoid the city being "caught off guard" by an application it isn't yet prepared to evaluate.

Why it matters: Miamisburg joins roughly 18 municipalities now pausing or restricting data-centre construction, underscoring how quickly local moratoriums have become the default first response to a proposed project.
City of Miamisburg → (Sept 1)
7

Coachella, California becomes second Golden State city to permanently ban data centres

Policy

Coachella's city council adopted Ordinance 1231 on August 26, permanently prohibiting large-scale AI and cloud data centres citywide after resident concerns over water, energy and other environmental impacts. The ban takes effect September 25, with narrow exceptions for small-scale storage, security and telecommunications equipment.

Why it matters: A second California city moving from moratorium straight to a permanent ban — rather than a temporary pause — signals opposition in the state's desert communities is hardening into outright exclusion rather than a wait-and-study posture.
KVCR → (Aug 28; ordinance effective Sept 25)
