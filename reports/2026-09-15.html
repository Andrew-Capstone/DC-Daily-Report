Bell quadruples Saskatchewan AI data centre to 1.2GW in Canada's largest-ever private investment

CanadaEnergyFinance

At Canada's first-ever investment summit on Monday, Bell Canada said it will quadruple the size of its planned Regina-area AI data centre to 1.2 gigawatts, with 300 megawatts sourced from the provincial grid and up to 900 megawatts more delivered by Bell under Saskatchewan's Bring Your Own Power principle. The expansion is expected to draw more than $50 billion in capital investment and create up to 500 permanent jobs plus roughly 3,000 additional positions in security, logistics and maintenance.

Why it matters: Prime Minister Mark Carney called it "the largest sovereign AI infrastructure network in Canada" — a striking marker of how aggressively federal and provincial governments are courting hyperscale capacity even as Canadian cities elsewhere move to pause it.

2

Meta tests robot arms to plug cables and reset servers inside its data centres

Tech

Meta has spent roughly a year testing two-armed robots from ABB, Kinova and Watney Robotics at its Altoona, Iowa campus, working to automate tasks like hot-swapping network cables and power-cycling server racks under human supervision. One staffer told reporters a Kinova arm could eventually take over up to 80% of a facility's routine workload, though today's robots are far from that efficient yet.

Why it matters: As AI-driven construction outpaces the skilled labor available to staff it, robotic maintenance is emerging as a hyperscaler answer to keeping ever-denser server halls running without proportionally growing headcount.
Yahoo Tech → (Sept 2026)

Utah megasite developer unveils "GIGAWATER" tech to make water from data centre exhaust

TechEnergy

Creekstone Energy, developer of the planned 10-gigawatt Delta Gigasite campus in Utah's Millard County, announced patent-pending "GIGAWATER" technology that captures water vapor from its own natural-gas power-generation exhaust and condenses it into usable water. The company's Project Oasis initiative projects roughly 4,000 acre-feet of water a year — more than the campus itself is expected to consume — though the technology remains years from large-scale deployment.

Why it matters: Water availability has become one of the sharpest flashpoints for local opposition to gigawatt-scale data centres in arid states; a credible net-positive water technology could defuse one of the industry's biggest community objections, if it works at scale.
The Salt Lake Tribune → (Sept 9)
4

EIA: US electricity use to hit record highs in 2026 and 2027 as AI demand surges

Energy

The Energy Information Administration's latest Short-Term Energy Outlook projects US power consumption climbing from a record 4,195 billion kilowatt-hours in 2025 to 4,270 billion in 2026 and 4,349 billion in 2027, driven chiefly by AI and crypto data centres plus broader electrification. Commercial-sector electricity sales are set to surpass residential demand for the first time in 2026, at roughly 1,550 billion kilowatt-hours.

Why it matters: It's the clearest federal confirmation yet that data-centre load growth, not efficiency gains, is now the dominant force reshaping US electricity demand, with natural gas holding steady at 40% of generation through 2027 even as renewables grow.
EnergyNow.com → (Sept 9)

ERCOT to survey 461 Texas data centres as part of Abbott's grid audit

PolicyEnergy

Texas grid operator ERCOT will launch a roughly 130-question survey of 461 data centres — 247 "large" projects requesting 75+ megawatts and 214 "medium" projects requesting 25-75 megawatts — covering financial assistance, power self-sufficiency, water and cooling, and community impact, with responses due by mid-October. The survey implements Governor Greg Abbott's August order for a comprehensive audit of the roughly 474 gigawatts of data-centre interconnection requests currently sitting in ERCOT's queue.

Why it matters: With data centres representing about 90% of new load requests on a grid already stretched thin, the audit's December findings could reshape how, and how fast, Texas lets new AI capacity connect.
KERA News → (Sept 14)
6

Michigan environmentalists press a stalled legislature for a data centre moratorium

Policy

Advocacy groups rallied at the Michigan Capitol demanding a one-year statewide moratorium plus stronger water-use, NDA and community-benefit rules for data centres, as competing Democratic bills have sat untouched in committee since June. Governor Gretchen Whitmer has said she won't sign a moratorium but supports stricter regulation instead.

Why it matters: Michigan's stalemate previews the fight playing out in most statehouses: environmental and community groups pushing for a hard pause while governors and industry favor regulate-in-place approaches.
Michigan Advance → (Sept 14)
7

San Francisco supervisors vote today on a 45-day data centre moratorium

Policy

San Francisco's Board of Supervisors is scheduled to vote September 15 on a 45-day freeze on new data-centre approvals, aimed at projects like NOVVA's Bayview expansion, after residents raised concerns about noise and grid strain. The vote lands the same week Hayward's council is separately weighing a full ban referral over a 76.6-megawatt facility approved with 28 backup diesel generators.

Why it matters: A moratorium vote in a major West Coast tech hub, rather than a smaller exurb, would mark a significant escalation in how far local opposition to data centres has spread.
Hoodline → (Sept 15)
8

Richmond, California considers an urgency 45-day data centre moratorium

Policy

Richmond's City Council meets Tuesday, September 15 to consider an urgency ordinance imposing a 45-day moratorium on new data-centre development, joining a wave of Bay Area municipalities acting on the issue this same week. The proposal would pause new applications while the city studies its own rules.

Why it matters: Richmond's move shows the moratorium wave has spread beyond marquee cities to industrial East Bay communities weighing their own grid and land-use exposure.
Contra Costa News → (Sept 14)
9

Southport, North Carolina enacts 15-month data centre moratorium

Policy

Southport's board of aldermen voted unanimously to impose a 15-month moratorium on new data centres while the town rewrites its unified development ordinance to address the sector. The pause gives officials time to set standards before any new applications can move forward.

Why it matters: A unanimous vote in a small coastal town underscores how uncontroversial pausing new data-centre applications has become for local governments still writing their first rules for the industry.
Port City Daily → (vote Sept 11; reported Sept 14)
10

Beaufort County, South Carolina postpones vote on proposed data centre moratorium

Policy

A proposed one-year moratorium on new data-centre development in Beaufort County was postponed rather than voted on this week, with no new date immediately set. The delay leaves the county's stance on pending and future data-centre proposals unresolved for now.

Why it matters: Not every moratorium push is landing cleanly — Beaufort's postponement is a reminder that local political will for an outright pause isn't uniform even amid the broader national wave.
