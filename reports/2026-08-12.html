PJM proposes landmark "bring your own power" rules for large data centers

EnergyPolicy

PJM Interconnection, the largest US grid operator covering 13 mid-Atlantic and Midwest states, filed a plan requiring data centers of 50MW or larger to secure their own new power generation or face curtailment during grid emergencies. A companion proposal would pay up to $20 billion to build new plants through a backstop capacity auction running September 30–October 21, aimed at closing a 6.8GW shortfall.

Why it matters: It's the most consequential grid-operator policy shift of the year, forcing every large data center across a 13-state territory to rethink how it procures power.

2

SEC exempts data center financing from key securitization disclosure rules

FinancePolicy

The SEC clarified that securities issued through data-center financing arrangements don't count as asset-backed securities, exempting a major category of data-center debt from disclosure and risk-retention requirements. Data-center-tied ABS issuance has grown from $2.4 billion in 2020 to $15.5 billion in 2025.

Why it matters: It's a federal deregulatory move that makes it cheaper and easier for operators to raise the debt fueling the AI buildout, just as investors are growing warier of AI-linked bonds.

Koch explores $15B+ sale of waterless-cooling data center developer Edged

FinanceConstruction

Koch Inc. is exploring a sale of Edged, the waterless-cooling data center platform it co-founded, in a deal that could value the company above $15 billion; Goldman Sachs is running the process and has received multiple bids. Edged operates and develops facilities across Missouri, Arizona, Texas, Georgia, Iowa, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Illinois.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest data-center M&A valuations floated this year, underscoring investor appetite for platform-scale AI infrastructure companies.

Beltline Energy withdraws $16 billion Pacific, Missouri data center after backlash

PolicyConstruction

Beltline Energy withdrew its zoning application for a proposed $16 billion, 500-acre data center campus in Franklin County, Missouri after residents packed public meetings to oppose it. The fight now shifts to the Franklin County Commission, which can still approve the project outside city limits.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single data center projects killed by local opposition so far this year.

5

NV Energy sues data center developer Tract over who pays for grid infrastructure

EnergyPolicy

NV Energy sued developer Tract, alleging it tried to bypass Nevada's Public Utilities Commission process via private arbitration to set power-delivery terms for its 810MW Peru Shelf and 1.215GW South Valley projects, which the utility says would shift infrastructure costs onto ratepayers. Tract counters it has committed nearly $1 billion to support NV Energy's grid buildout.

Why it matters: It's a first-of-its-kind legal fight over who foots the bill for AI-driven grid expansion, a dispute other utilities are watching closely.

Massachusetts judge blocks diesel generator expansion at state's largest data center

EnergyPolicy

A judge halted installation of four new diesel generators at the Markley Group data center in Lowell — Massachusetts' largest — while a legal challenge from neighbors over air-quality concerns proceeds.

Why it matters: It's a fresh example of community and legal pushback specifically targeting fossil-fuel backup power at data centers, not just the facilities themselves.

7

Canada: Alberta protest movement grows as province announces data centre town halls

CanadaPolicy

A University of Alberta student is organizing renewed protests against AI data centres, including at government town halls the province is holding to address water, electricity and environmental concerns — the first set for August 19 in Ponoka. Critics say the consultations come too late since approvals, including Meta's $13 billion Sturgeon County facility, have already been granted.

Why it matters: It shows Alberta's data centre boom colliding with organized public opposition even as the province markets itself as Canada's most welcoming jurisdiction for AI infrastructure.

8

Indianapolis council votes 23-1 to advance data center moratorium through 2027

PolicyConstruction

The Indianapolis City-County Council voted 23-1 to pause new data center permitting in Marion County through the end of 2027, sending the measure to the Metropolitan Development Commission for possible final approval as soon as August 19. New standards include a 400-foot industrial buffer and a 65-decibel noise limit; three already-approved projects are exempt.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest US cities to formally advance a multi-year data center moratorium.

FinanceTech

Why it matters: It's consolidation among the data providers investors and lenders increasingly rely on to underwrite AI infrastructure deals.

Digital Core REIT sheds North American data center stakes to fund Asia expansion

Finance

Digital Core REIT agreed to sell its stakes in three North American properties — including sites in Toronto and Northern Virginia — back to Digital Realty for roughly $316 million, using the proceeds to buy into data centers in Singapore and Osaka, Japan. The deal is expected to improve leverage and lift distributions per unit by about 4.1%.

Why it matters: It's a sign capital is starting to rotate out of some North American data center assets and into faster-growing Asian markets.
