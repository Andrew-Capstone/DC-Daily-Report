Morning brief: The US Department of Energy has selected 31 grid projects worth 1.9 billion dollars in federal funding, aimed at adding more than 23 gigawatts of transfer capacity for data centres and other large loads, though the awards are not yet final. Policy pressure continues, with Loudoun County pausing new applications and Finland ordering Google to halt site work over permitting. DOE selects $1.9B for 31 grid projects meant to unlock 23 GW of transfer capacity for data centres

EnergyPolicy

DOE's SPARK initiative would fund 31 projects in 26 states, with $3.35B of recipient cost-share bringing total value to $5.25B. Work covers upgrades on more than 1,500 miles of lines. These are selections, not final awards; agreements are due between October 2026 and January 2027, and TechRadar notes the money is largely repackaged infrastructure-law funding.

Why it matters: Moving more power over existing corridors is the fastest lever for connecting large loads.

Bell to quadruple Saskatchewan AI data centre to 1.2 GW, with investment above $50B

CanadaEnergyFinance

Announced at the Canada Investment Summit with the Prime Minister and Premier Moe, the plan keeps the original 300 MW grid supply near Regina and adds up to 900 MW that Bell provides under Saskatchewan's Bring Your Own Power principle. Total investment is cited at more than $50B, with up to 500 permanent jobs.

Why it matters: It is Canada's largest announced sovereign AI infrastructure project.

Power swings from AI workloads force chip-to-grid design changes

TechEnergy

Panelists at Data Center World Power warned AI load can rise and fall by hundreds of megawatts in seconds, faster than turbines can respond. Nvidia cited rack power of about 150 kW for Blackwell and 240 kW targeted for Vera Rubin, up to 1 MW with 800 V DC, and is adding rack-level storage to smooth swings.

Why it matters: Load volatility is now a grid-reliability issue, not just a capacity one.

McKinsey: data centre power demand to grow 24% a year to 2030, with supply short of compute demand

Energy

McKinsey's Global Energy Perspective 2026 says its most likely scenarios all have power supply below IT compute demand through 2030. More than 60% of operators combine on-site generation with grid access, and gas is seen as the fast path to firm power despite pipeline competition.

Why it matters: Access to power, not capital, sets the pace of the build-out.

Finland orders Google to suspend site work at two planned data centres over permitting

Policy

Regulators found large-scale clearing at Muhos and Kajaani began before environmental impact assessments finished, and ordered work stopped by Oct 23. Fingrid says grid capacity was not the constraint. Google says it acted in good faith and has until Oct 14 to respond.

Why it matters: A non-North American case, but it shows permitting sequence can halt hyperscaler projects regardless of power.
