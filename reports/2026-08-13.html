NextEra and Brookfield to build $100B AI campus at former Kentucky nuclear site

EnergyTechConstruction

The US Department of Energy selected Brookfield to lease land at the shuttered Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant and NextEra Energy to build and own on-site power for a more than $100 billion AI data centre and power complex, with up to 2GW of new gas-fired generation and 2.6GW of battery storage. The campus is expected to support 1.8GW of utility capacity and 1.2GW of computing capacity once fully built in 2032, with initial operations beginning in 2028. (Story dated July 29–30, 2026; included as the most recent well-sourced coverage of a still-developing project.)

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single AI infrastructure commitments announced this year and a template for pairing new gas and battery generation directly with a Cold War-era federal site.

2

North American data centre demand hits record 25GW absorbed in H1 2026

Finance

JLL reported that North America absorbed 25GW of data centre capacity in the first half of 2026, doubling year-over-year and up fivefold from two years ago, as hyperscalers, neoclouds and AI companies compete for scarce space. Vacancy held at just 1% for a third straight year even as hyperscalers now account for 59% of 2026 tenant demand and 28GW of owner-occupied capacity is under construction.

Why it matters: It's the clearest market-wide confirmation yet that supply still can't catch up with AI-driven demand, keeping pressure on power and land nationwide.

3

Canada: AMD acquires Toronto AI-chip startup Taalas to challenge Nvidia on inference

CanadaTech

AMD signed a definitive agreement to acquire Taalas, a Toronto-based startup founded in 2023 that hardwires trained AI models directly into silicon rather than using general-purpose chips, cutting compute and memory bottlenecks. AMD plans to integrate the approach into its Instinct GPU and Helios rack-scale roadmap; financial terms were not disclosed.

Why it matters: It's a Canadian-founded technology at the centre of a major US chipmaker's push to cut AI inference's power draw, one of the industry's biggest technical and energy bottlenecks.

4

Meta expands Louisiana's Hyperion campus to 5GW, investment tops $50 billion

EnergyConstruction

Meta is scaling its Hyperion AI supercluster in Richland Parish, Louisiana from 2GW to 5GW across 3,200 acres, pushing its investment above $50 billion — up from $10 billion when first announced in December 2024. The first 2GW is expected online by 2030, with full build-out around 2032 and completion by 2036. (Story dated July 13, 2026.)

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single AI infrastructure buildouts in the US and a case study in how fast hyperscaler power and capital commitments are escalating.

Amazon's "Project Eagle" near Houston grows to 10 buildings, tops $3 billion

Construction

Filings with Texas regulators show the AWS-linked "Project Eagle" campus in Boling, Wharton County has expanded from four to ten data centre buildings plus a water support facility, pushing the estimated construction cost above $3 billion. Six additional buildings were filed August 5, with the latest update published August 11.

Why it matters: It shows hyperscaler campuses continuing to scale up in real time even as Texas's broader interconnection pipeline sits under Governor Abbott's audit freeze.

6

OpenAI details $20B, 3.2GW "Project Camellia" campus in Georgia

TechEnergy

OpenAI outlined plans for a privately funded $20 billion, four-building, 3.2GW data centre campus at the Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub in Effingham County, Georgia. Georgia Power will deliver power in phases from 2028 through 2032, OpenAI says it will fully fund the required grid infrastructure, and the project includes an $80 million community investment package. (Story dated July 22, 2026.)

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single AI campus commitments to date and a test case for OpenAI's promise that existing ratepayers won't subsidize its power buildout.

7

Arista's chip purchase commitments triple to $9.7B on AI networking push

TechFinance

Arista Networks said its multiyear purchase commitments for chips and AI components reached $9.7 billion at the end of Q2, up from $3.6 billion a year earlier, as it tightens supply chains to expand beyond Nvidia-based networking into scale-up and scale-out gear for alternative accelerators, including Google's TPUs.

Why it matters: It signals networking vendors are now building dedicated supply chains around non-Nvidia AI accelerators, a structural shift in how AI data centres get wired together.

8

Six US grid operators face August 17 FERC deadline on data-centre interconnection rules

EnergyPolicy

Under a June 18 show-cause order, FERC gave PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO New England and NYISO 60 days — until August 17 — to justify their existing large-load interconnection tariffs or file revisions addressing how they'll study and integrate data centres and other big new loads onto the grid.

Why it matters: It's a hard regulatory deadline hitting nearly every major US grid operator at once, forcing a coordinated national response to data-centre-driven interconnection queues.

9

Fort Worth council unanimously begins 90-day data centre moratorium process

Policy

Fort Worth's City Council voted unanimously on August 11 to start the legal process toward a 90-day data centre moratorium, with the pause potentially taking effect February 16, 2027. Companion votes created an 11-member Data Center & Infrastructure Committee and now require developers to prove state utility approvals before applying.

Why it matters: It's a major Texas market moving toward formal restrictions even as the state overall remains one of the most data-centre-friendly in the country.

10

Developer sues over Colorado River water for California's largest data centre

PolicyEnergy

Imperial Valley Computer Manufacturing sued the Imperial Irrigation District after it denied the company's request for 287 million gallons of water annually to cool a proposed 330MW data centre, which would be California's largest. The Colorado River supplies 40 million people across seven states and is already under severe strain.

Why it matters: It's a concrete legal test of whether AI data centres can claim scarce Western water rights over agricultural and municipal users.

11

Mercer County, Kentucky passes moratorium with a city-sized annexation loophole

Policy

Mercer County's Fiscal Court unanimously passed a one-year moratorium on new data centres in unincorporated areas, but the pause won't apply if the small city of Burgin annexes county land as planned — land where developer Panattoni has pitched "Project Bluegrass," a hyperscale data centre. Burgin has no zoning rules to restrict such a project.

Why it matters: It shows how easily local annexation can route around a county-level moratorium, a loophole other Kentucky jurisdictions are watching closely.

12

DC BLOX sues Nashville Metro over data centre moratorium near the zoo

Policy

Atlanta-based DC BLOX filed a federal lawsuit against Nashville's Metro government on August 11, calling its July 21 data-centre permit moratorium a "targeted attack" on the company's planned facility next to the Nashville Zoo. The suit alleges violations of due process, equal protection and constitutional takings protections.

Why it matters: It's part of a growing wave of developers suing local governments directly over moratoriums, a new legal front in the data-centre siting fight.

Policy
