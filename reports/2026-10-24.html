Morning brief: Power and AI capital dominate. Amazon is funding a study of a 960 megawatt small modular reactor plant in Indiana with AEP and X-energy, Microsoft will finance 1,100 megawatts of Indiana generation under a 20-year NiSource deal, and Google expanded its flexible-capacity contract with Voltus to more than 1 gigawatt in PJM. X-energy and AEP to study 960 MW SMR plant in Indiana to power Amazon

Energy

A joint development agreement covers feasibility work on 12 Xe-100 small modular reactors, grouped in three sets of four, at the retired Breed Generating Station in Sullivan County, Indiana. Amazon would fund the work in milestones and buy the power under a long-term contract if the plant is built; no cost or construction date is published.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are underwriting new-build nuclear on retired coal sites, an option on capacity for the 2030s.

Nvidia unveils DSX POD, a prebuilt AI data centre module

Tech

The containerized module packs seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with networking, cabling and cooling pre-installed. Nvidia says it can cut deployment at a prepared site from about six months to as little as 14 days, with 17 partners including Dell, HPE, Schneider Electric and Vertiv and availability planned for early 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built AI pods could compress build timelines when power and sites, not hardware, are the bottleneck.

Anthropic signs first Canadian lease at EverWind's C$10B Nova Scotia campus

EnergyCanada

Anthropic signed a lease of at least 20 years for the 95 MW first phase at EverWind's Point Tupper site, with phase one starting in late 2027 and a roughly 420 MW full build-out. Why it matters: It anchors Canada's largest AI campus on renewable power and gives a frontier AI lab its first Canadian footprint.

Microsoft to fund 1,100 MW of Indiana generation under 20-year NiSource deal

Energy

Microsoft will pay for about 1,100 MW of new generation and transmission and buy the output for 20 years to serve its La Porte campus, with service starting in 2028. NiSource projects about $1.75 billion in savings for customers, and a separate 600 MW / 2,400 MWh battery agreement depends on contracts closing by January 31, 2027.

Why it matters: The 'bring your own power' model, sold on ratepayer savings, is becoming the template for large-load deals.

Google expands Voltus flexible-capacity deal to more than 1 GW in PJM

Energy

Google will buy 1,070 MW of additional flexible capacity from Voltus, lifting the total above 1 GW on a contract running through 2040. Voltus aggregates batteries, thermostats and industrial processes into a virtual power plant, and PJM demand response revenue rose 240% from 2024 to 2025.

Why it matters: Demand flexibility is emerging as a faster route to grid capacity than building new generation.

NERC stakeholders approve reliability standards for AI data centres and crypto miners

Policy

The standards would apply to computational loads of 50 MW or more connected at 100 kV or higher, covering interconnection studies, modeling data and disturbance monitoring. They follow a May Level 3 alert after data centres unexpectedly dropped load, and now go to NERC's Board of Trustees, expected in Q4, before FERC filing.

Why it matters: Large-load ride-through and reporting rules will shape interconnection for every new campus.

Lumentum, Astera Labs and Corning demo optical PCIe at rack scale

Tech

At the OCP Global Summit in San Jose the companies showed error-free PCIe 5 links over fibre in a rack-representative system, using multiple 32 GB/s channels. Optical links can span tens of meters versus a few meters for passive copper, though Lumentum calls it a proof of concept needing further integration and qualification work.

Why it matters: Longer-reach links could enable multi-rack scale-up and disaggregated AI designs.

New PCE metric aims to recover stranded data centre power

Tech

Power Compute Effectiveness divides provisioned compute power by total provisioned power, complementing PUE. A paper cited in the piece measured 0.705 at a 20 MW colo and 0.79 at a 100 MW campus; liquid-cooled sites could reach the high 0.80s, though no validated industry average exists yet.

Why it matters: Reclaiming cooling overhead as compute capacity can add AI megawatts without a new utility request.
