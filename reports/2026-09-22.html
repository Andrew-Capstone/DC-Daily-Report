ABB launches Infinitus, the first source-to-rack DC power portfolio built for gigawatt-scale AI data centres

TechEnergy

ABB unveiled Infinitus, a direct-current power portfolio spanning medium-voltage powertrains, DC sources, distribution, protection and cooling optimization, all running at 800 volts DC instead of conventional AC. The system builds on ABB's Nvidia collaboration first announced in October 2025 and targets next-generation AI racks drawing up to 1 megawatt each, claiming over 5% energy-efficiency gains; ABB says a 500MW facility could gain 25MW of extra computing capacity worth $300 million-plus in annual revenue.

Why it matters: Replacing AC with native DC power distribution from grid to rack is a genuine architectural shift, not an incremental upgrade, and a sign the industry is redesigning core electrical infrastructure around AI's exploding power density.

AMD crosses $1 trillion market cap for the first time as AI chip demand rally extends

TechFinance

AMD shares hit an intraday record of $613.92 on September 21, pushing its market capitalization past $1 trillion and making it the fourth U.S. semiconductor company — after Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron — to reach that milestone. The stock has climbed more than 180% this year, capping a five-day, roughly 24% winning streak driven by AI and data-centre chip demand as the broader semiconductor rally pushed the Nasdaq to its first record close since June.

Why it matters: A second merchant chipmaker joining the trillion-dollar club shows the AI data-centre buildout is now minting value well beyond Nvidia, widening the field of companies whose fortunes are tied directly to hyperscaler capex.

Nebius hikes on-demand GPU cloud prices as AI compute capacity stays tight

TechFinance

Nebius told customers it will raise prices for on-demand Nvidia H100, H200, B200 and B300 cloud instances effective October 1, along with certain CPU and memory resources — the company's second round of increases in three months. Nebius shares rose more than 4% and Nvidia gained on the news, as the move underscored how little slack remains in the GPU rental market even as new capacity comes online.

Why it matters: Neoclouds raising prices rather than cutting them to win share is a clear signal that AI compute demand is still outrunning supply, reinforcing the case for continued data-centre capex despite financing jitters elsewhere in the sector.

Generac locks in deal worth up to $8 billion to supply Amazon with data-centre backup generators

EnergyFinance

Generac signed a long-term agreement with Amazon covering $2.4 billion of generator deliveries in 2027-2028, with total purchases through 2033 potentially reaching $8 billion; Amazon received a warrant for up to 1.69 million Generac shares at $200.93 that vests as purchases are made. Generac's stock surged roughly 20% on the disclosure, which revealed Amazon as the previously unnamed hyperscale customer behind a deal first flagged in June.

Why it matters: Warrant-linked, multi-year supply contracts are becoming the template for how hyperscalers lock in scarce backup-power equipment, and the scale here shows how central diesel and gas generator capacity remains to data-centre reliability even amid the shift toward grid and renewable power.

5

Gov. Abbott orders Texas regulators to pause all data centre environmental permits statewide

EnergyPolicy

Governor Greg Abbott directed the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality to halt approval of air and water permits tied to data centre construction until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board complete audits of electricity consumption, water usage, community impacts and tax-incentive use. "Data centers must pay their own way, protect our grid and water and complete the ERCOT and TWDB audits," Abbott said, broadening an earlier grid-connection moratorium to cover behind-the-meter projects that had bypassed grid-based oversight.

Why it matters: Texas has been the epicenter of the U.S. data-centre boom, so a governor-ordered statewide freeze on environmental permits — not just grid interconnections — is the most sweeping regulatory action yet and could stall billions in projects until audits conclude.
The Texas Tribune → (Sept 21)
6

BC Hydro set to notify winners of Canada's first competitive power-allocation contest for AI data centres

CanadaEnergy

BC Hydro is finishing its review of a competitive process that drew 15 applications requesting nearly 800 megawatts of capacity, with successful projects — set to share a 400-megawatt allocation over the first two years — expected to be notified this month. Selection criteria weigh price alongside economic, community, data-sovereignty and environmental factors, with projects using heat recovery and other efficiency measures favoured.

Why it matters: British Columbia is the first Canadian province to force AI and data-centre projects to compete for scarce clean power rather than granting it on a first-come basis, a model other provinces facing similar grid strain are likely to watch closely.

7

Virginia's Spanberger signs Executive Order 22, the country's most aggressive data centre accountability framework

Policy

Governor Abigail Spanberger's order bans executive-branch non-disclosure agreements on data centre projects, eliminates by-right approval and fast-track permitting for facilities over 25 megawatts, ends state site-development subsidies, and requires large loads to cover their share of PJM interconnection costs. It also sets new water-use, noise and emissions standards and directs agencies to review diesel backup generators for cleaner alternatives. "Virginia's Data Center Accountability Framework is about delivering a better deal for Virginians and holding data center companies accountable," Spanberger said.

Why it matters: Virginia hosts the world's largest concentration of data centres, so a governor-led rollback of fast-track approvals and NDAs there sets a template other states are likely to copy as community pushback mounts nationwide.

$18 billion of loans backing Oracle's Project Jupiter data centre trade at steep discount amid delays

Finance

Loans financing Oracle's 1,400-acre Project Jupiter campus in Doña Ana County, New Mexico are quoted at 89-91 cents on the dollar, implying roughly $1.6-2 billion in losses the market has already priced in. The project — tied to Oracle's AI-capacity deal with OpenAI — is about seven months behind schedule after New Mexico rejected a gas-pipeline permit and local opposition mounted over water and air quality, prompting Oracle to redesign the site around Bloom Energy fuel cells; S&P downgraded Oracle's credit rating to BBB- in July.

Why it matters: A marquee Stargate-linked project's debt trading at a stressed discount is a concrete credit-market signal that the AI infrastructure financing boom is starting to face real skepticism, not just headline construction delays.

Finance

SoftBank Group priced $10 billion in dollar-denominated notes and €1 billion in euro notes across maturities from 3.5 to 7.5 years, with proceeds funding a $10 billion payment due October 1 on its $30 billion, three-tranche OpenAI investment commitment. Crusoe raises $3.9 billion Series F at a $30.9 billion valuation for data centres and modular "AI factories"

FinanceTech

The round was co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with Founders Fund, GIC, Nvidia, Qatar Investment Authority, Radical Ventures and TPG also participating — just ten months after Crusoe's $1.38 billion Series E. Funds will back existing projects including its Abilene, Texas campus serving OpenAI, plus development of modular, transportable "Spark" data centres that can be sited wherever power is available; CEO Chase Lochmiller said the goal is "controlling the infrastructure from electrons to tokens."

Why it matters: A more than 20x valuation jump in under a year for a vertically-integrated power-to-compute builder shows investors are still willing to pay up for infrastructure that can move faster than traditional gigawatt-campus timelines.

Finance

US House passes Ratepayer Protection Act 417-3, pushing states to make data centres cover their own grid costs

Policy

The bipartisan bill directs states to consider standards requiring large loads over 100 megawatts to provide financial assurances before grid upgrades begin, cover the full incremental cost of new generation, transmission and distribution infrastructure, and pay exit fees if they end service early. States would have up to two years to decide whether to adopt the standards, and analysts note the bill largely reinforces a trend already underway, since only 13 states currently lack large-load tariffs for data centres.

Why it matters: A near-unanimous 417-3 House vote signals rare bipartisan consensus that ratepayers shouldn't subsidize data-centre power costs, even though the bill's Senate prospects before the midterms remain uncertain.
Utility Dive → (Sept 16)

NPR: data centre backlash is reshaping American politics, cutting across party lines nationwide

Policy

A new Economist/YouGov poll of nearly 1,600 adults found roughly two-thirds of Americans oppose new data centre construction in their communities, with fights playing out in city halls and county commissions from coast to coast. Brookings' Darrell West said data centres have "upended politics as we know it," while the report notes Loudoun County, Virginia alone collected about $1.2 billion in data-centre property tax revenue last fiscal year — nearly 39% of its budget — even as secretive NDA-covered deals fuel public suspicion.

Why it matters: Two-thirds public opposition and cross-partisan local revolts show data centres have become a durable political fault line rather than a passing controversy, which is exactly the pressure now producing state actions like Virginia's and Texas's this week.
