Morning brief: With yesterday's edition already covering the week's biggest finance and grid-policy stories, today's news skews toward technology and local policy. Underwater and seawater cooling emerge as a fix for data centres' freshwater problem

TechCanada

A Canadian construction-industry feature examines a Shanghai project that sealed roughly 2,000 AI servers into units submerged 35 metres off the coast, using passive seawater flow instead of chillers or evaporative cooling. Marine engineers quoted call ocean and lake water an "essentially unlimited" cooling source that isn't consumed the way municipal freshwater is, with the debate explicitly framed against ongoing water-stress fights in the US Southwest, Georgia, Northern Virginia and Alberta.

Why it matters: Freshwater use is one of the most potent community objections to new campuses; if submerged or shoreline cooling designs prove viable at scale in North America, they could defuse a major line of local opposition without requiring more electricity, unlike Nvidia's hot-water closed-loop approach.

2

Nevada regulators rule today on data centre developer's bid for two private gas plants

EnergyPolicy

Fleet, a Tract Capital Management-backed data centre developer, is asking Nevada's Public Utilities Commission to approve a temporary 144MW plant and a 218MW "Peru Ridge Self-Generation" plant — over 360MW combined — to power its Tahoe Reno Industrial Center campuses for two to three years until NV Energy can deliver permanent grid service. A PUCN decision is expected September 8. Opponents argue the gas plants would be the first of their kind approved specifically for data centre use in the state and could conflict with Nevada's 50%-by-2030 renewable portfolio standard.

Why it matters: It's a live test of how far "bring your own power" data centres can go around state clean-energy mandates, with a regulatory ruling landing the same day this report publishes.

AI data centres are reshaping rural land markets — and fuelling backlash

FinancePolicy

CNBC reports that power-ready land in key data centre markets has surged in price this year — in the most constrained submarkets like Ashburn, Virginia, powered parcels now exceed $3.5 million per acre — as developers race to secure sites ahead of the grid capacity to serve them. The rapid repricing of farmland and rural parcels is drawing new resentment from residents who see speculative land buys but few immediate local benefits.

Why it matters: Land speculation ahead of confirmed power access is becoming a fresh flashpoint layered on top of existing water and noise complaints, and is increasingly cited by local officials weighing moratoriums.

Up to half of planned US data centres may face delays, investment firm warns

FinancePolicy

Kimmeridge Energy Management estimates that as much as half of proposed US data centre capacity is at risk of delay or cancellation, citing mounting political backlash alongside the practical difficulty of securing power, land, water and permits fast enough to match announced timelines.

Why it matters: It's a sober counterweight to the steady drumbeat of gigawatt-scale announcements, suggesting investors should treat headline capacity figures as aspirational rather than committed.

Hidden China risks are emerging in America's AI data centre supply chain

PolicyTech

CNBC reports growing unease in Washington and among data centre operators over Chinese-made components — from transformers to networking gear — embedded deep in the US AI buildout's supply chain, as the administration weighs how aggressively to "de-risk" infrastructure before more Chinese-origin hardware becomes locked in.

Why it matters: Supply-chain exposure could become the next front in US-China tech tension, with national-security reviews potentially slowing procurement for an industry already racing against power and permitting constraints.

Northampton Capital and Provident Data Centers form JV for 54MW Dallas-area campus

ConstructionFinance

The newly formed joint venture will develop an AI-ready, water-free-cooled data centre on a 74-acre site in the North Dallas Corridor, targeting delivery in late 2027 in one of the most capacity-constrained metros in the country. The partners also agreed to a broader framework for additional inference-focused sites across other US markets.

Why it matters: It's another example of specialized private developers pairing with capital partners to move fast in supply-constrained Sun Belt markets, betting on inference (rather than training) demand as the next growth wave.

AI jobs boom shows up in construction and infrastructure hiring data

ConstructionFinance

New hiring data shows electricians, installation crews and other skilled trades now account for a rising share of data centre-related job postings, with US industry group ABC estimating nearly half a million new construction workers will be needed in 2027, up from about 349,000 in 2026, largely to build AI infrastructure.

Why it matters: It's concrete evidence the AI buildout is translating into real blue-collar labor demand, a data point that could reshape the political debate around data centres beyond just utility bills and water use.

8

Hyperscale Data ends Bitcoin mining at Michigan site to make way for AI compute

FinanceConstruction

Hyperscale Data shut down all crypto mining at its 617,000-square-foot, 340MW-capable Michigan campus effective September 1, signing a master services agreement with an unnamed California neocloud provider for an initial 20MW of AI compute. The deal is projected to generate over $1.2 billion in revenue over its 10-year term, with expansion options that could push it past $3 billion; the company has sold most of its Bitcoin holdings to fund the transition.

Why it matters: It's one of the clearest examples yet of a crypto miner going all-in on AI infrastructure rather than hedging between the two businesses, reflecting how much more valuable the site's power capacity is to AI tenants than to mining.

9

Elkhart, Indiana city council unanimously passes data centre and battery-storage moratorium

Policy

The Elkhart City Council voted 9-0 to pause new data centre and battery energy storage applications through December 31, 2027, after dozens of residents spoke in favor of a halt, citing concerns over the city's water supply and electricity capacity. Council members said any future development must first demonstrate it can protect both resources.

Why it matters: A unanimous vote in a mid-sized Midwest city underscores how broad and bipartisan the local moratorium movement has become, adding to a growing national tally that now numbers well over 100 municipalities.

10

Durham County, NC adopts nine-month pause on large data centres

Policy

Durham County commissioners voted 4-1 for a moratorium running until May 18, 2027 (or until new zoning rules are adopted), barring new hyperscale facilities while exempting smaller data centres under 100,000 square feet that use closed-loop cooling and skip diesel backup generators. Residents cited water and energy use, noise, and air-quality concerns; the lone dissenting commissioner objected to the small-facility carve-out.

Why it matters: It aligns Durham County with the City of Durham's own pause, giving one of North Carolina's fastest-growing data centre markets a unified, temporary block while it drafts permanent rules.
