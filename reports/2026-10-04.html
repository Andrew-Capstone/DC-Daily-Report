Banks line up $60B debt package to finance Anthropic's Broadcom AI chips

FinanceTech

Banks are raising $60 billion, including a $42 billion senior-secured tranche that lets Anthropic lease Broadcom chips, plus an $18 billion junior tranche led by Blackstone, which is putting in $9 billion itself. Anthropic is expected to be Broadcom's largest customer next year.

Why it matters: Chip-lease financing at this scale is a new route for funding AI compute, and a challenge to Nvidia's own financing push.

2

We Energies strikes deal to power Oracle data center with Point Beach nuclear energy

Energy

Oracle would subscribe to 10% to 20% of Point Beach Nuclear Plant's output for its Project Lighthouse campus in Port Washington, Wisconsin, co-developed with OpenAI. The utility says the arrangement could spare other customers about $300 million in fuel costs over five years; Wisconsin regulators must still approve it.

Why it matters: A nuclear subscription pitched as a ratepayer saver is a template other utilities may copy.
Wisconsin Watch → (Oct 2)
3

Amazon signs 20-year nuclear PPA with Constellation for Maryland's Calvert Cliffs

Energy

The deal covers the 1,790 MW plant and enables more than $3 billion in upgrades, including about 190 MW of new carbon-free capacity from uprates in 2030-2032. The retail supply agreement covers Amazon operations across 13 states in PJM.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers keep locking up existing nuclear baseload while funding added capacity.
EnergyTech → (Oct 1)
4

Michigan regulators approve DTE contracts for Google's 1 GW Van Buren Township data center

EnergyPolicy

The Michigan Public Service Commission unanimously approved 20-year contracts with an 80% minimum billing demand, and Google must fund 1,600 MW of renewables and 480 MW of battery storage. Service is expected in December 2027; the Attorney General had pushed for a 90% minimum.

Why it matters: A gigawatt-scale precedent for making a hyperscaler fund its own clean power and guarantee its bill.
Planet Detroit → (Oct)
5

Meta's $13B+ Alberta data centre pairs with a 932 MW gas plant and a Capital Power supply deal

CanadaEnergyConstruction

Meta's Sturgeon County campus is Alberta's largest tech infrastructure investment, with operations expected in the second half of 2030. The 932 MW Greenlight Electricity Centre, backed by Pembina, Morgan Stanley Infrastructure Partners and Kineticor, supplies power behind the fence, and Capital Power commits 250 MW from the second half of 2028.

Why it matters: Alberta's bring-your-own-generation model, with about 1.2 GW of spare grid capacity, is becoming Canada's template for gigawatt AI campuses.
Calgary.Tech → (Oct 1)
6

Bell marks construction milestone at Saskatchewan AI data centre as project grows to 1.2 GW

CanadaConstruction

Structural steel is rising on the Sherwood-area campus, with 500+ workers on site and operations expected in the first half of 2027. SaskPower supplies the first 300 MW and Bell will build a natural gas plant for another 900 MW; the province calls it the largest private investment in Canadian history at full build-out.

Why it matters: Canada's flagship sovereign-AI campus is moving from announcement to steel.

EnergyCanada

Calgary-based Enerflex will design, engineer and assemble about 450 MW of behind-the-meter gas generation units in its North American plants, with deliveries from 2027 to 2028.

Why it matters: On-site gas is a growing answer for developers that cannot wait for grid interconnection.

8

Google details grid-interactive AI data centers and a move to 800 VDC power

TechEnergy

Google is using batteries to peak-shave and replace diesel at gigawatt campuses, deploying 54 VDC rack-level power now and moving to 800 VDC within a couple of years. Google, Meta, Microsoft and Nvidia have jointly defined utility interface specifications.

Why it matters: Data centres are being redesigned from grid burden to grid partner.

9

Can supercapacitors smooth AI's power swings?

Tech

AI load spikes can exceed 150% of rated capacity; supercapacitors respond in milliseconds but sustain output only 2 to 10 minutes. IDTechEx projects the data centre segment reaching $950 million by 2037, still at an early pilot stage.

Why it matters: They add a fast layer alongside UPS and batteries rather than replacing them.
Data Center Knowledge → (Sept 28 roundup)

Canada launches Responsible Data Centre Development Principles backed by 23 industry groups

CanadaPolicy

The five principles cover local benefits, ratepayer protection, water and environmental impact, transparency and strategic value to Canada. Signatories include AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Bell and TELUS. Announced Sept 3, so older than 24 hours.

Why it matters: A national baseline for how hyperscale projects will be judged by communities and regulators.
