1

AMD's Helios rack-scale AI system enters full production, ships to Microsoft Azure

Tech

AMD's Helios — an integrated rack combining sixth-generation Epyc "Venice" CPUs, 72 Instinct MI455X GPUs, Pensando networking and ROCm software — is now in full production, with AMD confirming Microsoft Azure as a customer and first shipments to hardware partners due by the end of Q3 2026. The rack packs 31TB of HBM4 memory with 1.4 petabytes/second of aggregate bandwidth, delivering up to 2.9 FP4 exaflops for inference.

Why it matters: It's AMD's most serious single-vendor challenge yet to Nvidia's Vera Rubin/NVL72 in the data centre, giving hyperscalers a real second source for rack-scale AI infrastructure just as GPU supply remains the industry's tightest bottleneck.

2

TSMC's Arizona megaproject grows to $265 billion as AI chip demand accelerates

Tech

TSMC has committed another $100 billion to its Arizona campus, bringing total investment to $265 billion across ten fabs, two advanced-packaging facilities and an R&D center, with new capacity targeting 2-nanometer (and below) chips for GPUs, CPUs, networking silicon and custom AI accelerators. TSMC simultaneously raised its 2026 capital spending guidance to $60-64 billion, citing sustained AI-driven demand rather than federal policy as the catalyst.

Why it matters: It's the clearest signal yet that the chip supply underpinning every hyperscale AI data centre build in North America is racing to keep pace with demand rather than the other way around.

3

Georgia Power extends PSC deadline to August 26 on contract to power OpenAI's $20B campus

EnergyPolicy

Georgia Power pushed back the Public Service Commission's deadline to rule on its roughly 100-page electrical-service contract for OpenAI's 3.2-gigawatt Project Camellia campus in Effingham County to August 26, after commissioners and consumer advocates pressed for a longer review period and firmer proof that residential customers won't end up subsidizing the project. Georgia Power has previously struggled to document that earlier large-load rate protections were actually being enforced.

Why it matters: As the single-largest electricity customer Georgia has ever signed, how this contract is written will set the template regulators nationwide use to decide whether AI data centres — not ratepayers — bear the cost of the power they consume.

4

Canada: Nearly 9,000 sign petition against Sherbrooke, Quebec's proposed 96MW AI campus

CanadaPolicy

Keel Infrastructure — the successor to Bitfarms — won Sherbrooke city approval on July 15 to consolidate 96 megawatts of existing capacity from three former Bitcoin-mining sites (The Bunker, Leger and Garlock) into a single AI data centre campus targeting 2028 readiness. Nearly 9,000 residents have since signed a petition accusing city hall of pushing the project through with little transparency, after the city withheld development studies as confidential.

Why it matters: It's the latest and largest Quebec flashpoint over converting crypto-mining power allocations into AI capacity without new public environmental review, adding to a rapidly growing list of Canadian municipalities facing organized resident opposition this summer.

5

Canada: Toronto residents say council's request for a provincial data centre framework falls short

CanadaPolicy

Toronto city council voted 25-0 on July 31 to ask Ontario to build a province-wide data centre framework by Q1 2027, require local councillors be notified of new applications, and review the city's own approval policy — but stopped short of pausing two contested projects already in the pipeline in Scarborough and Etobicoke. Residents and advocacy group Common Horizon say the motion is too weak and are still pushing for an outright moratorium.

Why it matters: Toronto is Canada's largest city and a bellwether for how bigger urban councils balance data centre investment against resident backlash — its choice to seek provincial guidance rather than act unilaterally could shape how other large Canadian cities respond.

6

Amazon's $10B "Project Eagle" campus near Houston hits regulatory headwinds

PolicyConstructionEnergy

Amazon's proposed 10-building, nearly 3,000-acre data centre campus in Wharton County, Texas, is facing delays tied to Governor Greg Abbott's statewide audit of new data centre grid connections, which has effectively paused fresh interconnections pending review. Wharton County has also signed a non-disclosure agreement with an Amazon-linked consulting firm, limiting what local officials can disclose about the project's specifics.

Why it matters: It shows Texas's own grid-connection crackdown — meant to protect reliability — now snagging the state's marquee hyperscale projects, a preview of the friction other fast-growing data centre states are likely to hit as they tighten oversight.

Finance

Goldman Sachs is gauging investor demand for a roughly $1.15 billion high-yield bond that would fund construction of a data centre leased to AI cloud provider CoreWeave, the latest in a wave of single-tenant, lease-backed debt deals financing the AI buildout.

Why it matters: Lease-backed bonds tied to a single AI tenant are becoming one of the primary ways Wall Street funds new data centre construction — their pricing and demand are a live read on how much credit risk investors think AI infrastructure actually carries.

11

Fort Worth begins legal process for a 90-day data centre moratorium

Policy

Fort Worth's city council voted unanimously on August 11 to start the formal process for a 90-day data centre construction pause, with a possible effective date of February 16, 2027, and also voted 10-1 to create a standing Data Center and Infrastructure Commission to study noise, water and land-use impacts.

Why it matters: Fort Worth is one of the largest US cities to move toward a formal moratorium, and its months-long procedural runway shows how even business-friendly Texas cities are now building permanent regulatory infrastructure around data centres rather than reacting case by case.

Louisville Metro Council approves six-month data centre moratorium, 24-1

Policy

Louisville Metro Council voted 24-1 on August 13 to bar planners from approving new data centre proposals for six months, or until council adopts comprehensive zoning rules, following weeks of committee debate and a competing proposal that drew its own controversy.

Why it matters: A near-unanimous vote in a major Midwestern metro adds Louisville to a fast-growing list of US cities pausing data centre approvals outright rather than simply studying the issue.

Oregon Democratic lawmakers propose three-year moratorium on new large data centres

Policy

Democratic state lawmakers introduced a bill that would bar new large data centres in Oregon for three years, joining roughly 15 other states that have proposed similar pauses in the past year — with only New York's one-year moratorium on data centres over 20 megawatts actually passing so far.

Why it matters: A statewide legislative moratorium proposal — rather than a city-by-city patchwork — signals the backlash is climbing to a higher, harder-to-route-around level of government in more states.

14

Texas overtakes Virginia in under-construction data centre capacity despite new rules

Finance

A new JLL analysis finds Texas has passed Northern Virginia in gigawatt capacity currently under construction, with about 6.5 gigawatts underway — roughly a fifth of the 35 gigawatts the US added to its development pipeline last year — even as the state layers on fresh grid-connection audits and local moratoriums. Virginia still leads in total operating capacity.

Why it matters: It marks a symbolic shift in the center of gravity for US data centre construction from the long-dominant Northern Virginia corridor toward Texas's cheaper land, faster permitting and more abundant (if now more contested) power access.
