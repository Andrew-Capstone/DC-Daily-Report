Google expands Voltus virtual power plant deal to more than 1 GW in PJM

EnergyTech

Google will buy 1,070 MW of additional flexible capacity from Voltus in the PJM region, on top of 100 MW committed in June. Voltus pools batteries, thermostats and industrial load into a virtual power plant, with Google funding it under a contract running through 2040 and full ramp-up over three to four years.

Why it matters: Data centre developers are paying to create grid flexibility instead of waiting for new generation.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 23))
2

AEP and X-energy study 960 MW SMR plant to power Amazon in Indiana

Energy

X-energy and AEP will study an advanced nuclear plant at a retired coal station in Indiana, with Amazon funding development. The earlier Utility Dive report describes 12 Xe-100 reactors of about 80 MW each at the Breed Generating Station and no disclosed timeline.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are backing new-build nuclear on retired coal sites to secure baseload power.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 20))
3

Anthropic signs first Canadian data centre lease at EverWind's Nova Scotia campus

EnergyCanada

The lease covers a 95 MW first phase at Point Tupper, with a further 325 MW planned for 2028. EverWind plans more than C$10 billion in total, including C$2 billion to expand onshore wind, and says the campus will include battery storage and curtail grid draw at peak times. Timelines are developer projections.

Why it matters: It anchors one of Canada's largest AI campuses and ties data centre growth to wind power.
Source → (Data Centre Magazine (Oct 16-19))
4

Nvidia unveils DSX POD, a prefabricated Vera Rubin AI data centre module

Tech

The containerized module packs seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with networking and liquid cooling pre-integrated, and Nvidia says deployment at a prepared site falls from about six months to as little as 14 days. It uses dry cooling, and 17 partners including Dell, HPE, Schneider Electric and Vertiv plan availability in early 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built pods could compress build timelines and cut water use, provided power and sites are ready.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 21))
5

Microsoft to fund 1,100 MW of Indiana generation under 20-year NiSource deal

Energy

Microsoft will pay for all generation and transmission serving its La Porte, Indiana campus under a 20-year agreement with NiSource, with service starting in 2028. A separate 600 MW / 2,400 MWh battery agreement remains conditional until January 2027, and NiSource projects about $1.75 billion in customer savings.

Why it matters: It is a template for large users funding their own grid build-out while protecting ratepayers.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 19))
6

Venture Global lines up up to 11.5 GW of gas turbines for behind-the-meter data centre power

Energy

Venture Global signed frame agreements with GE Vernova and Baker Hughes for up to 16 more 720 MW combined-cycle power islands, targeting up to 10 GW of compute capacity by end-2034. Roughly $17.2 billion is contemplated if all orders proceed, with the first purchase order expected by end-2026.

Why it matters: Gas-fired on-site generation is becoming a core route to AI power.
Source → (DataM Intelligence (Oct 19; deal Oct 15))
7

Entergy adds 440 MW solar PPA for Google's West Memphis expansion

Energy

Entergy Arkansas announced a second phase of its service agreement for Google's West Memphis campus, adding a 440 MW solar PPA, two battery projects and new transmission, all subject to state approval. Google would cover roughly $600 million and Entergy targets a 2028 in-service date after regulators denied the standalone PPA in September.

Why it matters: Shows utilities folding clean generation into data centre contracts under regulatory scrutiny.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 20))
8

Microsoft introduces Maia 300 custom AI chip

Tech

Maia 300 is slated for production in 2027 and is projected to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems. Its predecessor Maia 200 is in production powering Copilot, and Microsoft plans multi-gigawatt Maia deployments in 2027. Maia is not yet offered to Azure customers.

Why it matters: Custom silicon aimed at power efficiency is a response to grid-limited AI growth.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 13))
9

Anthropic and Nscale reportedly in talks for second $45B deal at West Virginia campus

Finance

Nscale is reportedly negotiating to sell Anthropic about $45 billion of compute from a second building at its Monarch campus, providing 460 MW on Nvidia Vera Rubin chips. The deal would nearly double Anthropic's commitments there, and terms are not final.

Why it matters: A deal of this size shows the scale of AI compute commitments now driving campus financing.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 20))
10

Canada plans C$1.5-1.85B, 60 MW secure data centre in Ottawa

CanadaConstruction

Defence Construction Canada issued an advance procurement notice for a 60 MW facility, with procurement expected in winter 2027 and contingent on federal approval. The notice mainly lets bidders obtain security clearances, and the project could still be cancelled.

Why it matters: A sovereign-compute build is a rare public-sector data centre project in Canada.
Source → (W.Media (Oct 16))

PolicyEnergy

Why it matters: It reinforces self-supplied power and community payments as the political price of new campuses.

Policy

The standards cover loads of 50 MW or more connected at 100 kV or higher and still need a NERC Board vote expected in Q4 plus FERC approval. They follow a May alert over data centre load losses.

Why it matters: Ride-through and reporting rules will shape interconnection for every large campus.
