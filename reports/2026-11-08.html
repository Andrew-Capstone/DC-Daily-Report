Sunday, November 8, 2026 · North America focus
Morning brief: Fuel cells and nuclear lead the power story. Google will invest 17 billion dollars in its first Wyoming data centre, backed by a 702 megawatt fuel cell plant, and NTT signed a 900 megawatt fuel cell deal with Bloom Energy. Politics stays hot after voters backed limits in 40 of 41 ballot measures, and Texas governor-elect Hinojosa pledged a pause on approvals. Note: only a few stories are from the last 24 hours; most are carried forward from Oct 27 to Nov 6, with dates shown, and Canadian coverage was thin today.

Google to invest $17B in first Wyoming data centre, backed by a 702 MW fuel cell plant

EnergyConstructionFinance

Project Tembo covers 716 acres near Cheyenne. AEP will build and own a 702 MW Bloom Energy solid-oxide fuel cell plant under a 20-year PPA, with Tallgrass storing captured CO2. Google says it will pay for 100% of its power, uses air cooling, and targets full build-out in 2031.

Why it matters: A hyperscaler is pairing on-site fuel cells with carbon capture instead of waiting for the grid.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 6))
2

DOE offers Constellation up to $5B in conditional loans to uprate nuclear plants serving Google, Amazon and Meta

EnergyFinance

The conditional loan covers uprates and modernization at plants in Illinois, Maryland and Pennsylvania, adding about 1.1 GW. Constellation says its 20-year deals with Google and Amazon prompted more than $7 billion of investment for roughly 1,080 MW. The loan is not final until conditions are met.

Why it matters: Federal financing is speeding up the cheapest new nuclear megawatts, though TechCrunch notes no evidence yet of lower residential prices.
Source → (TechCrunch (Oct 28; carried forward))
3

SpaceX confirms plan for up to 6 GW AI campus in Cochise County, Arizona, as residents push back

ConstructionEnergyPolicy

An affiliate filed for a nine-building, 747-acre campus near Willcox, with phase one around 1.5 GW and up to $50 billion of investment. Power would come from Arizona co-ops using the adjacent Apache Generating Station, plus a 24 GWh battery and 90 MW of gas backup. Hundreds attended a Nov. 4 meeting where the presentation was booed; a hearing is set for Dec. 9.

Why it matters: One of the largest single-site proposals in North America tests water, power-cost and community-consent limits.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 7))
4

AI data centres are fueling a nuclear comeback, with about $68B of new plant spending expected through 2029

EnergyCanada

Industrial Info Resource estimates new nuclear spending of about $20 billion in 2026, rising to nearly $50 billion by 2029, across more than 100 North American projects including 61 SMRs. Ontario and Alberta together account for roughly $40 billion of the tracked spend. NRC reviews have reportedly been cut to about 18 months.

Why it matters: Canada sits at the centre of the nuclear build-out, but fuel, cost and workforce bottlenecks remain.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 5))

Ottawa to invest up to C$1.6B in Cohere, which pledges C$1.7B for Canadian compute by 2030

CanadaFinance

The staged equity stake was announced Oct. 29 alongside Nvidia, Bell and Schwarz Group. Cohere committed at least C$1.7 billion for computing infrastructure in Canada by 2030 and 1,000 technical jobs; it later said its Canadian effort would exceed C$4 billion through 2031. Cohere's chief AI officer said funds will go to building data centres and partnering with compute providers.

Why it matters: Canada's flagship AI company is tied to domestic data centre build-out, with federal money attached.
Source → (CP24 (Oct 29; carried forward))
6

Crusoe debuts Spark Liquid, a 3.5 MW factory-built liquid-cooled modular unit

TechConstruction

The sixth-generation modular platform supports Nvidia GB300 and Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with closed-loop direct liquid cooling and no evaporative loss. Crusoe claims five times the density of earlier Spark generations. Production at its Brighton, Colorado factory joins the existing line in 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built capacity eases construction delays, though sites still need power, water and network.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 4))

EnergyTech

NTT will use Bloom Energy fuel cells as primary power at data centres worldwide, with first shipments due in 2027.

Why it matters: Fuel cells keep gaining ground as a speed-to-power option where grid capacity is scarce.

8

Trump's onsite power push meets AI data centre reality

PolicyEnergy

In an Oct. 21 post, President Trump said large new data centers should generate their own power and sell or give excess to the community or grid. Some gas turbine models are reportedly sold out through 2031, and experts say running 100% onsite around the clock is hard, pointing to hybrid models. This is a stated position, not formal policy.

Why it matters: A possible federal expectation of self-supplied power collides with equipment backlogs.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 3))
9

Can optical AI cut inference power use? Lumai deploys Iris Nova servers in a UK lab

Tech

Oxford spin-out Lumai uses light-based matrix multiplication for the prefill phase of inference, leaving token generation to GPUs. The servers fit standard air-cooled racks. The article gives no measured power or performance figures, and the efficiency claims are the company's own.

Why it matters: Early-stage and UK-based, but a possible long-term path to cutting inference power demand globally.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 6))

CanadaPolicy

An opinion piece from the Macdonald-Laurier Institute notes Calgary, Rocky View County, Mississauga and Oakville have proposed or enacted moratoriums, while Alberta and Saskatchewan court investment. Why it matters: It captures the split between provinces courting data centres and municipalities restricting them.

Meta plans record $97.5B equity raise as Nvidia and AMD commit $4B to its AI buildout

FinanceTech

Meta priced a $25 billion common stock offering and $12.5 billion of convertible preferred, with up to $60 billion more planned through an at-the-market program. Nvidia is investing $3 billion and AMD $1 billion; Meta raised its 2026 capex floor to $135 to $145 billion after its first negative free cash flow quarter in over a decade.

Why it matters: Equity markets are now funding AI capacity, and chipmakers are financing their own customers.

12

OpenAI rebrands data centres as 'compute campuses' and publishes a community compact

Policy

The compact commits OpenAI to pay full electricity and grid costs, provide financial assurances if projects are cancelled, adopt per-campus water plans and publish site-level usage. Its policy blueprint asks government to make large users pay for the grid costs they create. Critics say the name does not address the objections.

Why it matters: A major developer is conceding that community trust and ratepayer protection are now licence-to-operate issues.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 4))

Voters back local data centre limits in 40 of 41 ballot measures across 10 states

Policy

Ballotpedia counted 41 measures on the Nov. 3 ballot, 21 of them in Ohio, where at least 14 communities passed bans or restrictions. Maine, Kansas, California, Florida and Wisconsin localities also acted. The Washington Post tallied at least 38 jurisdictions, fewer than Ballotpedia.

Why it matters: Local opposition is now an electoral force that developers must plan around.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 4))
14

Texas governor-elect Hinojosa pledges to pause new data centre approvals until lawmakers set rules

Policy

Gina Hinojosa, who takes office Jan. 19, wants approvals halted pending new rules, a ban on NDAs, local votes and proof that households will not pay more. Gov. Abbott already ordered an audit of ERCOT queue projects. ERCOT is weighing over 474 GW of connection requests, about 90% data centres.

Why it matters: Texas holds about a quarter of planned US capacity, so a pause would reshape the pipeline.
Source → (Baxtel (Nov 5))

Construction
