Ecolab and CoolIT launch a 3 MW coolant distribution unit for AI racks at SC26

TechEnergy

Ecolab introduced an integrated liquid cooling platform at SC26 in Chicago on Nov 16. At its centre is the CoolIT CHx 3000, a liquid-to-liquid CDU rated for 3 MW at a 3°C approach, with support for 45°C warm-water operation and a flow rate of 4,500 litres per minute. It has built-in coolant health monitoring, partners include Alfa Laval and Siemens, and shipments begin in Q1 2027.

Why it matters: One unit can cool about 18 racks at 190 kW each, so facility-scale CDUs are catching up with rack densities. The performance figures are vendor claims.
Source → (HPCwire, Nov 16)

TensorWave signs a six-year deal to supply OpenAI with 457 MW of AMD-powered AI capacity

TechFinance

TensorWave will provide 457 MW of capacity dedicated to OpenAI across several US sites. It will use AMD Instinct MI455X GPUs in Helios rack systems. The deal falls under OpenAI's October 2025 partnership with AMD for up to 6 GW, and neither side disclosed its value.

Why it matters: This is the first publicly announced neocloud deal under the OpenAI–AMD partnership, a real test of AMD as an alternative to Nvidia at scale. Delivery risk remains.
Source → (The Next Web, Nov 16)
3

NRC grants Dow and X-energy a construction permit for four Xe-100 SMRs in Texas

EnergyTech

The NRC approved a construction permit for the Long Mott Generating Station at Dow's Seadrift site. It covers four Xe-100 high-temperature gas-cooled reactors that would supply power and steam to Dow's chemical operations. The NRC says this is the first US construction permit for this reactor type in more than 50 years, and the review met its 18-month target.

Why it matters: The project is not a data centre, but it is the clearest regulatory proof yet that SMRs can be permitted quickly. Hyperscaler SMR deals depend on that. A final investment decision and an operating licence are still required.
Source → (ANS Nuclear Newswire, Nov 10)

Howden says US data centre power demand has grown 19% a year while the grid grew 2%

EnergyPolicy

Insurance broker Howden says data centres would need up to 58% of their power, about 110 GW, from off-grid sources by 2030 to keep their share of US grid capacity. Marsh expects 50% to 60% of projects to be delayed beyond plan. A Cornell study cited in the piece found data centres paid only 38% of the grid costs tied to their growth in 2025.

Why it matters: This is the strongest evidence yet that on-site and behind-the-meter generation is now central to data centre development.
Source → (Fortune, Nov 22)
5

Alberta tables Bill 42, with 600-metre setbacks from homes and near-zero cooling water use

CanadaPolicy

Alberta's proposed amendments would bar data centres of 10 MW or more within 600 m of a home or on the province's best or irrigated farmland. Municipalities could set stricter setbacks. The bill sets a province-wide standard aiming for near-zero cooling water use and raises maximum corporate fines to $6M for serious contamination. The province estimates about 30% of current proposals would be affected.

Why it matters: Alberta holds more than 90% of Canada's proposed data centre capacity, so these rules will shape most of the country's pipeline.
Source → (Global News, Nov 18)

Telus pulls out of two planned Vancouver AI data centres totalling up to 163 MW

CanadaConstruction

Telus said on Nov 15 that it will not proceed with two AI data centres it was co-developing with Westbank. The sites, a downtown new build and a converted Mount Pleasant office, were planned for about 63,000 Nvidia chips. Westbank will continue with an unnamed partner, and Telus will keep expanding its Kamloops facility to 20 MW by 2028.

Why it matters: This is a major Canadian sovereign-AI project setback. A source cited local opposition, and the move also fits a strategy shift under new CEO Victor Dodig.
Source → (Globe and Mail, Nov 15)
7

Senate advances a bipartisan permitting bill that would make large data centres pay their grid upgrade costs

PolicyEnergy

The Senate voted 73-25 on Nov 9 to take up a sweeping permitting overhaul. The bill shortens environmental review deadlines and limits court challenges for clean and fossil energy projects alike. One provision requires large data centre operators to bear the incremental generation, storage, transmission and distribution costs needed to serve them.

Why it matters: Grid Strategies estimates data centres would pay about $35B more over a decade, a structural cost shift for US projects. House passage is uncertain.
Source → (Axios, Nov 9)
8

Meta re-evaluates its 2030 net-zero goal as emissions jump 45% on its AI buildout

EnergyPolicy

Meta's 2026 sustainability report shows net emissions up 45% in 2025 to 11.8M tonnes CO2e, driven by purchased and capital goods. Executives say the company is re-evaluating its timelines and interim milestones because AI growth outpaced the assumptions behind its pledge. Meta also announced multi-year purchase commitments for lower-carbon cement and near-zero steel for its data centre suppliers.

Why it matters: Meta is the first hyperscaler to formally signal that its climate deadline may slip. Investors and communities will press others on the same question.
Source → (Axios, Nov 19)
9

US Commerce extends AI chip licensing to remote access at overseas data centres

PolicyTech

A new BIS rule requires licences for covered AI equipment when exporters know it will be used in data centres that provide more than 12 million aggregate TPP to companies in restricted countries. The threshold also counts digitally linked sites. Operators already serving covered companies get a 90-day window to obtain licences.

Why it matters: The rule extends US controls to the cloud model, with compliance burdens for US chipmakers and hyperscalers operating abroad, especially in the Gulf and Southeast Asia.
Source → (Skadden, Nov 2026)

Core Scientific files a $1.3B, 285 MW AI campus in Hunt County, Texas, with AMD as anchor tenant

ConstructionTech

Core Scientific's subsidiary Telios Quinlan One registered a $1.3B building with Texas regulators on Nov 13. Construction is listed from late November 2026 to September 2028. The 265-acre campus could support about 285 MW leasable (430 MW gross), and AMD has leased 110 MW for 15 years.

Why it matters: A former bitcoin miner leasing to AMD shows how quickly crypto sites are becoming AI infrastructure. Energization is expected from 2027.
Source → (DCD, Nov 2026)

Microsoft named as the company evaluating a 1.4 GW, up-to-$12B campus in Rockford, Illinois

ConstructionEnergy

The City of Rockford identified Microsoft as the company doing due diligence on Monarch-controlled land in south Rockford. The site is about 1,100 acres, with a phased 1,400 MW plan under a state agreement signed in June. No proposal has been filed, and power is unsettled after ComEd cancelled a transmission security agreement with Monarch in July.

Why it matters: This would be one of the largest Midwest campuses, but it shows that power access, not land, is the gating factor.

Ohio ballot restrictions put about $17B of Amazon's planned data centre spending at risk

PolicyConstruction

The Financial Times reported that local restrictions approved by voters on Nov 3 affect about $17B of Amazon's planned Ohio spending, tied to roughly 1.8 GW. Why it matters: This is the largest dollar figure yet attached to the post-midterm local backlash.

Harvard-led study ties 403 US hyperscale data centres to 25.7M tonnes of CO2 in a year

EnergyPolicy

Published Nov 16 in Nature Sustainability, the study estimates the sites used about 82 TWh from May 2024 to April 2025, roughly 1.8% of US electricity, with about 54% from fossil fuels. Virginia, Oregon, Ohio and Iowa accounted for more than half of the electricity analysed.

Why it matters: It is a peer-reviewed baseline that regulators and opponents will cite. It finds that grid mix, more than efficiency, determines emissions.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge, Nov 20)
16

Battery storage, rack batteries and microgrids emerge as alternatives to traditional UPS

TechEnergy

Rising power density and on-site generation are pushing operators to look beyond UPS units that cover 10 to 15 minutes. Options include battery energy storage that can run for hours, rack-level batteries that save floor space, buffer modules that bridge up to 20 seconds to generator start, and on-site microgrids.

Why it matters: Most operators are expected to adopt hybrid designs, a shift in backup-power architecture as AI loads strain grids.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge, Nov 23)

Why data centres have become the focal point of the Big Tech backlash

Policy

In an interview about his book 'Hyperscale', journalist Paris Marx links the midterm backlash to power bills, water strain and noise felt by residents near data centres. Companies have responded by dropping NDAs, offering to fund grid upgrades and moving to closed-loop cooling. Marx argues the scale of expansion is the core problem.

Why it matters: The piece shows the community-relations narrative the industry is up against heading into 2027.
