Nvidia lines up $500 billion-plus in AI compute financing with Wall Street's biggest asset managers

FinanceTech

Nvidia signed agreements with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to build independent "compute financing platforms" designed to mobilize more than $500 billion in third-party capital for AI data centre buildouts. The structure lets developers borrow against Nvidia chips as an asset class rather than loading the debt onto hyperscaler balance sheets.

Why it matters: It's the clearest sign yet that the AI infrastructure boom is shifting from being self-funded by tech giants to being underwritten by the same private-credit machinery that fuels the rest of the economy.

2

Nvidia cuts its OpenAI Ohio data centre financing guarantee to $105 billion, down from a reported $250 billion

FinanceTech

Nvidia's final backstop for SB Energy's Stargate-linked campus in Pike County, Ohio landed at $105 billion — $145 billion below the figure reportedly floated a month earlier — after passing through at least one intermediate cut to "less than $120 billion." The financing supports lease and power payments and guarantees the value of completed infrastructure if OpenAI were to default.

Why it matters: Investors read the shrinking guarantee as Nvidia pulling back from "circular financing" risk, a sign that even the industry's biggest backer is recalibrating how much exposure to take on for a single AI campus.

3

Broadcom seeks up to $70 billion in debt to finance AI chips for Anthropic

FinanceTech

Broadcom is in talks with lenders, including Blackstone and Apollo, to raise a debt package that could reach $70 billion — potentially $100 billion including a junior tranche — to fund custom AI chips and networking gear leased to Anthropic. Investors, not Anthropic, would own the hardware and lease it to the company, extending the AI Fund partnership the three firms struck in June.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest AI chip financing deals ever assembled outside a hyperscaler balance sheet, showing how central off-balance-sheet debt has become to keeping the AI data centre build-out on pace.
CNBC →
4

Nvidia takes minority stake in power developer Cloverleaf Infrastructure

EnergyTech

Nvidia is investing several hundred million dollars in Cloverleaf Infrastructure, a company that signs power agreements with utilities so data centre developers can secure reliable electricity before breaking ground. Cloverleaf, founded in 2024, has already sold more than 7 gigawatts of power capacity to developers.

Why it matters: Nvidia is now investing years ahead of construction to guarantee power supply keeps pace with chip demand, treating electricity access as strategically important as the silicon itself.
TechCrunch →
5

Amazon confirms 7.65GW private gas plant at GW Ranch, Texas — potentially the nation's largest single pollution source

EnergyConstruction

Amazon confirmed on August 11 it is financing a 35-turbine, 7.65-gigawatt behind-the-meter gas plant on an 8,400-acre site near Fort Stockton in Pecos County to power a new AI campus, with building permits already filed for three data centre buildings. The site's air permit allows up to 33 million tons of CO2 a year — more than double the largest existing US power plant's emissions.

Why it matters: It's the starkest example yet of hyperscalers building dedicated private power plants to bypass grid interconnection delays, with environmental costs on a scale that could reshape the debate over off-grid AI power.

6

PJM releases five-year strategy pushing data centres to bring their own power

PolicyEnergy

PJM's Board of Managers approved a strategy, released August 19, that envisions data centres driving most of the grid operator's new electricity demand through 2035 and calls for faster interconnection, stronger state alignment and new large-load customers to build, bring or buy their own generation. The plan stops short of resolving governance and capacity-market questions FERC is separately investigating.

Why it matters: PJM serves 67 million people across 13 states, and how it resolves the tension between data centre growth and ratepayer costs will set a template other regional grid operators are likely to follow.

7

AI data centre networking scales to 102.4 terabit-per-second Ethernet switches

Tech

Broadcom's Tomahawk 6 and Cisco's G300 chips now deliver up to 102.4 terabits per second on a single switch, with vendors converging on 51.2–102.4 Tbps-class silicon featuring deeper buffering and richer telemetry to keep bursty AI training traffic flowing without stalling GPU clusters.

Why it matters: As memory and power get most of the attention, networking bandwidth is quietly becoming the next constraint on how fast AI clusters can actually train — this generation of switches is what keeps thousands of GPUs fed in sync.

8

Texas will audit up to 300 mostly-data-centre projects before granting grid access

Policy

Following Governor Greg Abbott's early-August order, the Public Utility Commission and ERCOT will audit roughly 250 to 300 projects — the vast majority data centres — requiring disclosure of financial assistance received, power-sourcing plans, water use and ownership before any grid connection is approved. ERCOT is separately tracking more than 474 gigawatts of connection requests, about 90% of it from data centres.

Why it matters: Texas has marketed itself as the easiest state to build in; a state-ordered freeze on its own interconnection queue signals even the friendliest jurisdictions are hitting a political and grid-capacity wall.

EdgeCore says data centres should pay their own power costs, details 1.8GW-plus buildout

PolicyConstruction

EdgeCore Digital Infrastructure said it will fund the generation, transmission and delivery infrastructure needed for its expanding campuses — including a 1.1GW-plus site in Louisa County, Virginia, a 496MW campus in Mesa, Arizona, and a 216MW campus in Reno, Nevada — rather than pass costs to ratepayers.

Why it matters: As "who pays for the grid" becomes the industry's defining political fight, a major developer publicly committing to self-funded power is a competitive bet that could pressure rivals to match it.

Canada

Canada: Montreal-based 5C secures $605 million from Brookfield, pushing total funding past $1.4 billion

CanadaFinance

5C, a Montreal-headquartered AI data centre developer, closed $605 million in Brookfield-led debt financing on August 10 to fund its Memphis campus acquisition and expand sites in Ohio and Phoenix, adding to the $835 million it raised in 2025. The company says it has more than 1.5 gigawatts of roadmap capacity across North America.

Why it matters: It shows a Canadian-headquartered developer competing directly for the same US hyperscale financing pool as American rivals, underscoring how integrated North American AI infrastructure capital has become.

Georgia regulators to rule on whether a "pop-up" gas power plant for a data centre is even legal

PolicyEnergy

Georgia's Public Service Commission voted unanimously to hold hearings on whether Houston-based VoltaGrid can legally assemble 33 gas-burning engines next to a Covington data centre under construction by developer Serverfarm, after allegations the project began before obtaining required air permits.

Why it matters: The ruling could set the legal precedent for whether privately operated "pop-up" power plants can serve data centres outside normal utility oversight — a model spreading fast as developers race to skip interconnection queues.

AWS withdraws from planned data centre campus next to Maryland's Calvert Cliffs nuclear plant

Policy

Amazon abandoned plans for a 2.46-million-square-foot, roughly 500MW campus in Calvert County, Maryland that would have drawn power from Constellation's Calvert Cliffs nuclear plant, after a Change.org petition against the project drew more than 10,000 signatures and all three county commissioners who backed it lost their re-election races.

Why it matters: It's one of the clearest cases yet of local political backlash directly killing a major hyperscale project, even one tied to carbon-free nuclear power.

Fort Worth City Council begins process for a 90-day data centre moratorium

Policy

Why it matters: It adds a major Texas metro — a state seen as the most development-friendly in the country — to the growing list of cities formalizing a pause rather than just fielding complaints.

Land banking becomes the go-to strategy as data centre land sales jump 141%

FinanceConstruction

Why it matters: Land is emerging as its own scarce, strategic asset class alongside power and chips, and developers with the deepest pockets for land banking may end up controlling where the next decade of AI capacity gets built.

16

Oregon Democratic lawmakers propose a three-year moratorium on large data centres

Policy

Four Oregon Democratic state legislators, backed by two legislative candidates, will introduce a three-year pause on new large data centres in the 2027 session, citing energy and water demand, farmland loss, tax fairness and a lack of industry transparency. It follows a one-year pause the legislature already placed on a data centre tax incentive in March.

Why it matters: A three-year moratorium proposal — among the longest floated by any state — shows the political mood hardening well beyond one-year pauses in traditionally left-leaning states, not just conservative ones fighting rate hikes.
