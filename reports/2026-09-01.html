ONE Nuclear signs binding LOI for 2.88GW gas-and-battery power project in Louisiana

EnergyConstruction

ONE Nuclear executed a binding letter of intent with a Louisiana landowner group to build a 2.88-gigawatt natural gas power plant paired with a 700-megawatt/2.88-gigawatt-hour battery storage system in Ascension Parish, near the RiverPlex MegaPark outside Baton Rouge and New Orleans, to feed a co-located data centre campus. Community meetings with local parishes are scheduled from September through December 2026 as the project advances.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single gas-plus-battery power builds tied to a single data centre project yet announced, underscoring how far developers will go to lock in dedicated, dispatchable power ahead of grid capacity.

Nvidia opens NVLink to custom hyperscaler chips through $3.5B MediaTek deal

TechFinance

Nvidia is opening its proprietary NVLink interconnect to third-party custom AI chips via NVLink Fusion and its MGX rack architecture, with MediaTek providing system-on-chip design and packaging support in exchange for $3.5 billion in convertible bonds from Nvidia. The move lets hyperscaler in-house silicon — including AWS's Trainium, Google's TPU and Microsoft's Maia — plug directly into Nvidia's rack-scale infrastructure.

Why it matters: It's a significant architectural concession that could keep Nvidia's ecosystem central to AI data centres even as hyperscalers push their own chips, and it pre-empts AMD's rival open UALink interconnect standard.

SLB to buy cooling specialist Kelvion for $4.1B, doubling down on AI heat rejection

FinanceTechEnergy

Oilfield services giant SLB agreed to acquire thermal-management firm Kelvion for $3.4 billion in cash plus $700 million in assumed debt, pairing it with SLB's existing modular construction business. The combined unit is targeting $2 billion in 2026 data-centre revenue, rising to $4.5–5 billion by 2028.

Why it matters: It shows oilfield-services capital pivoting hard into AI infrastructure cooling — a segment analysts now call the fastest-repricing part of the data centre supply chain as rack densities climb past 100 kilowatts.

Chevron's 20-year, 2.67GW gas power deal with Microsoft now outweighs its earnings, analysts say

EnergyFinance

Fresh analysis of Chevron's Project Kilby — a 20-year take-or-pay power purchase agreement signed with Microsoft in June — argues the deal, which will build roughly 2.67 gigawatts of on-site gas generation using GE Vernova and Caterpillar's Solar Turbines equipment, has become more consequential to Chevron's investment case than its quarterly results. Power is expected to begin flowing to the Microsoft-operated data centre by 2028.

Why it matters: It's a bellwether for how oil majors are re-rating themselves around long-term, contracted AI power revenue rather than volatile commodity prices.

Trump declares national emergency, restricts foreign-made grid equipment as data centres strain the grid

PolicyEnergy

President Trump declared a national emergency over the bulk power system, restricting the purchase and installation of foreign-made transformers, inverters and grid controls on national-security grounds. The Department of Energy has 120 days to write implementing rules, and even already-installed foreign equipment could face new operating conditions.

Why it matters: It risks deepening an already severe transformer shortage that is delaying more than half of planned US data centres, right as demand for grid connections is surging.

Commerce drafts rule to close loophole letting China rent Nvidia GPUs through third-country data centres

PolicyTech

The Commerce Department is drafting a replacement for the rescinded Biden-era AI diffusion rule, aimed at Chinese firms that rent Nvidia GPU compute remotely through data centres in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand. The draft could circulate to industry as soon as September, though export-control lawyers say Commerce may lack statutory authority to police remote access without new legislation.

Why it matters: It extends the US-China chip fight from hardware shipments into cloud access itself, directly implicating North American cloud providers whose capacity is used by third-country resellers.

Canada: Ontario's new Data Centre Playbook makes facilities cover 100% of their own grid costs

CanadaEnergyPolicy

Ontario opened a 30-day comment period on a new Data Centre Playbook requiring large facilities to cover the full cost of their grid connections with no cash incentives, as roughly 7,000 megawatts of pending connection requests — double the level seen in March — pile up with grid operator IESO.

Why it matters: It positions Ontario as a "pay your own way" counterpoint to Alberta's more permissive bring-your-own-generation approach, and could become a template for other provinces.

Canada: Alberta data centre connection requests hit 19.6GW, dwarfing 1.2GW interim cap

CanadaEnergyPolicy

Alberta's grid operator AESO says its Phase 1 interim allocation of 1,200 megawatts for new data centre connections is fully subscribed against nearly 19.6 gigawatts of total requested load. The province's "Bring Your Own Generation" priority-queue process, expected to launch in late July or early August, still had not gone live as of mid-August.

Why it matters: It's stark evidence that even Alberta — Canada's most data-centre-friendly province — can't keep pace with demand under its current interim rules.

Finance

11

New Jersey governor signs law requiring data centres to disclose energy and water use

Policy

Governor Mikie Sherrill signed a law requiring data centres over 100 megawatts to self-report energy and water use — including the cooling-versus-IT split, peak water use and backup power sources — to the state's Board of Public Utilities twice a year. It completes a four-pillar data centre plan that already includes a "bring your own clean energy" mandate and a new large-load utility rate class.

Why it matters: It's one of the most comprehensive state disclosure regimes yet, though critics note it lacks independent audits, usage caps or penalties — a template other states will likely study.

Wixom, Michigan ends data centre moratorium early with strict new zoning; developer sues

Policy

Wixom's city council voted to pass data centre restrictions — including residential setbacks, mandatory sound studies and water-use limits — into its zoning ordinance, with the rules taking effect no later than September 6, ending the city's six-month moratorium about two and a half weeks early. Developer Wixom Industrial One has filed a federal lawsuit calling the moratorium "illegal."

Why it matters: It's a concrete example of a moratorium resolving into permanent, tighter zoning rather than an outright ban — but the lawsuit shows these rules will be tested in court.
