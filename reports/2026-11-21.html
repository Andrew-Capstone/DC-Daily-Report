Google commits $17B to its first Wyoming data centre, a campus near Cheyenne with full completion targeted for 2031

EnergyConstruction

Google announced on Nov 5 a campus in Laramie County, called Project Tembo, on 716 acres in the Switchgrass Industrial Park. Initial operations are targeted for the second half of 2028 and full completion for the first half of 2031. Google says it will pay for 100% of the power it consumes and will air-cool the site to limit water use. Partners include Tallgrass, Black Hills, AEP and Related Digital.

Why it matters: Google has not disclosed capacity, and the 2.7 GW figure in some reports comes from county filings. A 702 MW fuel-cell link reported elsewhere is not confirmed in Google's post.
Source → (Google blog, Nov 5)
2

Blue Energy and GE Vernova Hitachi advance a 2.5 GW gas-plus-nuclear plant in Texas to power a Crusoe campus

EnergyTech

The companies will advance engineering, licensing and safety work on a 2.5 GW plant at Victoria, Texas, combining GE Vernova 7HA.02 gas turbines with GVH BWRX-300 small modular reactors. The plan is about 1 GW from gas turbines for a nearby Crusoe campus in 2030, then up to 1.5 GW from SMRs from 2032, subject to a final investment decision in 2027.

Why it matters: It is a concrete template for pairing fast gas power with SMRs, though the SMR capacity is years from delivery. The article is undated in the search results, and the agreement is reported as 2026 news.
Source → (DCD, undated in search)

CanadaEnergy

4

Bell plans to expand its Saskatchewan AI campus to 1.2 GW under a non-binding provincial MOU

CanadaEnergy

On Sept 14, Bell and the Saskatchewan government signed a non-binding MOU to add up to 900 MW to the existing 300 MW Bell AI Fabric campus, a path to 1.2 GW. The expansion would be phased, with partner-developed gas generation, and Bell says up to 10 MW of wholesale capacity would go to SaskTel for data sovereignty. Bell says the full build-out is not guaranteed and depends on demand and approvals.

Why it matters: It is the largest Canadian AI campus plan in the sources reviewed, but its non-binding status and the reported gap in environmental assessment mean it is a plan, not a commitment.
Source → (DCD, Sept 2026)
5

Alberta's grid operator is preparing draft rules for temporary grid access for bring-your-own-generation data centres

CanadaEnergy

The Logic reports that the Alberta Electric System Operator is set to unveil draft rules that would let large data centre projects temporarily draw from the grid while they build their own gas-fired generation. One report puts the pool at 1.6 GW for up to three years, on top of the 1.2 GW cap set aside in 2025 for large-load connections until 2028.

Why it matters: If adopted, temporary grid access would let projects connect before their own generation is built. The article is undated, the 1.6 GW figure comes from a single outlet, and the timing needs checking against AESO's own consultation documents.
Source → (The Logic, undated in search)
6

Power quality, not just grid access, is limiting AI growth, a Fluke survey of 500 US operators finds

TechEnergy

Fluke's survey of 500 US data centre operators and professionals found that nearly a third said power-quality problems had limited their capacity to run high-density AI and GPU workloads. Only 36% said their electrical infrastructure is fully ready, 74% said insufficient power and grid capacity limit expansion, and 78% lack comprehensive real-time visibility of critical electrical systems.

Why it matters: It moves the bottleneck from the utility connection to the facility's own electrical plant, where most operators have little monitoring.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge, Nov 19)
7

PJM's review of a July 22 Northern Virginia transmission fault finds about 3,800 MW of data centre load switched to backup power

EnergyPolicy

A 230 kV transmission line in Dominion's zone faulted on July 22 and cleared as designed, but data centres' protection systems shifted them to backup generation. PJM's review puts the load transfer at about 3,800 MW, above the roughly 3 GW in early reports. PJM says the transfers caused generation-load imbalance and voltage and frequency swings, and it is weighing ride-through requirements so large loads stay connected during disturbances.

Why it matters: Regulators are moving from interconnection rules toward how large loads behave during faults, which will shape future data centre design requirements.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge; event July 22, reported later)
8

Google adds TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max, letting select customers run TPUs in their own data centres

Tech

Google Cloud announced on Nov 9 TPU 8tx, a more secure version of the TPU 8t training chip with hardware-level encryption, paired with custom AMD CPU hosts. TPU 8i Max combines two TPU 8i chips in one package with 384 GB of high-bandwidth memory, roughly doubling compute, SRAM and memory bandwidth over TPU 8i. Google will let select customers deploy TPU 8tx in their own data centres.

Why it matters: Letting customers run custom AI silicon on their own premises is a shift away from cloud-only access.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge, Nov 9)
9

AMD makes sixth-generation Epyc SP7 generally available, with up to 256 cores, and launches Helion AI racks

Tech

AMD's sixth-generation Epyc SP7 is generally available with up to 256 cores, and servers are shipping. AMD says hardware partners have committed to more than 240 Venice platforms, 20% more than for the prior Turin generation. Helion agentic AI racks, in Liquid, Air and Air Inference versions, begin shipping this month, and AMD claims up to 3.7 times the performance of an Nvidia Vera CPU-based system.

Why it matters: Denser CPUs free power and floor space, which matters now that operators are power-limited. The 3.7x comparison is AMD's own claim.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge, Nov 10)
10

Why immersion cooling still hasn't gone mainstream, despite strong efficiency and water-free operation

Tech

A Nov 17 Data Center Knowledge analysis says immersion cooling offers strong efficiency and water-free operation but remains niche. One study puts immersion at about $1 million per MW of IT load versus $650,000 for direct-to-chip, though a 10-year cost model for a 64-rack AI cluster projects immersion slightly cheaper than direct-to-chip ($28M vs $31M). Rack redesign, maintenance and technician training slow adoption.

Why it matters: It is a useful check on which cooling approach operators choose for dense AI racks, since the cost figures cut both ways.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge, Nov 17)

13

A White House push for onsite power meets the practical limits of AI data centre builds

PolicyEnergy

In an Oct 21 post, President Trump said new large data centres should generate their own electricity and return any surplus to the grid or local community, and suggested dividend-style payments to residents. The article notes hybrid designs are emerging: Stargate Abilene uses gas, fuel cells and gas engines during construction, and Applied Digital's Ellendale campus has arranged 400 MW of stranded wind.

Why it matters: A federal preference for self-supply would matter most for projects that rely on grid connections. It is a policy signal, not yet a rule.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge, Nov 3)
14

After the midterms, more cities move to restrict data centres as voters back local limits

Policy

Voters approved nearly every local data centre restriction on the Nov 3 ballot. Newsweek counts 46 of 47 measures backing tighter controls, while another tally counts 40 of 41, so the counts differ by source. Edmond, Oklahoma extended its moratorium to June 30, 2027 on Nov 9, and Boise, Idaho approved a moratorium on mega data centres.

Why it matters: Ballot results give local opposition a mandate that is harder to override than a council vote.
Source → (Newsweek, Nov 4 to 10; counts vary by tracker)

PolicyConstruction

In Wisconsin, Gov.-elect David Crowley reversed his opposition to a moratorium, backed a pause, and supported ending the industry's state sales-tax exemption.

Why it matters: Losing tax breaks and adding conditions raises developers' costs. GlobalData's estimate of about two years from announcement to construction shows the effect already in schedules.
