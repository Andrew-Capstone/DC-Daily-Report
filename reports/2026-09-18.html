Samsung and SK Hynix memory inventory falls below 10 days as HBM4 race starves the DRAM market

Tech

Finished-memory inventory at Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix dropped below 10 days of supply on September 7, an unprecedented tightness as both manufacturers reallocate wafer capacity toward HBM4 chips destined for Nvidia's next-generation AI accelerator platform. Analysts estimate up to 70% of global memory chip output in 2026 will go to AI data centres, with Micron's CEO warning the resulting DRAM and HBM shortage will likely persist into 2027 and beyond.

Why it matters: Every hyperscale AI buildout in North America now depends on a memory supply chain running near zero slack, meaning server delivery timelines — not just power and land — are becoming a binding constraint on how fast new data centres can actually come online.

Meta deepens $3B+ Eagle Mountain, Utah campus with near-zero-water cooling and 150MW of battery storage

TechEnergyConstruction

Meta announced a fourth expansion of its Eagle Mountain data centre campus, pushing its total Utah investment past $3 billion, and detailed a water-efficient closed-loop liquid cooling system paired with dry cooling that keeps operational water use below that of a typical regional golf course. The buildout includes a 150-megawatt Faraday battery storage facility and is part of more than 1.7 gigawatts of clean energy Meta has added across 13 sites, alongside over $2.5 million in new local community and school investments.

Why it matters: As water scarcity becomes a leading flashpoint for community opposition nationwide, Meta's near-zero-water design at gigawatt-adjacent scale is emerging as a template other hyperscalers will be pressured to match.

Google signs 396MW deal with Fervo Energy, the largest enhanced-geothermal PPA ever made

EnergyTech

Google agreed to buy 396 megawatts of power from Fervo Energy's Cape Station enhanced-geothermal project in southwestern Utah starting in 2028, with an option to expand purchases to a full gigawatt by mid-2030. Fervo calls it the largest enhanced-geothermal power purchase agreement ever signed; unlike conventional geothermal, the technology only needs hot rock within drilling reach, dramatically widening where it can be deployed.

Why it matters: Enhanced geothermal offers hyperscalers continuous, clean power without the multi-year interconnection queues plaguing solar, wind and gas — a deal this size signals the technology has crossed from pilot project to serious grid-scale option.

Salt River Project board approves 1,675MW Marigold Energy Center to serve Phoenix-area data centre demand

Energy

SRP's board approved the Marigold Energy Center in Stanfield, Arizona, a mixed-resource facility combining 600 MW of solar, 400 MW of 8-hour battery storage and 675 MW of natural gas generation with its own substation and new transmission line. The project needs sign-off from Arizona's Power Plant and Transmission Line Siting Committee, which meets September 21-25, with a state decision expected in November.

Why it matters: The vote came from a board reshaped by an April clean-energy majority takeover, showing utilities can still fast-track large gas-plus-storage capacity for data centres even as their governance shifts toward renewables-first policy.
AZ Big Media → (Sept 14)

Bell and PM Carney unveil $52.5B quadrupling of Saskatchewan AI data centre to 1.2 gigawatts

CanadaFinanceEnergy

Bell Canada and Prime Minister Mark Carney announced at Canada's first investment summit that Bell's Saskatchewan AI campus will grow from 300 megawatts to 1.2 gigawatts, in an expansion carrying more than $52.5 billion in capital investment — the largest private investment in Saskatchewan's history. Of the new capacity, 300 MW draws from the provincial grid while up to 900 MW comes through Bell's own "bring your own power" generation, and the project is expected to create up to 500 permanent jobs plus roughly 3,000 additional construction and support roles.

Why it matters: Carney called it "the largest sovereign AI infrastructure network in Canada," positioning the project as the centrepiece of Ottawa's bid to keep Canadian AI compute and data on Canadian soil.

Ontario splits: Mississauga bans new data centres 11-0 as Vaughan rejects a similar pause a day earlier

CanadaPolicy

Mississauga city council voted 11-0 on September 17 to enact a one-year moratorium on new data centres over 10 megawatts, immediately affecting a proposed 40MW Prologis project, becoming the third Ontario municipality to do so. A day earlier, Vaughan council — where a multibillion-dollar Microsoft expansion is under construction — voted 9-1 against an identical proposal, instead asking staff to report back in November on how to incorporate the province's forthcoming data-centre framework into local planning rules.

Why it matters: The same week produced opposite verdicts twenty kilometres apart, showing Ontario's data-centre boom now hinges as much on individual council politics as on any provincial or federal policy.
Canada's National Observer → (Sept 17)

Ottawa launches national data centre development principles; AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft and OpenAI sign on

CanadaPolicy

The federal government launched Canada's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles, a five-point framework requiring developers to create lasting local benefits, protect electricity ratepayers, minimize water and environmental impacts, maintain community transparency, and provide strategic value to Canada. Twenty-three companies, including AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft and OpenAI, have already signed on as supporters.

Why it matters: A voluntary federal baseline gives municipalities like Mississauga and Vaughan a common reference point as they set contradictory local rules, though it stops short of binding regulation.

Hydro-Québec's proposed data centre rate hike still stuck in regulatory limbo eight months on

CanadaPolicy

Hydro-Québec's February request to create a new billing category charging large data centres (5MW-plus) roughly 13 cents per kilowatt-hour — about double the standard industrial rate — remains unresolved before Quebec's energy regulator. A Coalition of Data Centres representing six operators, including QScale and eStruxture, running 23 sites across the province is formally challenging the increase.

Why it matters: The prolonged fight over who pays for grid upgrades in Quebec mirrors California's ratepayer battle, underscoring that this cost question is now a defining regulatory fault line across North America, not just a US issue.

Oracle's contracted AI cloud backlog swells to $664 billion after $30B in new deals

Finance

Oracle disclosed remaining performance obligations of $664 billion, up $209 billion year-over-year, after booking more than $30 billion in new AI cloud contracts last quarter — with cloud infrastructure revenue up 121% to $7.39 billion. Most of the new contracts are structured through prepayment or customer-supplied hardware so they don't require additional Oracle capital, even as quarterly capex of $28.5 billion pushed free cash flow to negative $5.4 billion.

Why it matters: The scale of Oracle's backlog is one of the clearest market signals yet of how much AI compute demand is being pre-sold years in advance, even as the financing structure shows how capital-intensive the buildout has become.

California data centre ratepayer-protection bills reach Newsom's desk with September 30 deadline

Policy

SB 886 and AB 2383, which would require the California Public Utilities Commission to set separate electricity tariffs for large data centres so residential customers don't absorb transmission and generation upgrade costs, now await Governor Newsom's signature or veto by September 30. A Little Hoover Commission report estimated data centre demand in PG&E territory alone could generate up to $1.8 billion in transmission-upgrade costs; the bills are backed by consumer and utility-worker groups and opposed by the Data Center Coalition, Google, Microsoft and PG&E.

Why it matters: As the largest US data centre market, California's decision will set a bellwether precedent for whether states force hyperscalers to pay their full share of grid upgrade costs.
KQED → (Sept 1)
12

Oregon Governor Kotek pauses data centre development on state land until mid-2027

Policy

Governor Tina Kotek ordered an immediate, roughly one-year pause on new easements, leases, land-use permits and property transfers for data centre projects on Oregon state land, running through July 1, 2027, after about 100 demonstrators rallied at the Capitol for a three-year moratorium. Kotek's Data Center Advisory Committee will issue preliminary findings this month and final recommendations by year-end.

Why it matters: Kotek stopped short of demonstrators' full demands but became the third West Coast-adjacent governor this month to use executive action rather than wait on the legislature, adding to a fast-growing patchwork of state-level pauses.

Connecticut Governor Lamont: state is "the wrong place" for massive AI data centres

Policy

Governor Ned Lamont said Connecticut will not host large AI data centres because of their electricity demands, citing commercial power rates of 21-23 cents per kilowatt-hour versus a roughly 13-cent national average. He left the door open only to smaller, self-supplied projects that bring their own power and water and clear local approval.

Why it matters: Lamont's blanket "no" is a sharper stance than most governors have taken, and lands amid a state campaign fight over Connecticut's underused data-centre tax incentive program.

Policy

Why it matters: Michigan shows the limits of the moratorium wave: legislative inertia can leave even well-supported transparency rules stuck for months while construction proceeds unchanged.

Policy

Energy

NRG's proposed 1.2-gigawatt combined-cycle natural gas plant, tied to an as-yet-unnamed hyperscaler's Texas data centre, has been conditionally included as a Studied Load in ERCOT's Batch Zero large-load interconnection process.
