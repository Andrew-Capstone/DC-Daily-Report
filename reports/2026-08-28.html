AWS and Nvidia to deploy 2 million more GPUs for AI in 2027-2028

TechFinance

AWS and Nvidia announced plans to deploy 2 million additional GPUs — including Blackwell Ultra, Rubin and Rubin Ultra silicon — across AWS's global infrastructure in 2027-2028, alongside new Nvidia Vera CPUs coming to AWS for agentic AI workloads. The expansion follows AWS's prior commitment at GTC 2026 to add more than 1 million GPUs starting this year, a pledge the companies say demand has already outpaced; the deal also includes building AI factories carrying 100,000 GPUs for the US government.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single GPU-supply commitments disclosed this year, and a signal that even hyperscalers who thought they had secured enough Nvidia silicon are still running short.

Blue Energy and GE Vernova Hitachi advance 2.5GW gas-plus-SMR hybrid plant in Texas

EnergyTech

Nuclear developer Blue Energy and GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear Energy signed an agreement (Aug 17) to advance engineering, licensing and safety work on a 2.5-gigawatt hybrid power plant in Victoria, Texas, combining GE Vernova 7HA.02 gas turbines with GVH's BWRX-300 small modular reactors to supply up to 1.5GW to a nearby data centre. The plant would let gas generation come online first while the SMR units are licensed and built out in a later phase.

Why it matters: Pairing gas and small modular reactors on one site is a new template for bridging the years-long gap between when data centres need power and when nuclear capacity can realistically be licensed and built.

TerraPower says it will name a data centre-dedicated nuclear customer by year-end

EnergyTech

TerraPower CEO Chris Levesque said the Bill Gates-backed nuclear startup will unveil its first project explicitly designed to power a data centre before the end of 2026, with construction targeted to break ground in 2027; the customer has not yet been named. The company's Natrium reactor design uses molten-salt energy storage that Levesque says gives it an edge for the spiky, fast-ramping power demand of AI computing.

Why it matters: A named data-centre customer would be TerraPower's first direct entry into the AI power market, adding a fourth major advanced-reactor developer alongside Oklo, X-energy and Kairos to the race for hyperscaler nuclear deals.

Canada: Saskatchewan says new data centres must be Canadian-owned and supply their own power

CanadaPolicy

Saskatchewan unveiled a six-principle data centre framework — requiring Canadian ownership, proven telecom operators, "bring your own power," Saskatchewan jobs, and a centralized provincial intake process — announced at the site of Bell Canada's under-construction $1.7 billion AI data centre near Regina. Minister Jeremy Harrison framed the policy around Canadian AI and data sovereignty as the province courts further investment.

Why it matters: It's one of the most restrictive ownership requirements any Canadian province has attached to data centre investment, and puts Saskatchewan alongside Ontario as provinces trying to set ground rules before, not after, the next wave of projects arrives.

Canada: Ontario unveils Data Centre Playbook requiring full electricity cost coverage

CanadaPolicy

Premier Doug Ford released Ontario's "Data Centre Playbook," which will only approve projects that cover the full cost of their electricity — including a new, higher rate class than existing large-industrial programs — and prioritizes closed-loop cooling systems that use minimal water. Ford said the province will not offer financial incentives to attract data centre investment, and the framework is open for 30 days of public comment.

Why it matters: As Canada's most populous province and a top target for AI investment, Ontario's rules — no ratepayer subsidy, no incentives, mandatory closed-loop cooling — will likely become the reference point other provinces and municipalities measure their own policies against.

Canada: Hamilton and Burlington councillors say Ontario's playbook still lacks detail

CanadaPolicy

Local councillors reacting to Ontario's new Data Centre Playbook said the framework leaves key questions unanswered, with Hamilton's Nrinder Nann noting it "doesn't get into details in terms of policy and regulation," and no indication of whether it will complement or override municipal rules already in progress. Burlington council separately rejected a proposed one-year moratorium, instead directing staff to review local rules while it weighs a pending 17-megawatt data centre application.

Why it matters: It shows the gap between a provincial framework and the on-the-ground certainty municipalities say they still need, at a moment when Ontario cities are simultaneously fielding live development applications.

Why it matters: When the chipmaker driving the AI buildout says the industry has run out of hyperbole to describe its own bottlenecks, it's a signal that infrastructure — not chip supply — is now the limiting factor on AI capacity growth.

How data centers are using AI to run cooler and smarter

TechEnergy

Data Center Knowledge details how operators are deploying AI-driven digital twins and predictive thermal-management software to optimize cooling in real time, adjusting airflow and liquid cooling loops before hotspots form rather than reacting after the fact. The approach is being layered on top of the industry's broader shift to direct-to-chip liquid cooling for high-density AI racks.

Why it matters: Using AI to manage the cooling load AI itself creates is becoming a meaningful efficiency lever as facilities push rack densities and power draw to levels traditional cooling design wasn't built for.

Micron puts $10 billion behind US AI memory research

TechFinance

Micron committed $10 billion toward expanding US-based research and production of high-bandwidth memory used in AI accelerators, aiming to reduce reliance on overseas memory supply as demand from Nvidia, AMD and hyperscaler custom-silicon programs continues to climb. Why it matters: Memory bandwidth, not just GPU compute, has become a binding constraint on AI system performance, and a domestic supply push reduces one more chokepoint in the AI hardware supply chain.

Volato Group signs $500 million merger with AI infrastructure firm Alignment Engine

Finance

Small-cap aviation company Volato Group signed a definitive agreement to merge with Alignment Engine, an AI infrastructure developer whose Ohio campus carries 480 megawatts of capacity for GPU training and inference, in a deal valuing Alignment Engine at roughly $500 million. Why it matters: It's another example of a reverse-merger-style shell transaction being used to fast-track public-market access for AI infrastructure assets, a financing pattern now recurring across smaller data centre developers.

Energy

Haffner Energy launched a dedicated energy offering for data centres aimed at three problems operators face at once: securing high electrical power quickly without straining local grids, decarbonizing the power supply, and managing water needs — with the company saying it already has initial commercial proposals underway. Data centre backlash reaches the ballot box ahead of the midterms

Policy

Data Center Knowledge reports that at least 18 incumbent officeholders across the US have lost elections in the past year after taking action on or becoming associated with a data centre fight, with four Festus, Missouri city council members swept out after a dispute over a proposed $6 billion project. Why it matters: When local data centre votes are costing incumbents their seats, siting decisions stop being a planning-department matter and start being a direct electoral liability for the politicians who approve them.

More data centers comply with Abbott's Texas standards as up to 1,800 projects stay halted

PolicyEnergy

A growing list of developers — including Galaxy's Helios campus, Compass Datacenters and Montera Infrastructure — have committed to Governor Greg Abbott's data centre standards requiring projects to fund their own grid interconnection, reuse water, and avoid disrupting residential neighborhoods. The commitments come as Abbott's statewide review, launched in early August, continues to hold up roughly 1,800 proposed projects seeking a combined 474 gigawatts of grid connection.

Why it matters: Texas holds the largest data centre interconnection queue in the country, so how quickly this compliance-driven unfreezing plays out will shape how much of the sector's announced capacity actually gets built on schedule.
