EverWind lands 20-year frontier-AI lease for $10B Point Tupper, Nova Scotia data centre

CanadaEnergyConstruction

EverWind says an unnamed leading frontier AI developer signed a 20-year lease for an 18,600-square-metre data centre at Point Tupper that will draw 95 MW in a first phase due late 2027, with a 325 MW expansion in 2028 taking demand to 420 MW. The C$10B plan is C$8B for the campus and C$2B for onshore wind; a Reuters syndication names Anthropic as the tenant, but other outlets say the tenant is undisclosed.

Why it matters: It is billed as the largest private investment in Nova Scotia history, and the opposition is already questioning how ratepayer protections would be enforced.

Canada's defence construction agency flags $1.5–1.85B, 60 MW secure government data centre in Ottawa

CanadaConstruction

Defence Construction Canada issued an advance procurement notice for a 60 MW facility in the National Capital Region, estimated at CA$1.5–1.85 billion. It would supply secure computing capacity for the Communications Security Establishment, with procurement expected from winter 2027 subject to government approval.

Why it matters: A sovereign, government-owned compute build of this size signals federal appetite for domestic AI infrastructure, though the project could still be cancelled.

Google signs Constellation nuclear deals: 890 MW of reactor uprates plus 2,700 MW supply

Energy

A 20-year agreement adds 890 MW by upgrading 11 reactors at six sites in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, with Constellation spending over $4.3B and the first upgrade online in 2028. A separate 15-year deal supplies another 2,700 MW from Constellation's fleet on the PJM grid.

Why it matters: Uprating existing reactors is one of the fastest routes to new firm clean capacity, and it extends hyperscaler nuclear buying beyond new-build SMRs.

NERC reliability standards for AI data centres and crypto miners clear stakeholder vote

PolicyEnergy

Stakeholders approved standards under Project 2026-02 for computational loads of 50 MW or more connected at 100 kV or higher, covering interconnection modelling, operational data and load-protection coordination. NERC's Board of Trustees is expected to consider them in Q4 before FERC review; they follow a May Level 3 alert on sudden data centre load drops.

Why it matters: A continent-wide rulebook for how large loads behave on the grid could shape interconnection for every new campus; NERC projects 224 GW of summer peak growth over 10 years.

Loudoun County pauses new data centre and substation applications for up to 12 months

PolicyConstruction

Virginia's Loudoun County Board voted 7-1-1 on Sept. 15 to pause legislative applications for data centres and substations while it works on new standards, with an exception for grid-reliability substations. A resolution formalizing the pause is on the Board's Oct. 20 agenda.

Why it matters: The world's densest data centre market is stepping back from approvals, a signal for other local governments weighing similar limits.

DOE selects $1.9B for 31 grid projects to speed data centre connections

EnergyPolicy

DOE's SPARK program backs 31 projects in 26 states, with $5.25B total including $3.35B in recipient cost share, and says the work could add more than 23 GW of transfer capability. Funding agreements are due between October 2026 and January 2027, and trade press notes only a few project summaries explicitly tie the work to data centres.

Why it matters: Reconductoring and grid-enhancing technologies are among the quickest ways to open capacity on existing corridors.

McKinsey: data centre power demand to grow 24% a year through 2030

Energy

McKinsey's Global Energy Perspective 2026 says its most likely scenarios all have power supply below IT compute demand through 2030. Constraints include interconnection, permitting, moratoriums and equipment lead times, and over 60% of operators now plan to combine on-site generation with grid access.

Why it matters: Power availability, not chips, will set how much compute comes online.
