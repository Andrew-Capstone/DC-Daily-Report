Brookfield and NextEra launch $100 billion AI campus at DOE's Paducah, Kentucky site

EnergyFinance

Brookfield and NextEra Energy unveiled a privately funded, more than $100 billion AI data-centre campus at the Department of Energy's former Paducah uranium-enrichment site, backed by up to 4.6GW of dedicated new generation. NextEra will build and own up to 2GW of new gas capacity paired with 2.6GW of battery storage, while Brookfield develops and operates the data centre; full build-out is expected by 2032 with roughly 8,000 construction jobs.

Why it matters: It's the second former DOE nuclear-enrichment site in months (after Ohio's Portsmouth site) to be repurposed for gigawatt-scale AI infrastructure, showing federal land and co-located generation becoming a go-to playbook for sidestepping grid queues.

2

Canada: Meta breaks ground on its first Canadian data centre, a $13B, 1GW campus in Alberta

CanadaEnergy

Meta broke ground on a 1-gigawatt, AI-optimized data centre in Sturgeon County, Alberta — its first facility in Canada and 33rd globally — representing over CAD $13 billion in direct investment plus roughly CAD $60 million for local roads and water infrastructure. Meta says it will fully fund new generation and grid infrastructure to serve the site, use a closed-loop dry-cooling system with near-zero operational water use, and match its power draw with 100% clean and renewable energy.

Why it matters: It's the clearest signal yet that Alberta has become the epicentre of Canada's data-centre boom, and Meta funding its own generation could become the template other hyperscalers use to get ahead of Canadian grid constraints.

3

Crusoe and Aalo Atomics to pair a nuclear microreactor with an AI data centre at Idaho National Lab

TechEnergy

Crusoe and Aalo Atomics announced a partnership to deploy a Crusoe Spark modular, shipping-container AI data centre powered directly by Aalo's 50MWe "Pod" — five sodium- and air-cooled Aalo-1 microreactors sharing one turbine — at Idaho National Laboratory in 2027 as a proof of concept. The companies say it would be the first data centre powered by an on-site nuclear microreactor and plan to roll the pairing out across Crusoe's portfolio by 2029.

Why it matters: Water-free, grid-independent microreactors sized to a single data centre — rather than utility-scale SMRs feeding the grid — would remove two of the biggest siting constraints (water and interconnection queues) facing AI campuses today.

PJM: new AI data centres must bring their own capacity or face first-in-line curtailment

PolicyEnergy

Grid operator PJM's board approved a rule that, starting June 1, 2027, any new "Large Load" of 50MW or more that hasn't secured its own generation or contracted supply will be first curtailed during capacity shortages. PJM framed the move as protecting existing ratepayers from bearing the cost of capacity built to serve rapidly growing AI and data-centre demand.

Why it matters: It's one of the most consequential grid-policy shifts of the year for data-centre developers in the largest U.S. grid region, likely accelerating the industry's pivot toward dedicated generation, storage and flexible-load contracts over simple grid interconnection.

Amazon raised its expected 2026 capital expenditure by $20 billion to roughly $220 billion, driven largely by higher memory costs, as AWS posted its fastest revenue growth since 2021 and a backlog that has swelled to $496 billion. Virginia regulators face August 1 deadline on who pays $1.5 billion in data-centre-driven transmission costs

Policy

Virginia's State Corporation Commission heard two days of arguments in mid-July on how to divide roughly $1.5 billion of Dominion Energy's Rider T-1 transmission costs between data centres and residential ratepayers, with a binding decision due by August 1. Governor Spanberger's office is pushing a "but for" standard assigning costs directly to the large loads that triggered the new infrastructure, while the data-centre industry says it already pays its legally allocated share.

Why it matters: As the country's largest data-centre hub, how Virginia resolves this fight will heavily influence cost-allocation fights now playing out in state utility commissions nationwide.

8

Virginia report: eastern groundwater can no longer support new data-centre withdrawals

Policy

A long-delayed Virginia DEQ study found that no location east of I-95 can support major new groundwater withdrawals under current permitting standards, meaning no new data centre using evaporative cooling could get a groundwater permit in the region. Virginia currently hosts 371 operating data centres with another 438 planned, and the report calls for tighter regulation as an aquifer already cut by more than half between 2014 and 2017 keeps declining.

Why it matters: It's the first comprehensive state study tying data-centre growth directly to a hard natural-resource ceiling, adding water scarcity to power and land as a binding constraint in the world's densest data-centre market.

Veolia to operate 350MW grid-independent microgrid for Ohio AI data-centre campus

EnergyTech

Veolia will operate and maintain a 350-megawatt microgrid — combining gas engines, linear generators and a 430MWh battery energy storage system — designed to supply 100% of the power for an AI data-centre campus in New Albany, Ohio, entirely independent of the traditional grid. The contract is expected to sustain 35–40 full-time positions and builds on Veolia's broader Data Center Resource 360 offering.

Why it matters: Fully grid-independent microgrids let developers bypass years-long interconnection queues entirely, and central Ohio's rapid build-out of this model could make it a template other constrained grid regions look to copy.
