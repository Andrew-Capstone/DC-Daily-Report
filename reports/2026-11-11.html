Riot leases entire 910 MW Corsicana campus to Anthropic in ~$28B, 15-year deal

EnergyFinanceConstruction

Riot Platforms signed a 15-year triple-net powered land lease for its whole Corsicana, Texas campus, 910 MW of critical IT capacity, with a frontier AI lab its 10-Q identifies as Anthropic. Average rent is about US$1.8B a year, with delivery in three phases from September 2027 to July 2028 and total capex of about US$5.5B. Riot says value could reach US$54B if both five-year extensions are exercised.

Why it matters: A bitcoin miner's power-rich site becomes one of the largest single AI leases yet, showing grid-connected megawatts are the scarcest asset.
Source → (w.media (Nov 9))
2

Venture Global plans up to 16 off-grid gas power islands, ~11.5 GW, for AI data centres in Louisiana

Energy

The LNG exporter has agreements with GE Vernova, GE Grid Solutions and Baker Hughes for up to 16 combined-cycle power islands of roughly 720 MW each at Cameron Parish and Plaquemines Parish sites, running independently of the public grid. It targets the first 250 MW of compute in late 2028, 5 GW by end-2031 and potentially 10 GW by 2034, ordering one plant before year-end.

Why it matters: Behind-the-meter gas at gigawatt scale is becoming a mainstream answer to grid delays; it is a plan matched to future customer commitments, not contracted capacity.
Source → (Venture Global Q3 remarks (Nov 10))
3

AEP and Google advance $4B, 702 MW Bloom fuel-cell project to power Cheyenne data centres

EnergyTech

AEP plans to invest about $4 billion in a 702 MW solid oxide fuel-cell facility near Cheyenne, Wyoming, supplied by Bloom Energy, with output sold to Google under a 20-year PPA. Construction is expected to start before the end of 2026, with full operations targeted for late 2028; pricing was not disclosed.

Why it matters: One of the largest fuel-cell deployments for data centres, offering a faster, on-site alternative to waiting for grid upgrades.
Source → (Data Center Frontier (Nov 4))

AMD ships 256-core Epyc SP7 and rolls out Helion AI racks through November

Tech

AMD's sixth-generation Epyc SP7, with up to 256 cores, is generally available and OEMs have begun shipping servers. Helion racks, built from AMD's agentic-AI blueprint, arrive this month in liquid- and air-cooled CPU-only and air-cooled CPU-plus-GPU inference versions. AMD claims up to 3.7x the performance of an Nvidia Vera CPU system, and OEMs have committed to more than 240 platforms.

Why it matters: Rack-scale CPU options for agentic AI widen supplier choice and shift power and cooling planning for inference halls.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 10))

Google adds TPU 8tx and 8i Max, and will let select customers run TPUs in their own data centres

Tech

TPU 8tx uses TPU 8t silicon with hardware-level encryption for secure inference and small-scale training, paired with custom AMD CPU hosts. TPU 8i Max packages two TPU 8i chips for double the compute and 384 GB of HBM. Google says select customers will be able to deploy TPU 8tx on premises, and Oracle Cloud will offer Google TPUs.

Why it matters: TPUs leaving Google's own facilities opens a new accelerator option for colocation and enterprise sites.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Nov 9))

TransAlta, Brookfield and CPP consult on Boreal Technology Hub, up to 1.6 GW west of Edmonton

CanadaEnergyConstruction

The partners are evaluating a data centre in Parkland County, directly south of TransAlta's Keephills Generating Station. Alberta's grid operator has granted 230 MW for an initial phase and the site could reach 1,600 MW at full build-out. About 180 people attended a first open house, with residents raising power, water and noise concerns.

Why it matters: A generator-adjacent site backed by two major Canadian institutional investors would be among Canada's largest campuses.
Source → (Canadian Press via JOC (Nov 6))

Alberta's gigawatt play: Meta's Sturgeon County campus and paired 932 MW gas plant move the province from plans to construction

CanadaEnergy

Data Centre West founder Vlad Oujegov says Alberta had under 100 MW of data centre load a few years ago. Meta's 1 GW AI campus in Sturgeon County, announced in July with over CAD $13 billion of investment and about 3,000 peak construction jobs, is paired with a 932 MW natural gas plant. The interview argues Alberta must also build the workforce and supply chain.

Why it matters: Alberta is Canada's leading gigawatt market, and on-site gas is its template.
Source → (Data Center Frontier (Nov 10))

Texas governor-elect Hinojosa pledges a moratorium on new data centre approvals

Policy

Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Nov. 3 race and has called for pausing data centre approvals until the Legislature passes protections. She takes office Jan. 19; Gov. Abbott has already frozen some data centre environmental permits and grid connections during an ERCOT queue audit expected to finish this year.

Why it matters: Texas is the largest US growth market; a prolonged pause would reshape siting decisions nationwide.
Source → (Texas Tribune (Nov 4))

Georgia election brings a reckoning: Gov.-elect Bottoms plans a pause and new PSC majority eyes power bills

Policy

Gov.-elect Keisha Lance Bottoms plans a temporary pause on new data centre approvals, and a new Democratic Public Service Commission majority plans an affordability review of power bills tied to data centres. Why it matters: Two top-tier Sun Belt markets now face state-level pauses at once.

OpenAI unveils a National Community Compact: pay for grid upgrades, no NDAs with officials, flexible loads

PolicyEnergy

OpenAI pledged to cover power and grid upgrade costs, cut use during peak demand, drop nondisclosure agreements with local officials, and publish construction plans, noise estimates and job projections before building. It urged Congress to make some commitments mandatory. The move came two days after midterm results favoured candidates backing limits on data centres.

Why it matters: Sets a de facto community-benefits baseline that rivals and regulators are likely to reference.
Source → (SiliconANGLE (Nov 5))

About two-thirds of new US data centre projects now target rural locations

Construction

Citing Pew Research data, the analysis says about two-thirds of new US projects target rural areas, though nearly nine-tenths of existing facilities sit in developed hubs like Northern Virginia, Texas and California. Drivers include land, simpler permitting and on-site power; fiber is a challenge but cheaper to extend than long-distance power.

Why it matters: Siting is following power, reshaping where construction labour and supply chains are needed.

Tech

Why it matters: Larger CDUs simplify liquid cooling for multi-megawatt AI halls.

Contractors can win data centre bids by planning early for the Investment Tax Credit

FinanceConstruction

An alliant analysis argues the federal ITC can materially improve economics for projects with on-site solar, batteries and microgrids, depending on sourcing, prevailing-wage and apprenticeship compliance, and documentation. The authors estimate early planning can raise the value contractors deliver from 6% to as much as 60%.

Why it matters: Opinion piece, but on-site energy assets are increasingly standard, so tax credits shape bids.
