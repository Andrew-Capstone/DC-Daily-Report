Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns; CFO Colette Kress named interim chief as analysts weigh the AI infrastructure roadmap

TechFinance

Nvidia said on Nov 12 that founder and CEO Jensen Huang had resigned, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO while the board weighs internal and external successors. It followed reports of an affair with an employee who reported to him and an earlier confirmed relationship with a person suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset. Analysts told Data Center Knowledge little changes for buyers near term, but execution 12 to 24 months out is the risk; Nvidia reports earnings next week.

Why it matters: Nvidia holds about 80% of the data centre AI accelerator market, so its succession is a supply-chain question for every AI build.

Anthropic reported as buyer in $3.4B deal for 1.7 GW of gas generators

EnergyFinance

Citing the Financial Times, Fortune reports Anthropic agreed to buy 1.7 GW of heavy-duty natural gas generators from San Antonio-based OnePWR Solutions for $3.4 billion, taking over OnePWR's Caterpillar commitments, with engineering and construction included. The generators are expected within two years; sites were not named. Anthropic's IPO filing lists $900.9 billion of compute and infrastructure commitments and flags gas price, pipeline and permitting risks. Neither company commented.

Why it matters: Described as the largest direct purchase of generation equipment by an AI company: labs are now buying their own power plants.
Source → (Fortune (Nov 10))

Pembina-linked company files for Lynx, a data centre of up to 3.6 GW beside Meta's Alberta campus

CanadaEnergyConstruction

The Logic reports a newly incorporated company linked to Pembina Pipeline bought about 134 acres in Sturgeon County in October and filed with Alberta's grid operator for Lynx, a data centre of up to 3.6 GW, plus two gas-fired plants targeting service in June 2031. That is more than three times Meta's 1 GW campus next door. The project is early in the connection process with no customer named, and Pembina declined to comment on the property.

Why it matters: Alberta's Industrial Heartland is turning into a multi-gigawatt gas-powered cluster, with provincial data centre legislation expected soon.
Source → (The Logic (Nov 11))
4

Oklo and Centrus finalize HALEU fuel supply for the 1.2 GW Ohio nuclear campus serving Meta

EnergyTech

Centrus and Oklo signed definitive agreements, following a June letter of intent, to supply high-assay low-enriched uranium for up to five Aurora powerhouses at Oklo's planned 1.2 GW Ohio campus. Deliveries from Centrus's Piketon, Ohio plant are scheduled to start in 2029. Meta's prepayment for future Oklo power funds the fuel purchase. No fuel quantity was disclosed.

Why it matters: Fuel is the main bottleneck for advanced reactors; a hyperscaler's prepayment is now financing domestic enrichment.
Source → (World Nuclear News (Nov 13))
5

Torus raises $500M and launches Foundry, a prefabricated 1 to 20 MW power-and-compute unit

TechFinanceEnergy

Salt Lake City energy-storage firm Torus raised $500 million led by Magnetar and launched Foundry, a prefabricated unit combining power, storage, cooling, networking and compute for sites of 1 to 20 MW. The company says units install at existing commercial and industrial sites in 12 to 16 weeks. Its GigaOne factory line is expected to reach 500 MW of IT load, expanding to 1 GW; Hydra Host is an early customer.

Why it matters: Small modular units that use spare capacity on existing grid connections sidestep the interconnection queue for inference.
Source → (Utah Business (Nov 12))

Finance

CanadaPolicyEnergy

14

Minneapolis council passes data centre restrictions 7-6; full ban fails

Policy

The Minneapolis City Council voted 7-6 on Nov 12 for an ordinance requiring council approval for any new data centre, limiting them to existing buildings above the second floor, and barring them as a building's primary use. Facilities over 50,000 square feet must pay into an Xcel Energy renewable program. The measure goes to Mayor Jacob Frey; MPR reported beforehand that he was expected to veto a council-approval requirement. The moratorium expires Nov 21.

Why it matters: If the ordinance is vetoed and nothing replaces it, the old zoning code applies when the moratorium lapses.
Source → (FOX 9 (Nov 12))

Policy

Three Effingham County residents sued county commissioners, the county manager, the industrial development authority, OpenAI and its Georgia affiliate, alleging a secret conspiracy to prepare a site for an OpenAI data centre. It is the third suit tied to the project. The Current reports a nondisclosure agreement signed in October 2025 and a memorandum under which OpenAI would become the county's largest taxpayer. Why it matters: NDAs and closed-door site deals are becoming legal liabilities for developers and local officials alike.

Boulder County caps data centres at 50 kilowatts, ending its moratorium with an effective ban

Policy

Boulder County commissioners voted unanimously to limit data centres in unincorporated areas to 50 kW, lower than the 75 kW the planning commission proposed. The rule replaces a moratorium adopted this summer and does not apply inside cities such as Boulder or Longmont. Nearby Larimer County has extended its own moratorium through February.

Why it matters: One of the strictest permanent size limits in the US, and a model other Colorado counties may copy.
Source → (KUNC (Nov 12))

Tech

Gates Industrial introduced the ThermalCool 2100SS, a 4U-compatible stainless steel pump for coolant distribution units delivering up to 200 litres per minute at 3 bar. Why it matters: Industrial suppliers keep entering the liquid-cooling component chain, easing a known supply constraint.

Ottawa, Kansas voters approve a 10-year ban on large data centres with about 72% support

Policy

Voters in Ottawa, Kansas approved a citizen-led ordinance on Nov 3 barring data centres drawing more than 25 MW for 10 years, with about 72% in favour. The campaign began after a 450 MW facility was proposed at Proximity Park; that project had already been dropped. Emporia and El Dorado approved limits in the same election.

Why it matters: Decade-long, voter-imposed bans are a harder barrier than the council moratoriums developers are used to.
