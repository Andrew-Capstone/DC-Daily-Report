Tuesday, October 6, 2026 · North America focus
Morning brief: Nuclear and on-site power dominated the day. Oracle would subscribe to up to 20 percent of Wisconsin's Point Beach nuclear output for its OpenAI-linked campus, Valar Atomics proposed a 456-reactor, 9.6-gigawatt nuclear data centre campus in Utah, and Enerflex landed a contract for about 450 megawatts of behind-the-meter gas generation. In Canada, Meta's 13-billion-dollar Alberta campus is backed by a 932-megawatt gas plant, Bell's Saskatchewan campus is growing to 1.2 gigawatts, and Ottawa's responsible-development principles set a national baseline. On the technology side, Exascale and Compal plan an 800-volt DC validation platform and Renesas launched new gallium nitride power devices. Financing stayed hot with Nscale's 3.36-billion-dollar raise, Akamai's 11.6-billion-dollar Anthropic deal and a reported 15-billion-dollar Bain bid for Edged, while Memphis voted on a moratorium and Amazon pledged a billion dollars to host communities. Several stories are from the past week, and some details come from search summaries because publisher pages blocked fetching.

Oracle would subscribe to 10-20% of Wisconsin's Point Beach nuclear output for its OpenAI-linked Port Washington campus

Energy

We Energies announced the subscription on October 2 to power Project Lighthouse in Ozaukee County. The utility says the arrangement could spare other customers about $300 million in fuel costs over five years, and the Wisconsin Public Service Commission must still approve it.

Why it matters: A nuclear subscription pitched as a ratepayer saver is a template other utilities may copy.

2

Valar Atomics proposes 456-reactor, 9.6 GW nuclear-powered data centre campus on Utah public land

EnergyTechPolicy

Project Beehive would span more than 9,000 acres near Price, Utah, with 25 MW helium-cooled reactors using TRISO fuel. Utah's trust lands agency approved a 640-acre lease on September 17, and construction could begin by year-end with first reactors in 2028 and full build-out by 2032, per search summaries.

Why it matters: The most ambitious nuclear-for-AI proposal yet, and a test of whether regulators can keep pace.
NPR → (Sept 30)

Meta's $13B+ Alberta AI campus is backed by a 932 MW behind-the-fence gas plant

CanadaEnergyConstruction

Meta's Sturgeon County campus, its first in Canada, is paired with the Greenlight Electricity Centre from Pembina, Morgan Stanley Infrastructure Partners and Kineticor, due in service in the second half of 2030. Capital Power will supply 250 MW from the second half of 2028, and Alberta says it has about 1.2 GW of spare capacity for large loads.

Why it matters: Alberta's bring-your-own-generation model is becoming Canada's template for gigawatt AI campuses.

Exascale Labs and Compal plan a U.S. native 800 VDC validation platform for GPU data centres

TechEnergy

The non-binding letter of intent combines solid-state transformer power conversion, production GPU servers, direct liquid cooling and modular data centre infrastructure to test 800 VDC end to end. Compal intends to acquire the Exascale-built portion after acceptance testing, subject to a definitive agreement.

Why it matters: 800 VDC is the next rack-power architecture for AI, and integrated testing is the missing step before volume production.

Renesas launches first low-voltage GaN FETs aimed at AI data centre power and 800 V HVDC systems

Tech

The four 100 V enhancement-mode devices claim 40-70% lower switching losses than silicon, up to 2x system power density and 1-3% higher efficiency, with MOSFET-compatible footprints. They are available now with evaluation boards.

Why it matters: Higher-efficiency power conversion is a direct lever on data centre energy use and rack density.
