FERC chair warns PJM faces a "grave legitimacy crisis," gives it until September to reform

EnergyPolicy

At a July 23 technical conference, FERC Chairman Laura Swett told PJM Interconnection — the nation's largest grid operator, spanning 13 mid-Atlantic and Midwest states carrying much of the country's data-centre load — that it faces a "grave legitimacy crisis" as some transmission owners openly discuss leaving the RTO. Swett gave PJM until the end of September to adopt governance and stakeholder-process reforms, including board independence and an expanded state role, or FERC will impose them itself, with a time-bound dispute-resolution forum set to convene in September to finalize a reform package.

Why it matters: PJM's territory hosts the country's densest concentration of data centres, so a regulator-forced governance overhaul could reshape how quickly — and under what rules — new AI campuses get connected to the grid across the entire mid-Atlantic.

Rigetti, HPE and Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center to build "TangleLab" hybrid quantum-classical testbed

Tech

Rigetti Computing, HPE and the Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center announced on July 27 a $5 million NSF-funded testbed called TangleLab that integrates a 9-qubit Rigetti Novera quantum processor with HPE ProLiant, DL385 and Cray high-performance computing and storage systems. Construction begins September 1, 2026 inside PSC's new data centre, with full operation targeted for 2027 to support research into hybrid quantum-classical workflows, AI and simulation.

Why it matters: It's a concrete, funded step toward running quantum processors directly alongside classical HPC and AI infrastructure inside a production data centre, rather than in an isolated lab.

3

PG&E's data-centre pipeline more than doubles to 12.7GW in a single quarter

Energy

PG&E told investors on its Q2 earnings call that its queue of proposed data-centre projects jumped to 12.7GW, up from 5.1GW in Q1, with 26 projects totalling 8.2GW having paid application fees. Only four projects, 490MW combined, have signed interconnection construction agreements so far, and CEO Patti Poppe said the utility is now prioritizing "quality" smaller loads under a gigawatt rather than chasing only the biggest hyperscale campuses.

Why it matters: A quarter-over-quarter doubling in California's largest utility territory shows the AI buildout accelerating even in a state better known for grid constraints and wildfire liability than cheap power.

Nebius details 1.2GW, 600-acre AI campus planned for Schuylkill County, Pennsylvania

EnergyConstruction

AI cloud firm Nebius, which closed on the roughly 600-acre Highridge Business Park site in Butler Township for $187.5 million in May, detailed plans for a six-building, 1.2GW campus backed by a dedicated energy service agreement with PPL Electric Utilities. An initial 260MW phase is targeted for October 2027, scaling toward 660MW by 2029, with a battery energy storage system for backup power and closed-loop liquid and air cooling to limit water use.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single-site AI campuses announced for Pennsylvania this year, adding to the state's bid to become a major East Coast hub for gigawatt-scale AI infrastructure.

Mississauga councillor to push one-year freeze on new AI data-centre applications

CanadaPolicy

Ward 9 Councillor Martin Reid is bringing a motion to Mississauga City Council on July 29 asking the city to pause new data-centre applications for one year while staff draft a dedicated planning framework, with at least six of eleven councillors reportedly supportive. The motion follows sustained opposition to Prologis's roughly 220,000-square-foot data-centre proposal near Tenth Line West and Argentia Road, one of the largest such facilities proposed in the Greater Toronto Area.

Why it matters: A pause in Ontario's second-largest city would add fresh momentum to the "study or pause first" pattern spreading through Canadian municipalities, even as Ottawa keeps courting hyperscale investment.

6

Ontario's grid operator briefs industry on new technical rules for connecting data centres

CanadaEnergyPolicy

The Independent Electricity System Operator held a public webinar on July 23 to walk industry stakeholders through new technical requirements for "large computational loads" — the IESO's term for data centres — connecting to the Ontario grid, following a draft posted in May and a stakeholder comment period. The rules set load-composition, voltage and equipment-data requirements aimed at preventing large AI loads from destabilizing system reliability, as data centres are projected to account for 13% of Ontario's new electricity demand by 2035.

Why it matters: Formal technical rules, rather than case-by-case negotiation, give Ontario a clearer and faster path to approve or reject data-centre connection requests as its queue keeps growing.

7

House defense bill provision would bar Chinese-made equipment from data centres on Pentagon land

Policy

A provision from Rep. Cory Mills (R-FL), included in the House Armed Services Committee's fiscal 2027 defense authorization bill, would bar the Pentagon from leasing land to private data-centre developers unless they agree not to install printed circuit boards, semiconductors or chipsets made in China, Russia, Iran or North Korea. Army officials have objected that the domestic industrial base can't yet supply some of that equipment, warning the rule would create a "federal land penalty" and could drive over $1.3 billion in private capital away from Army land; Rep. Rashida Tlaib has separately introduced a bill to ban private data centres on federal land altogether.

Why it matters: With the Pentagon actively courting private data-centre developers for its bases, a hardware-sourcing mandate this strict could reroute billions in planned investment away from federal land entirely.

Data Center Alley moves toward a moratorium as Loudoun rejects 3.25-million-square-foot campus

PolicyConstruction

Loudoun County's Planning Commission rejected Active Infrastructure's Spring Valley Technology Park application, which sought to rezone 325 acres along Evergreen Mills Road for 3.25 million square feet of data-centre space across ten buildings, three substations and a backup power facility. Separately, the Board of Supervisors voted 6-1 to direct staff to prepare options, ahead of a September 15 meeting, for pausing new data-centre applications, site plans and substations while the county attorney reviews whether a moratorium would hold up legally.

Why it matters: Loudoun is the world's biggest data-centre market by capacity, so even a legally uncertain move toward a pause there sends a far louder signal than a moratorium in a smaller jurisdiction.

9

Developer scraps East Texas data centre after failing to meet Governor Abbott's new standards

Policy

Diode Ventures withdrew its proposal for a data centre near Cedar Creek Lake in Henderson County, Texas, saying the site's proximity to a rural residential community "does not meet the higher standard we believe should guide data centre development." The project, expected to take 12 years to build out and draw up to 5 million gallons of lake water a day by its fifth year, fell short of executive directives Governor Greg Abbott sent the Public Utility Commission and ERCOT on June 10 to protect residents from data-centre expansion; Abbott called the withdrawal "the right thing."

Why it matters: It's a concrete case of a governor's directives, rather than a formal law, killing a specific project, showing how much leverage state executives now hold over site selection even in a famously developer-friendly state.

Compass Datacenters raises $413 million in asset-backed bonds

Finance

Compass Datacenters priced $413 million in new asset-backed securities across three tranches, secured against seven completed, operational data centres. S&P rated the sole rated tranche — the $50 million Class A-2-III notes — at A, citing a 1.69x debt-service coverage ratio and liquidity reserves of $28.3 million, $5.2 million and $18.5 million across the tranches.

Why it matters: Steady ABS issuance against already-operating facilities, rather than pre-leased future capacity, shows lenders growing comfortable treating stabilized data centres as a conventional fixed-income asset class.

Panattoni's 900-acre "Project Bluegrass" data centre campus proposed near Burgin, Kentucky

Construction

Panattoni Data Centers is seeking to develop "Project Bluegrass," a four-building data-centre campus on more than 900 acres of farmland near Burgin, roughly 28 miles southwest of Lexington, using a closed-loop cooling system to limit water use. City officials are weighing annexing about 500 acres for the project, and while an initial council reading passed, some residents argue the developer is routing around county-level zoning by using a small city that has no planning and zoning rules of its own.

Why it matters: It's the same routing-around-local-rules pattern seen in Georgia and elsewhere this month, this time surfacing in Kentucky's fast-growing but still lightly regulated data-centre pipeline.

14

NOAA moves its operational weather-forecasting supercomputers to Google Cloud

Tech

NOAA selected Google Cloud as the primary infrastructure provider for its Weather and Climate Operational Supercomputing System, becoming one of the first major national weather agencies to run operational numerical weather prediction directly on public-cloud HPC hardware. The migration, using Google's H4D virtual machines, is expected to run through 2027 and aims to speed up forecast-model development compared with NOAA's on-premise systems.

Why it matters: Moving life-safety-critical forecasting workloads into a hyperscaler's data centres is a notable vote of confidence in cloud HPC reliability, and a template other government weather services may follow.
