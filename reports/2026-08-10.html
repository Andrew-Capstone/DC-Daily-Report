Tesla and SpaceX commit $16.8 billion to build "Terafab" chip plant in Texas

TechConstructionFinance

Tesla and SpaceX will initially spend $16.8 billion on a more than 100-million-square-foot semiconductor fab in Grimes County, Texas, built next to a former coal-plant reservoir that will supply its cooling water. Elon Musk says the two companies together need to produce over a terawatt of compute a year — for Optimus robots, Cybercabs and orbital data centers — at a scale no existing chipmaker can supply, with total investment potentially reaching $119 billion across future phases.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single commitments yet to reshoring chip manufacturing specifically for AI and robotics workloads, and signals that hyperscaler-style vertical integration is now extending all the way back to fabrication.

2

AMD acquires Toronto-founded inference chip startup Taalas

TechCanadaFinance

AMD agreed to acquire Taalas, a startup founded in Toronto in 2023 by former AMD and Nvidia chip architect Ljubisa Bajic, which has raised $219 million to build chips that etch specific AI models directly into silicon rather than storing weights in HBM. AMD plans to fold the technology into its Instinct GPU, EPYC and Helios rack-scale roadmap to sharpen its position in the fast-growing inference market. Financial terms were not disclosed.

Why it matters: It's a Canadian-founded team's technology heading straight into a major chipmaker's core inference roadmap, and another sign that model-specific silicon — not just general-purpose GPUs — is becoming a serious competitive front.

3

Rep. Ro Khanna introduces federal "Data Center Bill of Rights"

Policy

California Democrat Ro Khanna introduced H.Res.1471, a sense-of-the-House resolution that would bar data centers from residential areas and within 2,500 feet of homes, schools and hospitals, preserve local governments' power to reject or pause projects without state preemption, and require impact reports on electricity demand, utility bills, noise and housing costs. It's a non-binding resolution and competes with a separate, more aggressive federal moratorium bill from Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez.

Why it matters: It shows opposition to data center siting has moved from local council fights to a organized federal push, giving communities a Congressional talking point even without the force of law.

NRG nears 1.2GW gas plant deal with unnamed hyperscaler despite Texas pause

Energy

NRG Energy says it has agreed on principal commercial terms with an unnamed global cloud and AI hyperscaler for a $3.2 billion, 1.2GW combined-cycle gas plant in Texas, with room to expand to 2.4GW, under a 15-year deal where over 95% of revenue comes from fixed capacity payments rather than market prices. Commercial operation is targeted for late 2029, and CEO Robert Gaudette said the "bring your own power" structure should insulate the project from Governor Abbott's new interconnection audit.

Why it matters: It's a real-time test of whether dedicated, off-grid-style generation deals can keep moving forward even as Texas freezes the conventional grid-interconnection pipeline.

5

Energy Vault signs its largest-ever deal: 1.25GW of power infrastructure for a hyperscaler AI campus

Energy

Energy Vault announced a strategic agreement to deploy 1.25GW of integrated power infrastructure — combining dispatchable Caterpillar-genset generation, battery storage, grid-forming inverters and AI-driven controls — for a hyperscaler AI data center in Texas, expecting to book roughly $500-600 million in revenue from it across the second half of 2026 and 2027. It's the largest single contract in the company's history.

Why it matters: It's another sign that "speed-to-power" providers who bundle generation, storage and software into a turnkey package are becoming a preferred route around slow utility interconnection queues.

AWS in talks to build next to a 4.5GW gas plant on Pennsylvania's old Homer City coal site

EnergyConstruction

Amazon Web Services is in discussions with Homer City Redevelopment about a data center campus next to its 4.5GW natural gas plant under construction on the 3,200-acre site of the former Homer City Generating Station, once Pennsylvania's largest coal plant, about 50 miles east of Pittsburgh. No definitive agreement is in place yet, but the talks fall within Amazon's broader $20 billion Pennsylvania investment pledge.

Why it matters: It's the second major hyperscaler-scale gas plant tied to a retired coal site in the state this month, underscoring how fast old fossil generation footprints are being repurposed for AI load.

Ontario's Nuclea Energy signs MOU to evaluate small modular reactors at US data centers

CanadaEnergyTech

Mississauga, Ontario-based Nuclea Energy signed a non-binding MOU with Miami's New Mining Co. to evaluate co-locating Nuclea's modular reactor technology at New Mining's behind-the-meter US data center sites, starting with engineering scoping for its current ~30MWe of load and scaling toward a cumulative 100MWe. Nuclea would provide reactor design and regulatory strategy; any actual generation would be built and operated by a separate licensed utility or nuclear operator.

Why it matters: It's a Canadian advanced-nuclear developer angling for a foothold in the US data center SMR market, which so far has been dominated by American players like Oklo, X-energy and Kairos.

8

Samsung Heavy Industries and Mousterian move floating data centers into engineering phase

TechConstruction

Samsung Heavy Industries and Texas-based Mousterian Corporation signed an engineering agreement to complete basic and production design for moored, floating data center units, each delivering 50MW of critical IT capacity, with initial deployments planned for Texas and other US markets. The design uses non-evaporative water cooling that consumes no potable water and discharges no process water, and the partners are targeting commercialization by Q2 2028.

Why it matters: Floating platforms sited next to existing generation could let operators skip land acquisition, permitting and water-supply fights that are increasingly stalling land-based projects.

Quantum Corridor, Ciena and Toshiba hit 1.6 Tb/s of quantum-safe encryption on a live US network

Tech

Quantum Corridor, Ciena and Toshiba say they completed the first 1.6 Tb/s quantum-safe optical encryption run on a live commercial network, between data centers in Chicago and Hammond, Indiana, using Ciena's WaveLogic 6 Extreme hardware alongside Toshiba's quantum key distribution technology and NIST-certified post-quantum algorithms.

Why it matters: It moves quantum-safe encryption from lab demonstrations to production-scale inter-data-center links, directly addressing "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks against today's traffic.

Finance

Las Vegas-based Switch, majority-owned by DigitalBridge and IFM Investors since an $11 billion take-private in 2022, has confidentially filed for a US IPO that could come as soon as November, with reported target valuations ranging from about $50 billion to $80 billion including debt. Bank of America, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley are underwriting, and Andreessen Horowitz co-founder Ben Horowitz is joining the board.

Why it matters: It would be one of the largest pure-play data center IPOs on record, testing public-market appetite for AI infrastructure exposure just as bond markets show signs of indigestion on the same sector's debt.

Texas data center pause puts 20% of the US pipeline at risk, BNEF finds, as Oncor's 300GW queue hangs in limbo

PolicyEnergy

BloombergNEF estimates Governor Abbott's interconnection pause puts roughly 20% of the entire US data center pipeline — about 49.8GW of Texas projects — at risk of delay, with potential revenue losses reaching $8 billion by early 2027 if 60% of the stalled capacity is AI-related. Separately, Oncor CEO Allen Nye said the utility's own service requests have grown to nearly 300GW, with ERCOT's new "Batch Zero" review process for large loads now on hold pending clarification from the governor's office.

Why it matters: It's the clearest early quantification of how much economic damage a single state's regulatory pause could inflict on the national AI buildout timeline.

Americans are rallying against data centers — but surprisingly few are actually getting built, CNN finds

Policy

A CNN analysis published August 6 finds that despite a wave of national protests and local moratoriums, the number of proposed AI data centers that are actually cancelled or permanently blocked remains relatively small compared to the total pipeline, with delay — not outright cancellation — the more common outcome of community opposition.

Why it matters: It complicates the narrative on both sides: opposition is real and growing, but so far it's reshaping timelines and terms more than it's stopping projects outright.

PolicyFinance

Why it matters: It's a federal attempt to keep AI-enabled research from becoming concentrated only at institutions that can already afford hyperscaler-grade compute.

TechPolicy
