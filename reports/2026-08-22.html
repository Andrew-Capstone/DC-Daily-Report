1

Micron commits $10 billion to break AI's memory bottleneck

TechFinance

Micron unveiled a $10 billion, decade-long U.S. research push into advanced memory technologies, memory-compute architectures and packaging, arguing that "data movement, not math, is where the energy goes" in modern AI accelerators. Groundbreaking on the associated research facilities is expected in 2027, with partnerships spanning the U.S., Europe, Japan, India, Singapore and Taiwan.

Why it matters: Memory, not compute, has become the binding constraint on AI data centres — this is the largest single bet yet that architectural innovation in memory, not just faster chips, is what unlocks the next generation of AI infrastructure.

2

Prometheus Hyperscale and Istmo unveil 2.5GW gas-powered campus in Reeves County, Texas

EnergyConstruction

Prometheus Hyperscale and Permian Basin energy firm Istmo Energy will build a Pecos, Texas campus starting at 1.5 gigawatts and scaling to 2.5 gigawatts, pairing behind-the-meter gas generation with co-located fractionation assets for what the companies call an unprecedented dual-fuel setup. First power is targeted for next year.

Why it matters: It's another sign that West Texas oil-and-gas infrastructure firms, not just utilities, are becoming primary power suppliers for AI campuses that can't wait for grid interconnection.

3

TVA approves separate, ~10% higher power rate for data centres

EnergyPolicy

The Tennessee Valley Authority's board approved a new wholesale rate class for data centres, moving them off manufacturing rates and onto terms that include an upfront capacity-commitment charge to fund grid upgrades. The change takes effect October 1 and is expected to raise data centre billing by roughly 10% on average, phased over three fiscal years, under TVA's "Ratepayer Protection Pledge" to keep the cost off household bills.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest U.S. public power providers formally separating data centre costs from residential rates, a template other utilities under similar political pressure are likely to copy.

4

FERC's six grid operators file overhauled rules for connecting AI "large loads"

PolicyEnergy

PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO New England and NYISO faced an August 17 federal deadline to either justify their existing interconnection tariffs or propose reforms, after FERC found the rules likely inadequate for handling data centres and other loads over 50 megawatts. The filings will build six separate regulatory records that FERC will use to decide what region-specific changes are required for network upgrades, cost allocation and co-located generation.

Why it matters: This is the federal government's most direct intervention yet into how AI data centres connect to the grid, and its outcome will shape interconnection queues nationwide for years.

5

Pennsylvania's Shapiro signs sweeping "GRID Requirements" executive order on data centres

Policy

Governor Josh Shapiro's August 18 executive order strips AI data centre proposals from the state's Fast Track permitting process and bars developers from using nondisclosure agreements, requiring a legally binding commitment to pay their own power costs, hire locally and meet water-conservation standards before state agencies will even review a permit.

Why it matters: It's one of the most restrictive state-level frameworks yet from a governor who previously courted data centre investment, showing how far the political ground has shifted even in pro-development states.

Canada: Alberta minister faces "fury" at second AI data centre town hall in Sturgeon County

CanadaPolicy

Technology Minister Nate Glubish was met with jeers and profanity at a Thursday town hall in Redwater, a day after a similarly hostile session in Lacombe, as Alberta's government tours the province to defend its AI data centre push, including Meta's $13-billion Sturgeon County campus. Residents pressed Glubish on the Smith government's use of the Charter's notwithstanding clause to advance projects, and he promised new setback rules to keep megaprojects away from residential areas.

Why it matters: Back-to-back hostile town halls show Alberta's government losing the public-messaging fight over its data centre strategy even as it accelerates approvals, with Premier Smith set to join a further session August 27.

Vantage Data Centers explores IPO at a $100 billion valuation, the largest ever for the sector

Finance

Vantage, backed by Silver Lake and DigitalBridge, is weighing a stock listing that could raise around $10 billion at roughly $100 billion — a valuation no data centre company has approached in a public offering — alongside a possible sale as an alternative exit path. The company has held informal talks with financial advisers as AI-driven demand keeps lifting sector valuations.

Why it matters: A successful listing at this scale would be a landmark test of public-market appetite for pure-play data centre exposure, and a benchmark every other operator eyeing an IPO will be measured against.

Policy

A new tally finds 80% of Democratic and Republican gubernatorial candidates — and 63% of all candidates — have staked out a stance on data centres ahead of November's midterms, which will decide 39 governorships. Most positions, across party lines, call for guardrails preventing utilities or developers from passing infrastructure costs onto ratepayers.

Why it matters: It's the clearest evidence yet that data centres have become a first-tier electoral issue nationwide rather than a local zoning dispute.

Zayo locks up Corning fiber supply for 15,000-mile AI network build-out with Nvidia

TechConstruction

Zayo expanded its supply agreement with Corning to reserve a material share of the fiber it needs through 2030 for a 15,000-route-mile long-haul expansion — 8,000 of them new or overbuilt — with Nvidia collaboration accounting for 24 of the program's 28 routes. Why it matters: The AI buildout's next bottleneck is increasingly the long-haul fiber connecting distributed data centres, not just the chips inside them, and locking up manufacturing capacity years in advance is becoming as strategic as securing power.

11

Data centre construction starts hit $81.5 billion for the year, already topping all of 2025

FinanceConstruction

ConstructConnect's tracking shows June construction starts totaled $22.3 billion, the second-highest monthly figure on record, pushing year-to-date spending through June to $81.5 billion — more than 2025's entire $72.5 billion and over three times 2024's full-year total. Median construction costs have risen 38% year-over-year to $570 per square foot, with North Carolina, Indiana, Illinois and Michigan each topping $10 billion in starts.

Why it matters: The raw scale of capital breaking ground — even before accounting for the $78.2 billion in late-stage projects still queued to start — shows the AI infrastructure boom is still accelerating, not plateauing.

DRAM prices hit record $42.45 a chip as AI data centres consume 70% of world memory output

Tech

A benchmark DDR4 memory chip hit an all-time-high $42.45 on August 7 as memory makers redirect fabrication capacity toward high-margin, AI-server-bound HBM and away from commodity DRAM and NAND flash. Why it matters: The AI buildout is now visibly reshaping consumer electronics pricing worldwide, the clearest sign yet that data centre demand is directly competing with, and squeezing, everyday computing hardware supply.
