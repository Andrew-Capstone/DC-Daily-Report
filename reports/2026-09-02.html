Fervo Energy signs world's largest enhanced-geothermal PPA with Google for Utah data centre

EnergyTech

Fervo Energy and Google signed a 396-megawatt power purchase agreement to enable continued development of the Cape Station enhanced geothermal systems project in Beaver County, Utah, delivered in four 99-megawatt sub-tranches beginning in Q3 2028 on a 15-year term. Google will use the carbon-free output as a foundational power source for a potential Utah data centre, and holds an option to expand its offtake by roughly 600 megawatts — nearly 1 gigawatt total — by June 2030.

Why it matters: It's the largest enhanced-geothermal PPA ever signed, showing next-generation geothermal emerging as a serious, always-on complement to solar, gas and nuclear for AI power.

Nvidia details 88-core Vera CPU, its first server processor built for agentic AI, at Hot Chips 2026

Tech

At Hot Chips 2026, Nvidia disclosed deep architectural detail on Vera, its in-house Arm server CPU built around 88 custom Olympus cores with spatial multithreading, eight LPDDR5X memory controllers and 1.2 TB/s of SOCAMM2 memory bandwidth. Nvidia positions Vera as part of the seven-chip, five-rack Vera Rubin platform alongside the Rubin GPU, BlueField-4 and Spectrum-6 networking.

Why it matters: It's Nvidia's clearest move yet into standalone server CPUs, aimed at eliminating infrastructure bottlenecks for agentic AI workloads and directly challenging AMD's EPYC line inside AI factories.

Solid-state transformers emerge as a fix for AI data centres' power-delivery bottleneck

TechEnergy

Solid-state transformers — which replace copper windings and cores with silicon-carbide power semiconductors and software-defined control — are gaining traction as a way to convert medium-voltage AC directly to 800V DC inside AI data centres, improving efficiency and shrinking equipment footprints. Most new installations are expected to adopt them by 2028, with vendors also pitching SSTs as a way to ease a medium-voltage transformer shortage that has stretched lead times to as long as three years.

Why it matters: Traditional transformer supply is one of the single biggest constraints on new data centre capacity right now, so a viable alternative technology could unlock delayed projects industry-wide.

Anthropic signs $35B cloud deal with Nvidia-backed Lambda for new Texas data centre

FinanceTech

Anthropic finalized a $35 billion computing agreement with Nvidia-backed cloud provider Lambda to secure roughly 350 megawatts of GPU capacity at a 525-acre Hut 8-developed campus in Nueces County, Texas, targeting initial energization in Q1 2027. Nvidia itself holds the lease on the site, putting the chipmaker in the unusual position of landlord to its own investment.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single compute commitments an AI lab has made to a neocloud, and it comes as Anthropic's revenue run rate tops $65 billion ahead of a potential IPO.

NANO Nuclear and Tillman Digital Gateway target 6GW of advanced reactor power for AI zones by 2040

Energy

NANO Nuclear Energy signed a non-binding strategic commercial framework with Tillman Global Holdings' Tillman Digital Gateway platform to deploy NANO's 15-megawatt KRONOS MMR high-temperature gas-cooled reactors across planned U.S. AI industrial zones, with Tillman financing and owning the power infrastructure under an independent-power-producer model. The companies are targeting at least 2 gigawatts of advanced nuclear capacity by the mid-2030s and 6 gigawatts or more by 2040.

Why it matters: It's another sign that data-centre developers are pre-committing to next-generation reactor technology years before commercial deployment, betting nuclear licensing catches up with AI power demand.

Lambda secures another $1B in private debt to buy Nvidia GPUs for its Microsoft lease

Finance

Neocloud provider Lambda secured $1 billion in short-term private debt, arranged by JPMorgan, to purchase Nvidia GPUs that it leases onward to Microsoft, coming just weeks after a separate $926 million senior secured loan facility. Lambda is reportedly also in talks for a $3 billion pre-IPO funding round.

Why it matters: It shows neoclouds leaning ever harder on leveraged debt to keep pace with hyperscaler GPU demand, a financing model regulators and credit markets are watching closely for signs of strain.

Canada: Saskatchewan requires new data centres to be Canadian-owned and bring their own power

CanadaPolicyEnergy

Saskatchewan unveiled a new data centre framework prioritizing Canadian ownership and data sovereignty, requiring proponents to demonstrate industry experience, self-supply power generation, create significant local jobs and route applications through a centralized provincial intake process. The province says Indigenous participation is "key" to future project approvals.

Why it matters: It's one of the most explicit "Canadian-ownership-first" data centre policies yet from a province, contrasting sharply with Alberta's more permissive, investment-first approach next door.

Trump administration weighs new tariffs that would strip data centre exemptions and hit servers

Policy

The Trump administration is drafting a second round of semiconductor tariffs that would eliminate the data-centre, startup and consumer-device exemptions in place since January and extend duties to servers, laptops and gaming consoles built with already-tariffed chips. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick reportedly favors tying tariff relief to a foreign company's investment in U.S. chip production.

Why it matters: Industry groups warn the move could raise costs for Nvidia, AMD and server makers, cut GDP by roughly $90 billion annually, and delay data centre projects already squeezed by transformer and power shortages.

California judge orders full environmental review of 330MW Imperial County data centre

Policy

Imperial County Superior Court Judge L. Brooks Anderholt tentatively ordered Imperial County to set aside its approvals for Imperial Valley Computer Manufacturing's proposed 330-megawatt, 950,000-square-foot data centre and conduct a full Environmental Impact Report, ruling the county improperly relied on a 2017 review that never addressed the project's gas generation, battery storage, substation and cooling infrastructure.

Why it matters: It's a significant win for California environmental-review requirements over data centre developers, and a template other counties facing similar suits may be forced to follow.

California legislature passes bill requiring data centres to cover their fair share of grid costs

PolicyEnergy

California's legislature unanimously approved SB 1168 — 38-0 in the Senate after a unanimous Assembly vote — directing the Public Utilities Commission to ensure data centres pay for their share of transmission and distribution upgrades and non-emitting power procurement, rather than passing those costs on to residential ratepayers. The bill now awaits Governor Newsom's signature.

Why it matters: It's a rare bipartisan, unanimous state response to the ratepayer-cost-shifting concerns driving moratorium fights nationwide, and could become a model for other states' rate-design rules.

11

Missouri and Kansas towns unite across party lines against data centre developers

Policy

Grassroots opposition to proposed data centres is spreading across Missouri and Kansas, with Festus, Missouri voters ousting pro-data-centre city council members in March, St. Charles passing a one-year moratorium on new applications in August, and Tonganoxie, Kansas residents packing a Leavenworth County planning meeting to fight the proposed "Project Bluestem." Organizers say the coalition spans both Democrats and Republicans, united by concerns over water use, electricity costs and property values.

Why it matters: It underscores that data centre opposition has become a genuinely bipartisan, hyper-local phenomenon rather than a partisan or coastal one, complicating siting decisions across the Midwest.

Kingspan taps bond market as AI data centre demand lifts profit outlook

Finance

Irish building-materials firm Kingspan is seeking roughly €800 million ($925 million) from a two-part green bond sale after its ADVNSYS data-centre infrastructure unit — which makes liquid-cooling and air-handling equipment — grew first-half sales 34% with order backlog more than doubling. Kingspan has raised its full-year trading profit guidance to about €1.13 billion on the strength of that demand.

Why it matters: It's further evidence that AI data centre cooling demand is now a material earnings driver for traditional industrial manufacturers well outside the core tech supply chain.

US data centre construction starts hit $84.1B year-to-date, nearly triple last year's pace

ConstructionFinance

ConstructConnect's latest data centre report puts year-to-date US construction starts at $84.1 billion through July, nearly three times the level of a year ago, even as the sector's share of total nonresidential building spending slipped from almost 30% to under 25%. North Carolina ($10.4 billion) and Texas ($9.9 billion) lead all states, with the South accounting for 71% of planned year-end spending.

Why it matters: Costs per square foot are up 57% year-over-year, a sign that even as the buildout accelerates, land, equipment and labor constraints are pushing every new project's price tag sharply higher.
