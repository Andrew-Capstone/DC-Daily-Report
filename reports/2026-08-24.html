Samsung unveils zHBM, a 3D memory architecture stacked directly on AI accelerators

Tech

At the Future of Memory and Storage conference, Samsung showed concept models of zHBM, which vertically stacks high-bandwidth memory directly above an AI accelerator rather than beside it. Samsung says the design points toward roughly eight times the performance of HBM5, more than ten times the memory density, tripled energy efficiency and less than half the thermal resistance once paired with new wafer-bonding technology.

Why it matters: Memory bandwidth, not just compute, is becoming the binding constraint on AI training speed — a workable path to stacking memory on the chip itself could reshape how the next generation of AI accelerators and data centre racks are designed.

2

TerraPower taps Hyundai E&C to build up to eight Natrium small modular reactors

EnergyTech

TerraPower signed agreements naming Hyundai Engineering & Construction as EPC contractor for up to eight of its 345-megawatt Natrium reactors, with completion, price and performance guarantees meant to make the reactors financeable through conventional commercial debt. The deal, alongside a parallel agreement with SK Innovation, is designed to turn the sodium-cooled Natrium design into a repeatable, fleet-scale construction process across the US and South Korea.

Why it matters: Natrium is the reactor line Meta has already committed to for up to eight plants to power AI data centres by 2035; a proven, price-guaranteed builder is the missing piece that turns SMR promises into an actual power supply for hyperscale campuses.

3

Canada: Alberta moves to effectively bar new data centres from using renewable power

CanadaEnergyPolicy

Alberta's electric system operator is proposing a rule requiring any new large load over 75 megawatts, including data centres, to be fully self-sufficient for power — a bar that, under current technology and pricing, only gas-fired generation can clear economically. A single data centre the size of Meta's approved Sturgeon County campus would emit roughly three million tonnes of CO2 a year, about 10% of Alberta's current electricity-sector emissions, and industry estimates suggest the added demand could triple residential power rates.

Why it matters: It locks Alberta's data centre boom into natural gas by regulatory design just as the province markets itself as Canada's AI hub, setting up a direct clash between Premier Danielle Smith's economic pitch and the province's own emissions and affordability goals.

4

Gas turbine prices set to nearly triple as AI data centre demand overwhelms supply

EnergyFinance

Data centre operators building behind-the-meter gas plants to bypass grid interconnection queues are bidding up heavy-duty turbine prices, with wait times across GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power's H, J and HA-class frames now stretching five to seven years. GE Vernova alone is targeting a 116-gigawatt-plus order backlog by year-end, roughly a fifth of it tied directly to data centres.

Why it matters: A gas turbine shortage is now as real a bottleneck on data centre construction as chips or transformers, and the resulting price spike is flowing straight into the cost of every behind-the-meter power plant hyperscalers are racing to build.
The Motley Fool →
5

Dell'Oro raises data centre capex forecast to surpass $3 trillion by 2030

Finance

Dell'Oro Group nearly doubled its 2030 global data centre capital-expenditure outlook from its January forecast, citing higher hyperscaler spending guidance, rising commodity costs and expanding power capacity assumptions. AI accelerators are projected to account for roughly a third of the total, with the four largest US cloud providers alone expected to represent about half of global data centre capex.

Why it matters: A near-doubling of a five-year-old forecast in just seven months shows how fast Wall Street's baseline expectations for the AI buildout keep resetting upward, with real consequences for how much capital, power and land the industry will need to find.

6

Hudson River Trading signs multibillion-dollar CoreWeave deal for AI compute

TechFinance

Quantitative trading firm Hudson River Trading signed a multi-year, multibillion-dollar agreement with CoreWeave for compute clusters built on Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 and HGX B200 GPU systems, supported by Spectrum-X Ethernet networking, to power its trading research and model development. It extends an existing partnership between the two firms.

Why it matters: A non-AI-native financial firm signing a multibillion-dollar neocloud contract shows demand for CoreWeave-style GPU capacity is broadening well beyond frontier AI labs, reinforcing the revenue base behind the wave of debt financing built on CoreWeave leases.

7

InfraTech Capital advances $2.7 billion, 5,000-acre data centre campus in the Texas Panhandle

ConstructionFinance

InfraTech Capital is assembling a consortium of institutional investors to fund a roughly $2.7 billion, 5,000-acre-plus data centre campus in Carson County, Texas, near Amarillo, with a major hyperscaler expected to serve as anchor tenant pending due diligence. The plan also includes funding new fiber-optic manufacturing lines with a global cable partner to supply the project's connectivity needs.

Why it matters: It extends the Texas data centre buildout beyond the state's established hubs into the Panhandle, and the bundled fiber-manufacturing investment shows developers increasingly having to fund their own supply chains to keep projects on schedule.

8

Texas regulators push ERCOT's data centre grid-classification deadline to August 31

PolicyEnergy

The Public Utility Commission of Texas approved ERCOT's request for a good-cause exception to its original August 7 deadline for classifying large loads — mostly data centres — as Base, Studied or Batch Zero Ineligible, granting ERCOT until August 31 to conditionally classify them. The extension stems directly from Governor Abbott's early-August order for a comprehensive audit of projects in the interconnection queue.

Why it matters: It's the first concrete regulatory mechanics behind Abbott's audit directive, showing how a single gubernatorial order is now reshaping the technical rulebook ERCOT uses to decide which of the roughly 300 mostly-data-centre projects get grid access and when.
Baker Botts →
9

Louisville Planning Commission moves to require rezoning and closed-loop cooling for data centres

Policy

Following four public hearings dominated by residents demanding tighter rules, Louisville's Planning Commission voted to require nearly all new data centres to go through a public rezoning process, mandate closed-loop cooling systems to limit water use, and expand required buffer distances from homes, schools, nursing homes and hospitals. The commission declined to open downtown commercial zones to data centre development.

Why it matters: It adds substantive, enforceable design and siting requirements on top of the six-month moratorium Louisville's council already passed, giving the city some of the strictest data centre rules of any major US metro once finalized.
Louisville Public Media →
10

California data centre's fight for Colorado River water heads to Imperial County court

Policy

Imperial Valley Computer Manufacturing is suing the Imperial Irrigation District after it denied the developer's request for roughly 260 million gallons a year of Colorado River water to cool what would be California's largest data centre, a 330-megawatt facility. The case remains unresolved as Imperial County's board has extended its own moratorium on new data centre approvals through June 2027 while it rewrites zoning rules.

Why it matters: It's a concrete test of whether drought-stressed water agencies in the US Southwest can legally refuse to serve AI data centres, with implications for every developer eyeing water-scarce regions for cheap land and power.
