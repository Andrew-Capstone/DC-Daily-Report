Data Centre Weekly Overview

Weekly Overview

September 8 – 14, 2026 · North America focus
Week in review: Power itself was the week's central story: Georgia regulators approved Georgia Power's 3.2-gigawatt OpenAI contract — one of the largest data-centre power deals ever cleared by a US utility commission — while the Department of Energy closed a $1.9 billion loan to restart Iowa's Duane Arnold nuclear plant for Google, and gigawatt-scale builds advanced in Pennsylvania and Texas. Capital kept flowing at a startling pace, with Oracle raising its capex guidance to $90–95 billion, Microsoft disclosing plans to triple its footprint to 38 gigawatts by 2032, Qualcomm landing a $60 billion custom-chip deal with Amazon, and the Pentagon negotiating a $5 billion loan to shore up the domestic supply chain. Canada moved from talk to formal policy on several fronts at once: Saskatchewan now requires new data centres to be Canadian-owned and self-powered, Ontario's Data Centre Playbook consultation closed, Anthropic signaled a serious Alberta buildout, Meta detailed its $13 billion Sturgeon County campus, and Mississauga heads toward a final vote that would make it the first Canadian city to freeze new data centres outright. Underneath the deal-making, technology kept pushing forward — IonQ's Superion 256 quantum platform, TSMC's roadmap for chip-level liquid cooling as AI power demand could rise sixfold, a DRAM/HBM4 supply squeeze, and Google and Blackstone's Crux AI neocloud going live — even as antitrust regulators opened a formal probe into Nvidia's Groq deal. And running through nearly every daily edition this week, local and political opposition kept compounding, from statehouse bill counts hitting record highs to a state governor's race being reshaped by the fight over who pays for the buildout.

Georgia PSC clears Georgia Power's 3.2GW OpenAI contract, cites $950M in annual customer savings

Energy

Georgia Public Service Commission staff approved Georgia Power's 25-year electricity contract with OpenAI for a 3.2-gigawatt hyperscale data centre in Effingham County, with power delivered in phases from 2028 to 2032. Georgia Power says OpenAI will cover the full infrastructure and service costs and provide financial assurances, and that the contract is part of a large-load portfolio expected to deliver about $950 million in annual savings, cutting a typical residential bill by at least $15 a month starting in 2029; watchdog groups Sierra Club and the Southern Alliance for Clean Energy say the protections still don't go far enough.

Why it matters: At 3.2GW — enough to power Atlanta three times over — it's one of the largest single data-centre power contracts ever approved by a US regulator, and its ratepayer-savings structure is being watched as a template by other state commissions.

DOE closes $1.9B loan to restart Iowa's Duane Arnold nuclear plant for a Google power deal

EnergyTech

The Department of Energy's Office of Energy Dominance Financing closed a loan of up to $1.9 billion to NextEra Energy to restart the 615-megawatt Duane Arnold Energy Center in Linn County, Iowa, which shut down in 2020 after 45 years as the state's only nuclear plant. The restart is anchored by a 25-year power purchase agreement with Google, which will buy the plant's output as 24/7 carbon-free power for its Iowa cloud and AI operations, with a target return to service in the first quarter of 2029 pending NRC approvals.

Why it matters: It's one of the clearest examples yet of federal financing being used to bring a shuttered reactor back specifically to meet hyperscaler demand, and an economic study projects it will generate more than $9 billion in value for Iowa over 25 years.
U.S. Department of Energy → (Sept 8)

Oracle's cloud infrastructure revenue surges 121% as it raises capex guidance to $90–95 billion

FinanceTech

Oracle reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $19.3 billion, up 30% year over year, with cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubling to $7.4 billion — the ninth consecutive quarter of infrastructure-revenue acceleration. The company raised its full fiscal 2027 revenue target to at least $90 billion and reaffirmed capital spending of $90–95 billion, driving quarterly capex to $28 billion and negative free cash flow of $5 billion as it races to build out data-centre capacity for OCI and Stargate commitments.

Why it matters: It's one of the clearest financial confirmations yet that AI data-centre construction has become the dominant driver of a legacy enterprise-software company's entire growth story, and the scale of the capex reaffirms just how much cash the industry is burning to keep pace with demand.

Microsoft plans to triple data-centre capacity to 38 gigawatts by 2032

TechFinance

Bloomberg reports Microsoft's internal roadmap calls for total data-centre power capacity to surpass 38 gigawatts by 2032, up from roughly 12 gigawatts today, with AI-specific silicon growing from about 2 gigawatts to roughly a third of the total. The push follows capacity bottlenecks that forced Azure to turn away paying customers across 2025 and into 2026 — Temu reportedly signed with Oracle instead after Microsoft couldn't supply capacity — and comes alongside guidance for $50 billion of capital spending in fiscal Q1 2027 alone.

Why it matters: It's one of the most concrete long-range capacity targets any hyperscaler has disclosed, and a direct admission that lost business — not just AI ambition — is now driving the scale of the buildout.

Qualcomm signs deal worth up to $60 billion to supply Amazon with custom AI chips

TechFinance

Qualcomm and Amazon announced a multi-generational partnership under which AWS could purchase up to $60 billion in custom AI inference silicon and related optical connectivity technology (scaling to 1.6T links). Qualcomm issued Amazon a warrant to acquire 25 million shares at $161.26 each, vesting as purchase orders are placed and fulfilled; Qualcomm stock jumped roughly 10% on the news.

Why it matters: It's Qualcomm's first major Western hyperscaler win and a direct shot at Nvidia and Broadcom's dominance of data centre silicon, adding a third credible custom-chip supplier to AWS's stack alongside in-house Trainium.
CNBC → (Sept 8)

Aligned Data Centers breaks ground on 2GW, $10 billion Project Phoenix in Pennsylvania

EnergyConstruction

Aligned Data Centers held a groundbreaking for Project Phoenix, a three-facility, 2-gigawatt campus at the Shippingport Industrial Park in Beaver County, Pennsylvania, representing roughly $10 billion in regional investment. The site draws dedicated onsite power from a redeveloped former power station being converted into an advanced natural-gas energy campus rather than the local grid, uses closed-loop cooling requiring zero operational water, and is expected to support about 3,000 construction jobs and 640 permanent positions.

Why it matters: It's a template increasingly favored by developers under grid-capacity strain — self-generated power plus water-free cooling — that sidesteps two of the biggest community objections (grid stress and freshwater use) driving moratoriums elsewhere.

7

IonQ unveils Superion 256, its sixth-generation quantum computing platform

Tech

IonQ launched Superion 256 at its 2026 Investor Day: a 256-qubit trapped-ion quantum computer built around Electronic Qubit Control, which uses on-chip semiconductor electronics rather than laser systems to control the qubits. The company has already fabricated its first fully integrated 256-qubit processors at its SkyWater subsidiary and is accepting orders now, with customer deliveries beginning in 2027.

Why it matters: IonQ says the shift from laser- to semiconductor-based control could cut cost-per-qubit by more than 300x across its roadmap, a manufacturing breakthrough aimed at making fault-tolerant quantum systems producible at the volumes data-centre-scale deployment would require.
IonQ → (Sept 8)
8

TSMC eyes chip-level microchannel cooling as AI system power could rise sixfold in five years

Tech

TSMC's advanced-packaging R&D director told SEMICON Taiwan that total AI system power could rise roughly sixfold over the next five years, pushing future CoWoS packages from about 600W today toward 4,100W, and said the foundry's roadmap now includes microchannel cooling built directly into the chip package to move heat away fast enough. TSMC has already tested vehicles dissipating over 5 kilowatts using CoWoS-R structures, with manufacturing repeatability and coolant-leak risk the main open engineering questions.

Why it matters: As the dominant foundry for Nvidia, AMD and other AI chipmakers serving North American hyperscalers, TSMC's cooling roadmap effectively sets the thermal ceiling for how dense next-generation US and Canadian AI data centres can get.

9

Saskatchewan requires new data centres to be Canadian-owned and bring their own power

CanadaPolicy

Saskatchewan's new data-centre framework requires that any new proponent be a Canadian-owned company and supply its own electricity rather than draw on the provincial grid, so new projects don't add pressure to the system or displace interprovincial export capacity. More than 30 new data-centre applications are now in the province's queue to be assessed under the rule; Bell's existing Saskatchewan data centre, which runs on SaskPower's grid, is not subject to it.

Why it matters: It's among the strictest ownership and self-supply requirements adopted by any Canadian province so far, framing data and AI infrastructure explicitly as a matter of national sovereignty rather than just an economic-development opportunity.
BNN Bloomberg → (Aug 27)
10

Ontario's public consultation on its Data Centre Playbook closes

CanadaPolicy

Ontario's 30-day comment period on its draft Data Centre Playbook — released August 13 — ended September 12. The framework would require large data centres to pay the full cost of the electricity they use, establish a separate electricity rate class for projects over one megawatt, and deliver community and economic benefits in exchange for provincial support; some Hamilton and Burlington councillors criticized the consultation window as too short and too transparent-light.

Why it matters: Ontario is the largest Canadian jurisdiction to attempt a formal cost-recovery and benefits framework for data centres rather than a blanket moratorium, and the closed comment period sets up the province's final rules ahead of its AI Industrial Strategy this fall.
CBC News → (Sept 12)

Anthropic job postings signal it's eyeing Alberta for a Canadian data centre buildout

CanadaFinance

Anthropic has posted new roles for a Canadian compute country lead — tasked with "bringing gigawatts of compute online in Canada" and handling site selection, leasing and financing — and a data centre community engagement manager based in Alberta. Anthropic confirmed the postings to BetaKit but declined to elaborate on its Canadian strategy or say why it is specifically targeting Alberta, and has not committed to a site, investment figure or timeline.

Why it matters: It's the clearest signal yet that a major AI lab is preparing a serious Canadian buildout, and Alberta's welcoming grid-connection policy makes it the leading contender among the provinces.

Meta promotes $13 billion Alberta data centre buildout at industrial conference

CanadaConstruction

Meta used the Alberta Industrial Heartland Association's annual conference to publicize progress on its $13 billion Sturgeon County campus, with community engagement manager Matt Sexton citing 3,000 construction jobs, 300 permanent positions and more than 25 million work hours, plus $60 million in local infrastructure commitments. The one-gigawatt facility will eventually draw power from the Greenlight Electricity Centre, a natural-gas plant coming online in 2030.

Why it matters: It's Meta's most detailed public accounting yet of the jobs and local spending behind its largest data centre outside the US, at a moment when Canadian communities are increasingly skeptical of exactly these claims.
BetaKit → (Sept 10)
13

Mississauga set to vote on what would be Canada's first data-centre moratorium bylaw

CanadaPolicy

Mississauga city staff are bringing an Interim Control By-law before council for approval on September 16 that would prohibit new large-scale digital-infrastructure development — AI, cloud and hyperscale data centres — for up to a year, following council's 11-0 vote in July directing staff to prepare it. The pause is meant to give the city time to complete a comprehensive review of impacts on municipal infrastructure, the environment, noise, and energy and water consumption before updating its Official Plan and zoning bylaw.

Why it matters: If approved, it would be the first bylaw of its kind in Canada, a bellwether for how other fast-growing Ontario municipalities weighing similar measures decide to handle data-centre pressure on land and grid capacity.

Pentagon in talks to lend AI cloud startup Fluidstack $5 billion

FinancePolicy

The Department of War's Office of Strategic Capital is negotiating a $5 billion loan to Fluidstack, a New York-based AI infrastructure startup that operates GPU clusters and data centres, to strengthen U.S. manufacturing capacity and supply chains for data-centre components. The loan would be six times the size of Fluidstack's recent $830 million Series A round and by far the largest the OSC has issued to date.

Why it matters: It signals the Pentagon now treats AI data-centre supply chains as strategic infrastructure worth direct federal financing, not just a commercial buildout for hyperscalers to fund themselves.

Google and Blackstone's TPU neocloud officially launches as Crux AI

TechEnergyFinance

Crux AI, the joint venture first unveiled in May pairing Google's Tensor Processing Units with Blackstone-managed capital, officially launched under its new brand with a $5 billion initial commitment from Blackstone funds and a target of deploying its first 500 megawatts of capacity in 2027, scaling toward 2 gigawatts. The company is led by longtime Google infrastructure executive Benjamin Treynor Sloss and has hired Meta's former data-centre engineering head, Alan Duong.

Why it matters: It formalizes Google's strategy of selling TPU capacity to third parties through an independently branded neocloud, giving enterprises a non-Nvidia path to AI compute backed by one of the largest infrastructure investors in the world.

16

DOJ opens antitrust probe into Nvidia's $20 billion Groq "licensing" deal

TechPolicy

The Department of Justice sent Nvidia a formal information request over its roughly $20 billion arrangement with AI chip startup Groq, structured as a nonexclusive licensing agreement rather than an acquisition even though Groq's founder and several key engineers moved to Nvidia and its low-latency chips were folded into Nvidia's AI-factory architecture. Antitrust officials are examining whether the "reverse acqui-hire" structure was used specifically to avoid the merger review a conventional takeover would trigger, a question that could expose similar deals by Microsoft, Google and Meta to retroactive scrutiny.

Why it matters: It's the first serious legal test of the acqui-hire playbook every major AI lab and chipmaker has used since 2023 to consolidate talent and technology without a formal merger filing, and could reshape how future AI infrastructure deals get structured.
SDxCentral → (Sept 10)
17

Samsung and SK Hynix's memory inventories fall below 10 days as HBM4 devours DRAM capacity

Tech

Finished DRAM inventory at Samsung and SK Hynix — which together control roughly 64% of global DRAM revenue — fell below 10 days of supply on September 7, a level analysts say is unseen in prior shortage cycles. HBM4 production for Nvidia's next AI-accelerator platform consumes roughly three times the wafer capacity of conventional DRAM, and Samsung alone is now running about 150,000 DRAM wafers a month through its HBM lines, with roughly half dedicated to HBM4.

Why it matters: Up to 70% of memory chips made globally in 2026 are destined for AI data centres; the resulting squeeze is already pushing memory prices sharply higher and threatens to become a hard ceiling on new AI-accelerator deployments through 2027.
Tech Times → (Sept 7)
18

SLB presses ahead with $4.1 billion Kelvion acquisition to expand data-centre cooling business

TechFinance

Oilfield-services giant SLB is acquiring German thermal-management maker Kelvion from Apollo-managed funds for about $3.4 billion in cash plus roughly $700 million in assumed debt, a total transaction value of $4.1 billion. Data centres are Kelvion's largest and fastest-growing segment, and SLB's CEO said the deal more than doubles the company's revenue opportunity per gigawatt of delivered data-centre capacity; the transaction is expected to close in the first half of 2027.

Why it matters: It's another sign that heavy industrial and energy companies see AI-driven thermal management as a bigger growth market than their traditional businesses, further consolidating the data-centre cooling supply chain.

NRG's 1.2GW Texas gas plant advances through ERCOT's Batch Zero review

Energy

NRG's proposed 1.2-gigawatt, $3.2 billion "bring your own power" combined-cycle gas plant — backed by a 15-year, investment-grade hyperscaler contract — has been conditionally included as a studied load in ERCOT's Batch Zero large-load interconnection process. The project, targeted for late-2029 commercial operation and expandable to 2.4 gigawatts, is moving forward even as Texas Governor Greg Abbott's order for a statewide data-centre verification review has delayed Batch Zero's broader timeline.

Why it matters: It's an early test of whether the "bring your own power" model can keep gigawatt-scale gas projects moving through Texas's grid queue even as the state tightens oversight of data-centre power demand.

Data centre backlash becomes a defining issue in the 2026 US midterm campaigns

Policy

Bloomberg reports that as data centre spending hits new highs in 2026, grassroots opposition to the buildout — over rising electricity bills, water use and land impacts — has moved from a niche local issue to a defining topic ahead of November's midterms, with candidates across the political spectrum reshaping their messaging in response. The Washington Post separately found state legislators introduced 375 data-centre-related bills in 2026 through mid-July, versus 243 in all of 2025, while nearly 700 local bans have now been proposed nationwide and Maryland's data-centre fight is reshaping Governor Wes Moore's re-election race.

Why it matters: When data-centre siting becomes a national electoral issue rather than a local zoning fight, it raises the odds of federal-level intervention — for or against the industry — after the vote.
