Data Centre Weekly Overview

Weekly Overview

September 1 – 7, 2026 · North America focus
Week in review: Community opposition delivered its biggest win yet when Brookfield and Blackstone abandoned the $100 billion "Digital Gateway" campus in Virginia — the largest data centre cancellation on record — even as the industry's financing kept accelerating, with Anthropic disclosing $517 billion in compute agreements ahead of a reported near-$2 trillion IPO, Broadcom posting a record AI-chip quarter, and Vantage Data Centers and SB Energy each pursuing IPOs above $50 billion. Genuine technology breakthroughs multiplied: an electricity-free elastocaloric cooling prototype, Samsung's zero-water floating data centres for Texas, IBM's faster Nighthawk r2 quantum processor, and Nvidia's near-waterless 45°C cooling design all point to engineering answers for the sector's power and water strain, while Fervo Energy and Google signed the largest enhanced-geothermal power deal ever for a Utah campus and PJM pressed FERC to make new large loads bring their own generation or face curtailment. Canada had a landmark week of its own: Ottawa unveiled national Responsible Data Centre Development Principles signed by 23 companies including Amazon, Google, Meta and Anthropic, CPP Investments and Equinix closed a $4 billion Nordic acquisition, Montreal's 5C topped $1.4 billion raised for US campuses, and fresh reporting exposed both the cost of Alberta's power bargain to residents and a transparency scandal around Kevin O'Leary's "Wonder Valley" project.

1

Brookfield and Blackstone abandon $100B "Digital Gateway" data centre campus in Virginia

PolicyConstruction

Compass Datacenters (backed by Brookfield) and QTS (owned by Blackstone) both withdrew from the Digital Gateway project near Manassas National Battlefield Park — a planned 37-building, 800-plus-acre campus demanding 3.5 gigawatts of power. Virginia courts voided the project's zoning approval after finding public notices for a marathon 27-hour hearing were spaced every three days instead of the required six, a procedural defect residents and groups including the Sierra Club spent five years fighting to expose.

Why it matters: It's the largest data centre cancellation on record, and it shows a narrow procedural win — not just political pressure — can unwind a $100 billion, gigawatt-scale campus, a playbook now spreading to contested sites nationwide.

Fervo Energy signs world's largest enhanced-geothermal PPA with Google for a Utah data centre

EnergyTech

Fervo Energy and Google signed a 396-megawatt power purchase agreement enabling continued development of the Cape Station enhanced geothermal systems project in Beaver County, Utah, delivered in four 99-megawatt sub-tranches beginning in Q3 2028 on a 15-year term. Google will use the carbon-free output as a foundational power source for a potential Utah data centre, with an option to expand offtake toward nearly 1 gigawatt total by June 2030.

Why it matters: It's the largest enhanced-geothermal PPA ever signed, showing next-generation geothermal emerging as a serious, always-on complement to solar, gas and nuclear for AI power.
ThinkGeoEnergy → (Sept 1)

Anthropic signed $517bn in compute agreements over the past 11 months, IPO reportedly days away

FinanceTech

According to The Information, Anthropic has locked in roughly $517 billion in compute capacity since last October — securing at least 14.8 gigawatts of power — far above the roughly $180 billion through 2029 it had previously guided investors to expect, led by an 11-gigawatt, $300 billion-plus, 10-year deal with Amazon and Google. The disclosure lands just weeks after reports that Anthropic's IPO prospectus is imminent, targeting a valuation near $2 trillion.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest compute-buying sprees ever disclosed by a single AI lab, and a real test of whether public markets will underwrite AI-lab economics built on tens of billions in forward compute commitments.
Data Center Dynamics → (Sept 7, citing The Information)
4

Electricity-free cooling system for data centres prototyped by researchers in Germany and Japan

TechEnergy

Researchers from Germany's Karlsruhe Institute of Technology and Japan's University of Tsukuba built a working elastocaloric cooler using two ultra-thin nickel-titanium shape-memory foils: one converts waste heat into mechanical motion, and the second uses that motion to trigger a phase change that produces cold, with no electrically driven actuator required. At an 86°C source temperature the prototype achieved a 4°C cooling differential, published in Nature Energy on August 28.

Why it matters: Cooling can account for up to 40% of a data centre's power bill, so a technology that recycles a chip's own waste heat into refrigeration — rather than consuming grid electricity to generate it — attacks one of the industry's largest and fastest-growing costs.
Data Center Dynamics → (Sept 2)
5

Canada: Ottawa releases data centre policy framework signed by Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic

CanadaPolicy

AI Minister Evan Solomon unveiled Canada's Responsible Data Centre Development Principles in Markham, Ontario, with 23 AI, cloud and real estate companies signing on. The five non-binding principles require projects to create lasting local benefits, avoid shifting electricity costs onto Canadians, minimize water and environmental impacts, be transparent about local impacts, and deliver strategic value, setting a national baseline alongside provincial and municipal rules.

Why it matters: It's Ottawa's first coordinated national response to the trust deficit fueling municipal moratoriums, and getting every major hyperscaler and AI lab to sign on gives the federal government real leverage even without binding law.
Canada's National Observer → (Sept 3)
6

PJM asks FERC to force new data centres to bring their own power or face curtailment

EnergyPolicy

PJM's board has proposed a one-time "backstop" capacity auction (Sept 30–Oct 21, 2026, cost cap raised to $555/MW-day from $325) to cover a 6.8-gigawatt shortfall, alongside a framework requiring new large loads without dedicated generation to curtail consumption during grid emergencies starting June 1, 2027. The market monitor attributes $29.4 billion in higher capacity costs across the last four auctions to data centre load growth, which PJM projects could add 70 gigawatts of demand across its 13-state, 67-million-person footprint by 2038.

Why it matters: It's the clearest move yet by the largest US grid operator to shift reliability risk and cost back onto data centres rather than existing ratepayers — a template other regional grids are watching closely.
Utility Dive → (board proposal released July 27; FERC process ongoing)
7

Floating data centres head to Texas as Samsung Heavy Industries and Mousterian target zero-water cooling

TechEnergy

Samsung Heavy Industries and Mousterian Corporation are planning moored floating data centres for Texas and other US markets, each with 50 megawatts of IT capacity and deployment beginning in 2028. The design uses non-evaporative water-surface cooling that consumes no potable water and returns zero process discharge, following China's operational 24MW underwater server deployment near Shanghai, which has cut power use by 22.8% and land use by over 90%.

Why it matters: Water-stressed states are increasingly blocking data centres over cooling-water demand, and a design that needs none at all — while freeing up scarce land — could unlock sites that would otherwise face community and permitting opposition.
Daily Commercial News (ConstructConnect) → (Sept 3)
8

Broadcom posts record $29.6B quarter, guides to "double-double" AI revenue through 2028

Finance

Broadcom reported record consolidated revenue of $29.6 billion for its fiscal Q3, up 86% year-on-year, with AI semiconductor revenue surging 221% to $16.7 billion on demand from custom-chip customers including Google, Anthropic and OpenAI. CEO Hock Tan raised full-year AI revenue guidance to $58 billion and guided to doubling AI revenue to $115 billion in fiscal 2027, then doubling again to $230 billion in fiscal 2028.

Why it matters: Broadcom's custom AI-chip business is now a direct barometer of hyperscaler capex commitments, and a guided quadrupling of AI revenue by 2028 implies the industry's biggest buyers are locking in silicon demand years in advance.
Data Center Dynamics → (Sept 4; results reported Sept 2)
9

AI data centres could nearly triple water use by 2030 — but Nvidia's 45°C cooling design says it can nearly eliminate it

TechEnergy

A new analysis finds global data centre water consumption for cooling could rise from 222 billion liters in 2025 to 644 billion liters by 2030 without changes. Microsoft and AWS say they've cut water-use intensity 25% and 37% respectively since 2022 through closed-loop cooling, and Nvidia says its DSX system — running coolant at 45°C instead of a typical 32°C — can rely on simple fans instead of evaporative chillers, "eliminating water consumption almost entirely."

Why it matters: Water has become one of the sharpest points of community opposition to new campuses; a genuine engineering fix rather than a PR pledge could defuse fights in drought-prone sites, though it shifts the burden onto electricity demand instead.
Tech Xplore → (Sept 7)
10

Canada's data centre rules are diverging sharply by province

CanadaPolicy

Ontario is proposing premium electricity rates instead of subsidies for facilities over 1 MW, with its public comment period closing September 12; Alberta favours a "bring your own power" model with no incentives; Quebec wants to double rates for new large facilities to about 13 cents/kWh, a move operators are challenging before the Régie de l'énergie; British Columbia caps available power at roughly 400 MW over two years and scores bids partly on data sovereignty and First Nations participation; and Saskatchewan requires new data centres to be Canadian-owned and self-supply power.

Why it matters: The province a company builds in now effectively sets its power cost and approval odds, creating a genuine regulatory patchwork that investors and developers have to navigate case by case.
Digital Journal → (Aug 21; Ontario consultation deadline Sept 12)

SoftBank's SB Energy files for Nasdaq IPO seeking up to $7B, with Nvidia and OpenAI already in

Finance

SB Energy, SoftBank's AI infrastructure and power-generation unit, filed to list on Nasdaq under "SBE," targeting a $5 billion to $7 billion raise at a valuation above $50 billion despite reporting a $3.21 billion net loss on just $138.7 million of revenue for the first half of 2026. Nvidia has committed to a $1.5 billion private placement at the IPO price, and OpenAI holds warrants worth roughly $5.5 billion; pricing is expected the week of September 21.

Why it matters: A pre-revenue power-and-data-centre developer targeting a $50 billion valuation, backstopped by the same companies that would buy its capacity, is a stark illustration of how circular — and stretched — AI infrastructure financing has become.

12

Meta's $1.2B Kuna, Idaho data centre goes live with closed-loop cooling

TechConstruction

Meta officially switched on its 750,000-square-foot data centre in Kuna, Idaho — its 28th US facility and first in the state — capping a four-year, $1.2 billion build. It's only the second Meta site to use a closed-loop, radiator-style cooling system that needs no extra water for most of the year, backed by more than $100 million Meta invested in a dedicated water and wastewater system for the city.

Why it matters: Water-free cooling at gigawatt-adjacent scale directly answers the drought and water-stress objections driving local opposition elsewhere, and Meta positioning it as a template suggests closed-loop designs are moving from pilot to standard practice.
Data Center Dynamics → (Sept 4)

Vantage Data Centers is in early talks with advisers on a potential IPO that could raise about $10 billion at a roughly $100 billion valuation — which would be the largest data centre IPO ever — or alternatively an outright sale. Backers include DigitalBridge, Silver Lake, AustralianSuper, ADIA and GIC.

Why it matters: A raise or sale at this scale would be a major signal of how far public and private capital markets are willing to go to fund the AI data centre buildout.

Canada's CPP Investments and Equinix complete $4B acquisition of Nordic operator atNorth

CanadaFinance

Canada Pension Plan Investment Board and Equinix closed their US$4 billion joint acquisition of atNorth, with CPP Investments taking roughly 51% ownership (a US$1.3 billion commitment) and Partners Group re-investing for a 10% stake. atNorth operates eight data centres across all five Nordic countries plus four mega-sites under development, and will keep operating under its own brand.

Why it matters: It's one of Canada's largest pension funds directly bankrolling a hyperscale-adjacent data centre platform abroad, underscoring how aggressively Canadian capital is chasing AI infrastructure returns even as domestic projects face local pushback.
Equinix Newsroom → (Sept 2)

IBM brings Nighthawk r2 quantum processor online, claims 25x throughput jump

Tech

IBM has made its 120-qubit Nighthawk r2 processor broadly available on the IBM Quantum Platform. A new dissipative qubit-reset design eliminates the idle time chips spend resetting between circuit runs, pushing throughput past 100,000 circuits per second — a claimed 25-fold improvement over IBM's existing Heron fleet.

Why it matters: A step-change in commercial quantum throughput lands just as hyperscalers debate how quantum compute fits alongside GPU-heavy AI data centre buildouts already straining power and cooling budgets.
Data Center Dynamics → (Sept 4)

Montreal's 5C tops $1.4B raised to build AI campuses in Memphis, Phoenix and Ohio

CanadaFinance

Montreal-headquartered AI data centre developer 5C has secured $605 million in new Brookfield-led debt financing, adding to the $835 million it raised in 2025 for a combined total exceeding $1.4 billion. The funds will support acquisition and construction of a campus in Memphis, Tennessee, plus sites in Phoenix, Arizona and Ohio, as part of a roadmap exceeding 1.5 gigawatts of capacity.

Why it matters: A Canadian-headquartered developer raising over $1.4 billion to build American AI campuses shows Canadian capital and expertise increasingly financing the US buildout, not just projects at home.

17

PwC forecasts $31.6 trillion in global data centre spending through 2050, nearly half of it in the US

Finance

PwC's Global Data Centre Outlook, modeled by Oxford Economics across 46 countries, projects cumulative data centre capital spending of $31.6 trillion through 2050 as annual capex climbs from roughly $800 billion this year to $1.8 trillion by mid-century. The US is forecast to capture $15.1 trillion of that total — nearly half — followed by Asia Pacific at $8.2 trillion and Europe at $5.6 trillion; an accelerated-adoption scenario could push global spending as high as $50 trillion.

Why it matters: The scale dwarfs prior infrastructure build-outs like electrification or the railways, underscoring how central North America's data centre capacity remains to the global AI buildout — and how much is riding on power and permitting keeping pace.
PwC → (Sept 2)

Canada: Algoma Steel and Red Jar Energy pitch 400MW AI data centre for Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario

CanadaConstruction

Algoma Steel and private infrastructure firm Red Jar Energy Partners have submitted an application to Ontario's Independent Electricity System Operator for a 400-megawatt AI data centre in the Algoma region, targeting an October 2029 in-service date. The companies say the project is at an early, non-binding stage and would still need to clear Ontario's newly published data centre approval playbook and local review.

Why it matters: It's another gigawatt-scale AI proposal landing in Ontario just as Oakville's moratorium holds and Mississauga's own one-year pause heads to a final council vote — testing whether the province's new framework can get ahead of municipal backlash.

19

Canada: Pembina Institute finds Meta's $13B Alberta data centre could add $460/year to power bills

CanadaEnergy

A Pembina Institute analysis found Meta's planned $13 billion AI data centre in Alberta could raise residential electricity costs by $270 to $460 annually between 2027 and 2031, concluding that market impacts from the new load will "far outweigh" the province's claimed 6% transmission-bill savings. The report ties the increase to Alberta's "Bring Your Own Generation" rules, which let data centres connect to the grid before their own power plants are running.

Why it matters: Albertans already pay among Canada's highest electricity rates, and the finding directly undercuts the province's pitch that data-centre growth will lower costs for residents — fueling the affordability backlash already visible at government town halls.
The Tyee → (Sept 2)
20

Canada: Alberta municipality signed Kevin O'Leary's Wonder Valley data centre deal before disclosing it at public hearing

CanadaPolicy

Newly surfaced records show Greenview, Alberta signed a binding letter of intent with O'Leary Productions USA LLC in October 2024 for the proposed 65-square-kilometre Wonder Valley AI data centre — weeks before a November 12 public hearing on the zoning change needed to permit it, at which the project itself was never mentioned. Wonder Valley, branded the "world's largest" AI data centre, would draw seven times the power output of BC's Site C dam and remains short of required provincial, Indigenous and regulatory approvals.

Why it matters: It's a fresh transparency scandal feeding Canada's broader data-centre trust deficit, showing a municipality committing to a massive project before residents had any chance to weigh in on it.
Canada's National Observer → (Sept 2)
