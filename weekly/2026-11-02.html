Data Centre Weekly Overview

Weekly Overview

October 27 – November 2, 2026 · North America focus
Week in review: Hardware speed and density led the week: Nvidia's DSX Pod promises AI data centres in as little as 14 days, Oracle will sell Google's newest TPUs, and Microsoft's Maia 300 and new cooling chips target efficiency per megawatt. Power remained the binding constraint, with gas turbines sold out through 2029, so developers turned to on-site fuel cells and gas, SMRs, virtual power plants and utility-funded generation, while FirstEnergy's pipeline hit 26 gigawatts. In Canada, Anthropic anchored EverWind's Nova Scotia campus, Bell plans up to 900 MW more in Saskatchewan, Alberta's grid allowance is fully subscribed and Hydro-Québec defended a near-doubling of data centre rates. Capital markets stayed enormous: Anthropic filed for an IPO disclosing about $417 billion of compute commitments, Microsoft posted $45.2 billion of quarterly capex, and Nvidia and AMD committed $4 billion to Meta.

1

Nvidia unveils DSX Pod: a prebuilt AI data centre it says deploys in as little as 14 days

TechConstruction

The containerized pod packs seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with networking, cabling and cooling preinstalled, cutting deployment at a prepared site from about six months to as little as 14 days, per Nvidia. It is being developed with 17 partners including Dell, HPE, Schneider Electric and Vertiv, with availability slated for early 2027.

Why it matters: Factory-built capacity could compress build timelines that are now gated by construction.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 21))
2

Oracle Cloud to offer Google TPUs in multi-gigawatt deal

Tech

Oracle will make Google's eighth-generation TPUs (TPU 8t for training, TPU 8i for inference) available on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, the first large cloud outside Google to offer them. Capacity launches in 2027; Oracle runs the data centres and supplies power, cooling and networking, with rack-scale direct liquid cooling.

Why it matters: A multi-gigawatt commitment to non-Nvidia silicon could reshape how AI campuses are designed and powered.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 28))
3

Microsoft unveils Maia 300 inference chip, claiming 40% more tokens per megawatt

Tech

Announced October 12, Maia 300 is Microsoft's third-generation in-house AI accelerator, built for inference. Microsoft claims about 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU systems and plans multi-gigawatt Maia deployment in 2027. Maia is not yet offered to Azure customers, and these are company claims.

Why it matters: Custom silicon aimed at power efficiency targets the binding constraint on new AI capacity.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 13))
4

xMEMS solid-state micro-cooling chips target AI storage hotspots

Tech

The XMC-2400 and XMC-4800 piezoMEMS chips move air with no moving blades, drawing about 150 to 240 mW. They target hotspots on enterprise SSDs that liquid cold plates miss; xMEMS positions them as a complement to rack-level cooling, not a replacement.

Why it matters: Cooling AI racks now means chasing hotspots beyond the GPU.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 28))
5

ZutaCore unveils two-phase flow boiling design for AI cold plates

Tech

Announced at the OCP Global Summit, the design uses 0.6 to 0.7 litres per minute per kW versus 1.5 to 2.0 for single-phase cooling, according to ZutaCore, with cold plates as thin as 11.6 mm. Deployments for Nvidia RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition are underway this quarter, per the company.

Why it matters: Higher-density cooling with less coolant flow targets a key limit on rack power.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 26))
6

Project Jupiter tops out first building, switching to Bloom fuel cells for on-site power

EnergyConstruction

Clayco and Kiewit topped out the first 855,000 sq ft building of the Doña Ana County, New Mexico campus leased by Oracle for OpenAI. On-site Bloom Energy fuel cells replace gas turbines and diesel generators in the design, cutting nitrogen oxide emissions by about 92%, according to the article.

Why it matters: A major campus is moving to fuel-cell microgrid power instead of waiting on grid or pipeline.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 27))
7

FirstEnergy data centre pipeline reaches 26 GW, with West Virginia at 6 GW

Energy

FirstEnergy's pipeline grew 4.8% in Q3 to 26 GW, and its West Virginia portion rose from 4.3 GW to 6 GW. Contracted large-load demand is 7.9 GW, with about 4 GW more under negotiation.

Why it matters: Utilities are now building generation specifically for data centre load.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 30))
8

Google expands Voltus virtual power plant deal to more than 1 GW in PJM

EnergyTech

Google will buy 1,070 MW of additional flexible capacity from Voltus in PJM, on top of 100 MW committed in June. Voltus pools batteries, thermostats and industrial load, under a contract running through 2040.

Why it matters: Developers are paying to create grid flexibility instead of waiting for new generation.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 23))
9

AEP and X-energy study 960 MW SMR plant to power Amazon in Indiana

Energy

X-energy and AEP will study an advanced nuclear plant at a retired coal station in Sullivan County, Indiana, with Amazon funding development work. It is at study stage only and no timeline has been disclosed.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are backing new-build nuclear on retired coal sites to secure baseload power.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 20))
10

Microsoft to fund 1,100 MW of Indiana generation under 20-year NiSource deal

Energy

Microsoft will pay for all generation and transmission serving its La Porte, Indiana campus under a 20-year agreement with NiSource, with service starting in 2028.

Why it matters: It is a template for large users funding their own grid build-out while protecting ratepayers.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 19))
11

Venture Global lines up gas turbines for behind-the-meter data centre power

Energy

Venture Global signed frame agreements with GE Vernova and Baker Hughes for up to 16 more 720 MW combined-cycle power islands, targeting up to 10 GW of compute by end-2034. Roughly $17.2 billion is contemplated if all orders proceed.

Why it matters: Gas-fired on-site generation is becoming a core route to AI power.
Source → (DataM Intelligence (Oct 19))
12

Hyperscale AI spending tops $700 billion, but gas turbines are sold out through 2029

EnergyPolicy

Citing Industrial Info Resources, announced spending by Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Google should exceed $700 billion this year. The US has 53 GW of operating data centre load, and larger gas turbines are sold out through 2029.

Why it matters: Equipment shortages and local opposition are now the binding constraints on growth.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 27))
13

Bell Canada plans to quadruple capacity at Saskatchewan AI campus, up to 900 MW more

CanadaEnergyConstruction

Bell plans to add up to 900 MW to its Saskatchewan campus, which could reach 1.2 GW and draw more than $50 billion of investment. The campus would use natural gas generation and closed-loop cooling that needs no municipal water, with phases tied to customer commitments. This item is from Sept 15 and is included for Canadian context.

Why it matters: Gas-powered, water-light design is emerging as a Canadian template for gigawatt campuses.
Source → (Baxtel (Sep 15))
14

Anthropic signs first Canadian data centre lease at EverWind's Nova Scotia campus

CanadaEnergy

EverWind announced more than C$10 billion (about US$7.1 billion) of private investment at its Point Tupper site in Richmond County, Nova Scotia. Anthropic confirmed a long-term lease for the first phase.

Why it matters: It anchors one of Canada's largest AI campuses and ties growth to Atlantic Canada's wind resources.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 19))
15

Hydro-Québec defends proposal to roughly double electricity rates for data centres

CanadaPolicyEnergy

Hydro-Québec proposes a new rate for data centres over 5 MW averaging about 13 cents per kWh, roughly double the current large-power price. Régie de l'énergie hearings began in early October and continue in December.

Why it matters: Quebec's cheap hydro has drawn data centres; a rate reset could redirect Canadian investment.
Source → (CTV News (early Oct))
16

Alberta's large-load allowance is fully subscribed as data centre requests top 19 GW

CanadaEnergyPolicy

Alberta's Data Centre Regulation favours projects that bring their own generation, and the interim 1,200 MW allowance for new large loads is now fully taken. AESO's list showed roughly 19,565 MW of requested data centre load as of July 30.

Why it matters: Alberta is Canada's most active test of how a grid can absorb AI-scale demand.
Source → (Government of Alberta (current page; request figures as of Jul 30))
17

Canada plans C$1.5-1.85B, 60 MW secure data centre near Ottawa for CSE

CanadaConstruction

Defence Construction Canada posted an advance procurement notice on October 7 for a 60 MW facility for the Communications Security Establishment. Procurement is expected in winter 2027 and the government has not yet approved proceeding.

Why it matters: A sovereign-compute build is a rare public-sector data centre project in Canada.
Source → (BNN Bloomberg (Oct 15))
18

Anthropic files for IPO, disclosing about $417B in compute commitments

FinanceTech

Anthropic's Nasdaq registration, filed Oct 26, discloses about $417 billion in future payments to compute providers.

Why it matters: The filing quantifies how much AI labs are betting on data centre capacity.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 27))
19

Microsoft posts $45.2B quarterly capex and extends data centre useful life to 25 years

Finance

Microsoft Cloud revenue reached $63.9 billion, up 30%, with Azure up 48%. Capex was $45.2 billion for the quarter with more than $50 billion guided, and 24 data centres were added in the quarter.

Why it matters: Spending at this scale sets the pace for the whole supply chain.
Source → (Baxtel (Oct 29))
20

Nvidia and AMD commit $4B to Meta as it plans a $97.5B equity raise for AI buildout

TechFinance

Nvidia announced a $3 billion investment in Meta and AMD a $1 billion commitment, as Meta disclosed plans to raise $97.5 billion through an equity offering for AI infrastructure. Meta will deploy more Nvidia Vera Rubin systems, while AMD Helios racks are in testing.

Why it matters: Chipmakers are financing their own customers to lock in long-term demand.
Source → (Data Center Knowledge (Oct 29))
